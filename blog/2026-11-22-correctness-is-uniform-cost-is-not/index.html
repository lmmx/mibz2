Correctness is uniform, cost is not¶

Every route in mibz2's bzip2 decoder is exact on every block, but what each costs depends on the block, so we search for speed in many places at once.

22 November 2026 · 12 min read · Research in progress

mibz2 is a bzip2 decoder in Rust, and most of its time goes on inverting the Burrows–Wheeler transform (BWT) of each block. Real compressed data offers that inversion no single way in. A block of a Wikidata dump is a JSON skeleton, copies of text already decoded, and random identifiers that no local structure predicts, in proportions that change from block to block. So the decoder is fast through several exact constructions, each cheap on a different structure, and its mathematics is as varied as its data.

The takeaways:

  1. Correctness is uniform and cost is not. A correctness proof needs one route; speed on real data needs many. Every route mibz2 ships returns exactly the reference output on every valid block. What differs between them is cost, and cost depends on the block and the machine.
  2. Each fast route bets on a structure. The bet is a cost hypothesis: when the block has this structure, this construction is cheaper. Finding structures worth betting on is the research.
  3. When the data refuses a hypothesis, there are three responses: weaken it; certify it more cheaply; or change the representation so it is no longer needed. Refusals are where the next construction comes from.
  4. Global changes of representation are rare. We have had one. Below it we pursue local constructions on purpose, and they may yet reveal a general one. We do not know how complete the family is.
  5. Scope: one decoder, bzip2's inverse BWT, judged on one workstation (an i9-10900K with a 20 MiB L3), with Wikidata as the main corpus. Nothing here is proved in Lean yet, and the literary analogy in §5 is not evidence.

1. One proof, two fates¶

lean-zip is a DEFLATE implementation in Lean whose round trip, inflate (deflateRaw data level) = .ok data, rests on "more than 1,100 theorems". Its README calls the result a ratchet: "the obligation inflate (deflate x) = x either still holds or the build goes red", so agents can optimise it hard without breaking it. It also says where it stands: its decoder trails "the C-grade decoders (miniz_oxide by ~1.45×, libdeflate by ~2.5×)". The theorem guarantees every optimisation is correct. It does not say which will be fast.

mibz2 has the same gap. Two recent changes are both byte-exact on every block, and the i9 judged them very differently at 10 threads on four copies of the Wikidata benchmark:

The construction stack cut 5% of CPU time in the inverse BWT, and its 10-thread wall time did not move. The block reader shortened the serial stage that feeds the workers, saved less CPU, and cut wall time by 7% alone and 10% on top of #739. With ten workers sharing one cache and one memory bus, saving work and saving time come apart. Correctness certifies that a change is admissible. Its cost lives in the data and the machine.

2. What heterogeneity looks like in a Wikidata block¶

Heterogeneity is measurable, and it shows at three scales.

Within a block. Most of a Wikidata block is a repeated JSON skeleton of keys and punctuation, which bzip2 already turns into long runs, cheap to invert. A smaller share is repetition at a distance: sha1 references and repeated strings, copies of text already decoded. Then there are the statement identifiers. They are random 122-bit GUIDs, and the birthday bound means their contexts shatter after about four or five hex digits. In the classical-tools audit they are 53.6–67.1% of the width-1 rows the Open kernel retains, and 28.7% of its emission time on the six blocks measured. Three structures, three costs, in one block.

Between corpora. One exact change gains differently on different data. The block reader of #745 on CI's paired bench, at 4 threads:

The change speeds up the serial stage that reads the compressed stream. The barely compressible records and random gain 6–7%, while runs and onebyte, compressed hundreds or hundreds of thousands of times, show nothing. That fits the serial stage's share of the work growing with the compressed bytes, though the bench does not measure that share per corpus.

Between families of data. A hindsight oracle chose, per block, the fastest of thirteen settings of one Open construction plus two walks. On the 431 Wikidata blocks it would save 3.17% over a fixed setting; on six non-Wikidata blocks, 12.8–13.1% (single-threaded, on a research host). The same family of kernels has a different headroom on different data.

3. Exact routes, each betting on a structure¶

The shipped dispatch, read from src/ on master and set out in the proof programme (§5.1), looks like this for OpenCells, the kernel workers hold at 8 threads and above:

A block's records are read when enough of its symbols are runs. On that route, the Open kernel takes the blocks its count gates admit, plus a dense band up to r/n 0.28 under a cap on retained rows (#703). Everything refused goes to Segmented, the walk that accepts every block.

The figure's point is its two kinds of line. Each box is a theorem of one shape: for route i, Pᵢ(x) ⇒ Fᵢ(x) = R(x), where R is the reference, the walk of the LF permutation on a valid block. For every shipped route, Pᵢ is only the entry condition, a valid block. Each is tested against R on arbitrary columns, not just on Wikidata. The gates are not part of any correctness argument. Each chooses for speed or for memory. Correctness is uniform across the routes; the structure each bets on is a cost hypothesis.

The Open kernel is the route where the unit of computation changes. The LF walk visits one row at a time, and on a block of runs most of those visits are predictable. Open treats the thin runs as strips and computes first-return maps between them, reached through Rauzy–Veech induction. The walk then jumps from strip to strip rather than from row to row. Its proof is in the journal. Inside it, two more bets: a width certificate (below) and repetition of a short marked cycle.

4. When the data refuses¶

Three refusals from the research lines show where the work is.

  • A solver that never fires. A pure-arithmetic solver for two-letter Sturmian blocks inverts them in O(log n) steps by the Euclidean algorithm, and it is exact on 5,627 Sturmian BWTs. On Wikidata, gcd(w) = 1 on every block and 0.00% of Rauzy–Veech steps have a quotient of 2 or more. The hypothesis is sound, cheap to test, and true of no real block.
  • The GUID wall. No predicate on local structure picks the GUID rows out for a shortcut, because random identifiers have none. The escape would be to store them other than as BWT rows, which bzip2's format does not allow.
  • The copy sector. Three independent mechanisms found the repeated sha1 and text rows and cut the hops spent on them by 16–58%, byte-exact. All three lose 1.14–2.64× end to end, because finding a copy costs more than emitting it saves: 10.1–10.5 ns a saved hop against 5.79 ns a row. The structure is real and common; recognising it is the obstruction, on the research host.

Each refusal has three possible answers, and each has an example:

  • Weaken the hypothesis. Open's correctness never needed the density line r/n < 0.15. A cap checked by the first pass replaced it, which admitted the dense band up to 0.28 (#703).
  • Certify it more cheaply. The fast elimination loop needs a block without long spirals. Rather than detect spirals, one comparison checks that the widest strip is at most 32,768 rows. No elimination step widens a node, so the widest strip bounds every spiral's length.
  • Change the representation. Open itself: the first return to the thin runs needs no assumption on the block, where earlier routes were gated by density and budget.

The first two add or move a branch of the dispatch. Only the third removes branches, and it is what keeps the decoder from becoming a tree that grows with every regime found.

We propose to keep a map of these moves: from which hypothesis, by which move, to which, with the witness, the status and the measured effect. The obstructions go in it too, each with its scope. A failed implementation is not an impossible construction. The copy sector lost on one machine against one base; only an exact obstruction with a witness rules a construction out, and only within its scope.

5. The stutter¶

The process in §4 has a shape, and an essay of Gilles Deleuze's names it well. In "He Stuttered", Deleuze separates a character who stutters in speech, which a writer can report, from a writer who makes the language itself stutter. In the second case the variation is not laid over a fixed language from outside. It comes from within the language's own system, putting it in continuous variation and disequilibrium. His examples include Melville, Kafka, Beckett, the poet Gherasim Luca and Charles Péguy, who moves from a word to its near-synonym with a slight shift of meaning each time.

The mapping to a decoder is close:

  • The fixed thing said is the conclusion, = R. It never moves.
  • The reported stutter is a special case bolted onto a uniform decoder: a branch that notices a regime and handles it on the side, while the decoder's objects stay what they were.
  • The language stuttering is a construction whose mathematical objects change with the data's structure. Rows become strips and returns; a hypothesis returns weakened, certified, or replaced by a representation that does not need it, and each return changes the question.

The difference in what drives the variation matters. For Deleuze it is the writer, working the language. Here it is the data's resistance, worked by us. The program is the language the proof is written in, so a proof that follows the data must vary with it too.

The analogy has limits, and they should be plain. Deleuze wrote about literature and language. He made no claim about algorithms or proofs, and we claim none for him. The analogy names the shape of the process. It is not evidence that the process works: the evidence is in §§1–4.

6. Local and global¶

Open is the one global change of representation so far. It is a classical idea, the induced map of Poincaré and Kakutani, reached through Rauzy–Veech induction. Everything since has been bound to a regime: the dense band, the width certificate, the block reader.

Local constructions can lead to a global one. In mathematics a different proof of a solved case has opened new routes before: the Riemann Hypothesis for curves over finite fields was proved first by Weil, then by Stepanov's elementary method, completed by Bombieri, and it was that method's descendants that travelled (Two centuries of tools). Inverse BWT has the same structure. The LF walk, cycle decomposition and first-return induction are different constructive proofs that the BWT is invertible, and each gives a different decoder. The proof programme names seven points, L0 to L6, where one step of the proof can be replaced; Open replaced the classical route at L3, L4 and L5.

What we cannot yet say is how complete the family is. The oracle's 3.17% bounds only the alternatives it chose between: one construction's two parameters and two walks. It says that family is nearly saturated on Wikidata, and nothing about a construction outside it. The 10-thread plateau points the same way: every construction gain of the last twelve research cycles left the decoder's DRAM traffic at 10 workers at 4.91 bytes per output byte.

7. What is proved, measured and open¶

Proved: nothing in Lean yet. The plan is the specification first, the LF walk, then the Open kernel as a conditional route, then the dispatch lemma that joins the routes. Measured: the i9 figures in §1, CI's per-corpus bench in §2, and each route's byte-exact parity against the reference. Open: whether the local constructions we keep finding are pieces of one representation we have not yet seen, or whether real data needs many routes for good.
