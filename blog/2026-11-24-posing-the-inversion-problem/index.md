# Posing the inversion problem

The search for a breakthrough became a measurable problem with a cost referee, measured ceilings and a law that bounds one class of routes.

24 November 2026 · 9 min read · [Research synthesis](<#4-the-problem-as-it-now-stands>)

The work began as a search for a new mathematical basis for undoing bzip2's block sort and ended with a problem that can be stated, measured and refereed. Three things now hold it in place. A cost referee says what would count as beating the floor, and the method for finding candidates is set apart from it ([What else moves together](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/blog/2026-11-24-what-else-moves-together/index.md>)). Oracle measurements say where whole-decoder time can be won at all: up to 26% at ten workers, hidden until now behind the serial scanner ([Masked bottlenecks in a two-stage decoder](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/blog/2026-11-24-the-scanner-hides-the-emitter/index.md>)). And the mathematics has a map of what has been ruled out, a conservation law that bounds one whole class of routes, and a short list of open threads. This post gathers them, drawing every figure from [the formulation report](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/b34a05daefefc81d5ab2d2cba4f58752e7d4287a/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reports/2026-11-24-from-search-to-formulated-problem.md>) and the records it cites.

## 1\. The formulation, and two corrections

The problem statement agreed by three independent answers asks for one uniform program, exact on every column, that builds its structures from the column within fixed budgets and pays at most half the old floor on every block. The currency is real machine cost: unpredictable memory reads (Q), the length of the chain of reads that wait on one another (D), passes over the data (P, at most two) and working memory (S), with the cost of building every structure included ([journal §33](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). How candidates are found, by changing what moves together and by making existing steps yield more, is the subject of the earlier post, and the cost rules referee those candidates only once they exist.

Two statements made in earlier summaries, including ours, had to be withdrawn ([journal §42](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)):

- **G1 is not a lower bound on machine cost.** It bounds charges in a calculus of rigid interval moves; carrying it into machine cost needs a bridge theorem that holds for most of the old family but not all of it.
- **The 16% figure was never a ceiling.** It described one intervention, supplying the emitter's codes, at one thread. The four-arm test has since shown that the same intervention is worth nothing measurable at ten workers until the scanner is removed, and then a further 8%.

The referee's rulings close the remaining loopholes. A breakthrough means fewer than one unpredictable read per [width-1 row](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/blog/2026-11-24-halving-the-single-row-steps/index.md>), with the target at half of one, within two passes; a large cut in the chain of dependent reads is a real but lesser find. The number of live cursors is limited to about one per letter of the alphabet, so a route that needed some two thousand concurrent cursors to reach its figure was ruled inadmissible ([journal §49](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

## 2\. The decoder as a system

The four-arm oracle supplied each of the decoder's two stages in turn, outside the clock, and measured what the whole decoder gained. Emission is the lever at one to six workers, worth 15 to 17%; at ten workers the serial scanner is, worth up to 19% on its own and 26% together with emission ([journal §44](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). The scanner does the Huffman decoding only; undoing move-to-front and run-length coding, the inversion and emission all run in the workers.

The decoder's work is now divided into six mathematical targets, each tied to a stage and, where one exists, to an oracle ceiling: emission of the inversion (T1), its construction (T2), move-to-front and run-length decoding (T3), the Huffman scan (T4), riders across stages (T5) and the final run-length expansion and checksum (T6). Only T1, T4 and T5 have ceilings so far; an oracle that splits the workers' time is the next measurement ([the targets](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/math-targets/targets.tsv>)).

Beyond the four arms, the decoder is being decomposed stage by stage and resource by resource, in the manner of [metabolic control analysis](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_control_analysis>), which asks how much each step of a pathway controls its overall rate ([the brief](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/decomposition.md>)). Three independent answers to that brief were compared and merged into one measurement plan ([journal §51](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/b34a05daefefc81d5ab2d2cba4f58752e7d4287a/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>); [the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/b34a05daefefc81d5ab2d2cba4f58752e7d4287a/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>)). They agree that one scanner and its workers is a coarse picture rather than a decomposition: the plan adds a serial tail for retiring and reordering blocks, treats move-to-front decoding and run formation as one fused activity, and treats the variation between blocks as a covariate. Two corrections from [one of the answers](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/b34a05daefefc81d5ab2d2cba4f58752e7d4287a/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/external/chatgpt-decomposition.md>) shape the design. Requested threads are not actual workers, since a request for ten or more runs seven workers, so the mechanism study names its worker counts directly. And the scanner's waiting is not proof of too few workers: admission is limited by the blocks issued but not yet taken in order, and by a 12 MiB threshold on finished output, so a slow early block can stall the scanner while later blocks wait to be written. And the summation theorem of control analysis is no test of completeness: for a stage whose rate is the minimum of two others, the coefficients sum to two under slowdowns and to zero under speedups, so completeness has to come from accounting identities instead. Each stage will be slowed by 20%, with 10% follow-ups to check linearity, at 1, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 20 workers, and a discrete-event simulation of the decoder, driven by each block's measured service times, is the primary model, with per-stage rooflines as constraints. A profile of the scanner across all twenty worker counts comes first, queued as [\#838](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/838>), and a parallel scanner gated by worker count follows it.

## 3\. The mathematics

Every route so far wins one cost by paying another

Schematic, with figures from the reviews ([the six senses](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-critique.md>), [the fourth cycle](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-cycle4-critique.md>)) and journal §§39 and 41. Routes that need few unpredictable reads take far more than two passes; routes that shorten the chain of waiting reads leave the reads where they were; routes that share reads need repeated text, which novel random IDs lack. With every charge counted, no route reaches the middle.

Twelve candidate senses of moving together were built, measured on the six real blocks and reviewed, and none brought the reads below one per width-1 row once every charge was counted. The best composed routes cost 1.3 to 2.8 reads per width-1 row all in, against 4.3 to 6.9 for [Open](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/blog/2026-11-19-open-returns/index.md>)'s own walk measured the same way ([the fourth cycle's review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-cycle4-critique.md>)). Pointer doubling and independent-set contraction reported zero unpredictable reads, but at 117 to about 600 passes, and building their inputs costs reads after all; both are known list-ranking algorithms for external memory ([the review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-critique.md>)). Landmark induction, recounted, brought the chain of waiting reads to 0.07–0.22% of the block, a record that has been reproduced, but left the reads at about one per row ([journal §41](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). Four further objects, boundary chords, radix-fibre cones, quotient slabs and unitigs, were ruled out, two of them repeating an investigation from four days earlier ([the review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/carbon-cycle1-critique.md>)), and lazily contracting the thin runs was closed by a rule registered in advance, because removing interior rows fragmented the order of reads 4 to 9 times over ([journal §49](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

Of the sweep that keeps its form and loses its compactness

One result explains why so many routes fail in the same way. [Record 119](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/8cf099eb24d9943417cfccdf3763b54fbcd9c26b/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-closure-problem.md>) shows that a sweep over the block is itself an induction, and that composing steps preserves the *form* of the access schedule but not its *compactness* on high-entropy rows. On the first block, the shipped decoder's next-row addresses fall into 2,148 orderly segments, 0.061 per width-1 row; one round of composition shatters them into 44,814–52,319, some 21 to 24 times as many ([journal §42](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). The law accounts for that figure, and settles the closure problem negatively within its class: no cleverer sweep of this kind can compose without fragmenting. Smaller results stand beside it ([the review of records 118–125](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/monodromy-118-125.md>)):

- a lower bound on failing steps for every interleaving of monotone cursors ([record 118](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/ad169f87d657edca999d503b3637da1b6ca0a597/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-interleaved-orders.md>));
- the block length, the run count, the number of width-1 rows and the run-width histogram are free by-products of the Huffman scan, and parsing blocks in parallel is exact, as [lbzip2](<https://github.com/kjn/lbzip2>) already does ([record 122](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/c348f8f5135959cf6d3dbf318001797673ec7201/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-scanner-corrected.md>));
- an exact composable summary of each chunk for move-to-front, run-length decoding and the run records ([record 121](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a3facefcb7d927e8284381a299c6a80f0b58409f/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-scanner-summary.md>));
- building the structures by interval-exchange acquisition costs 0.10–0.34 reads per width-1 row, which puts construction back at its true scale ([journal §49](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0430e0f714688f8edee1ebc4c1d2bd2498cb1fcd/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

Five threads remain open. Acquisition is the binding cost on real blocks, and a search-free strip contraction projects about 0.6 reads per width-1 row but is unmeasured ([record 120](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/5d0795c81f073c44cf1b2f9d62f0ac190ca07dd3/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-thin-strip-acquisition.md>)). A representation outside record 119's class could compose without fragmenting, which by the law no cleverer sweep can. The relation that pairs the same offset in repeated JSON records is open again, since the obstruction claimed against it proved invariance only. Riders across stages are not excluded by the theorems against them, which rule out final values before the block ends but not deferred ones. And at the scanner there is engineering to do: a parallel scan gated by worker count, which the four-arm test prices at up to 19%.

## 4\. The problem as it now stands

Fix the machine (an i9-10900K with AVX2, ten cores and twenty threads), the six admitted blocks with synthetic hex beside them, and the referee's four figures with two passes allowed. The problem then has three parts.

1. **Mathematics.** On the random-looking content inside JSON, find a representation of the inversion whose acquisition and emission together cost under half an unpredictable read per width-1 row, and which lies outside record 119's class. A no-go result within a stated class also counts as progress.
2. **System.** Measure each stage's share of the time, and how much it controls the throughput, at every worker count from one to twenty; fit and validate a model; and use it to place the point at which the scanner should go parallel, and to price any candidate in seconds. The exchange rate from a read to a second comes from a regression over the 232 census blocks ([record 124](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/cf527324b20d183b68f6f3ab57e3d244096e3985/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-what-to-measure.md>)).
3. **Payoff.** The ceilings bound what either can deliver: emission is worth up to 15–17% at one to six workers and about 8% behind the scanner at ten; the scanner is worth up to 19% at ten; together they are worth up to 26% at ten.

The ceilings came from oracles run before any candidate was built, and the routes' figures from reviews made independently of the work they judged; most of the headlines shrank under that review. What remains is smaller than the morning hoped and much better defined: a referee for any candidate, a price for each stage, and a record of the routes already closed.
