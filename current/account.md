mibz2 0.5.4 is published. On Louis's measured ten-core machine, the decoder now uses fewer workers when that helps: seven at ten or more requested threads, and additional reductions at four, six or eight threads only when the process is pinned one CPU to each requested core. Those lower-count gains came from freeing a core for the other stages, not from easing cache contention. Release-tag scaling and crossover checks for 0.5.4 are queued; the downstream Wikidata pipeline's update is deferred.

## Why choose fewer workers?

The [earlier high-thread plan](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6913804367) improved when one worker was removed and more blocks were kept in flight. Follow-up tests admitted seven lazy workers with a 28-block window on the i9 from ten requested threads, and this shipped in [0.5.3](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6946439743). A release comparison found about 6.5% less wall time, 25% less processor time and 22% lower peak memory at twelve to twenty threads. At ten, that release comparison pinned the process to ten logical CPUs and accidentally prevented the trim from activating; its ten-thread row cannot confirm the trim. A separate [unpinned test](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6949008682) did confirm seven over ten workers there, with a wall-time ratio of 0.873.

At lower thread counts the answer depends on where the process may run. With one CPU per requested core, the team measured gains from six lazy workers at eight threads, five eager workers at six, and three eager workers at four. Without that pinning, the same trims were level or slower. The [0.5.4 plan](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6949841165) therefore applies those reductions only to the measured pinned shape; unpinned it trims only from ten threads. Other machines keep their prior plan. The [0.5.4 release](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6952096740) also reads machine facts once per process, fixing repeated reads that caused a small-input slowdown in the first release check. The replacement four-core check found no slower row, though its intervals were wide.

## What limits further scaling?

The [crossover test](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6947191226) for 0.5.3 found no team plan faster than the shipped worker plan. Its speed plateau began around eight or nine threads. A [diagnostic](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6948385717) found workers busy 93–95% of the time when unpinned, while memory reads per output byte rose as workers were added. It ruled out the scanner, output consumer and block window as the main starvation source in that test. Direct memory-controller counters were unavailable, so saturation of main-memory bandwidth remains a lead, not a finding. The unpinned result also invalidated the interpretation of pinned four-, six- and eight-thread gains as cache-contention evidence. That part of the worker-count model needs refitting.

A [fused finishing pass](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6947507581) was not adopted: its roughly 1.5% point gain missed the predeclared multiple-test bar. A compact-rule writer passed some counts but did not qualify as a released default. Work to reduce memory traffic or finishing cost remains separate from the measured worker-count policy.

## What is the independent research doing?

MONODROMY continues its mathematical programme on the induction and its representations. A stop-and-walk construction was implemented and tested, then [not adopted](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6922810916) under its frozen performance rule. A parallel compaction and several word-storage ideas likewise produced useful structure without a qualifying production gain. Its later worker-count model is now being revised because the lower-count pinned results also reflected scheduling, as the [unpinned test](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6949008682) exposed. Those research claims do not by themselves alter the released decoder.

## What reaches users next?

[Version 0.5.4](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/releases/tag/v0.5.4) passed release checks and was published on 17 November. Its tag has release-scaling and crossover runs queued; no result from them is in this settled record. [wikidata-pq's open update](https://github.com/lmmx/wikidata-pq/pull/227) still targets 0.5.2 at inspection, while the Coordinator has deferred its bump because the pipeline is not being run today. Its cap and whole-pipeline benefit remain unsettled. No action from Louis is needed for the released decoder; the queued measurements and downstream test are the next evidence.
