mibz2 0.3.0 now ships a gated Cells decoder for fewer than six worker threads, with measured Wikidata gains at one, two and four threads on Louis's i9 and in the published comparison with 0.2.9. Louis granted narrow exceptions for memory use and imprecise high-thread measurements before release. A downstream update is open, release scaling is queued, and the team is preparing a separate study of whether Cells can help larger thread teams.

## What did the workstation diagnostic find?

Louis ran the [frozen live-reader comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6850347828) after its reviewed source branch entered the queue. The Coordinator downloaded the 16.2 MB bundle, verified its SHA-256, checked the recorded source, compiler, processor, input hashes, output hashes, schedule and 120 measured decodes plus nine checks, and recomputed the analysis byte-for-byte from saved rows. The archive omits three large Wikidata decoder byte files; their recorded hashes and lengths agree with an independently reconstructed oracle, but those missing bytes could not be compared again. The run was complete despite having no `outcome.txt`.

With observation hooks off, the experimental cells decoder's whole-read wall time was 0.882 of the baseline on Wikidata. With hooks on, it was 1.338 of the baseline. That change means the instrumented phase costs cannot explain the uninstrumented Wikidata saving quantitatively. The same limitation holds for one-byte input. On patterned input, where phase pricing remained admissible, the largest positive instrumented cost was writing output (about 177 ms); this is a measured diagnostic, not proof that writer work caused an uninstrumented slowdown. The original result belongs to its exact source, compiler and machine; it is not a production speed qualification.

## What did the follow-up change?

The team screened seven input families and built a [small-spine guarded writer candidate](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/379) to remove some output-construction bookkeeping on a narrow path. The independent reviewer [closed its specific missing Miri and mutation evidence objection](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6852460604) for the reviewed writer and fixture-only heads. A later preparation change was inspected separately; it did not inherit an unclaimed reviewer endorsement. On the final head, applicable Actions and legacy checks pass, including the full hosted Miri job with six cells witnesses. These checks support the scoped implementation and safety record, not a speed gain.

The first candidate comparison on one AMD host lost on both principal inputs. A final comparison on another AMD host, after genuine fixture and preparation repairs, still found no resolved Wikidata gain: cells/baseline wall ratio 1.005, with interval 0.995–1.014. Patterned input was about 1.72 times slower, one-byte input about 1.09 times slower, and native Wikidata peak memory was 31% higher. The two hosts cannot be pooled or used to isolate the guard's causal cost; there was no matched old-versus-new cells arm. The earlier 0.882 Wikidata result remains valid for its original build, not this changed candidate.

## What did the new comparison show?

Codex's [registered matched comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6857539994) decoded all seven protected input families, ten times each, with the previous Cells build, the selective build and the ordinary decoder. The selective build's whole-read Wikidata time was 0.976 of previous Cells, about 2.4% faster; patterned input was about 22.5% faster. The other families had no resolved loss against previous Cells, and peak memory was essentially unchanged. This establishes an incremental benefit for the experimental build on the tested AMD runner, not a shipped or workstation gain.

The ordinary default path in this pull request was 2.3% slower on Wikidata and 3.9% slower on text at four threads, with another resolved loss on random input. That [fails its existing rule](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6857539994) that every default arm remain level. The default comparison includes earlier Cells work and different compiled code, so the current evidence does not isolate which change caused those losses. Against the ordinary decoder, selective Cells was still about 76% slower on patterned input and 8% slower on one-byte input; its Wikidata peak memory was about 31% higher. The experimental speedup does not remove those adoption costs.

The original Researcher [resolved the source-identity hold](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6857443323) after independently checking the compiled mutation results and their correspondence to the pull request's native code. That decision covers the reviewed native path, not the whole pull request or a performance result. The current head's hosted checks, including Miri, and legacy pre-commit have now passed; passing checks do not override the failed performance rule.

## What did the matched workstation test show?

Louis [submitted the registered three-way comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6867819559). The Coordinator verified the bundle digest, all 252 recorded reads, input and output identities, source and compiler records, and recomputed the paired intervals from every row. On Wikidata, the selective Cells reader took 0.926 of the earlier Cells reader's wall time and 0.863 of the ordinary reader's time. Text remained level. Patterned input improved against earlier Cells but was still about 44% slower than the ordinary reader, and Wikidata peak memory was about 31% higher. These are matched measurements on Louis's machine, not a calculation combining results from different machines.

One short run's coarse after-start activity estimate was high. The independent Guardian [checked the frozen rule and archived machine data](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6867956361) and found that every pre-start activity check met its limit; the later estimate did not establish a violation or prove an absence of interference. The Coordinator accepted that bounded finding and kept every observation and its uncertainty. Throttling counters were unavailable, not measured zero. This closes only the activity qualification question, not the separate decoder adoption or integration questions.

## What happens next?

The earlier public proposal [lost badly on patterned input](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/382#issuecomment-6868503439). The Coordinator's successor adds a cheap check that keeps sparse blocks on the older decoder and routes smaller worker groups through Cells. An independent Researcher corrected the proposed acceptance test before the workstation data. On the tested CI machine, the new route was faster on Wikidata without the earlier patterned or one-byte slowdown; it used about 56 MB more peak memory at four threads. Louis [explicitly accepted that cost](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6877546534).

A second workstation run became decisive because Louis reported that desktop browsers were open during the first; its rule was set before either result was read. The [decisive i9 run](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6878643235) found Wikidata wall-time ratios of 0.858, 0.977 and 0.927 at one, two and four threads, with intervals wholly below one. Its acceptance rule still failed at ten and twenty threads: the upper bounds were 1.021 and 1.024 against a 1.02 ceiling. Both cells used the unchanged team decoder and had point estimates of 0.999. The Coordinator acknowledged that the registered margin lacked a suitable noise allowance; Louis [granted an exception for those two cells only](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6878656114). The failed rule remains visible rather than being relabelled as passed.

The candidate [merged](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/383), the earlier public proposal closed as incorporated, and [version 0.3.0 was published](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6878874148) on crates.io and PyPI, with docs.rs built. A paired release benchmark on a four-core CI machine found Wikidata about 10–12% faster than 0.2.9 at one to four threads, alongside the known memory increase. That is decoder evidence, not an end-to-end result for a consumer. The [wikidata-pq update](https://github.com/lmmx/wikidata-pq/pull/225) is open and keeps its four-thread cap, where Cells is active; its own benchmark will decide its merge. A separate i9 release-scaling run is now in the workstation queue.

Louis has directed a later, untimed phase census followed by a possible cooperative Cells design for larger teams. The [pre-data rule](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6878326831) proceeds to a prototype only if the serial share is at most 40% and the work can be split three ways within 10% of equal spans. The earlier forced-worker gain at high thread counts motivates this, but does not show that a cooperative design will work. The Coordinator will build the counting harness and the Optimiser will analyse its records; no census result is established here. A separate “feathered induction” research proposal is parked behind this work.

## What else remains separate?

The earlier one-thread comparison's diagnostic and memory-accounting preparation is integrated without a new workstation result. The first cells implementation remains unmerged after its own corrected-head performance failure. A proposed warm-stage comparison is still unadmitted, and the one-core dossier is deferred. Louis's detailed technical companion has merged into site source, but deployed publication was not separately verified in this record. The Cells default and release have changed through 0.3.0; earlier experimental findings remain distinct from the shipped result.
