mibz2 0.5.6 is released and has reached the downstream Wikidata pipeline; its measured quick sample stayed level, while a model of the full dump shows a small gain. The team is now preparing a production study of another decoding method that ran faster in an isolated workstation test. A separate combined experiment was faster still, but missed its larger target and has not been adopted.

## What improved for users?

The [one-pass block writer](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/581) shipped in 0.5.6 after independent mathematical and safety checks. Its paired tests showed faster Wikidata decoding at one and two threads. The release's [workstation scaling](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6990756076) was about 2–3% faster at eight, sixteen and twenty threads; ten threads were level. Those measurements do not isolate the writer from the rest of the release.

The [downstream update](https://github.com/lmmx/wikidata-pq/pull/227) is merged. Its i9 quick-tier benchmark measured no whole-pipeline change beyond noise on the sample. The full-dump estimate was [0.6% faster, with a 0.2% uncertainty band](https://github.com/lmmx/wikidata-pq/pull/227#issuecomment-6994012414), chiefly from the split stage. That is a modelled estimate, not a measured full-dump run. Output parity passed.

## Why study another decoder?

The [open-return comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6992226855) tried a different way to reconstruct decoded blocks where the current method already admits its smaller-memory path. Its gated version ran about 6–8% faster in the registered high-thread cells. An independent [check](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6992362897) reproduced the verdict and found a small setup difference in the comparison, leaving a kernel gain. A later [matched-kernel sweep](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6994380056) found a 5–7% gain from two to ten workers, with text level throughout.

That sweep also overturned the proposed explanation. The gain did **not** grow with worker count or come from fewer last-level cache stalls. Independent [analysis](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6994466885) counted about 24% fewer instructions per output byte, partly offset by lower instruction throughput. A production integration study is [queued](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6992388066) to check correctness, memory, placements and the complete decoder before any default changes. Its proposed scope now includes the ungated form, which used [substantially less peak memory](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6997140568) in a separate experiment. The study must register and review its adoption rule first.

## What did the other experiments establish?

An independently [verified endpoint reconstruction](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6990919772) reproduces the grammar, but its ordering cost fails the practical screen; the [research record](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/591) merged without a speed claim. Four further [mathematical studies](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6993843423) were verified and merged as research records. They proved useful constructions or limits, but none became a production change.

A [combined experimental decoder](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6997132553) was the fastest arm yet measured on Louis's ten-core workstation: about 9–11% less wall time than the current smaller-memory method at seven to ten workers. It missed its 20% reduction target. At eight to ten workers it was indistinguishable in wall time from the ungated open form, while using about 20% more peak memory. This was a research-kernel test, not a full production run. The [delimiter mathematics](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6997219042) has now been independently verified: the stated run bounds, marker correspondence, lower bound and selection bound held under a separate proof and test. One receipt's swap count needed a narrow correction. The Researcher's separate parsing comparison remains open, and none of these claims establishes decoding speed.

## What happens next?

The team will carry the open-return candidate through the queued correctness and adoption study, including the lower-memory arm, and keep the combined experiment as research evidence. The [scorer calibration](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6997161408) got the direction right on the i9 but grew too optimistic at higher worker counts, so its forecasts remain advisory. The downstream update is already merged; no action is requested from Louis by this record.
