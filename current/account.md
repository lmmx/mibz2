mibz2 0.3.3 is released with a smaller decoding-table layout, and its four-thread Wikidata release benchmark is about 4% faster than 0.3.2. Louis has set the next aim: move the point where decoding switches from independent blocks to a cooperative team. A downstream update to 0.3.3 is still being benchmarked; the latest workstation scaling bundle has been submitted, but no judgment on that bundle appears in the channel covered here.

## Why did the direction change?

The team wanted to know which memory traffic limited the decoder. A [valid phase measurement](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6886389292) found that counted demand reads explained only 3.73% of physical memory reads at four workers. A proposed decomposition of the rest [closed without a run](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6892999618): it needed Louis's permission for processor-control writes, and without that intervention it would not decide what to build. A later concurrency screen [stopped at preflight](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894597405) because a performance-counter pass lacked the required privilege; it took no measurements and was closed after direct builds had already answered its engineering questions. The Coordinator and Louis [recorded the lesson](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894673607): when candidates are cheap to build and test, build them before commissioning another diagnostic chain.

The direct comparisons separated the routes. Writing each decoded block in one front-to-back pass [made Wikidata 5–7% slower](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894223006) and closed unmerged. [Recycling a dead table slot](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894374065) instead of allocating a new one cut touched slots by 29.7% on Wikidata; its four-thread CI run improved wall time by 1.7% and reduced peak memory. The change shipped in [0.3.2](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/releases/tag/v0.3.2). A [reported-only workstation comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894519106) found its CPU benefit increased with the number of independent workers. The later [0.3.2 release-scaling record](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/417) found a four-thread gain but also a one-thread loss in that run; high-thread losses occurred on the untouched cooperative path and were read as drift, so the one-thread cause remains uncertain.

The next [eight-byte bottom-slot layout](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6895145558) reduced Wikidata slot bytes from 443.6 to 310.5 MB despite adding a dependent load to many reads. Its safety check finished after merge and [passed on both heads](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6895432213). Louis directed the [0.3.3 release](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6896077769) before its tag-to-tag benchmark. That later [benchmark](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6896261240) found Wikidata four-thread wall time 4.2% lower, CPU time 4.0% lower and peak memory 102 to 98 MB; one and two threads were within noise.

## What stopped?

An [equal one-row-cell shortcut](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6895882037) looked common in a count but added branches and dependent reads. Its build was 8–12% slower at one to four threads, so it closed unmerged. The team considers the current Cells induction campaign finished.

A [corrected locality proof](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894415432) still closes reordering within the present tree as a memory-traffic route at a modelled five-megabyte cache share. It also disclosed that earlier proposed fixed-address reordered executions were not realizable because they overwrote data too early. The certified compulsory floor, which did not depend on those executions, preserved the narrow verdict. Smaller-cache cases and a different layout are outside it.

## What happens next?

Louis's [next goal](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6896261240) is to move the thread-count knee: below six physical cores the released decoder gives independent blocks to workers using Cells; at six or more it switches to cooperative teams. The next built work must address that split. The earlier within-block Cooperative Cells idea did not have a four-core case, because independent blocks already fill those workers; its high-thread value needs a new, costed construction rather than an inference from the closed diagnostics.

The [downstream pull request](https://github.com/lmmx/wikidata-pq/pull/227) has been retargeted to 0.3.3 and is open while its own benchmark is rerun. Its version-ladder workstation recipe is being prepared, not yet an observed result. The [0.3.3 workstation scaling bundle](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6896262916) has arrived, while its verdict has not; the inspected master queue is empty after the consumed entry was removed. A slot-recycling article was [commissioned](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6894469460) from Louis's relayed text, with graphics to be recreated and checked against the project style before publication. No published article is established here.

## How sure are these conclusions?

Release tag and pull-request states were checked independently. The performance figures and bundle judgments above are the Coordinator's recorded readings of CI or Louis's workstation evidence; this account did not replay the archives. The first phase run's invalidity remains distinct from its valid repeat, and neither the abandoned residual study nor the stopped concurrency preflight supplies a new measurement. The open downstream benchmark and the newly submitted scaling bundle must not be described as gains.
