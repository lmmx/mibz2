The run-fed work has produced its first positive registered result: a helper-side design was 3.3% faster than the current nine-thread decoder on a pinned CI worker. Most of that gain also appears when only its register-based symbol decoder is changed, so the team is separating that simpler improvement from the extra effect of run records. A workstation comparison still needs its own frozen recipe and review; nothing from this result has entered production.

## What did the new test establish?

After the team repaired a [real empty-block fuzz failure](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732545801), the Guardian cleared the revised experiment, and its [registered CI run](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732659255) was valid. The primary helper-side direct-placement arm returned a wall-time ratio of 0.9669 (95% interval 0.9594–0.9744) against the released default at nine threads, meeting its registered lead rule. Its output matched across all 210 runs. The attribution control stayed near the incumbent.

The [result journal](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/d1260c59047846401ed0e717ba6c66d33b699f70/docs/journal/2026-11-09-run-fed-leader-result.md) also compares the parts. The register-based decoder without run records was about 2.5% faster on its own. Direct placement added about 0.8% beyond that. A leader-sorted run-record arm was about 5% faster overall, but it was a secondary arm and received no registered verdict. These are measurements on an oversubscribed CI worker, not a demonstrated gain on Louis’s workstation or a production qualification. The next work is a separate registration for the simpler kernel and a reviewed ten-thread workstation recipe for the run-fed design.

## Why does the broader search continue?

Two earlier registered run-fed arms stopped on their CI workers: a serial record-emission stage was [slower](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730764444), and the run-intersection pair-table path was [much slower](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6731656317) as a whole. The pair builder did save work against an older pair builder. Its [corrected journal](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/6b36fd8c/docs/journal/2026-11-09-pair-from-runs-result.md) withdraws an overstrong claim that the measurements isolate the pair walk’s cost. The new positive result uses a different arrangement and does not erase those earlier measurements. Louis’s [recorded direction](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/cb02fd95/docs/team/PROTOCOL.md) keeps the line open across arm-level stops.

Another one-hop direct construction is [under revision](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732495591). Its evidence validator still needs coupled store-count checks, and the Guardian’s [scoped hold](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732441720) requires fuzzing that reaches this construction and auditable mutation results. It has no speed verdict.

## What else is pending?

The [service-core result](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6729896575) remains favorable at ten threads without adoption. The Guardian [lifted that scoped safety hold](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732679972) after corrected cross-case trace attribution and completed pinned Miri evidence. This covers the measured unguarded runtime snapshot and the registered lookahead runtime within the reviewed scope; it does not transfer the old performance verdict to the guarded integration. A feeder panic guard was [merged separately](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732471223), preserving the scope of the earlier performance snapshot. The lookahead follow-up still has its own [admission hold](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730774618) over false-valid evidence fixtures. The released pipeline’s specific safety objections were previously closed, while its low-compression-level default slowdown still motivates scheduling work. The [supporting levels](L2.md) give the evidence and remaining limits.
