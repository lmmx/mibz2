Two alternatives to the released decoder have now produced measured results. Two service threads made decoding faster at ten threads, but remain unadopted; building a table from symbol runs was slower when paired with either of two tested consumers, despite savings inside the builders. The team is pursuing a cheaper one-hop construction and consumer while safety and evidence holds still govern other pending tests.

## What did the new pair-table test show?

The team repaired [unsafe public-state paths](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6731230049), reconciled the result validator, and [ran a registered CI comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6731656317). Its run-intersection builder cost 31% less than the earlier row builder on the Wikidata workload. That was still **inconclusive** against the registered builder target, which required a reduction of at least 50%. The full pair-table path was 1.895 times as costly as production's inverse-transform entry point, a **negative STOP** for this candidate on that worker. The run was valid. Run-heavy inputs saved more building work, but their full pair path still lost.

The [result journal](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/96b0794d9605614e691b4356032a9120f1bb6baa/docs/journal/2026-11-09-pair-from-runs-result.md) estimates the pair-table walk at about the cost of production's whole entry point. That estimate subtracts separate medians, so it is an explanatory approximation, not its own measured interval. A cheaper builder alone cannot establish a faster whole path with this consumer. The next proposed construction applies run intersections to the existing one-hop table; the team is also investigating a cheaper consumer. Louis's [recorded direction](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/cb02fd95/docs/team/PROTOCOL.md) keeps the broader run-fed search open after an individual arm stops.

## What had the first run-fed test found?

The earlier [frozen CI test](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730764444) was valid and stopped its serial run-fed stage arm on one runner: 1.159 times the current stage on Wikidata. Its table fill was cheaper, but emission, sorting and fallback costs outweighed the saving. The [Optimiser cautioned](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730842547) that the fallback phase excludes selection and that phase medians cannot reconstruct the judged whole-stage result. A helper-side direct-placement design is frozen, but [its timing remains held](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730740928) for a surplus-index defect and missing composed-path and worker-panic evidence.

## What can proceed in the released and service-core paths?

The released 0.2.6 decoder has a [measured low-compression-level default slowdown](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6720088760), and no replacement scheduling policy has qualified. The pipeline's targeted fuzz and outer-worker panic recovery objections were [closed by the Guardian](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6731042100); the corresponding [repair merged](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6731074830). The recorded limits remain, including untested panics outside the run, but those resolved holds no longer block this specific path.

The [service-core comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6729896575) remains valid and favorable at ten threads—4.5% faster with 12.7% less processor time—without production adoption. Its separate [service-path safety hold](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730103867) remains. A lookahead-threshold follow-up has an independent [admission hold](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730774618): four evidence-validation fixtures still return valid incorrectly. Neither the service result nor the pipeline's repaired worker clears those service-path requirements. Louis has no new workstation run ready from these fronts. The [supporting levels](L2.md) retain the evidence and limits.
