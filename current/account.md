A new registered test found that placing run records directly into the decoder’s table was slower than the current method on its CI worker. On that same worker, keeping the sort and filling the table sequentially was faster—but an earlier run of that construction lost on a different worker and baseline, so the reason for the reversal is unresolved. Separately, a helper-side run-fed design still has a positive nine-thread CI result; its ten-thread workstation test is being prepared, and production has not changed.

## What did direct placement establish?

After the Researcher approved the corrected registration and the Guardian’s [conditions were met](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6733033907), the [registered direct-placement run](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6733116376) was valid. It took 1.0525 times as long as the current stage on Wikidata, meeting its STOP rule on this runner. Direct placement was also 1.078 times the sorted-record construction. Its placement phase cost more than sorting plus a sequential fill, although the two paths wrote roughly the same number of table slots. The [journal](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/c41ef38d/docs/journal/2026-11-09-run-fed-direct-result.md) points to scattered writes across many destination buckets as the likely cost; this is a mechanism reading from phase timings and source counts, not a measured hardware-counter diagnosis.

The sorted-record construction was 0.9763 times the current stage on this AMD worker. Its earlier [registered run](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6730764444) measured 1.1592 on an Intel worker. The incumbent baseline changed too, so neither machine nor baseline can be singled out from these two results. The earlier STOP stands; this new contrast is exploratory. The team is weighing a matched comparison before drawing a transferable conclusion.

## Where does the positive run-fed result stand?

The helper-side [registered CI result](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732659255) remains a valid attributed lead: its primary was 3.3% faster than the nine-thread default on a pinned worker. A register-based symbol decoder without run records was about 2.5% faster by itself. The [Researcher’s replay](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732761085) and [Optimiser’s review](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6732797905) caution that the extra paired difference cannot be assigned solely to direct table filling: the arms also differ in rejected-block policy and construction work. A secondary leader-sorted arm was faster at nine threads but lost relative to the register-only control at three threads, so its ranking does not simply transfer.

The team is revising the registered workstation recipe to keep its primary and controls, specify ten-thread rounds and fail-closed admission, and review the frozen identities. The simpler register-based decoder is being built as a separate production prototype with its own safety and performance test. No workstation result or production adoption follows from the CI lead.
