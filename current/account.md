Version 0.2.8 has shipped two measured decoder improvements: a faster run-record path for Wikidata and text, and guarded service on Louis's ten-thread workstation target. On the four-core CI comparison with 0.2.7, Wikidata decoding was 12–19% faster and text 10–26% faster across one, two and four threads. The team has moved from qualifying those changes to checking the release on the workstation, preparing downstream delivery, and pursuing its next decoder design.

## What was shipped, and with what trade-offs?

Louis [adopted the run-record change](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6764294229) despite its original no-harm test failing on some fallback inputs. That failed verdict remains in the record; the owner accepted the cost for the larger primary gains. Correctness and safety checks were completed before [merge](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/324). The change speeds the single-worker decoding path; a later [workstation comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6764875247) found an 8–10% gain up to four threads but no gain where team kernels take over at higher counts.

Guarded service also [merged](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/316) as a hardware-gated default. Its earlier workstation comparison showed 6.8% more throughput and 14.7% less processor time at ten threads, with all 420 runs in the fast mode. The planned twelve-dispatch statistical programme was retired after its proposed inference proved unsound. Instead, the adopted route relies on the target-machine result, a deterministic check that other machines do not select the service path, neutral paired CI measurements, and safety review. That is a change in acceptance policy, recorded without relabelling the old findings.

The [0.2.8 release](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6767075813) includes those two decoder changes and a benchmark-only held-spinner option. Its CI tag comparison found one small random-input regression and up to 7.6% higher peak memory in one text cell; these are disclosed. The release is out on crates.io and PyPI. An i9 release-scaling run, changelog update and downstream wikidata-pq bump are next, with no confirmed results for those steps at this account's watermark.

## What did the other candidates teach?

Three cooperative-decoder ports saved counted instructions, but their [workstation comparison](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6764570856) did not meet the rule set before the run: none of the specified ten-to-twenty-thread cells was faster beyond noise. The ports were parked unmerged. A universal backward walk was also [closed as built](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6765942197) because it showed no primary gain over the shipped run-record path. Its input-admission idea survives as a maintenance change under review.

A valid [cold-start test](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6765016578) showed that keeping one spare core busy throughout decoding prevents the workstation's slow mode; briefly spinning at launch or pinning workers did not. The team merged an optional held-spinner for benchmarks. It did not change the library's decoder to address that mode.

## What is the next direction?

Louis has asked the team to support a new inverse-transform investigation, [MONODROMY](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/351). Its first prototype reconstructs text by inducing on runs and emitting a grammar instead of walking each output row through a large table. It checks bytes against the existing walker on its stated cases, but its indicative one-thread Wikidata kernel takes 1.276 times the current build-and-walk time—about 27.6% slower as built. The reported independent merge rounds are a mathematical opening, not a measured parallel speedup. The Researcher, Optimiser and Architect have been assigned distinct supporting work; MONODROMY chooses which findings to use. No production gain is claimed from this line yet.

The team now records delivered speed and follows a new owner-set performance policy. The Observer's record retains the older failed tests and their uncertainty while tracking which changed policy and owner decisions led to shipment.
