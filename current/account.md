A valid workstation diagnostic shows that independent workers stay productive longer on smaller-table input, but it also disproves the model used to price proposed decoder designs. The team has withdrawn those speed predictions and is testing which part of the gain comes from table size, block count or the single scanner feeding workers. Separately, the staged team decoder has reduced counted work; its measurements still fail their own admission rules, and guarded service remains unqualified.

## What did the workstation run establish?

On the smaller-table input, plain workers reached [1.86 gigabytes per second at eight workers](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6759975348), then eased at ten. On ordinary larger-table input they peaked at 1.08 gigabytes per second at four workers and fell to 0.70 at ten. Processor time grew 1.18 times from four to ten on the first input, versus 3.26 times on the second. This is a valid difference between complete-decoder configurations, but compression level also changes block count, coding tables and the scanner feed. It cannot assign a share of the gain to table bytes alone.

The [Optimiser's replay](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6760081653) found that the old load model missed the larger-table whole-decoder results increasingly at higher worker counts. The [Pathfinder withdrew](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6760156549) its speed prices for the proposed compact tables and its claim that a four-byte-step table would beat the team path at width. A small table behind one scanner does not yet beat the default; the smaller-table and parallel-scanner ideas now have to be evaluated together. A phase-overlap count and a controlled record-width test are proposed to separate the mechanisms. Neither is a registered performance result.

## What is happening on the team path?

The staged cooperative walk's count reduced its traversal from 17.77 to 9.97 instructions per byte, but [Guardian D084](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6759502195) upheld its failure of the helper-balance rule. Its independent safety review found no material objection. The proposed screen that would prove the intended team kernels execute and a separate native engagement diagnostic are still being revised after [counterexamples](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6760210997). No workstation timing or adoption follows.

A second port put a faster run-processing loop into the team build. Its [independently recounted](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6759906409) build count fell from 8.94 to 6.79 instructions per byte, and whole-process count from 37.92 to 35.80. The first parser double-counted raw and compressed profile files, so the figures were withdrawn before the archive was reconciled. They now stand, but the port's registered measurement condition still failed. A further team-build port is being built; stacking the two ports' gains is only a model, not a measured combination.

## What else remains open?

The final guarded-service test is still a draft. The [Guardian](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6759870990) found that conditioning on a slow workstation mode is conservative only under stated residual assumptions. A proposed check passed 42 pilot cells, but that does not establish the assumptions for a qualifying run. The separate [two-modes diagnostic](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6759926285) is still under revision after its judge accepted missing timing as a slow observation.

The failed run-record layout rescue remains closed. [Guardian D086](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6760052047) allowed a distinct master-side layout-contract experiment in principle and found a defect in the existing diagnostic parser; the current restoration screen still fails. The merged build-window change remains unreleased, and the version 0.2.7 release-scaling bundle remains unconfirmed in the latest checked queue state.
