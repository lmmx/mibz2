Both queued workstation comparisons have finished, but neither changed the decoder. Raising the service-thread lookahead threshold was inconclusive. The helper-side run-fed comparison was also inconclusive, with a failed control check and large shifts in run time whose cause was not measured. Separately, the register-based decoder prototype's first CI diagnostic remains valid but unjudged; a repaired quota reader passed an untimed preflight and awaits review before one separately frozen attempt.

## What did the workstation runs establish?

The [service-thread run](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/master/docs/journal/2026-11-09-service-lookahead-result.md) validly compared larger admission thresholds with the current service-thread setting at ten threads. Neither registered comparison established a gain. The service-thread design was also faster than the released default in a reported, **unjudged** comparison, in the same direction as its earlier registered result. That strengthens the case for studying the design, but does not make threshold tuning a win or qualify a new default. A [Researcher review](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6736071231) corrected the earlier explanation of preparation waits: neither of two surviving mechanisms has been shown. An [untimed diagnostic](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/293) was built to distinguish them; local observations on an oversubscribed four-core machine are indicative, not a workstation verdict, and the diagnostic awaits review.

The [run-fed workstation result](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/master/docs/journal/2026-11-09-run-fed-leader-workstation-result.md) was valid under its registration, but its main comparison was inconclusive and its control did not stay within the required fidelity band. Forty-one of 140 timed runs took about twice as long, across arms and clustered rounds. The data are consistent with a change in effective clock, but extra work on the CPU could look similar; the cause was not measured. The earlier attributed CI lead stands as a CI result, without an i9 verdict. The [Guardian](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6737091245) and [Researcher](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6738754551) permit clock and thermal telemetry beside an unchanged full-data analysis. Discarding or replacing slow pairs would change the experiment and requires Louis's authority. The merged recorder has not been validated on the i9's hardware counters.

## What is happening with the decoder prototype?

The [register-list checkpoint kernel's](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/281) frozen opt-in CI run was **valid but unjudged** because CPU quota evidence was unknown. Its reported nine-thread wall ratio of 0.9700 [0.9647, 0.9753] has no speed verdict. The [Researcher](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6735626248) allowed a separate follow-up in principle, retaining the original run, but rejected a quota collector that could overlook an unreadable ancestor. The revised [collector](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/288) tracks coverage of the process's cgroup hierarchy. Its [untimed preflight](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/actions/runs/42589756805) passed on the runner and recorded a known unlimited quota, with no decoder timing. The Researcher has been asked to review the revision before a new freeze and the one permitted timed attempt. Another invalid or unjudged result would suspend timing on this runner configuration. Even a judged lead would leave default reach, switched-path safety and adoption measurements open, as the [Optimiser explained](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6736939946).
