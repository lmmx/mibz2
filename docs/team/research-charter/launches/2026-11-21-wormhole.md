# Wormhole — a chain of existing results from runs to text order

You are starting a new, independent research line for lmmx/mibz2: a high-performance Rust bzip2
decoder whose hardest stage is the exact inverse Burrows–Wheeler transform (BWT). Three other
Gemini sessions have worked on it, named by what they do:
- Mechanisms (research/carbon-word-transport) builds and times reconstruction mechanisms;
- Kitchen Sink (research/carbon-kitchen-sink) explores broadly across mathematics;
- Depth (research/carbon-depth) studied cost and lower bounds, and has finished.

Read their published work freely. Don't touch their branches.

## The idea behind this line

A number theorist described Levent Alpöge's claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis
(https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf; its Lean formalisation, of RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, was reported accepted by three independent proof kernels on 20 November) like this: not new theory built toward the goal,
but "an incredibly complicated tour through the infrastructure built for a hundred other mineral
reserves, situated far from the destination", which "somehow creates a wormhole to the original
destination". It is a 25,000-page chain of deep existing results, each built for something else,
whose end is the goal.

Your task is to look for such a wormhole for our problem.

## The target, stated once

**Start:** what a bzip2 decoder has cheaply after Huffman, MTF and RLE2 decoding: the block's run
structure. That is r runs (symbol, length), their counts, the primary index, and anything computable
from these in about O(r) work.

**Destination:** the exact text order. That is the output bytes, or equivalently each row's text
position τ(x) = SA[x], for a block of n ≈ 900,000 rows with r ≈ 0.06n runs.

**The wormhole:** a chain of existing results, each used for something other than its original
purpose, that takes you from Start to Destination. Its final link must be an operation whose cost
can be reasoned about, and which is cheaper than what the shipped decoder pays.

The shipped decoder (Open, in mibz2 0.5.8, `src/open.rs`) does this:
- an elimination on about r intervals (Rauzy–Veech-like induction);
- a dependent walk over the retained rows, about 0.1n of them;
- one 16-byte copy per step.

## Where to start looking

These are seeds, not an agenda. Replace them freely.
- **Kitchen Sink, cycle 9:** a directed feedback vertex set of only 0.3% of runs breaks every cycle of
  the run-overlap graph. Record 84 showed that copying from completed output fails because 85–94% of
  copies lie on cycles. Is there a chain of results that turns a tiny FVS into an exact acyclic
  schedule? Or does Kitchen Sink's "phantom path" duality (2^35–2^54 combinatorial paths against
  0.13–0.17r metric pieces) forbid it?
- **Open as a discrete Morse collapse.** Thick runs are matched away, retained rows are critical cells,
  and words are gradient-path labels. Algebraic Morse theory (Sköldberg; Jöllenbeck–Welker), filtered
  Morse theory (Mischaikow–Nanda) and Perseus 6's reductions over Z[t, t⁻¹] give a library of exact
  operations. See FRONTIER.md's Morse lead.
- **Mature bodies of results built for neighbouring problems:**
  - run-length BWT and compressed-index theory (r-index, move structures, the Φ function, toehold lemmas);
  - interval-exchange and Teichmüller dynamics (Rauzy–Veech, the Zorich cocycle, Bratteli–Vershik numeration);
  - S-adic systems and combinatorics on words;
  - persistence and discrete Morse theory;
  - graph algorithms (feedback sets, list ranking, Euler tours);
  - external-memory and cache-oblivious algorithms;
  - anything else your judgement finds.

## What counts

- **The best result** is a complete chain with every link a cited, existing result, verified exactly on
  small cases and on the six authenticated blocks, ending in an operation with a costed route to
  beating Open.
- **A partial chain is valuable too.** Record which links are proved, which are verified and which are
  missing, with the precise statement each missing link would need.
- **A precise reason no such chain exists, within a stated scope, is a result.**
- **Length is not a defect.** A long chain through unrelated fields is the point, provided the final
  operation is cheap.

## How performance is judged

- **The baseline is mibz2 0.5.8's native Open kernel on master,** not older Open or a C re-implementation.
- **One-thread wins are not enough.** An 8-lane emit won 13% on one thread natively, then lost 5% at 8
  threads on the maintainer's i9 (`docs/team/research-charter/CONTEXT.md` §5a, the fidelity ledger).
  Production runs 6–10 workers sharing L3 and memory bandwidth.
- **So for any candidate, report:**
  - its per-worker working set;
  - its DRAM bytes per output byte;
  - its acquisition cost.
- **Cost models are diagnostic.** The Coordinator ports anything promising to Rust and measures it
  natively and on the i9.
