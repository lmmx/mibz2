## 2. The foundational challenge

Our major advances arose when familiar computational objects were recognised as instances of
richer mathematical structures.

LF traversal became a discrete interval exchange. Rauzy–Veech induction then made collective
reconstruction operations available. Open subsequently exploited first-return structure.

I want you to look for the next change of mathematical world.

Three promising doorways have emerged from our recent research:

**Origami monodromy and branched surfaces.** The pair (LF, lexicographic successor) defines a
square-tiled surface and a branched torus cover. Investigate whether alternative directions,
geometric transport, surface decompositions or transformations of the monodromy representation
expose an exact reconstruction operation unavailable in the original permutation coordinates.

**Thurston's train tracks and graph dynamics.** Explore measured tracks, path-carrying graph maps,
Bestvina–Handel folding, Stallings folds, illegal turns and ordered symbolic substitutions. The
run-overlap network has a small branching core, but this is only a starting observation. Seek a
genuinely useful transformation, not a new name for existing induction.

**Agol–Hass–Thurston interval pairings.** These methods manipulate enormous implicit collections
of strands through compact interval-pairing operations. Investigate whether an exact word-carrying
transformation could recover the marked symbolic orbit without tracing its constituent positions
or reconstructing an equivalently expensive intermediate object.

Use the research journals and mathematical literature to understand these connections. Identify
prior art precisely.

These are invitations, not restrictions. You may abandon all three if a stronger mathematical
formulation emerges.

## 3. Generate mathematics freely; evaluate it rigorously

Don't make the existing computational-obligation register a cage around invention.

Explore surprising representations, structural analogies, algebraic operations, invariants,
geometric lifts and unexpected mathematical connections.

A genuinely useful classical theorem applied in a new computational setting can be more
transformative than an entirely novel but expensive construction.

But distinguish mathematical discovery from computational competitiveness. For a proposed decoder,
determine:

- which expensive existing operations disappear;
- which operations replace them;
- what information the replacement requires and how it is acquired;
- its full construction, materialisation, consumption, memory and output costs;
- whether it preserves the exact marked byte order, not merely symbol counts, cycle structure or
  connectivity.

The current production baseline is Open (qi16). Existing research has shown that many attempts
merely relocate costs between construction and dependent traversal. Treat those failures as scoped
evidence, not universal lower bounds.

Use executable verifiers, adversarial small cases and authenticated real blocks. Treat
uncalibrated cost models as diagnostic, not proof of native performance.

Don't prematurely reject a mathematically significant structure because its first implementation
is expensive. Equally, don't allow an elegant mathematical construction to become an endless
engineering rescue campaign.

## 4. Continue autonomously

Your objective is not to optimise Open. It is to discover a mathematical operation that makes much
of Open's work unnecessary—and then establish whether that operation is genuinely cheaper.

Pursue the mathematics ambitiously, independently and experimentally. Seek the bouleversement.
