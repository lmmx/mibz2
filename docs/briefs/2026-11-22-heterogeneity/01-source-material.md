## B. The coordinator's answer that Louis called "fire", verbatim

I agree with you. Read narrowly, "verification enables performance" is an engineering claim, not a
theory of discovery. Fix one theorem, `inflate ∘ deflate = id`, and agents can make aggressive
changes because the proof rejects any change that breaks it. That works as a ratchet on correctness.
It said nothing about which changes would be fast; lean-zip's decoder is still about 2× behind
libdeflate. The cache, the critical path and the plateau are still where speed gets decided, and the
i9 is still the judge.

But mibz2 already has the structure you're describing, and naming it turns the proof programme from
performative into structural.

**mibz2 is already "A to Z through many routes".** The production decoder isn't one proof. It's a
family of exact routes, each chosen by a cheap predicate on the block:
- dense blocks admitted to Open;
- LazyCells;
- the column route for high-entropy records;
- the run route;
- spiral contraction only on its worst cases;
- the selectors (Spec1/3/4) choosing a strip length.

[CORRECTION, which also applies here: Spec1/3/4 are research selectors, not shipped. See C3.]

Each one is correct only under its own condition. The shape of the correctness claim is not one
theorem but

> for each route i: P_i(x) ⇒ F_i(x) = R(x), plus a total fallback F_0 (the LF walk) with F_0(x) =
> R(x) for all x.

[CORRECTION: the LF walk is the mathematical reference R, not necessarily the runtime fallback.]

Once that's in place, the dispatcher's correctness comes almost for free. The interesting objects
are the P_i: the hypothesis under which a shortcut exists. In a clean single-route proof, hypotheses
are things to minimise. Here they carry the substance, because a hypothesis is a regime, and a regime
that's common in Wikidata is worth a whole kernel.

**That's where the stutter is substantive rather than stylistic.** Your differential recurrence in
MODE.RATIONALE returns to a term and changes what it determines: cost, then dependency, then exposed
latency, then wall time. Here the term that recurs is the hypothesis, not the conclusion. The
conclusion (reconstruct the text) never moves. The block keeps refusing the hypothesis, and each
refusal splits it:
- "Sturmian, so pure arithmetic" never fires on Wikidata. So: which part of the Sturmian structure
  survives in the thick strips? That became the A-Tube contraction in cycle 46.
- "Contract the thin sea" hits the GUID wall: incompressible identifiers, 28.7% of emission. That's
  a place where the current representation can't say the block cheaply. The language stutters
  there, and the stutter is information: either a new route specialised to that regime, or a proof
  that none exists within this representation.

Each failure produces a new predicate, a new route or an obstruction. That's an exact procedure, not
a mood.

**There's already a measurement that tells you when you need a new language rather than another
route:** Sentinel's headroom oracle from cycle 46. It found that ideal per-block choice among all the
existing routes would gain only 3.17% on Wikidata.

[CORRECTION: the oracle covers per-block choice of (L, μ) parameters only, not "all routes". It
bounds that family, and says nothing about alternatives it never considered. See C3.]

[The remainder, about the phase rule, the census, stutter points and a Deleuze caution, is in C3 and
02-editorial.md.]

## C. ChatGPT's commentary, as Louis forwarded it (verbatim excerpts; distinctions worth keeping)

### C1. On what "verification enables performance" means

> The project starts with a correctness theorem [...] Then it repeatedly changes the implementation
> while requiring the theorem to remain true. The authors describe that proof obligation as a
> ratchet: autonomous coding agents can introduce aggressive optimisations, but changes cannot merge
> if they break the formal correctness argument. [...] But notice what it does not establish: That
> the proof reveals a faster algorithm. That different proofs necessarily correspond to different
> algorithms. That the verified implementation uses memory efficiently. That its mathematical
> representation is the best one for the hardware.

> Suppose two algorithms both provably reconstruct the same BWT: A(x)=B(x)=inverseBWT(x). That
> theorem says nothing about their relative performance. One might use fewer instructions but
> produce more random memory accesses. Another might perform additional arithmetic while reducing
> the number of dependent cache misses. Another might reduce total CPU time but fail to improve wall
> time because the serial scanner or memory bandwidth is limiting throughput.

> The specification is not neutral with respect to what researchers are likely to imagine, even
> when its formal semantics are equivalent. [...] A proof system can certify that two formulations
> are equivalent. It cannot, by itself, tell us which formulations we have failed to imagine.

> Take the notion of a row in inverse BWT. A row initially seems like an unavoidable computational
> object. Then induction changes the problem's units: perhaps we should operate on strips, return
> words or induced transformations instead. [...] The old computational unit hasn't merely been
> optimised. Its necessity has been challenged.

### C2. On the stutter (after reading MODE.RATIONALE)

> Your rationale distinguishes three mechanisms: Grammatical compression removes material from an
> expression, but its conceptual terms remain fixed. Paratactic cascade moves from one term to the
> next, each precipitating another, but leaves earlier terms behind. Differential recurrence,
> drawing on Deleuze, repeatedly returns to a term and transforms what it means. [...] "Cost. Cost of
> what? Instruction? No — the dependency it feeds. Dependency? No — the latency it exposes. Latency,
> or failure to overlap latency? Where does cost become wall time?"

> There are at least three levels of differentiation: Implementation (same mathematical procedure,
> different execution: SIMD versus scalar); Representation (same reconstruction, different
> intermediate mathematical objects: LF traversal versus induced return maps); Conditional
> mathematics (different theorems or constructions become available under different hypotheses).
> [...] A theorem that applies to a restricted class of objects need not be a disappointing partial
> result. If that class occurs frequently enough in real data, its restriction can be precisely what
> makes it computationally valuable.

> Global → local: A new mathematical framework generates specialised cases and optimisations.
> Local → global: A family of apparently unrelated specialisations reveals a common invariant,
> suggesting a more general theorem or construction.

> I would be careful not to claim that Deleuze formulated a theory of conditional algorithms. He
> didn't. But I think his distinction between variation within an established language and
> variation that affects the language's own functioning is relevant.

### C3. Corrections to the coordinator's version (accepted)

> First, the 3.17% headroom result is being overinterpreted. [...] the benefit of choosing the best
> parameters per block across the 431 Wikidata blocks. That establishes limited remaining headroom
> for this particular parameter-selection family. It does not establish that all conditional
> reconstruction routes are nearly exhausted.

> Second, the proposal mixes production and experimental routes. Spec1/3/4 were research selectors
> [...] the classical LF decoder is a useful total reference on valid BWT inputs, but we shouldn't
> assume it is literally the runtime fallback in every production code path.

> Sometimes an unsuccessful conditional algorithm reveals that its predicate is expensive to
> recognise. [...] Can we weaken the hypothesis while preserving the conclusion? Can we find a
> cheaper certificate that the hypothesis holds? And sometimes the more consequential move is
> neither. It's to find a different representation in which that property is available without
> explicitly testing it. That third possibility is what keeps the research from collapsing into an
> increasingly complicated dispatch tree.

> [...] the most interesting research artefact would be a map of transformations between the
> hypotheses themselves. [...] The conclusion stays fixed. The means of reaching it differentiate,
> proliferate, sometimes converge, and sometimes demand a new mathematical language.

### C4. On Louis's correction (A5)

> The central claim isn't one theorem, many routes. That's a logical description of the resulting
> program, but not an explanation of why it has that form. The heterogeneity of the compressed data
> demands a corresponding heterogeneity of mathematical strategies. The program must differentiate
> because the problem differentiates.

> And there's a genuine distinction between: A universal proof with several implementation
> optimisations. A collection of special cases bolted onto a general decoder. A differentiated
> mathematical construction whose conditional routes arise from the structure of the problem itself.
> You're interested in the third.

> I wouldn't use the 3.17% headroom oracle to conclude that we have exhausted the available
> mathematical differentiation. [...] The real problem is that we don't know whether the family of
> conditional mathematical reconstructions we've discovered is anywhere close to complete. That
> uncertainty is the research opportunity. [...] I think the article should have the confidence to
> leave that process open.

## D. The coordinator's outline (superseded: kept so you can see what was rejected and why)

Title options were: "One theorem, many routes: correctness and speed in a decoder that adapts to
its input" / "A proof of correctness is not a cost model" / "Conditional correctness: how mibz2
decodes by regime". **Louis rejected all three as weak and slop-shaped (A5).**

The sections were: §1 what a correctness proof does and doesn't fix (lean-zip; R5 against the block
reader); §2 the decoder as a family of conditional routes; §3 where the hypothesis fails is where
the work is (Sturmian, GUID wall, Deleuze); §4 knowing when to stop adding routes (the 3.17%
oracle; it overreached, see C3); §5 a new representation as a different proof of one lemma (Weil,
Stepanov, Bombieri; T3 substitution points; 9/66 load-bearing); and a closing on what is proved and
measured. Everything in "Sources" below still applies.
