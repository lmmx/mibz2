# The knee: why the wall stops falling from 8 to 20 threads (registration)

*Registered 17 November 2026, before any data. The recipe is `just bench-knee` (`.just/bench.just`, `bench/knee.py`). This is a diagnostic: it reports, adopts nothing, and changes no production default. Its only output is the order of the follow-ups, by the decision rule below.*

## The question

From 0.5.3's release-scaling run (evidence branch, `release-scaling/run-20261117T111525Z`; Wikidata, 210 MB of output), production's median wall on the i9-10900K (10 cores, 20 CPUs, a 20 MiB L3, dual-channel DDR4) is:

| threads | wall | plan |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.110 s | 8 lazy workers |
| 10 | 0.115 s | 10 workers (pinned 0–9) |
| 12, 16, 20 | 0.106–0.107 s | 7 lazy workers |

That is about 2 GB/s of output, flat from 8 threads up. lbzip2 plateaus too, at about 0.49 s.

The runs' per-thread CPU over wall at 20 threads shows that nothing serial is saturated:
- the scanner (`mibz2-scan`): 0.47;
- `main`: 0.19–0.28;
- the reader (`mibz2-read`): about 0;
- each of the 7 workers: 0.75.

So the workers idle about 25% of the wall, yet no serial thread is busy all the time.

What is already known:
- Total DRAM traffic has never been measured (the Researcher's REVIEW 6946839547, point 4).
- MONODROMY's records 47 and 51 (#145) measured an L3-miss latency of 551–595 cycles, a memory-level parallelism of 2.2, and fill buffers carrying 53% of the sampled latency.

## What runs

**The decoder:** production's own, `ab child mibz2 T FILE` (`BzReader::parallel`), built once by rustc 1.99.1.

**The cells:**
- unpinned at 4, 8, 10, 12 and 20 threads;
- pinned as `ab run --spread` pins (the first CPU of each of the first T cores, by `taskset`; the CPUs are recorded in each run) at 4, 8 and 10;
- each on Wikidata ×1 and ×4;
- 10 rounds, each cell once a round, the order rotated a step each round and reversed every other cycle.

**A known difference between placements:** pinned at 10, the process sees 10 logical CPUs, so `trims_to_seven` (which needs the machine's 20) does not apply, and it runs 10 workers. Unpinned at 10, it runs 7. The busy-worker count is recorded per cell (`thread_cpu`), and the timeline records the plan the decoder built.

**The judged cells** are 12 and 20 threads, unpinned, on Wikidata ×4 (×1 too for H3). Every other cell is context, reported and not judged.

Each run is wrapped, from the inside out:
1. in `taskset`, for pinned cells;
2. in `perf stat` of its own core and software events (as this user, where `kernel.perf_event_paranoid` ≤ 1);
3. in `perf stat -a` of the memory controller (system-wide: paranoid ≤ 0, or `sudo -n`; never a password prompt).

Each event is kept only if `perf list` names it and a trial counts it. Under `sudo -n`, the decode runs as root; it writes nothing.

**The timeline** is examples/occupancy.rs, built with the `trace` feature. Its child decodes exactly as `BzReader::parallel` does: `parallel_with(f, T, 4T)`, the same plan, the trim to seven, and the window.
- It runs at the same cells, 10 rounds, at level 0 (no marks) and level 1 (the timeline), in alternating order.
- Level 1's wall over level 0's, paired by round, is the timeline's cost.
- The `trace` feature is never in production's build. Every `mark!` in src/parallel.rs is `#[cfg(feature = "trace")]`, so production compiles no instruction for it (code inspection; `ab` is built without the feature).
- This PR fixes the instrument. It waited for `Plan::for_threads`' workers, 10 on the i9, where the decoder runs 7. It now waits for the plan the decoder built (`built_plan`).
- It adds the decode's edges (`t.e.*`): the first block issued, decoded and taken; the last block issued (the last dispatched) and taken; and the tail between them.

**The ceiling** is `ab stream` (new, untimed by `ab run`). It runs scale (a = s·b) and triad (a = b + s·c) over three arrays of 256 MiB of f64, first-touched by their own threads.
- It runs at 1, 2, 4, 10 and 20 threads, pinned as above where the cores allow, at 2 and 12 repetitions in alternating order, 3 times.
- Its rate in the memory controller's bytes, and in the proxy's, is the difference of the two runs' bytes over the difference of their kernel times. The first touch and the process's start cancel.
- Its own rate with write-allocate (24 or 32 B an element) is recorded beside it, without perf.
- The ceiling is the best median over thread counts and kernels.

**The idle traffic:** five readings of the memory controller over `sleep 2` before the runs and five after. The median rate, times each reading's elapsed time, is taken off that reading.

**The DIMMs' speed:** recorded where `dmidecode` or `lshw` give it without root. On the workstation they don't (`miss-cost/run-20261117T104648Z`'s `dimm.txt` has no speed), so the ceiling is measured rather than assumed.

## The hypotheses, the predictions and the rules

All the readings are medians over rounds, at 12 and 20 threads, unpinned. "Idle" is the workers' idle share of their time on the timeline (`t.w.idle`: on the jobs' lock or in `recv`). A wait category's share is its part of that idle time. The thresholds are frozen in `bench/knee.py` (`PLATEAU`, `H1_*`, `IDLE_MIN`, `SHARE_*`, `MAIN_BUSY_*`, `H3_*`, `TRACE_COST_MAX`).

### H1: DRAM bandwidth

**The measurement:** the decode's net memory-controller bytes (reads plus writes, less the idle) over its wall, against the measured ceiling. Where the memory controller can't be counted, the proxy is used instead: lines the L3 missed (`longest_lat_cache.miss`) × 64 B, per process. That is reads and RFOs only, no writebacks, so the verdict is marked `(proxy)`.

**The rule:**
- **SUPPORTED:** both counts are at ≥ 80% of the ceiling, and they are flat (20 against 12 within ±10%).
- **REFUTED:** both are < 50%.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** otherwise.
- **UNMEASURED:** neither unit was counted for both the decode and the ceiling.

**The prediction:** 4–10 B of DRAM traffic per output byte, so 8–20 GB/s at about 2 GB/s of output. That is 25–60% of a triad ceiling expected near 35 GB/s, so REFUTED or INCONCLUSIVE. SUPPORTED would be a surprise.

### H2: a serial front end

**The measurement:** the workers' idle time while the scanner is reading a block or waiting for input (`t.w.idle.reading` + `t.w.idle.input`), and the scanner's reading share.

**The rule:**
- **SUPPORTED:** at both counts, idle ≥ 0.10 and the front end's share of it ≥ 50%.
- **REFUTED:** its share < 20% at both, or idle < 0.05 at both.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** otherwise.
- **VOID:** the timeline costs more than 10% of the wall at either count.
- **UNMEASURED:** no complete level-1 trace.

**The prediction:** a share of 20–50%, so INCONCLUSIVE. The scanner is busy 0.47 of the wall, enough to starve 7 workers in bursts but not throughout.

### H2w: the in-order window

**The measurement:** the workers' idle time while the scanner waits for a slot and the earliest untaken block is still decoding (`t.w.idle.slot_earliest_decoding`: head of line).

**The rule:** H2's, on this share.

**The prediction:** < 20%, so REFUTED. Doubling the window changed nothing in #510.

### H3: fixed costs

**The measurement:** the wall's fixed part, F = w₁ − (w₄ − w₁)/(k − 1), with k the ratio of the two files' outputs (4). It is reported as a share of the ×1 wall. Beside it:
- the plateau's movement: the ×4 speedup from 8 to 20 threads less the ×1 speedup;
- reported and not judged: minor faults per MB (perf's, else `getrusage`'s with perf's own included), the start and exit outside the wall (elapsed less wall), and the timeline's head (the first block taken), tail (the last block issued to the last taken) and idle while the scanner is outside its loop.

**The rule:**
- **SUPPORTED:** F ≥ 25% of the ×1 wall at both counts, and the plateau moves ≥ 0.05.
- **REFUTED:** F < 10% at both, and the movement < 0.02.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** otherwise.

**The prediction:** F at 10–25% and a movement under 0.05, so INCONCLUSIVE.

### H4: the in-order consumer (main)

**The measurement:** the workers' idle time while the window is full of ready, untaken blocks (`t.w.idle.slot_earliest_ready`); main's consuming share (`t.r.consuming`); and main's CPU (`thread_cpu`). main also hashes the output in the harness, so its share is an upper bound on a release consumer's.

**The rule:**
- **SUPPORTED:** H2's rule on the consumer's share holds, or main is consuming ≥ 90% of the span at both counts.
- **REFUTED:** the share is < 20% (or idle < 0.05) and main is consuming < 60% at both.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** otherwise.
- **VOID / UNMEASURED:** as for H2.

**The prediction:** REFUTED. main's CPU is 0.19–0.28 of the wall.

### VOID for the whole reading

The report gives no reading (exit 1) if either of these holds:
- the runs' outputs differ for a file;
- a cell has no clean run.

**The plateau itself is reported, not assumed:** the ×1 walls at 8 and 20. If it is not reproduced (20 against 8 outside 0.90–1.05), the readings stand but say so.

## The decision rule: which follow-up first

1. **If H1 is SUPPORTED, it goes first.** A bandwidth ceiling caps every other gain. The follow-up is fewer DRAM bytes per output byte: fused passes, the output's stores, and the reader's copy. Worker-count work is deprioritised.
2. **Then the other SUPPORTED hypotheses, by the share of time each could recover at 20 threads:**
   - for a wait, its share of the workers' time;
   - for H3, F's share of the ×1 wall.

   The follow-ups:
   - H2: the scanner, a parallel or faster block scan (the par-scan line);
   - H2w: the window's ordering;
   - H4: main's copy and hash (a zero-copy read path);
   - H3: pre-faulting or reusing buffers, the first block's latency, and splitting the tail.
3. **If none is SUPPORTED,** the per-worker contention model (c(W) = a + b·W², MONODROMY's records on #497) stays the lead. The next step is cycles per byte decomposed by thread count.

INCONCLUSIVE ranks nothing; it is reported, with its figures.

## Time on the i9, and what may be unmeasurable

**Time:** `knee.py estimate` gives about 8 minutes:
- the build, about 2.5 min;
- the 160 timed runs, about 1.2 min;
- the 320 timeline runs, about 2.4 min;
- the ceiling, about 45 s;
- the idle readings, about 20 s.

That is within the 15 minutes asked. It is a model from the release-scaling walls, assuming 0.08 s of process and perf start a run.

**What may be unmeasurable:**
- **The memory controller.** The workstation's last recorded `kernel.perf_event_paranoid` is 1 (`workers-at-cores/run-20261117T083701Z`, `miss-cost/run-20261117T104648Z`), where system-wide counting is refused. Unless `sudo -n` works there, H1 is read from the proxy, and is marked so.
  - `perf list` there names `uncore_imc/data_reads/` and `data_writes/`, so with `sudo -n` or paranoid 0 they count.
  - The proxy undercounts DRAM reads by the L2 hardware prefetcher's misses that `longest_lat_cache.miss` doesn't see, and it has no writes. So a proxy SUPPORTED is strong, and a proxy REFUTED is weaker.
- **The DIMMs' speed** without root: probably unknown. The ceiling is measured instead.
- **The pinned 10-thread cell** runs 10 workers, not 7, as above: context, not judged.

## The dry run

In the development VM (4 vCPUs, `perf` not installed, paranoid 2), the recipe ran with tiny counts: `just bench-knee 2 2 2 "4,8,12,20" "2,4" "1,2" 32 1`, on the real Wikidata sample. As expected:
- the memory controller and the proxy were UNMEASURED, so H1 was UNMEASURED;
- the timeline (complete at every cell, its cost 0.95–1.08), the ceiling's own rate and the fixed-cost split were measured, and every other hypothesis was read;
- the plateau was reported as not reproduced, as it shouldn't be on 4 vCPUs;
- the verdicts on its 4-CPU shape mean nothing.
