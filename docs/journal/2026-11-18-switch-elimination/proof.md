# Forest sections, boundary parentheses and marked words

## 1. A presentation closed under first return

A site is a copy of the integers in $[0,w_v)$. Each arc $e$ has a source site $u$, a target site $v$, two local offsets $x_e,y_e$, a positive width $b_e$, and a word $W_e$. On the source slice it sends $x_e+k$ to $y_e+k$. At each site the outgoing slices tile the site, and the incoming slices do too. Hence the arcs specify a permutation. We store words in decoder output order. If an LF trajectory takes arcs $a$ and then $b$, its inverse reads $W_b W_a$.

Initially a site is a maximal constant run of the bzip2 last column $L$. Its LF image is an interval, whose start is the byte-bucket start plus the widths of preceding runs of that byte. Intersect that image with the run sites. Each intersection is one arc, labelled with the source byte. There are $E$ arcs and $V=r$ sites. Scanning the two endpoint lists in image order is linear after stable byte bucketing. The forest prototype instead locates the first target of each run by binary search, at cost $O(r\log r+E)$. It merges the capped MTF run records first. All these quantities are available before inverse BWT.

Fix some arcs as cuts. Their source occurrences form the retained section. A residual site for a cut $i$ has width $b_i$ and coordinate $k$ at its source. The next transition traverses $i$, lands at its head, runs through interior arcs and stops just before the next cut $j$. A band from $i$ to $j$ therefore has the same form as an original arc. The primary is kept by cutting its entire current outgoing arc, including at subsequent levels. Its new coordinate is its old offset minus that arc's start. This convention works even if the primary's arc is the heaviest one.

Investigation 2 gives the factorisation of the initial first return into stable order and open transport. Here the cut arcs themselves supply both endpoint coordinates of their wiring. At every later level the translated, labelled bands supply the same information, even when the map on the retained sites has no byte-stable description. First returns compose because stopping at a smaller section groups consecutive excursions to the larger one. [Open-Return proof](../2026-11-18-open-return/proof.md)

## 2. A forest determines its interior from its boundary

Keep a forest in the **undirected multigraph** of sites and arcs. A loop and a second parallel edge each close an undirected cycle. The remaining arcs are cuts, called chords. Kruskal in descending width, excluding the required marked chord, minimises the sum of chord widths among forests with that requirement. This minimisation follows the spanning-forest exchange property. It optimises the retained occurrence count for the specified sites. Other costs and other partitions have separate optima.

**Shared coordinate.** For a kept arc $u\to v$ choose $a_v=a_u+x_e-y_e$. Root each tree and propagate this equation, using its negative on a reverse edge. The absence of undirected cycles makes it consistent. The coordinate $z=k+a_u$ of a point with local offset $k$ is constant along every interior arc. Each end of a chord is now an interval of integer heights. Different trees use independent origins.

**Boundary uniqueness.** At a fixed height attach a leaf to each active chord landing and another to each active chord departure. At each occupied site exactly one incoming and one outgoing arc are active, including the leaves. The active interior and its leaves are therefore disjoint paths. They cannot form a cycle inside a forest. Take an interior edge and delete it from its tree. The edge is used precisely when one side contains an odd number of active port leaves. Counting degrees on that side proves the assertion. Equivalently the active edges are the unique tree $T$-join with the specified odd leaves $T$. Two solutions would differ on an even subgraph of a forest, which is empty. This argument includes a landing and departure at the same site. Distinguishing their leaves gives the correct empty interior path.

For a particular landing and departure in a tree, the only possible interior route is their unique tree path. The path must have the LF orientation. The heights giving that pairing are the intersection of the two port intervals and the intervals of all edges of that path. An intersection of intervals is an interval. A pair of named ports therefore contributes at most one nonempty band. This convexity uses a single translated interval on each retained tree edge.

**A necessary hypothesis.** Take two landing sites $A,C$ and two departure sites $B,D$. Allow all four edges from the first two to the last two. At height zero route $A$ to $B$ and $C$ to $D$, and at height one use the opposite matching. Swapping the two matchings preserves every port interval, every site width, and the zero shift of each edge. Each interior edge still occupies one unit interval. The return at height zero changes. This directed acyclic diamond has an undirected cycle. Its interior can carry information invisible at its boundary.

## 3. One port order compiles the tree into parentheses

Regard a landing leaf as directed into its site, and a departure leaf as directed out. Root the tree anywhere at a site. Recursively list every child subtree directed **into** the current site, then its own landing leaves, then its departure leaves, then every child subtree directed **out** of it. The order within a group is arbitrary and fixed. Equivalently insert a site token between the incoming and outgoing groups and erase the site tokens after ordering. This gives two simultaneous properties.

1. Each subtree occupies a contiguous part of the leaf order. It is the leaf order of a planar embedding of the tree.
2. The site tokens and leaves give a topological order of its directed edges. For an incoming child its whole subtree precedes the parent token. For an outgoing child its whole subtree follows it. Every directed path therefore starts at an earlier leaf and ends at a later one.

Discard the ports whose intervals miss $z$. Two paths at height $z$ are vertex-disjoint. Their end pairs cannot alternate around the leaf order. One way to see this is to thicken the plane tree to a disk. An arc between one alternating pair separates the other two leaves, forcing an intersection. Every active path thus pairs an earlier landing with a later departure, and these pairs are noncrossing. Such a matching is unique. At a departure one must take the last still unmatched landing, since taking an earlier one would force a crossing. Induction gives the usual stack algorithm for a balanced parenthesis word, also called a Dyck word.

Consequently the unlabelled transport takes as input just a component identifier and an ordered list of signed intervals for each component. All interior interval placements and even the tree topology can be erased at this interface. The initial tree calculation is charged. The tree is kept separately when the caller wants words.

**An eleven-row example.** The primitive binary column `11010110100` with mark zero has LF images `[5,6,0,7,1,8,9,2,10,3,4]`. Descending-width Kruskal with source-rank tie order cuts three chords. $A$ has source rows 0 and 1, $B$ has row 8 and $C$ has row 10. One valid port order, with $+$ for a landing and $-$ for a departure, is $C^+,A^+,B^-,B^+,C^-,A^-$. Their half-open intervals in this order are $[1,2),[-1,1),[-1,0),[1,2),[1,2),[0,2)$. At $z=-1$ only $A^+,B^-$ remain. At zero only $A^+,A^-$ remain. At one the sequence is $C^+,B^+,C^-,A^-$, with the nested matching $B^+C^-$ and $C^+A^-$. Restoring local offsets gives the cycle $A_0,B_0,C_0,A_1,A_0$. In the original rows these are $0,8,10,1,0$. Ordinary LF gives exactly these successive first returns. [Executable example](screens.py)

**Two executable evaluations.** The Rust decoder groups consecutive integer heights between successive port endpoints. On each nonempty slab it scans the currently active ports in the precomputed order, matches parentheses, and coalesces consecutive answers for each landing. If $J$ is the sum of the counts of active pairs over the slabs, routing takes $O(q\log q+J)$ time after the forest and heights are known. Here $q$ is the number of chords. Each range event updates an ordered set. Every operation on a matched pair and its band's tail takes constant time.

The independent interval implementation scans the **port order** once. Keep each unmatched opening fragment with its opening time. For a closing interval $I$, find the latest live opening fragment intersecting $I$. It is the top of the stack throughout their intersection. Emit that intersection, delete it from both, and repeat on their remainders. Each emission corresponds to one maximal pair interval. Earlier closings can leave holes in an opening only where it has already matched. A later opening splits a closing only where that later opening wins. Neither can fragment a connected true answer for the same two ports.

An interval index on the compressed endpoint coordinates stores each active fragment in $O(\log(q+1))$ canonical nodes. Each node has a priority heap and its subtree maximum. An overlap query touches $O(\log(q+1))$ partial and canonical nodes, using the subtree maximum on a fully covered node. Fragment insertion and deletion, including amortised stale-heap removal, cost $O(\log(q+1)\log(q+B+1))$ each. There are $q$ initial openings and $B$ reports, each generating at most two leftover opening fragments. This evaluates the transport with $O((q+B)\log(q+1)\log(q+B+1))$ time and $O((q+B)\log(q+1))$ space including lazy entries. The bound is independent of the ordinate range, the number of slab matches, and the lengths of interior paths. [Interval implementation and independent all-row check](boundary.py)

For a separation, use a directed chain of $k$ sites of width $k+1$. At heights $0$ to $k-1$ each site has a self-loop, split at its own site number where that gives two positive pieces. At height $k$ use the full chain closed by a chord from its last site to its first. The internal chain is a valid forest. The slab scan makes $k^2+1$ matches across the heights. The interval algorithm reports $2k$ bands. This multicycle example witnesses the work difference between the two algorithms. [Executable construction](screens.py)

## 4. How large can a return be?

Write $V,E$ for the current site and arc counts and $c$ for the number of forest components, including isolated sites. Then $q=E-V+c$. For a positive-width permutation network each arc belongs to a directed closed walk, so excluding the marked arc does not disconnect a weak component. A primitive block has $c=1$.

**Band bound.** Place a tape of $b_i$ consecutive offsets at each chord landing $i$, initially one fragment. Process the interior in a topological order. At a site of outgoing degree $b$, align its incoming tapes in coordinate order and cut them at its $b-1$ outgoing boundaries. At most $b-1$ currently unsplit fragments are divided. Route the results to their destinations, collecting the departing chords as output. Concatenating incoming lists creates no fragments. The total number arriving at departures is at most
$$
B\ \le\ q+\sum_v(\deg^+(v)-1)=q+E-V=2q-c .
$$
The schedule is a counting proof. The parenthesis algorithms obtain the answer without constructing its intermediate tapes. This counting argument applies to any directed acyclic interior whose local intervals are known. The boundary-only conclusion uses the undirected forest.

The next network has $V'=q$ and $E'=B$. Thus $E'-V'\le E-V$ at **every** recursion. With a primitive input the number of sites at the next cut is exactly the present excess plus one. This is a bound on combinatorial state. The retained mass is the total width of the chords, and the slab work also depends on how their height intervals overlap. Neither is determined by the excess. Each nonempty forest removes positive mass. A layer with no eligible interior edge uses the sparse inverse at the current mark. The Rust prototype also budgets the total slab-pair visits at $4n$ and can use that same inverse on budget exhaustion.

An interpretation of the excess makes the link to Investigation 1 explicit. A current network of mass $M$ has $M-V$ adjacent gaps inside its sites. Exactly $M-E$ are inside an outgoing translated band. Their partial gap map is injective. The difference $E-V$ counts gaps with no outgoing continuation and, equally, gaps with no incoming continuation. It depends on the presentation. Higher-block refinement can trade a smaller excess against more sites. [Frozen refinement screen](evidence/screens.txt)

## 5. Length and spelling after routing

Give the forest a second potential $h$ with $h(v)-h(u)=|W_e|$ on each directed edge $u\to v$. For a landing at $s$ followed by a departure at $t$, after the first chord $i$, the exact return length is
$$
 |W_i|+h(t)-h(s).
$$
Only two potential values and the chord length are needed. The positive word is the reversed order of the previous edge words along the unique path $s\leadsto t$, followed by $W_i$. Record $(s,t,i)$ with that length and the forest layer. An empty path reuses $W_i$. Building a band never expands the path, forms a word dictionary, or compares spellings. More generally any group-valued edge product can be stored as root potentials, using inverses on a backward edge. Length suffices for this decoder.

The output interpreter follows inverse residual arcs from the marked coordinate. On its first request for a path recipe it enumerates its children using parent links. Later requests can copy the already emitted bytes. Only the last emission may be truncated. A recipe refers strictly to earlier layers, so the first expansion completes before a second request for the same recipe unless the entire job has ended. Each expanded nontrivial recipe has at least two children. Fully expanding a marked lap of length $d$ into single original bytes gives at most $d-1$ internal nodes and at most $2d-2$ edges for a single root. The root-forest version satisfies the same linear estimate. First-use caching can only reduce that work. The marked lap has length at most $n$. Repeat its already emitted prefix to reach $n$ on a short cycle. This works on arbitrary columns and primaries, including a period which does not divide $n$.

Every permutation cycle contains a chord because the interior is acyclic. Grouping into first returns conserves $\sum_e b_e |W_e|=n$ even off the marked cycle. The marked answer uses only one of these cycles. The native tests independently walk dense LF from every retained point at every constructed layer and check both destination and spelling. The full decoder passes the emitted pre-RLE1 bytes to mibz2's RLE1 and both CRC checks. [Arrow and word audit](forest_audit.rs) · [Independent stream checks](verify.py)

## 6. Two other executions of the same local algebra

A loop-free site can be removed directly. If it has $a$ incoming and $b$ outgoing intervals and $j$ coincident interior cuts between their local partitions, their overlay has exactly $a+b-1-j$ pieces. Replace each by its predecessor-to-successor band with the concatenated return word. The network loses $1+j$ arcs and one site. Its excess falls by exactly $j$. Recode surviving **outgoing bands** as sites by intersecting incoming and outgoing lists to name consecutive bands. This recoding is a conjugacy of the current system. With $J_0$ common interior cuts it sends $(V,E)$ to $(E,2E-V-J_0)$, also reducing excess by $J_0$.

The rank-order executable protects the marked site, removes each available loop-free site in turn, recodes the remaining bands, and repeats. Every live arc width and list position has a cost. A small excess gives no general work bound for a sweep. For example, take a chain of $t$ sites of common width $w$ closed through a protected site. Scramble the first transition into $w$ separate unit bands, and use one full-width band for each successive transition. Sweeping along the chain transmits all $w$ pieces through each of $t$ sites. This gives $tw$ splices in a measured network with $O(t+w)$ initial bands.

The second execution keeps two ordered lists. If a whole image tile equals a whole domain tile away from the primary, connect the two bands. These connections are disjoint chains and possibly unmarked cycles. Join each surviving chain into one variable-arity word, then refine its image at the next domain boundaries. For $b$ bands let $c_0$ be the number of common cells between consecutive common cuts, and $h_0$ the number of removed exact matches. The number of resulting overlap bands is exactly $2b-c_0-h_0$. Writing $d=b-c_0$, at most $2d+1$ common cells survive since every nonstraight common cell contains at least one of the $2d$ unshared boundaries and the mark can protect one more. Hence the next list has at most $4d+1$ bands. Each surviving chain also establishes a mandatory common cell boundary in the refined presentation, giving $d_{\rm next}\le d$. A chain closed wholly off the mark can be discarded. The proof of the inequality allows these unmarked cycles because they contribute equally many removed sites and bands. The projection audits force up to five rounds even on tiny columns which the ordinary decoder would finish directly. [Two-list audit](coincidence_audit.rs) · [Local-splice audit](switch_audit.rs)
