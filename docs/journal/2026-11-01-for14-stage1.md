## 2. The record specification

**One record, 64 bytes, cache-line aligned, holds rows j = 32g … 32g + 31:**

| bytes | contents |
|---|---|
| 0–55 | 32 offsets × 14 bits; offset i at bits 14i … 14i + 13, little-endian bit order |
| 56–63 | the header, a little-endian u64 |

**The header's fields:**

| bits | FOR record | RAW record |
|---|---|---|
| 0–19 | base: the group's smallest ψ | the group's index in the raw area |
| 20–27 | F: the group's one byte | 0 |
| 28 | 0 | 1 |
| 29–31 | 0 | 0 |
| 32–63 | start mask: bit 32 + i while row 32g + i starts a segment | the same |

**The formats:**
- **FOR:** the group's 32 rows lie in one F bucket, and its ψ span is at most 2^14 − 2.
  Then ψ(j) = base + offset and F[j] = F.
- **RUN groups are FOR records** with offsets 0…31. So there is one ordinary format,
  and no format branch on the common path.
- **RAW:** everything else. All 32 offsets are the sentinel S = 2^14 − 1. The raw area
  holds 128 bytes a RAW group, two whole cache lines: entry i is a
  u32 with ψ in bits 0–19 and F in bits 20–27.
- **The sentinel costs 8 groups** of 6.47 M (span exactly 2^14 − 1).

**Why the header is last:** every offset's 4-byte load starts at byte ⌊14i/8⌋ ≤ 54 and
ends inside the line. Header and offset are both addressed from the row alone, so they
are two independent loads of one line.

**The byte, carried:**
- At row r the walk writes L[r] = F[ψ⁻¹(r)], the F of the row the lane came from.
- At each step the lane writes the byte it carries, takes F[r] from the header, and moves
  to ψ(r).
- A segment's first byte, L[start], is read from the last column (`ll`), once a
  segment: 512 reads a block. `ll` is the MTF stage's output, live in the decoder anyway.
- The oracle asserts L[ψ(j)] = F[j] on every row of every block.

**What each kind of step loads:**

| step | lines | loads and branches |
|---|---|---|
| ordinary (FOR, 98.1% of steps) | its record's line: 1 | header and offset (independent, same line); one compare with S |
| a start | the same line | S, then the header's mask bit: the segment ends |
| a raw row (1.9%) | the same line, then the entry's line | S, mask clear: prefetch the entry, write nothing, move to PARK |
| a parked lane (1.9%) | the parking line (L1-resident), and the entry prefetched a turn ago | S, row = PARK: ψ and F from the entry |

**How the walker reaches a raw successor without an extra lookup on ordinary rows:**
- The sentinel folds three tests into the one compare an ordinary step makes anyway:
  is this a start, is it a raw row, is the lane parked.
- A lane at a raw row parks: it moves to the first row of one extra record whose
  offsets are all S and whose mask is empty. Its next turn lands on S again, sees PARK,
  and reads the entry it prefetched a turn earlier.
- So a raw successor costs one lane turn and one extra line. An ordinary step tests
  nothing about it.
- In the room walk, a turn that writes nothing moves the lane's base back a byte, so
  every later byte still lands where it belongs.

**Indexing:**
- record = row ≫ 5; i = row & 31;
- raw entry = 32 × header[0–19] + i (u32s), for a RAW record;
- PARK = 32 × (the block's record count).

**Segments:**
- The starts are production's (511 xorshift rows plus the one the output begins in).
- Each start's ψ and F, and its true offset, are read before the walk marks it. Marking
  writes S and sets the mask bit; the end of the walk restores both.
- Termination, the room passes, the handoffs and the tail are production's, line for
  line.

**Worst case:** an all-RAW block takes 6 bytes a row (2 + 4), and every step two turns.
That is the 100%-forced check in §4.

## 3. The implementation

- **`Records::from_block`, the oracle:**
  - It builds production's T-vector (the `o` arm's copy of `bwt::build`) and asserts
    T's byte = L at every row, and L[ψ(j)] = F[j].
  - It makes the records, then decodes every row back to its ψ and F against the
    T-vector.
  - It never runs inside a timing.
  - `force_permille` makes a hashed share of the FOR groups RAW, for checks only.
- **`Compact::load_h`, h's "build":**
  - It copies the block's precomputed records into the worker's reused buffers. That
    leaves them as freshly written as a direct builder would.
  - This is the optimistic stand-in. Its build figures are not a builder's.
- **`room_h` and `plain_h`:**
  - production's room walk and plain walk, on records;
  - their own lane type (`LaneH`), so `o`, `v` and `u` compile exactly as before.
- **h's working set holds no T-vector.** Its `Compact` never builds `t32` and keeps only
  records and raw entries. Live blocks are bounded as in every grid arm: one a worker.
- **The grid:** h arms (`h1`, `h4`) make every block's records before round −1, on every
  CPU, with the row-by-row check. Then the usual hash check runs on every arm's every
  block, then the timed rounds.

## 5. The disassembly (`disassembly.md`)

**The ordinary step, in one binary** (x86-64 baseline target, as production builds it):

| walk | instructions an ordinary step | loads | stores | dependent lines a step |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `o`: production's entry (the control) | **16** | 3: lane row, entry, base | 2: row, byte | 1 |
| `v`: 32-bit link, byte from F (stage A) | 28 | 7, a dependent pair among them (window byte → bucket start) | 3 | 1 |
| `u`: 24-bit link, byte from F (stage A) | 31 | 7, as v's | 3 | 1, crossing a line for 3 rows in 64 |
| `h`, first cut (1b49c25) | 48 | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| **`h`, frozen** | **38** | 5: lane row, header, offset, lane byte, base | 3: byte carried, row, output | 1 |

**h's 22 extra instructions over o's step:**
- the 14-bit extraction: about 12 (14i by shifts, the byte offset, the variable shift
  by `cl`, the mask);
- the sentinel's compare and branch: 2;
- the carried byte: 4 (load it, take F from the header, store it);
- base plus offset: 2;
- the line address: 2.

**What the rework saved:** from 48 to 38, by the sentinel (the pend test and the
start-mask test left the common path).

**What is left is inherent to the format:**
- the extraction;
- the carried byte;
- one more store a step, which matters on the i9's core, with one store port.
- **BMI2 does not remove it (§5a, measured).** The first version of this record
  estimated about 27 instructions with BMI2, uncompiled. Compiled, the BMI2 step is 37.

The dependent chain is one line a step in every walk: h's header and offset loads are
independent of each other.

## 5a. The BMI2 code-generation test (report 135; after the review)

**The question (the maintainer's, report 135):** is h paying an avoidable instruction
tax for the packed-field extraction, which the i9's BMI2 could remove? The record format
is unchanged, and so is the source.

**The arm:**
- `b` is `room_h`'s body compiled a second time, inside
  `#[target_feature(enable = "bmi2")]` (`room_h_bmi2`).
- The two share one source through a macro (`room_h_body!`).
- It is a separate candidate, never a silent change to `h`.
- Adding it changed `h`'s register allocation in this branch's binary (its function grew
  from 0x28eb to 0x243f bytes; the closures inline differently). So the frozen
  confirmation runs the frozen commit itself (§7), not this branch's head.

**What changed, from the disassembly (`disassembly-bmi2.md`):**

| walk | instructions an ordinary step | what changed |
|---|--:|---|
| `h` (baseline x86-64) | 38 | — |
| **`b` (BMI2)** | **37** | `mov` + `shr %cl` (the variable shift, 2 instructions and on Intel 2–3 uops) became one `shrx` with its load folded in |

- **Unchanged:** the 14·i index arithmetic (5), the byte-offset and shift split (2), the
  mask, the sentinel test, the carried byte and the lane traffic.
- BMI2 has no instruction for the rest. `bextr` would need its control word built each
  step, and the compiler does not use it.
- The ~27 estimate assumed the whole extraction would shrink. It does not.

**Correctness:** `h-check` walks b beside h. Exact output and CRC on wikidata-20261026,
mixed-independent, text-l1, period-3, zeros and random, at 0, 5% and 100% forced RAW,
with `COOP_H_STARTS=1` (`b-check.log`).

**VM, indicative only** (8 rounds, unpinned, `vm-grid-bmi2.txt`):

| | the walk, paired |
|---|--:|
| h1/o1 | 0.547 |
| b1/o1 | 0.577 |
| h4/o4 | 0.572 (2.054 / 3.588, each against o1) |
| b4/o4 | 0.649 (2.329 / 3.588) |

b is 5% (one block) and 13% (four blocks) faster than h on the VM here; the medians at one
block are equal (295 MB/s each). Unpinned, 8 rounds: indicative, not a measurement.
Either way, b1/o1 is below the 0.669 break-even.

**Decision, by report 135's rule:** code generation did not improve materially (38 → 37,
one fused shift).
- So `b` is **not** added to the i9 run, and gets no protocol of its own.
- It stays on the branch as the record of the test.
- Reaching v's 28 would take hand-scheduling the index arithmetic, or another offset
  layout. That is further FOR-14 optimization or a new format, neither authorized. And
  v's 28 instructions measured 0.721 on the i9, still below 0.774.

## 6. The cost bound, and why the stop rule is met

**Stage A calibrates instructions against the i9.** It measured v's and u's walks
against o on the i9 (2026-10-29-compact-psi.md §5.3). Their instruction counts are
above, from the same code:

| walk | instructions | i9 walk ratio, 1 block | 4 blocks | instructions alone would give (16/I) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| v | 28 | 0.721 | 0.839 | 0.571 |
| u | 31 | 0.598 | 0.813 | 0.516 |
| h | 38 | predicted 0.59–0.66; VM 0.61–0.66 | VM 0.52–0.57 | 0.421 |

**A fit, not a measurement:**
- At one block, about half of o's walk time scales with its instructions: 0.032 of
  it an instruction, from v; the rest (0.48–0.67, from v and u) does not.
- That fit gives h1/o1 ≈ 0.59–0.66 on the i9.
- The VM's two smoke runs (5 and 8 rounds, unpinned, indicative only) gave 0.61–0.66.
- The VM is a wider core (a 2.1 GHz Xeon with 2 MB of L2 a core and a 260 MB L3). It
  holds all four tables in cache, so it cannot show the memory side at all, and its
  width favours h's extra instructions.

**The one-worker bound, a conditional planning bound** (qualified after the guardian's
review, report 135):
- It assumes the same walk's CPU per byte at four workers is at least its CPU per byte
  at one. That is the expected behaviour of competing memory-intensive workers, with
  contention and the lower all-core clock adding. **It is an assumption, not a physical
  law:** phase overlap, cache state, scheduling and measurement context can move it.
- With the decoder's traversal plus assembly at **1.236 CPU-s/GB at one worker and
  1.847 at four** (report 131 §II, from the whole-corpus reconstruction), h's
  four-worker cost is at least 1.236 / W1, where W1 = h1/o1, the walk's speed ratio.
  - **Break-even** with o at four workers needs 1.236 / W1 ≤ 1.847: **W1 ≥ 0.669**.
  - Astra's **0.25 CPU-s/GB saving**, with construction unchanged, needs 1.236 / W1 ≤
    1.597: **W1 ≥ 0.774**.
- Given those assumptions, the thresholds do not depend on what the smaller table saves
  in memory time.
- **They are conditional on three things:**
  - the phase-cost model (the 1.236 and 1.847);
  - unchanged construction cost (a materially cheaper compact builder would offset some
    navigation regression; nothing here shows one, and none was built);
  - the four-worker-at-least-one-worker assumption above.
- They are planning bounds for this decision, not universal limits on compact
  representations.

**The verdict:**
- The prediction (0.59–0.66) and the VM (0.61–0.66) are both below break-even, and well
  below 0.774.
- Even v's walk, with ten fewer instructions than h and no extraction, is 0.721 on the
  i9: below 0.774.
- An extraction-based format reaching 0.774 would need a common step cheaper than v's.
  BMI2, compiled, gives 37 (§5a).
- So there is **no credible path to an affordable common navigation path** for
  FOR-14/raw: step 7's stop condition.
- By report 131's table: "Common navigation path cannot repay its overhead → defer this
  fixed-record architecture; no direct builder."

**What this rests on:**
- The 1.236 and 1.847 are planning figures from a reconstruction, not one measurement.
  The bound moves with them: a one-worker figure 10% higher raises the 0.25 threshold to
  0.851, and 10% lower to 0.697.
- The stage-A fit has two points per condition.
- The grid's "walk" includes assembly, as the decoder's traversal figure does. Both
  arms assemble the same way.
- The four-worker-at-least-one-worker premise is untested here. It is expected for
  independent blocks competing for memory, but it is not guaranteed.

**What it does not show:**
- It does not disprove compact navigation in general (P40).
- Under the same conditions (these phase costs, construction unchanged, four-worker cost
  at least one-worker), it gives a compact format a planning budget: its ordinary step
  must run at ≥ 0.774× the shipped step's speed at one worker.
- The characterization the guardian set (report 135): **a working, half-sized
  representation whose current navigation implementation has a strongly unfavourable
  execution-cost model, making further construction work unjustified without new
  evidence.** It is an engineering stop, not a proof of impossibility.
- It says nothing about the builder (P84), which was not reached.

## 7. The frozen i9 protocol (authorized once by report 135; not yet run)

**The gates and decision, in order:**

| gate | rule | if it fails |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | every round pinned and placed; round −1 passed (the run aborts otherwise) | void |
| G1 | the walk's o1/p1 and o4/p4 each within [0.95, 1.05], half-widths below 0.03 (the copy reproduces production's walk, which is unchanged since stage A; only production's build changed, #114) | void |
| 1 | **STOP** if W1 = h1/o1 has its upper bound below **0.774** (§6) | — |
| 2 | **STOP** if W4 = h4/o4 has its upper bound below 1.00 | — |
| 3 | **EARNS STAGE 2** (a builder budget, G from the measurement) if W4's lower bound is at least 1.156 | — |
| 4 | otherwise **INCONCLUSIVE**: stops this format unless the reviewers decide otherwise; no automatic next variant | — |

**Predictions, fixed before any run:**
- W1 in [0.50, 0.70];
- W4 in [0.55, 1.00];
- the decision STOP, by rule 1.

A W1 above 0.70 would falsify the §6 calibration, and is worth knowing either way.

**What the grid leaves out:**
- It measures traversal with the records' build precomputed: optimistic for h.
- Its four-block condition has less memory pressure than the decoder at four workers
  (no MTF, RLE1 or reader traffic): pessimistic for h.
- Rule 1 does not depend on that pressure. Rules 2 and 3 do, so they can stop or earn a
  builder only on the grid's terms.

**The dry run:** one round on the VM checked the recipe, the hash check, the records'
check and the analysis (`recipe-dry-run.log`, `dry-run-coop-*`). With one round it is
void by G1, as it should be. It is not a measurement.

## 8. The minimal-walker diagnostic (planned, not built)

From the researcher's last contribution (report 132), bounded as the guardian set it
(report 134).

**Its question:** is the shipped 32-lane walker limited by miss concurrency, instruction
throughput or bookkeeping?

**The link to §6:** §6's stop rests on stage A's calibration, by which about half the
one-worker walk scales with instructions. That is the researcher's "execution throughput
is the limit" row. This diagnostic's first rungs test it directly. The historical 38.55
and 4.49 cycles a byte are not comparable and are not used.

**Scope:**
- **Workload:** one real T-vector, extracted from a full block of the P87 corpus by
  production's build, stored with its hash. Checks: the number of rows traversed, and
  the output's hash at the rungs that write.
- **The rungs**, each adding one cost to the one before:

  | rung | adds |
  |---|---|
  | R0 | k independent chains: next = T[cur] ≫ 8, the byte summed; no stores |
  | R1 | lane state in a memory array, as production keeps it |
  | R2 | software prefetch at production's distance (one lane turn) |
  | R3 | the start-flag test and segment termination |
  | R4 | output stores to segment buffers: production's room walk itself, called from the library (the top rung) |

- **Lanes:** 1, 8, 16 and 32 at first. Extended only if a decision below depends on it.
- **Counters:** audited on the i9 first (`perf list`), then counted in small groups with
  no multiplexing:
  - cycles, instructions, `uops_issued.any`, `uops_retired.retire_slots`;
  - `l1d_pend_miss.pending` and `.pending_cycles`: demand MLP, Intel's definition;
  - `l1d_pend_miss.fb_full`;
  - `cycle_activity.stalls_l1d_miss`, `.stalls_l2_miss`, `.stalls_l3_miss`;
  - `resource_stalls.any`, `resource_stalls.sb` (the store buffer);
  - `dtlb_load_misses.walk_active`;
  - `sw_prefetch_access.t0`, `load_hit_pre.sw_pf`.
  - Speedup ratios are never read as outstanding requests.
- **Four-core relevance:** R0 and R4 at 32 lanes, repeated as four copies on four
  physical cores, each with its own copy of the T-vector.

**Decisions, fixed before it is built:**

| result | decision |
|---|---|
| D1: R0 at 32 lanes within 15% of R4 (cycles a row) | the walker's scheduling and bookkeeping are not the limit: **no walker rewrite** (the stop rule); attention goes to fewer dependent misses or less work |
| D2: R0 at least 1.5× faster than R4 | find the rung where the gap opens, and budget a rewrite of that rung alone |
| D3: R4's uops a row at 4 a cycle account for at least 70% of its cycles a row | execution-bound: any compact format must be nearly instruction-neutral (this would confirm §6's premise) |
| D4: demand MLP plateaus as lanes rise, with fb_full cycles growing | miss capacity is the limit: no more lanes |
| D5: the R0/R4 gap differs by more than 15 points between one core and four | isolated-core conclusions do not transfer: report it and stop |

**Budget:** at most one engineer-day of VM preparation, then one i9 capture of about 10
minutes, with sudo for the counters. Neither is started, and neither is authorized yet.

## 9. What changes

- **FOR-14/raw stops at stage 1:**
  - the format is feasible, compact (0.519× the table) and exact;
  - its ordinary step costs 2.4× production's in instructions, and the one-worker bound
    makes that unrecoverable.
- **The budget is general:** any compact navigation format must keep W1 ≥ 0.774 (at
  these phase costs) before its memory saving can matter.
- **The minimal-walker diagnostic** is the natural next measurement. It tests the
  calibration this stop relies on, and where the shipped walker's time goes.
