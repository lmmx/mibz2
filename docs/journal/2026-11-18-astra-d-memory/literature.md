# External memory and small-space inversion

**The I/O model.** Aggarwal and Vitter charge one transfer per contiguous block of `B` items into internal memory of size `M`. A scan takes order `N/B` transfers. Comparison sorting has the familiar scan times logarithm cost, with base `M/B` and a minimum of one scan. Here a line holds 64 bytes, or sixteen 32-bit row entries. The model L2 holds 4,096 lines and the central LLC case 32,768. Uniform-cost I/Os omit the CPU distinction between an independently issued stream and a line whose address is still unknown. The run-restricted family and its implicit stable permutation also do not satisfy a generic arbitrary-permutation lower-bound argument. [Aggarwal & Vitter, *The Input/Output Complexity of Sorting and Related Problems*](https://research.ibm.com/publications/the-inputoutput-complexity-of-sorting-and-related-problems)

**Construction and inversion by scans.** Ferragina, Gagie and Manzini's *bwt-disk* constructs the BWT right to left in blocks, merging the new block's ordered suffixes into the accumulated transform using rank/gap information. For inversion (§5), they grow disjoint substrings from selected BWT rows, mark covered rows, and list-rank/sort substring headers to recognize adjacency and recover text order. Reducing sorting to order `n/log n` headers allows linear-bit disk workspace. Their pass bounds and space–pass lower bound concern particular disk access models. The part that transfers here is scheduling construction as scans over compact records. A concrete dense comparator shows how much a single extra pass can cost. A cold read and external rewrite of one 32-bit record for each of 899,931 rows transfers at least 7,199,488 bytes with aligned input and output, even with write allocation waived. That already exceeds the frozen Cells ledger. Their bit-packed algorithm has a different record count. Our builder uses two scans over 56,386 capped records and scatters each F character's pieces in increasing order. [Ferragina, Gagie & Manzini, §§3 and 5, Theorems 6–7](https://people.unipmn.it/manzini/bwtdisk/bwte.pdf)

**Small extra space.** Crochemore, Grossi, Kärkkäinen and Landau give an in-place BWT construction with quadratic time and constant extra cells beyond the text array, and a block-size/alphabet trade-off for obtaining and inverting the transform. Reusing writable storage transfers to a worker. The amount of extra space alone gives little information about how often it rereads the base array. Here the directory uses dead run records, and the literal data are moved at most once after discovery. The explicit set replay charges these visits. [Crochemore et al., *Computing the Burrows–Wheeler Transform in Place and in Small Space*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jda.2015.01.004)

**Cache-aware walks.** Kärkkäinen, Kempa and Puglisi distinguish reducing accesses (pairs of characters per successor, or copies across equal contexts) from overlapping misses (several independent parts). bzip2 supplies one primary and omits the part offsets. The pinned Segmented control pays to mark starts, fill per-segment buffers and assemble them. A compact single-chain traversal can remove those arrays and still expose much more dependent latency. The structure of the workload matters to a copy scheme. MONODROMY found a first boundary excess of 10,955 on frozen and a slowly decaying profile beyond depth one. This counts discontinuities under refinement. It supplies no machine-independent lower bound on inversion. [Kärkkäinen, Kempa & Puglisi, §§3–4](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/tpkarkka/publications/dcc2012.pdf) · [MONODROMY record 63](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/276db91c46df7027534785562a89bdac669e635c/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-18-q-profile.md)

**Run-length navigation.** A move structure represents a permutation using intervals on which it is a translation. Brown and Langmead show how length capping permits constant amortized navigation around a full single cycle and linear-time inversion in run-bounded workspace. Their measured workloads and splitting rule differ from this experiment. We take the exact translation property, split at fixed F-row tile boundaries, and price the local predecessor scan directly. Intersecting the images with 128-row tiles adds 6,652 pieces to 51,184 frozen runs. It takes 4,148,528 descriptor probes around the cycle. The longest shared contexts and the total number of runs by themselves would not give that figure. [Brown & Langmead, *Bounding the Average Move Structure Query for Faster and Smaller RLBWT Permutations*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SEA.2026.9) · [Exact LF and Ψ screen](tile-screen.csv)
