# Proofs

## Marked output convention

Let $L$ be the last column, $F$ its sorted multiset and $T$ stable LF. Thus $F[T(x)]=L[x]$. For primary $p$, the forward inverse-BWT bytes are $F[p],F[T^{-1}p],\ldots$ to length $n$. If the marked cycle has length $d$, reverse the $d$ LF departure bytes starting at $p$, then repeat that period to length $n$. Reversing an already truncated length-$n$ LF walk would change its phase when $d$ does not divide $n$. For example, column 010 at primary 1 spells 010 with period 2. All words in the transfer proofs below are in LF departure order.

## 1. Factoring by a reference permutation

Choose any permutation $Q$ on the rows and put $S=Q^{-1}T$. The moved points of $S$ are exactly the departures where $T$ differs from $Q$. Adjoin the primary to this set to obtain $E$. Then $S(E)=E$, including when the extra point is fixed. For $u\in E$, let $N(u)=Q^{g(u)}u$ be the first *strictly positive* return of $Q$ to $E$. Both $N$ and $S|_E$ are permutations. The first return of $T$ from $e\in E$ is

$$R(e)=N(S(e)),\qquad d(e)=g(S(e)).$$

Indeed the first step goes to $Q(S(e))$. Until the next point of $E$ each following step agrees with $Q$. This proof also specifies the word. Write $u=S(e)$ and emit $F[Q u],\ldots,F[Q^{d(e)}u]$ in LF order. Each symbol is the last-column byte of the actual departure, including the first. Following $R$ just around the primary and reversing the result gives the marked period. A reference cycle with no exception and containing the primary is handled by inserting that primary. Cycles disjoint from the mark can be ignored by the writer.

**Translation.** For $Q(x)=x+s\pmod n$, a run with translation $y-x$ agrees throughout exactly when that difference is $s\pmod n$. A row-weighted histogram over runs gives the strongest rotation, including all ties. Cycles have length $n/\gcd(n,s)$. For an exact rotation the output at $k$ is $F[(p-ks)\bmod n]$. At $s=\pm1$ whole pieces of the sorted byte histogram are emitted with sequential fills, with at most one cyclic wrap. General $s$ needs one first-column query per position of the marked period in the supplied check. For a near-rotation, scalar exceptions still have to be placed in cyclic phase order or directly indexed.

**A family with an unbounded shortcut.** For $L=1^{n-1}0$ and $p=n-2$, $n\ge4$, LF adds one modulo $n$. Three fills give $1^{n-2}01$. The right-end control first joins the final zero to the marked one at equality. The child has width one, its top end at $n-1$ and its bottom end at 1. The other front is the remaining run of $n-2$ ones. Each ensuing cut shortens that run by one and restores the child at the new top frontier with bottom end 1. The last cut is equal and the diagonal closes. There are $1+(n-2)=n-1$ cuts. Recognition costs a histogram and a scan of the capped records, with $\lceil(n-1)/127\rceil+1$ physical records. The output still contains $n$ bytes.

## 2. A permutation of tiles with lane shifts

Pad to $K B$ by fixing the dummy rows. A reference maps $(j,l)$ to $(\pi(j),l+s_j\bmod B)$. The condition for agreement is constant on the intersection of a source tile, one LF run and the inverse image of a target tile. Two arithmetic boundaries advance at each run fragment. A feasible reference can give a tile its strict majority (target, shift), then pair the remaining source and target tiles in increasing order. Two strict majorities cannot select the same target since it has only $B$ rows. The executable also selects the best shift for each leftover pair. That version checks $B$ shifts per leftover tile.

**Cheap oracle bound.** Let $W_{ij}$ count real rows from tile $i$ into tile $j$. A bijection between tiles, even with unconstrained permutations inside them, agrees on at most

$$\min\left(\sum_i\max_j W_{ij},\ \sum_j\max_i W_{ij}\right).$$

Each row or column in the matrix allows only one partner. For rigid lane shifts split each weight by $(y-x)\bmod B$ and take the maxima over partner *and* shift. These are relaxation bounds, so subtracting them from $n$ gives certified minima on errors, possibly unattainable. The bounds deliberately give the partial last tile free use of padding. A full real LF cycle visits every real exception.

**Computing the reference order.** On a cycle of $t$ tiles number them $i=0,\ldots,t-1$. Let $a_i$ be the sum of prior shifts and $s$ the shift of the whole lap. Normalize a point's lane to $z=l-a_i\pmod B$. It stays constant within a lap and advances by $s$ across the seam. The reference cycles correspond to $r=z\bmod h$, $h=\gcd(B,s)$. In lap $k$ for that residue the normalized lane is $r+ks$ and the phase at tile $i$ is $kt+i$, modulo $tB/h$. This gives arbitrary powers by arithmetic. A single tile, $B=3$, shift one and exceptions in lanes 0 and 1 gives return gaps 1 and 2. Treating each normalized lane as a separate cycle would lose the second return.

Scan each tile cycle once, appending its exceptions to lists by normalized lane. Within a list the tile positions are now increasing. Read the lists in lap order, link adjacent entries, and close each nonempty cycle with its positive wrap distance. This supplies $N$ without a comparison sort or any intermediate Cells event. Given the reference, the construction takes $O(K+B c_Q+|E|)$ entries or iterations for $c_Q$ tile cycles, in addition to the initial run-fragment overlay and the exception records. LF's words still have to be spelled. The check uses the formula for each output byte of the marked period. The real scalar-index screen gives that spelling for free. Padding cannot change the answer since actual $T$ preserves the real rows.

## 3. Removing whole paths of parallel tiles

A clean source tile fits inside a single constant-byte run, has a full aligned tile as image and excludes the primary's tile. Its arrow keeps the lane index. These arrows form an injective partial map on tiles. Its components are directed paths and cycles. Take as section the rows in the remaining source tiles. A path head has no incoming clean tile. An edge from the section entering a clean tile must land at a head, since any other clean tile already receives all its lanes from its clean predecessor. Following an entire path keeps the lane, accumulates the same sequence of bytes on all lanes and exits at its dirty terminal. A cycle composed exclusively of clean tiles has no entrance and cannot contain the mark. Removing it is exact even for a periodic or non-transitive column.

Let $A(q)$ count section rows below $q$. A retained source row $x$ whose image is in the section maps to $A(Tx)$ with word $L[x]$. If $T x=hB+l$ lands on a head with terminal $v$ and word $W_h$, it maps to $A(vB+l)$ with word $L[x] W_h$. Relabel the source by $A(x)$ and the primary by $A(p)$. This is the exact first return and its LF spelling. Adjacent arcs can coalesce when their targets are consecutive and their complete words agree. The residual uses that maximal coalescing for the labels just defined.

**Computing the transfers without constructing the tile graph.** On a run only the first and last partial tiles need clipping. Divisibility of its translation by $B$ identifies *all* full aligned arrows between them. Splitting around the mark leaves disjoint integer intervals of tile indices. Their images are available in stable byte order. Subtract the sorted image union from the sorted source groups to obtain all head intervals in one merge.

For the leftmost unreported head $h$ of such an interval, carry its rightward slack. At tile $y$ inside a group ending at $z$, intersect the slack with $z-y$, append the group's byte and add its translation to $y$. At the first exterior tile also clip against the next group's start. By induction every tile in the surviving half-open interval takes the same translations and exits in parallel. The bound at each step is exactly the first possible change of group or terminal status. Advance $h$ by its resulting positive width. There is no closed path reachable from a head. Thus every head is reported once, with all fit lanes and successive parallel tiles accounted for. Discovery visits the groups on each reported itinerary. The evidence counts those visits and their predecessor searches.

The list of transfers and the complementary direct intervals are already ordered by input rank. A two-way merge and one monotone cursor per byte route the retained input bands through them. The cursor order follows from LF stability within the occurrences of a byte. A bottom-order index for a conventional residual kernel still has construction cost. Example 1101000, primary 3, $B=2$, decreases support from 7 to 5 but raises cuts from 2 to 3. Shrinking support does not order the new two fronts or preserve their cut count.

## 4. Equal-byte excursions by arithmetic on two queues

In the first-column bucket $[b,b+m)$ for byte $c$, call rows with $L=c$ internal, and the others exits. Let $k$ be the number of $c$ bytes before row $b$ in $L$. If $j$ is the local position of an internal row and $e(j)$ counts exits strictly before it, then

$$T(b+j)=b+k+j-e(j).$$

The internal images therefore occupy one consecutive interval. The entrances from other buckets occupy the first $k$ and last $e(m)-k$ positions. Split just after the $k$-th exit, at the start when $k=0$. On the left every internal arrow stays left and moves weakly forward. On the right it stays right and moves weakly backward. To see the lack of crossing, the number of internal positions before the split at local $s$ is $s-k$. Their images are exactly $[k,s)$. Reflection gives the reverse statement on the right.

On the left, scan in increasing rank. Initialize a FIFO with the first $k$ entrance indices. At an internal row, move the first index to the back with its visit count incremented. At an exit, remove the first index, reporting its destination and visit count. The queue lists the owners of the next unscanned occupied rows. An internal row allocates the next unused image after them by the formula above. Removing an exit allocates none. Induction proves the queue invariant. Reflection gives the identical computation from the right end. If the live queue is empty, the remaining internal rows in that direction are fixed. More generally an internal-only cycle of an order-preserving partial injection is fixed pointwise, since an increasing permutation of a finite ordered set is the identity.

For a consecutive internal span of $w$ rows and $a>0$ live indices, write $w=qa+r$. Add $q$ to a shared depth, detach the first $r$ live indices, add one to their depths and append them. For an exit span consume that many indices in order. The implementation holds consecutive entrance numbers with a common depth in each deque fragment. Division needs no visit to the represented indices. A prefix move may touch several fragments and the ledger charges them. The entry-to-exit function need not preserve rank order. In 11000 the zero bucket's entrances 1, 2 exit at 1, 0 respectively. A residual constructor must recover which exit belongs to each entrance.

Use all noninternal rows as the section. From one such $x$, $c=L[x]$ and $T x$ is an entrance in bucket $c$. When its token exits after $v$ internal visits, the residual word is $c^{1+v}$. The first symbol is charged to departure $x$. Each reported fragment is a translation on an entrance interval with constant exponent. Stream the original noninternal parts in top order. Their images for each byte advance monotonically, so a cursor can consume the transfer fragment at a known start coordinate, caching its unused suffix. The implementation uses exact-start dictionary reads for newly encountered fragments. These are independently priced.

If the primary is external, its residual word has its mark at LF offset zero. If the primary is internal, interrupt its single containing rotation to identify its current entrance token and preceding internal visit count $v_0$. Its mark is at offset $1+v_0$ in that entrance's external owner's word. A queue-empty marked row yields $n$ copies of its byte immediately. Thus every possible marked component is recovered. The contraction does not assume a unique cycle, a full period or divisibility.

## 5. Residual induction and writer

Let a band describe a translation with equal width in two linear orders. The diagnostic stores its two exclusive ends. At a common frontier $f$, suppose the distinct front bands $A$ and $B$ have opposite ends $b_A$ and $t_B$. Peel $w=\min(w_A,w_B)$ from both, placing a width-$w$ child at top end $t_B$ and bottom end $b_A$. A residual parent loses $w$ from both ends. This is pointwise splicing of $B$ followed by $A$ in LF order. Its *forward* word is the forward word of $A$ followed by that of $B$. Equal widths exhaust both, a shared band closes, and an unmarked closure discards only its own suffix. Splitting around the marked source row ensures width one along its spine, including when the initial transfer word has several bytes.

The writer rotates by one past the marked byte in forward spelling. A repeated unmarked rule can copy a complete first occurrence since every child was born earlier. Only a uniquely occupied marked factor can straddle the physical wrap. Repetition of the marked period supplies any remaining output. The executable retains lengths and mark offsets and charges reads, copies, literal bytes and repetition. Its first-occurrence writer is a diagnostic for output costs. The pinned one-scan writer supplies the production comparison.
