# Astra B · Arithmetic coordinates for dependent LF paths

18 November 2026. Independent investigation on `research/astra-b-collective`.

## Competing hypotheses, before the frozen screen

1. A single modular translation may account for enough LF edges that one can invert just the exceptional rows. Its cycles have explicit coordinates. Census every translation by covered rows and price the strongest choice.
2. A permutation of equal-sized rank tiles, with a cyclic lane shift in each tile, can follow aperiodic paths. A trip around a tile cycle adds a known shift. This gives arithmetic coordinates on *every* reference cycle without traversing its rows. Count even an oracle's unavoidable disagreements before building any matching or shortcut index.
3. Exceptions may come in wide, ordered packets. In particular a whole tile with identical spelling on every lane and a bijective rigid transition can be removed as one link of a path. Test that certificate separately. A small interval encoding of many exceptional rows alone does not price the map that pairs exceptions in cyclic order.

The input is the capped last-column run stream and its primary. The experiments start with Wikidata large-block ordinal 0, then use the prespecified ordinals 24, 48, …, 216. Compare discovery, construction, dependent reads, 64-byte lines and marked emission with the shipped eager and lazy routes. Stop a row-based candidate if its mandatory work fails a structural screen, and expose exactly which representation pays it. Test positive constructions on short periodic columns and every primary, including components whose length does not divide the block length.

## First frozen observations

The pinned corpus was rebuilt from its twelve hashed windows. The production scanner and run front yielded 56,386 capped records, 899,931 rows and primary 264,045. The best single rotation misses 873,518 rows. Even with an oracle choosing each tile's target and any within-tile bijection, widths 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 leave at least 219,982 to 284,516 disagreeing rows. At width 16 the rigid-lane lower bound is 266,576. Stop the per-exception-row index. Its successor walk alone would take one dependent lookup per exception on a full cycle. These are bounds for this exception representation.

There is a reason to check the range variant. At width 2 only 6,492 input intervals name 229,983 clean tiles, but these tiles form 109,627 paths. The statistic does not say how many *different interval transfers* reach the residual decoder. Next give the path discovery and coalescing to this variant for free and measure its actual residual cuts; charge preparation separately if it survives. Giving clean paths their exact exits, words and maximum coalescing for free reverses the apparent gain. Width 2 leaves 439,965 ranks, yet its marked residual takes 93,746 cuts and 35,211 stored rules against the original 92,570 and 28,710. All six widths increase both counts on frozen. ## A separate hypothesis after the tile screen

Inside the first-column bucket of byte c, every internal edge for which L equals c has an order-preserving image. That image is one interval. Can a rotation and deletion of *ranges of entrance indices* evaluate all equal-byte excursions without knowing their individual LF paths? Test the count of these edges and the exact marked residual on frozen before any native structure. This certificate depends on byte order and permits unaligned coordinates and repeated returns to the same bucket.

The equal-byte calculation reduced frozen to 89,708 cuts. It represented 15,756 internal rows with 2,344 partial rotations and one full revolution of seven live tokens. It emitted 4,640 transfer ranges. Consuming those ranges against the original kept bands required 49,187 intersections. By comparison the rotation-column family at 4,096 rows collapsed 4,094 internal visits with integer division and left one residual join. The arithmetic gain exists; its frequency and the cost of the splice decide the Wikidata case.
