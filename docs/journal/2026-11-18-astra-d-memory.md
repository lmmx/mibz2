# Astra D · Memory traffic per Wikidata output byte

**An exact reconstruction can halve modelled DRAM traffic on the frozen block.** A 260,492-byte table reconstructs the block in text order and streams RLE1 and CRC. It needs **3.439 DRAM bytes per final byte**, against **7.328** for shipped Cells. The compulsory input-plus-output floor is **1.046**. Requiring cold write-allocated output raises the line-rounded comparator to **2.046**. The achievable frozen minimum under the stated ordinary-store model therefore lies between 2.046 and 3.439 B/B. The exact optimum remains open.

**The candidate for a machine comparison is the smaller change.** Keeping the Cells induction and one-scan writer, then repairing RLE1 inside the final slot, costs **5.595 B/B** on frozen. It trades 0.335 added guest instruction events for each modelled DRAM byte saved. The compact forward walk trades 15.417. It establishes a lower achievable traffic figure, but its serial searches are too costly at the stated exchange rate. [Exact prices](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/evidence/prices.csv)

## The shipped ledger

**Boundary.** Start with the compressed block, run the real scanner and the 0.5.6 lazy worker through its CRC-checked output slot, then drain dirty lines. Frozen has 41,618 compressed bytes in its envelope, 89,730 Huffman/MTF symbols, 56,386 capped run records, 899,931 BWT rows and 900,000 final bytes. The systematic sample starts with this frozen block and takes every 24th large block, for ten in total. A separately selected T-vector block serves as a control. The consumer's subsequent copy and the wider scope of the hardware counters are described in the replay notes. [Input identities](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/inputs.csv) · [Worker entry and output boundary](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/README.md)

**Model.** Replay the ordered native data references with 64-byte lines, 32 KiB eight-way L1D, 256 KiB four-way L2 and a 16-way effective LLC. The central LLC case is 2 MiB. Tags are inclusive, replacement is true LRU, stores write-allocate, and writebacks carry a full dirty line. Vector capacities are recycled and caches begin cold. The scanner hands off through the LLC. The model uses actual addresses and buffer aliases, and includes loads from libc copies, stack traffic and initialization stores. Other workers' interleaving and prefetch timing are outside this single-block replay. ## Two exact reconstructions

**Final-slot repair.** Frozen has 34 RLE1 edits, 22 contractions and 12 expansions. Its output lead reaches +79 bytes. A forward in-place copy could overwrite unread input. The writer uses the already-reserved worker output slot when its letter geometry fits. A scan then saves the edit positions and bytes in the dead run records. Moving left-shifting literal spans forward, right-shifting spans backward and finally filling the recorded runs works even when the displacement changes sign. Frozen needs 272 bytes of directory. The implementation has a capacity fallback before the first destructive move. This preserves the production grammar and command order. The conservative geometry guard covers eight of ten sample blocks and 169 of 233 large blocks. Output capacity and edit-directory space are checked separately at run time. [Proof and slot bound](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/proof.md) · [All displacements and actual directory sizes](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/edits.csv) · [Executable repair](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/direct.rs)

**Forward affine tiles.** Each maximal run maps by stable LF to an interval in F. On that interval Ψ adds a constant. Two scans of the capped run records build the inverse translations directly. Split at 128-row F boundaries, store one four-byte header per tile and four bytes per piece, then follow Ψ from the primary. The F byte at the current row supplies text in order. RLE1 needs only the preceding byte and streak, and CRC consumes the newly produced output every 4,096 inverse rows. There is no whole-block intermediate on this route. The construction also works on a short primary cycle. [Construction and exactness](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/proof.md) · [Executable traversal](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/tiles.rs)

Frozen needs 57,836 pieces, 7,031 headers and 256 symbol ends. Its 260,492 navigation bytes fit in a 262,144-byte L2 by 1,652 bytes before output and other accesses. Across the sample the navigation set ranges from 251,932 to 578,704 bytes. Three of ten fit in L2 by size. On frozen, 4,148,528 descriptor inspections give 805,753 L1 misses and 27,628 L2 misses on the descriptors during the walk. All those L2 misses hit the model LLC. Including output and CRC, the walk phase itself is about **2.01 DRAM B/B**. Construction, the scanner and the MTF front bring the total to 3.439. A 256-row table occupies 233,108 bytes but raises the frozen probe count to 7,476,656. The size and probe census preceded the runnable prototype. ## Address dependencies and latency hiding

**Shipped lazy Cells.** Frozen makes 92,570 cuts. The top side reads a stream byte, uses it to index its cursor, then uses the cursor to select an entry. Of 51,184 top stream entries, 28,455 are tagged and name a slot. A further 16,426 top refills come through links. On the bottom, 22,729 entries are tagged and 24,961 refills come through links. In all 47,690 bottom-slot refills the top payload address depends on the bottom slot. Fields of one top slot can share a line. The longest stretches of successive non-stream refills are 1,850 top and 3,467 bottom. There are 34,971 free-list pops, a 32-rule marked descent, and 9,628 command→position→source chains. These count load-address relationships in the algorithm. None specifies whether a load missed or how long a core waited. **Forward tiles and T.** Every tile step needs a row-indexed header, the header-selected descriptor interval, and the selected descriptor before the next row is known. The local scan has predictable successive addresses. Even with perfectly resident tables, there are 899,931 serial row transitions on frozen. Shipped T uses up to 32 independent lanes. Ordinals 41 and 270 each have 511 distinct segment starts and 900,000 row links. Their longest chains are 16,443 and 13,452 links. The selected control needs 771,584 bytes and 12,335,693 descriptor probes on the forward route. This explains why a large T with some latency hidden requires independent examination alongside a small dependent table. [Exact segment lengths](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/evidence/segments.csv) · [Pinned worker selection](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/1edb29fdc3d42dfddfc3d60e46456b611323760c/src/parallel.rs)

**An overlap-only opportunity.** The copy pass can resolve the next eight immutable command descriptors and their source positions before executing the current copies in birth order. Source bytes themselves can depend on an earlier copy. Issuing a prefetch consumes no future byte value. An eight-entry staging array of three 32-bit fields occupies 96 bytes. A memory-backed implementation stores and reloads 12 bytes per command and issues up to two first-line hints per command. Frozen offers 1,204 groups and 9,628 position lookups. Only 375 of those position loads miss L2 and none misses the 2 MiB LLC. The larger opportunity is starting the payload fills earlier. If a scalar implementation adds six staging loads/stores and two hints per command, its illustrative budget is 77,024 extra instruction events, or four exposed cycles to recover per command at the two-events-per-cycle price. The commanded copy lengths and bytes stay identical. This is a latency hypothesis, with no credit for fewer transfers or for eight achieved misses in flight. ## Prices, memory and stop decision

**Marginal price model.** At an assumed 4.8 GHz and two guest instruction events per core-cycle, credit one saved DRAM byte with `W × 4.8 / BW` aggregate worker cycles. Using the historical 24.8 GB/s at seven workers gives 1.355 cycles, or **2.710 instruction events per byte saved**. At ten workers and 28.6 GB/s the price is **3.357 events/B**. This is a sensitivity calculation. Seven workers had memory-bandwidth headroom, guest events include REP iterations, and load occupancy differs from exposed delay. The model assigns no automatic latency dividend to a saved line.

On the eight eligible blocks, final-slot repair saves **12,075,968 bytes** for **9,196,920 extra events**, or **0.762 events/B**. The seven-worker margin is exhausted if each of the 204,964 net additional LLC hits costs another **57.4 exposed cycles** above the work and bandwidth prices. Frozen alone moves from 22.37 to 22.95 guest events per output byte. This arm clears the structural screen for CI and a paired i9 test with IMC reads/writes, fill-buffer or offcore occupancy, wall time and peak RSS. It has no measured throughput result yet. [Break-even arithmetic](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/evidence/prices.csv)

**Stop the general forward-walk performance path here.** On the ten-block sample, forward tiles require **8.860 additional events per DRAM byte saved**. On the nine Cells-admitted blocks they need **13.292**. Frozen goes from 22.37 to 82.32 events per output byte. On the two T fallbacks the corresponding exchange rates are 3.285 and 3.668. Ordinal 41 narrowly clears the ten-worker bandwidth-only price before serial stalls, while the other fallback fails it. This evidence supports the compact table as a constructive bound. It gives insufficient grounds for a general kernel replacement or for tuning a dense-block variant. [Per-phase guest instruction counts](2026-11-18-astra-d-memory/evidence/instructions.csv)

**Peak memory.** The final-slot arm keeps the same output reservation as AVX2 finish and uses consumed records for edits. In deterministic 7/28, 8/32 and 10/40 worker/slot replays over all 431 blocks, total final-slot capacities are identical for the three arms. At 7/28, peak requested heap is **217,712,280 / 213,093,176 / 101,243,602 bytes** for shipped, final slot and tiles. At 10/40 it is **290,896,400 / 284,313,749 / 140,155,032**. These are allocation censuses for round-robin assignments. The user's i9 result that eight workers gained 2.6–3.7% but cost 13–14% more peak memory makes an actual RSS comparison material. ## Prior experiments, hardware and verification

**i9 measurements.** The 0.5.4 knee record gives 7.00 IMC read and 3.98 write B/B at seven unpinned workers, 10.97 B/B by its unrounded total, 24.8 GB/s and about 75% of the measured ceiling. The separate unpinned 10/40 run gives 14.11 B/B at 28.6 GB/s, 85.7% of its ceiling, and about 18% higher L3-miss latency than 7/28. Its registered queueing verdict was inconclusive. Earlier counters put 53% of sampled load latency at fill-buffer hits and estimated about 2.2 misses in flight while any was outstanding. A prefetched line can still be outstanding when demanded. The 0.5.6 release reduced CPU at the relevant counts, with 2–3% wall gains at 8, 16 and 20 threads and little movement at 10. The measured IMC numbers are whole-machine records from other runs. The traffic tables in this study describe the single-block replay of 0.5.6 and its two experimental variants.
