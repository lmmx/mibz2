# 2026-11-10: Tunnels: the census, the table, the price, and why a fixed stride beats them

**Status: a census and a price on paper, for the Optimiser's gate (SPEC.PATHFINDER 3a).
Nothing is built and nothing asks to be.**

- **The question** (Louis, through the Coordinator): can BWT tunnelling (Baier, CPM 2018) give
  the small table share-nothing needs, and does it beat the k-step table (D4-delta: 1.09 MB a
  block, about 18 instructions a row on paper)?
- **Counted:** on Wikidata 94.5% of rows sit in tunnels, and their summed area is 20 times
  the block. But a wide tunnel is short: 65% of a tunnel walk's steps emit two bytes or fewer.
- **Priced:** a table of whole tunnels is 0.7 to 1.3 MB a block and inverts for about 19 to 20
  instructions a row. It ties D4-delta. It does not beat it.
- **Why:** the k-step cells *are* the tunnels of height at least k, cut to height k. Cutting
  is what removes the short steps.
- **What tunnelling adds:** the tall ones. At least 48% of Wikidata's bytes sit in tunnels 33
  or more rows high, reached in 0.0086 steps a row. A k-step walk that copies those from their
  words would make about 0.074 table accesses a row at k = 8 against 0.125.
- **On text there is nothing to tunnel:** three bytes a step whatever the cap.

## 0. Correction (2026-11-10): the census had no certificate, and its heights were one too many

**The Optimiser's witness** (#145, 6758640506): the text "aab". L = baa, F = aab, Ψ = [1, 2, 0].
Cell [0, 2) has K[1] = 1. The census gave it height 1 + min K = 2; its rows emit "aa" and "ab".
The safe height is **min K**: rows q − 1 and q share a cell at steps 0 to K[q] − 1, and at step
K[q] row q's image is a cell start. The same error sat in the 9 November first-split count.

**Which failure:** the census's construction, not the theorem. §1's claim about K was right
and the code did not implement it; no assertion stood between them.

**Fixed, in `examples/phrase_census.rs`:**
- heights are min K;
- every word step is now asserted against the trajectory: its bytes are the bytes its cell's
  first row emits, and its exit is that row's exit plus the offset. With the old height the
  assertion fails on the first text block. With min K it holds on all 39 Wikidata and 25 text
  blocks counted;
- a second count, the one §5 and §6 proposed as the falsifier: the stride-k walk that copies
  tall cells, each tall step asserted against its Ψ^k cell's head.

**Recounted, whole-cell walk, Wikidata** (replaces §2's table; text in brackets):

| 32 | 0.156 [0.461] | 6.4 [2.2] | 0.285 |

At cap 64, 0.093 of the 0.132 steps emit two bytes or fewer, for 9.9% of the bytes. One-row

1.25 MB at caps 16, 32, 64; walk 10.7, 8.0, 6.7 instructions a row; build 5.1, 8.6, 14.2. It
ties D4-delta and does not beat it, and the count that disproves the advantage is now 0.093 of

**The tall tunnels, certified at the stride-k walk's own landings** (cap 64; cap 256 in
brackets):

| | k = 4 | k = 8 |
|---|--:|--:|
| table steps a row (plain stride: 0.250, 0.125) | 0.107 [0.078] | 0.057 [0.041] |
| bytes delivered by tall steps | 61.7% [71.4%] | 63.4% [72.1%] |
| tall steps a row | 0.0108 | 0.0112 |
| tall cells a row | 0.0129 | 0.0186 |
| their words, bytes a row | 0.763 [1.960] | 1.098 [2.746] |
| **words stored ÷ bytes delivered** | **1.24** | **1.73** |

- **The saving in table steps is real and larger than §5 guessed:** 0.057 a row at k = 8, not
  0.074.
- **And it is not a saving.** A tall cell is landed on 0.84 times at k = 4 and 0.60 at k = 8
  (tall steps over tall cells). Which cells are landed on is known only after the walk, so
  every tall cell's word must exist first: 1.24 to 1.73 bytes made a byte delivered. Making a
  word is walking it. Left to the first visit instead, there is under one visit a cell to
  reuse it.
- **§6's own closing rule is met:** the words pass 0.3 bytes a row (0.76 and 1.10), and they
  add 0.69 to 0.99 MB to a table whose target is 1.2.
- **Disproved, as an advantage: tall tunnels inside a k-step walk.** The witness is the
  ratio above. What would reopen it: a way to produce a tall cell's word for less than walking
  it. None is known.
- **Text:** tall steps deliver 12% of the bytes. Nothing there.

**So both forms of tunnelling are closed, each on a count, and the k-step table stands
alone.** §5's and §6's "not disproved" lines below are superseded by this section.

## 1. What a tunnel is here (claim, proved)

A *block* is w consecutive rows whose trajectories stay adjacent for h steps. Its rows emit
the same h bytes, and Ψ^h maps them by one translation. Baier's tunnelling stores one of the w
paths and collapses the rest.

- **K[q]** is the number of steps rows q − 1 and q stay adjacent. A block is an interval with
  K ≥ h − 1 strictly inside it.
- **The k-step cells of `2026-11-10-backward-table.md` §4a are blocks.** Rows q − 1 and q are in
  one Ψ^k cell exactly when K[q] ≥ k. So the Ψ^k cells are the maximal blocks of height k, and
  r_k = 1 + #{q : K[q] < k}. D is tunnelling at one fixed height.
- **A whole-cell tunnel** is the other extreme: a maximal run of L (a cell of Ψ), kept at its
  full width w, as high as it stays whole: h = 1 + min K over its interior.
- **A decoder needs no stack.** Baier's inversion pushes the row's offset on entering a tunnel
  because the collapsed rows are gone. A table that keeps each cell's start and target carries
  the offset by arithmetic: row' = target + (row − start).

- **Short steps dominate.** At cap 64 on Wikidata, 0.096 of the 0.148 steps a row emit two
  bytes or fewer, and carry 15% of the bytes. 0.043 of them are one-row cells.
- **Tall tunnels carry half the bytes.** In the 233-block census of
  `2026-11-09-phrase-structure.md`, words of 33 bytes or more are 48.1% of the bytes and
  0.0086 steps a row.
- **Few cells do the work.** At cap 64 the walk starts words in 0.0073 n distinct cells. Which
  ones is known only after the walk, so a table holds all 0.0210 n.
- **Text:** three bytes a step at any cap. A fixed stride of four already does better.

## 4. The whole loop (derived from #323's as-built counts; uncounted)

| part of a step | instructions | from |
|---|--:|---|
| the row's cell: directory word, mask, popcount, add | 12 to 14 | E's as-built step was 22 to 26 with a compare and a second popcount |
| the entry: load, start and target, the next row | 6 | |
| the word: one unaligned 16-, 32- or 64-byte copy past its end, advance by its length | 4 to 6 | the short-run stores of #170 |
| a marker test | 4 | below |
| the skeleton: a pointer a lane, an end test, the loop | about 22 | E's flagged skeleton, #323 |
| **a step** | **48 to 52, taken as 51** | |

- **Why the dear skeleton:** the room walk's 6 instructions of bookkeeping depend on every lane
  writing the same number of bytes a pass. Words differ in length, so each lane keeps its own
  pointer. A fixed stride keeps the room.
- **Markers and exits:** a lane must stop at the next marker row m, which may lie inside its
  word. The k rows before m on its trajectory (k up to the cap) are flagged in a bitmap; a
  lane landing on one takes only the bytes up to m. That is 512 × (cap − 1) rows a block found
  by backward steps: 0.26 instructions a row at cap 16, 1.1 at cap 64.
- **A row, the walk:** steps a row × 51: **10.6** at cap 16, **8.6** at cap 32, **7.5** at cap 64.
- **Construction:** each multi-row cell follows its image until it splits. That is one cell-step
  a word byte: 0.18 n, 0.30 n and 0.49 n steps at caps 16, 32 and 64, about 30 instructions
  each: **5.4, 9.0 and 14.6** a row, on top of the runs' emitter. A word is its cell's byte and
  a prefix of the word below it, so a memoised build may cost a few steps a cell; unpriced.
- **Branches:** one-row cells are 29% of steps, in no pattern. One entry format and one copy
  keep the step free of data-dependent branches. The marker test is taken 512 times a block.
- **Memory:** two dependent accesses a step (directory, entry) and a third to the words, which
  the entry's load addresses. The directory is 225 KB.
- **Coordination:** none. A worker's table is its own.

**Inversion, build and walk, a row:** 16 to 22 at caps 16 to 64 if the build is the plain one.
Production's is 15.3.

## 5. Against D4-delta (derived)

| | table a block | steps a row | walk, instructions a row | inversion |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| D4-delta, as the Coordinator has it | 1.09 MB | 0.250 | (in its probe) | about 18 |
| whole tunnels, cap 32 | 1.09 MB (0.84 with two entry kinds) | 0.169 | 8.6 | about 19 |
| whole tunnels, cap 64 | 1.26 MB (1.01) | 0.148 | 7.5 | about 22 |

- **It ties. It does not win.** A third fewer steps, each dearer by the skeleton, and a build
  that grows with the cap.
- **The construction that would win is the fixed stride with tall tunnels copied.** In the
  room skeleton a lane inside a tall tunnel copies k bytes a pass from its word with no table
  access; elsewhere it takes a k-step cell. With the census's lower bound (48% of bytes at
  0.0086 steps a row), k = 8 makes 0.0086 + 0.52 ÷ 8 = **0.074 table accesses a row** against
  D8's 0.125 and D4's 0.25. Each copied pass is a load and a store.
- **What that hybrid costs is not counted:** the tall cells' words (their total length), a
  lane's in-tunnel state, and how a tall tunnel is recognised without the rank step it is meant
  to save.

## 6. Which was it, and what is open

- **Disproved, as an advantage: whole-cell tunnelling as the small table.** The count that
  does it is 0.096 short steps a row of 0.148: width and height trade against each other inside
  a run, and a table of whole runs spends its steps on the wide, short ones. No construction of
  whole-cell tunnels removes that; cutting them is D.
- **Not disproved: tall tunnels inside a fixed-stride walk.** Advantage, as a number that can
  be false: on Wikidata, at k = 8, table accesses fall from 0.125 to at most 0.08 a row with
  under 0.2 bytes a row of extra words.
- **Cheapest falsifier:** a census, no kernel: in the stride-k walk from the origin, the share
  of steps that land in a Ψ^k cell of height 32 or more, and those cells' summed word length.
  One more counter in `phrase_census`. If the words exceed 0.3 bytes a row or the share is
  under a third, it closes.
- **Asked of the Optimiser (3a):** the price of a k-step lane that can be in two states, and
  whether the room skeleton survives it.
- **Limits:** the Ψ direction only (the LF direction's cells are the same blocks, cut from the
  other end; not recounted). Whole-cell words only: Baier's planner picks sub-blocks, which is
  what §5's hybrid does by hand. Instruction figures are E's as-built ones re-used, not counts
  of this loop.
