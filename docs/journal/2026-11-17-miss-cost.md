**The rule: a diagnostic, reported, not judged, adopts nothing.** No verdict is computed, no figure gates anything, and production's plan is not touched whatever the run reads. The recipe has no judged run.

## Why

- **At 10 threads on the i9,** cutting from 10 lazy workers to 7 cut cycles a byte by 25% and LLC misses a byte by 48% (#515, LOG 6943194948).
- **A smaller footprint** (W12, 12-byte rules) cut LLC misses by 7% at 7 workers, with no wall gain (LOG 6944610173).

So the misses that cost are not simply the misses that are counted. Two open questions:
1. Do the contention costs come from misses in flight together (L3 and interconnect queueing), or from DRAM bandwidth?
2. Do the costly misses sit on the walk's dependent chain?

This run counts what separates those readings. It does not choose between them by rule.

## The arms

All at 10 threads asked, one CPU on each of the 10 cores, as #515's at-cores runs are pinned. Each occupancy arm is `ab`'s `mibz2-occ-N-S`: `BzReader::parallel_workers(N, S, lazy = true)`, with 4 blocks in flight a worker.

| arm | workers | blocks in flight | role |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| `mibz2-occ-10-40` | 10 | 40 | **the reference**: the per-byte ratios' denominator |
| `mibz2-occ-9-36` | 9 | 36 | reported |
| `mibz2-occ-8-32` | 8 | 32 | reported |
| `mibz2-occ-7-28` | 7 | 28 | reported |
| `mibz2` | 10 | 40 | sanity: production's own plan at 10 threads, the same shape as the reference. Its ratios should sit near 1 |
| `mibz2-occ-7-28+thp` | 7 | 28 | side arm: 7/28 with transparent huge pages (below) |

**The huge-page side arm.** The decoder has no huge-page switch, and adding `madvise` would need `libc` and production code. So the arm sets glibc's tunable `GLIBC_TUNABLES=glibc.malloc.hugetlb=1`, and no code changes:
- *What the tunable does:* glibc 2.35 and later `madvise` their heaps and large mappings `MADV_HUGEPAGE`, which covers every allocation the decoder makes through the system allocator.
- *When the recipe drops the arm,* and logs why in `events.txt`: if the kernel's THP mode is `never`, or glibc is older than 2.35.
- *Whether it took effect is read, not assumed:* every run records the rise of `/proc/vmstat`'s `thp_fault_alloc` over it, and the report prints its median for each arm. The counter is system-wide, so this reading is context only.

## What is counted

`perf stat` as this user, through `bench/counters.py --groups`:
- **The runs:** REPS = 10 reps, with the arms alternating in order from rep to rep. Each run is one decode of 4 copies of the Wikidata sample (`wikidata-x4`).
- **Each group is cycles plus at most three events,** so nothing is multiplexed on the i9's four general counters a thread. `instructions` rides in the first group on a fixed counter.
- **Missing events:** every event is checked in `perf list` on the workstation. One it does not name is dropped and logged in `events.txt`. A group left with no events is dropped.
- **The candidate events were checked** against the i9's `perf list`, from #515's bundle: all are named there.

| group | events | what it gives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | cycles, instructions, `cycle_activity.stalls_total`, `cycle_activity.stalls_l3_miss` | IPC; the total and L3-miss stall shares |
| 2 | cycles, `cycle_activity.stalls_l2_miss`, `mem_load_retired.l2_miss`, `mem_load_retired.l3_miss` | the L2-miss stall share; retired loads' misses |
| 3 | cycles, `mem_load_retired.l3_hit`, `offcore_requests.demand_data_rd`, `offcore_requests_outstanding.demand_data_rd` | the L3 hit share of L2-miss loads; **demand-read latency** |
| 4 | cycles, `offcore_requests.all_data_rd`, `offcore_requests_outstanding.all_data_rd`, `offcore_requests_outstanding.cycles_with_data_rd` | **reads in flight while any is**; offcore read **bandwidth** |
| 5 | cycles, `offcore_requests.l3_miss_demand_data_rd`, `offcore_requests_outstanding.l3_miss_demand_data_rd`, `offcore_requests_outstanding.cycles_with_l3_miss_demand_data_rd` | **L3-miss latency and misses in flight**; L3-miss read bandwidth |
| 6 | cycles, `dtlb_load_misses.walk_active`, `dtlb_load_misses.walk_pending`, `dtlb_load_misses.walk_completed` | the page walks' share of cycles, and cycles a walk (MONODROMY's request) |
| system | `uncore_imc/data_reads/`, `uncore_imc/data_writes/` (or `unc_m_cas_count.rd`/`.wr` where those are what perf names) | DRAM bytes, read and written |

**The memory controller (the system group).** Its events are counted only system-wide (`perf stat -a`), because perf will not count the uncore for one process.
- *When it is counted:* only if `kernel.perf_event_paranoid` is 0 or less. The workstation's was 1 in #515's bundle, so it is expected to be **skipped and logged**. The offcore groups (4 and 5) are then the bandwidth reading.
- *Its scope:* it counts the whole machine for the run's duration, so other processes' traffic is included. Perf reports it in MiB, and the report reads that in bytes.

**The brief's latency pair, corrected.** The brief named `offcore_requests_outstanding.data_rd` over `offcore_requests.demand_data_rd`.
- `offcore_requests_outstanding.data_rd` is not in the i9's `perf list`.
- Little's law needs the occupancy and the arrivals of the same requests.
- So the run uses two matched pairs: demand data reads (`.demand_data_rd` over `.demand_data_rd`), and demand data reads that missed L3 (`.l3_miss_demand_data_rd` over `.l3_miss_demand_data_rd`).
- On this core, the outstanding counts are kept per core. The pinning leaves each decoder thread's hyperthread sibling idle, so they are the thread's.

## Load latency with IPs (`bench/memlat.py`; MONODROMY's request)

For `mibz2-occ-10-40` and `mibz2-occ-7-28`, MEMREPS = 3 reps each, alternating, pinned as above:
- **The sampler:** `perf record -e cpu/mem-loads,ldlat=30/P -W -d -c 10007`, the event `perf mem record` uses on Intel cores. It takes precise samples of retired loads slower than 30 core cycles, one in every 10,007, each with its latency, data source and address.
- **Sample count:** about 60,000 samples a run if roughly the L2 misses qualify (0.7 a byte × 840 MB).
- **Paranoid:** per-process sampling, which `kernel.perf_event_paranoid` 1 allows.
- **What the report gives, for each arm with the reps pooled:**
  - by data source (L1, LFB/MAB, L2, L3, RAM, other): the share of samples, the share of summed latency, and the median and mean latency in cycles;
  - the top 10 IPs by summed latency, each with its symbol, samples, median latency and dominant source.

  The IPs answer question 2: whether the costly loads are the walk's dependent loads.
- **The raw outputs are kept in the bundle:** `perf script`'s text (gzipped) and the first rep's `perf mem report --stdio --sort=mem`, so a parse can be checked by hand.
- **The parser:** it reads `perf script -F data_src,weight,ip,sym` as perf 6.8's `builtin-script.c` prints it (the workstation's perf is 6.8.12), and counts and prints any line it cannot read.
- **VOID** on a failed run or `perf script`, a missing or duplicated rep, no sample parsed, or the arms' outputs differing.
- **Sampling overhead:** the sampled runs are separate from the counted ones, so the overhead touches no counter.

## What the report gives

`bench/counters.py report --ref mibz2-occ-10-40` gives two things:
- **The per-byte table:** for each arm, the median over the reps of each event a byte of output. Then each arm over 10/40, as the median of the reps' paired ratios.
- **The miss cost's derived figures,** for each arm, each one printed only where its events were counted:
  - **stall share:** `stalls_l3_miss / cycles`, with the L2-miss and total shares beside it;
  - **miss latency:** demand-read latency and L3-miss demand-read latency, in core cycles, by Little's law;
  - **misses in flight:** L3-miss demand reads in flight while any is, data reads in flight while any is, and the share of cycles with a data read out;
  - **bandwidth in GB/s against the child's wall:**
    - offcore data reads × 64 B: L2 misses to the uncore, prefetches and L3 hits included;
    - L3-miss demand reads × 64 B;
    - retired L3-miss loads × 64 B, a lower bound on demand traffic to DRAM;
    - the IMC's bytes, where counted.

    The counts cover the whole process and the wall covers the decode alone, so each GB/s is an upper bound on the decode's.
  - **context:** the L3 hit share of L2-miss loads, the page walks' share of cycles and cycles a walk, and THP faults a run.

**VOID** (exit 1, nothing reported):
- a run missing, duplicated or failed;
- an event not counted, unsupported or multiplexed;
- a group counted otherwise than named (system-wide or for the process);
- a run without a wall where a bandwidth or a busy share needs one;
- the arms' outputs differing.

A VOID diagnostic is reported as VOID. Nothing is re-run on the spot.
