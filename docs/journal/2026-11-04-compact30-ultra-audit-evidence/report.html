A. Executive decision

CONDITIONAL ACCEPT of the research direction; no established compiler-achieved saving or economic advance.

The three transformations have a defensible mathematical basis. Their credited existing instruction sites and principal execution frequencies survive independent reconstruction. Under the original, jointly satisfied compilation caps, the conservative total remains 225,253,100 architectural instructions per corpus construction: 66,041,254 for epoch finalization, 87,622,080 for scanner cleanup, and 71,589,766 for hot pointer arrays. This is a falsifiable target, not a demonstrated lower bound on an implementation's savings.

The audit does not establish roughly 10–15% completion headroom. It also does not establish that most savings are overlapped. The archive identifies where work occurs and which workers finish slowly, but cannot identify the proposed changes' marginal execution time. The Guardian should distinguish a credible bounded correctness-and-compilation investigation from an economic GO.

The strongest first target remains a repaired epoch-tagged fast finalizer. It can remove a dependent snapshot lookup and generic frame work on a path enriched on slow large blocks. Scanner cleanup has the larger instruction target and the simpler invariant; descriptor splitting has broader integration costs. The recommendation is for the Guardian's decision only. No implementation, constructor compilation, benchmark, i9 access, repository change or experiment change was performed.

Material findings are: the slow finalizer must adopt the new ordinary/RAW discriminator; the proposed memory-loaded opening tags add 1,754,362 explicit reads even when instruction-neutral; the 1M and 5M overhead allowances are acceptance caps rather than established costs; split pointers can increase memory-write operations; nine fast finishes are necessarily partial; and blockwise accounting tightens the baseline and refill opportunity. None of these supports inventing either a performance gain or a wholesale rejection.

Mandatory design repair: the old d.raw == NO_RAW predicate at line 421 cannot remain in the slow fallback. For n=1 with one ordinary character, tag 0x80000000 fails the full-30 guard, then the old fallback interprets it as RAW index 2³¹. The existing safe RAW indexing would panic; making that indexing unchecked would make the defect unsafe. A sign/high-bit test can replace the sentinel comparison without inherently adding an instruction. The design is repairable, but merely grafting the fast guard onto the old function is incorrect.

Opening-epoch proof and boundary cases

For source row i, φ(i)=32⌊i/30⌋+(i mod 30). One epoch has 61,440 rows, with temporary labels increasing from 0 through 65,533. Source order gives nondecreasing epochs for every character's successive ranks. Scalar refill opens immediately before writing. A run opens only when m>0 and a positive fill follows; full bulk emission does not open a descriptor. Epoch changes occur only in the outer scan, outside these operations. Thus the opening epoch is the first appended row's epoch.

If opening_epoch = active_epoch at finish, all received rows have epochs between equal endpoints and therefore belong to that epoch. Their full-label span is ≤65,533, so last-label loading, span reconstruction and ordinary/wide classification are mathematically unnecessary on this guarded path. The final header is (tag & 0x7fffffff) | (first_u16 << 8) | character. Modular subtraction yields the exact fields.

Update active_epoch alongside snapshot creation, before processing an epoch's rows. Do not derive it from already advanced W.y: at exact EOF n=61,440, φ(n)>>16 is 1 although the last active epoch is 0. A group completed earlier but finished in a later epoch correctly takes the slow path. Repeated equal snapshots require max{e:Aₑ[c]≤r}; for snapshots [0,1,1,1], rank 1 belongs to epoch 3. The slow path must not assume its last received rank belongs to the currently active epoch.

The exact cross-epoch boundary remains: φ(61,440)−φ(3)=65,533 ordinary, but subtracting φ(2) gives 65,534 wide. A span 0→122,880 has full-label difference 131,072 but low-u16 difference zero; low bits alone are insufficient on the slow path. Empty buckets never open; shared groups are preset RAW; forced groups retain their actual indices; partial ordinary groups use bounded partial normalization.

Release-mode safety argument

The release build checks n≤MAX_N and the counts sum. Prefix limits lie in 0…n. Scalar code checks room and rejects exhaustion before writing; run checks rank+m≤lim. open establishes r<lim≤n, group floor(r/30)<g_count and portion length k=min(lim,30(g+1))−r in 1…30−slot. write progresses only within this portion. A nonempty completed ordinary portion with 60-byte progress must start at slot zero, have exactly 30 initialized u16 fields, and be wholly within one allocated Rec. Equality to the active tag does not by itself prove initialization; it is combined with these state invariants.

Rec storage reserves g_count+1 aligned records before scatter and never reallocates during it. Pointer provenance comes from that allocation; null and one-past pointers are never dereferenced. Five existing chunks cover exactly bytes 0…59, not the header or padding. A proposed leaf must use raw/appropriately scoped accesses rather than forming a reference to an incompletely initialized Rec. Shared crossing fields and header are already initialized by premarking. On successful publication all logical rows, padding, headers, RAW padding and PARK are initialized. On failed/reused builds no old pointer or tag becomes readable state. These are control-flow and allocation arguments; debug assertions and successful tests are not their basis.

Instruction accounting and falsifiable target

The old full-fast body is exactly 81: finish 0x61d30–0x61d93 (29), 0x61e08–0x61e17 (5), 0x61f1b–0x61fb8 (39), 0x6231b–0x62329 (8). Adding its entry call makes 82. It performs 17 explicit reads, eight explicit writes, six pushes and six pops. A ≤40 replacement body with unchanged entry call saves at least 41 per eligible event. Ultra's “complete boundary ≤40” wording is stricter; its arithmetic conservatively uses the body convention. All introduced caller instructions must be charged.

The 35-instruction illustrative shape is architecturally plausible; it is not proof rustc will emit it. Necessary compilation conditions are separation of slow register pressure, no generic callee-save frame or spills on fast success, no retained snapshot chain or last-label classification, and correct bounds/provenance with no extra recurrent row work. Slow tail-transfer/calling convention and actual argument preparation must be inspected in the resulting caller and callee.

Ordinary open has two actual sentinel assignments: refill 0x623df followed by store 0x62400, and run 0x34320 followed through 0x33c7c. A register-immediate assignment can become a register-memory load one-for-one. That adds 1,754,362 reads (all ordinary openings, including eventual wide groups) even when it adds zero instructions. Empty initial descriptors need not be retagged. Compiler-created extra opening instructions cost 1,754,362 each; added caller work is similarly chargeable. Ultra's net memory estimates remain conditional, not measured traffic.

The release-mode read proof is direct. The outer guard gives i+16≤end≤n≤983,010. Lookahead gives e+16≤end before either load. Therefore [i,i+8) and [i+8,i+16), or their e equivalents, lie in the initialized immutable input allocation with no integer overflow. A held pair remains associated with exactly the next tile index i=e. The final tail has end−i<16 and uses scalar reads, never a widened full-tile overread. Use unaligned reads: allocation/byte indices do not promise u64 alignment. Input storage remains alive and cannot alias mutable output or scratch through the safe build inputs.

For tile splitting, p starts at zero. advance receives k≤30: full tile 16, tail step 1, or a fill no larger than one portion. Since p<30, p+k≤59; at most one subtraction of 30 restores p<30. complete consumes multiples of 30 and preserves phase. With 0<len≤16, n1=min(len,30−p) satisfies 1≤n1≤len, proving both slice bounds and excluding p=30. The source/data loops retain their termination tests. Destination bucket exhaustion checks, run rank+m validation, RAW indexing, allocation checks and final rank validation earn no scanner credit.

An equivalent Rust expression using bounded pointers or slices is plausible. rustc may retain checks, reconstruct an address LEA, introduce jumps, or cause spills. If only the +8 address folding fails, the scanner target under the same 1M cap falls to 74,684,290. This is a conditional fallback budget, not a refutation of the mathematical redundancy. The scanner model verifies 87,381 symbolic tile sequences and all 930 phase/advance combinations; the written induction covers lengths beyond the finite model.

F. Hot descriptor pointer arrays

All four scalar loop copies—not only the two main full-tile copies—have 12 common instructions and two descriptor-address LEAs: 0x33a05/0x33a09, 0x34355/0x34359, 0x343e4/0x343e8, 0x344d5/0x344d9. Their gross attribution is 2 × 38,294,883 = 76,589,766. The baseline scalar loop has four explicit reads and two writes per row, including the pointer read-modify-write.

Colocated write[256] and end[256] can use one base with scale-eight addressing and a constant 2,048-byte displacement between arrays. Two live array-base registers are not inherently necessary. A 24-byte cold descriptor holds w0 and r0/lim/g/tag. The proposed 4,096+6,144=10,240 bytes equal 256×40, but actual Rust layout must be checked. This demonstrates an expressible layout opportunity; it does not establish register allocation or code generation.

All paths must use one coherent state: initialization, rank snapshots, open, close, scalar append/refill, fragment fill, run entry, bulk rank update, finish, final validation and sweep. Duplicating old write/end as a cache would create synchronization work not present in the proposal. The u8 character proves indices below 256. Initialization must cover every array entry, helpers must preserve pointer provenance and room invariants, and borrows must not overlap a whole-W mutable borrow across calls. The rec allocation remains distinct and stable as described in section D.

The five-million instruction allowance is not proved by unchanged byte footprint. Current refill callers preserve a descriptor address across the call; removing it can require preserving c or reloading the source byte on the cold edge. One extra instruction per scalar refill costs at least 1.123M and at most 1.795M under the reconstructed bounds. One extra instruction per snapshot character costs 935,424; per finished portion 1,795,482; per run 610,521; per block-character 111,104. A few such changes can consume the allowance.

Close currently zeroes adjacent write/end together: refill 0x62363–0x6236a, run 0x33d8e–0x33d97, sweep 0x346bd–0x346c6. Replacing xorps/pxor plus a 16-byte and an 8-byte store with three scalar zero stores can keep three instructions while increasing explicit write operations from two to three. This is shifted memory work, even when instruction-neutral. It is not automatically an extra instruction per close, nor does equal byte footprint establish equal cache behavior.

The unweighted statistic cannot support a claim that C's imbalance is economically better. Aggregate CPU/worker-wall ratios are 0.998660 for C and 0.998477 for P; this argues against large off-CPU waiting inside these windows, but on-CPU time includes memory stalls and does not identify their cause.

Conditional completion models

For each archived candidate build, the model assumes an explicit effective time reduction δᵢ=Σₖ nᵢₖaₖ, where aₖ is assumed nanoseconds saved per event. It holds recorded starts fixed, subtracts δᵢ from CPU and wall duration, and recomputes the latest end at every step. It solves by bisection for a specified geometric-mean reduction in candidate completion. No instruction count is converted to nanoseconds, cycles or micro-ops.

Original step completion: maxᵢ(endᵢ) − minᵢ(startᵢ)
Modeled completion: maxᵢ(startᵢ + max(0, wallᵢ−δᵢ)) − minᵢ(startᵢ)
CPU': Σᵢ max(0, CPUᵢ−δᵢ)

The eligible full-fast count uses the conservative per-block lower bound. The model reports clipping and worker changes; the reported solutions have no clipping. It assumes saved work shortens CPU and wall equally, preserves starts/scheduling, and does not model cache/frequency feedback. These assumptions make it an accounting sensitivity model, not an identified causal forecast.

Economic challenge from the opposite hypothesis

The strongest case against practical benefit is that many removed instructions are predictable checks, address arithmetic, or independent work already overlapped with other operations. Compare/branch pairs may fuse; a memory RMW represents more internal work than its single instruction count; fewer LEAs leave descriptor loads and pointer recurrence; and generic frame removal may be cheap if stack accesses hit. Splitting arrays may add memory operations or spills. No hardware observations here quantify these effects.

The strongest positive mechanism is elimination of the dependent snapshots-Vec-length/data-element chain before final classification, plus removal of endpoint loads and frame traffic. Direct pointer-array addressing can also shorten address preparation. The enrichment on current slow workers makes these hypotheses relevant. It does not measure the nonoverlapped part of either chain.

In a simple affected-component model, completion reduction d=f·r, with f the affected nonoverlapped critical-worker time share and r its fractional reduction. At r=30%, achieving the 10.28476% sensitivity needs f≈34.28%; achieving 15% needs f=50%. At r=50%, the required shares are ≈20.57% and 30%. The instruction ledger establishes neither f nor r. Therefore 10–15% completion headroom is not established, and the evidence cannot determine that savings primarily remove overlapped latency either. Future code shape and separately authorized valid measurement would be needed. The 2.56% CPU hurdle is materially easier and cannot substitute for this test.

Other alternatives were also reviewed. Subtracting the fixed conservative 225,253,100 target from revised K gives the bookkeeping residual 904,939,936–1,036,171,107, with the same baseline E retained and incremental overhead already charged. This is not an interval for generated cost or a mathematical execution floor. E is provably nonzero and not bounded above here. Further instruction minimization remains credible, but the smaller secondary ceilings cannot independently establish the missing completion margin.

The smallest useful future untimed observations are: fragment length histogram; refill initial/nonempty/preset/exhausted counts and call-site counts; full-to-full eligibility; bulk invocation parity; nonuniform split count; terminal/held scanner exits; fast-full versus fast-partial membership; epoch-search depths and RAW interval lengths; and call/setup frequency for initialization/canonicalization. They would narrow accounting, not identify cycles. None was newly instrumented in this audit.

The central answer is that substantial remaining machine-level work is mathematically avoidable, and Ultra identifies a credible route to removing it without a new row-sized table or second input scan. The proposed substitutions also introduce some memory operations and may introduce compiler work. The current evidence supports a tightly bounded engineering investigation; it does not support an implementation decision premised on 225M achieved instructions or 10–15% completion headroom.

Judgment would change with a byte-exact, release-safe candidate whose complete frozen-toolchain caller/callee listings meet or miss the explicit joint budgets; a discovered invariant counterexample; or separately authorized, formally valid measurements identifying the net completion effect on large low-bulk blocks. Until then, the most important unknown is the amount of nonoverlapped critical-worker time removed after all added work.
