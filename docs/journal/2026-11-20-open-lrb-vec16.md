# Open LRB-Vec16 natively: the queue pays, the fusion doesn't

*20 November 2026. Branch `research/open-lrb-vec16`, from `research/open-u16q` (#664) with
master merged. Source: Carbon Kitchen Sink cycle 3, `research/carbon-kitchen-sink` at
b682346a, §4 and `g5_cycle3_bench.c`.*

**The finding.**
- **The Vec16 queue pays natively.** Thin runs go into a buffer per byte that is kept at full
  length, with the byte's retained-row count as its cursor. Each run is one bounds check and
  two 32-byte stores. With it, the open kernel on the whole corpus at one thread drops to
  347–359 ms, from 433–441 on master and 390–393 on #664.
- **The LRB fusion doesn't pay natively.** It moves the first pass into the front's literal
  walk, with no records and no cut at 127. It is exact, but its route costs 408–433 ms
  against 384–398 ms for the front and Open separately on the same head.
- **Against Cells:** front + LRB-Vec16 does not beat front + Cells (about 403 ms). Front +
  Open with the Vec16 queue and no fusion does, by about 4% (0.95–0.96× in the same process).

## The machine and the tool

- **The machine:** a shared 4-vCPU container (Xeon @ 2.10 GHz, 2 MB L2 a core), one thread
  pinned to CPU 2 (`taskset -c 2`).
- **The corpus:** `corpora/wikidata.bz2` (SHA-256 `82f65ace…`), 431 blocks, 209.7 M rows.
- **The tool:** `attrib.rs` here is the stage-attribution example from
  `2026-11-20-open-stage-attribution/`, with one more column, `fused`.
  - `fused` times `OpenCells::invert_symbols` from the block's symbols, so it is the front and
    the inverse together.
  - On a block that path refuses, it also times the records path's front and `invert_runs`.
- **How it was run:** each figure is the best of 5 per block, summed over the corpus. Each
  build ran four times (runs 3–6), interleaved master, #664, head.

- **The head's records-path Open** is #664's kernel with the Vec16 queue, and the first
  pass's per-run closure written out so that it inlines.
- **Two-pass against fused:** in every run, front + Open (two-pass) was 20–36 ms below the
  fused route.
- **Repeated on one block** (`lrbcal.rs`, best of 25, corpus blocks 1, 3, 158, 268, 384 and
  387, quiet runs), fused / (front + Open) was 0.94–1.02. So the fusion is level when hot and
  loses in decode order.
- **The front:** the AVX2 front is now generic over its sink, the shipped records or the
  fused visitor. Timed alone (best of 15 a block, 383 blocks) it is level: 39.3–43.0 ms on the
  head against 39.8–40.7 on master. The 1–3 ms the head's `front` column shows in `attrib` is
  the extra arm's cache traffic, not the front.

## Why the fusion doesn't pay here

The C harness's `front + pass1` halved (0.49–0.54×), mostly through the queue store, which
both arms here share. The records themselves are cheap: about 2.5 ns a run to write and
then read back.

Fusing puts the first pass's per-run work into the loop that carries the MTF chain:
- the classification branch, which mispredicts;
- the queue store, scattered over 256 streams;
- the strip push.

The checks the fused visitor needs cost 3–5% on their own. They are a repeated byte, the
row bound and the density line, and with all three removed it measured 0.96–0.99×. One
branch for all three wins back most of that, not all.

## What didn't port

- **The coprime 257-line queue stride.** It wasn't ported: the queues are `Vec`s, whose base
  addresses the allocator chooses. The per-byte cursor gives the same store shape.
- **The Slot16 table.** It was already #664's `inline16`, so nothing changed there.
- **The fused front's coverage.** It exists only with the AVX2 front. With the scalar kernels,
  or on a block the AVX2 front leaves to its scalar loop, the block takes the records' path.

## Exactness

- **The existing suite:** `cargo test --release` passes in full, including `open::checks`,
  `open::b5`, `emit_matches_reference` and the runs tests.
- **The new tests** (`src/open/fused.rs`) check that the fused path, the records path and the
  plain inverse agree:
  - every binary column to 11 rows and every ternary column to 6, at every row (46,977 cases);
  - arbitrary columns of 1–256 bytes, with runs up to 10,000 rows, at four rows each;
  - the admission gates, and each refusal;
  - `front_runs`' runs, cut at 127, against `runs::front`'s records on 3,000 blocks;
  - the six admitted blocks (`MIBZ2_ADMITTED_BWT`, ignored by default), all byte-exact.
