## What ships

- **`src/open.rs` and `src/open/endpoint.rs`:** Astra's open-return decoder
  (`docs/journal/2026-11-18-open-return/`, #565), the instance the i9 measured: induced, queued,

- **What is dropped from the journal's code,** none of which this instance used:

  The `INDUCE` and `QUEUE` generics stay, so that the journal's arrow audit (`checks.rs`, ported

- **Two changes to the code,** both from the mutation check, below:
  - `emit`'s walk is bounded by the retained rows and clamped to `n` bytes;
  - `induced`'s band loop asserts that each band takes a row.
- **`OpenCells`** (`bwt::OpenCells`), the workers' kernel wherever production ran `LazyCells`
  (`StreamDecoder::workers`' lazy route, so the plan's lazy workers and the trims):
  - *the open kernel* on a run-route block that passes `LazyCells`' two record-count gates. Those
    are its density line (r/n < 0.15) and Cells' sparse gate (r/n ≥ 1/64), shared through

  - *Why those gates:* the census (`2026-11-19-open-admission.md`) found open faster than
    production only on the blocks `LazyCells` admits, and slower on every block those gates
    refuse.
- **`ForcedOpenCells`** (hidden): the open kernel on every run-route block, for tests and the
  fuzzer.
- **Unchanged:**
  - eager Cells below 8 workers;
  - the plans, windows and trims;
  - every other kernel.

## The safety argument

- **No `unsafe`.** None in the journal's code, and none added. Every index is bounds-checked, so
  a broken invariant panics; it never reads or writes out of bounds.
- **The `assert!`s are invariants of the construction for every column,** BWT or not. They are
  tested, not argued from the BWT's structure:
  - every binary column to 8 rows and ternary to 5, at every row and five limits, every arrow
    expanded independently (`checks::all_port_arrows`);
  - every binary column to 12 rows and ternary to 7, at every row, by the shipped instance
    against the plain inverse (`b5::every_small_column_at_every_row`, 111,438 cases);
  - arbitrary columns to 70,000 rows, runs past the 127 record cap, 1 to 256 bytes;
  - **the fuzzer:** 514,235 mutated streams (seeds 1 and 7, 120 s each), with both open routes
    among the candidates against libbzip2: 0 failures.
    - About 54,000 of those inverted a corrupted block that the block CRC then rejected (the
      `BlockCrc` and `StreamCrc` outcomes). Arbitrary corrupted columns reached the kernel, and
      none panicked.
- **The entry `assert!`** (0 < n ≤ 900,000, p < n) is unreachable from `OpenCells`, whose
  admission tests the same condition.
- **Every loop ends.**
  - *Before the port:* the journal's `emit` walked until it returned to the marked row. On a
    valid construction that happens within `edges.len()` steps.
  - *What the mutation check found:* two constructions (a band's destination, a band's width)
    whose walk never returned, and grew until the process was killed.
  - *Now:* the walk stops after `edges.len()` steps, or at `n` bytes, and the output is clamped
    to `n`. A wrong construction gives a wrong block, which the CRC rejects. So this walk can't
    hang, and its output can't grow past `n` bytes.
  - *What that doesn't show* (the Guardian, D115): the guard bounds this one loop. It is no proof
    that no input can exhaust memory; the kernel's other buffers are bounded only as the scratch
    section below says, by a count of logical elements.
  - *The band loop:* in `induced`, a band of zero rows would never advance. On a valid
    construction every band takes a row, and the loop now asserts it, so a wrong construction
    panics there rather than looping.

## Worst-case scratch

**The bound.** For one block of n rows: m retained rows, H thick strips, B bands, N elimination
nodes, R rules, and σ ≤ 256 bytes. The per-buffer counts:
- *edges, queues and the wide codes:* at most 12m bytes;
- *the spelled words:* at most (n − m) + B bytes. A word is spelled once per node, and its
  height times its width is thick area;
- *offsets and bands:* 16B;
- *strips:* 20H;
- *nodes, their orders and the bottom ranks:* 44N;
- *rules and the expansion stack:* 20R.

With B ≤ 3H + σ (the arrow audit asserts it on every case), N ≤ 3H + 3, H ≤ n/17 + 2 and
R, m ≤ n, the total is at most **44 n + O(σ) bytes** of logical elements: about 39 MB at
n = 900,000.

**What that bound is not** (the Guardian, D115): it counts the elements a block's construction
holds, not the memory a worker keeps. It doesn't bound:
- *retained capacity:* a `Vec` grows by doubling, and a reused kernel keeps each buffer's
  largest capacity from block to block;
- *the coexisting storage:* each worker's `OpenCells` holds the open kernel and a `LazyCells`
  together, and both keep their buffers;
- *the output's capacity.*

So it is no bound on a worker's peak RSS.

**Measured** (`worst_case_scratch_per_row`, and the adversarial `scratch_search` over 3,000
shapes built to shorten the elimination's steps). These are the buffers' capacities after one
block on a fresh kernel, so they include the growth's slack, but not what a reused kernel
retains across blocks, nor `LazyCells` or the output:
- the worst is **16.2 bytes a row**, a comb of thick and thin runs;
- the search's worst is 15.1;
- the most rules is 0.28 a row.

So the R ≤ n term is loose by more than 3×.

**Against production:** on the i9, ungated open's peak RSS was 0.66–0.69 of `LazyCells`' at 7 to
10 workers (the moonshot runs, LOGs 6997132553 and 7004261019). That is a measurement of those
runs, not a bound. The C6 registration's co-primary P2 and guard G measure the port's peak RSS
against v0.5.6.
