## 2. DRAM traffic per output byte (M, fitted)

Over the three lazy cells, against x = W·W_run:

  **reads ≈ 3.77 + 0.0663 x,  writes ≈ 2.93 + 0.0217 x  (bytes a byte; residuals ≤ 0.04)**

**Traffic follows the contention term.**
- *The constant, about 6.7 B:* the floor, which is writes of the output and buffers, and the
  floor's misses.
- *The term in W·W_run:* the capacity misses and the dirty evictions they force.
- *The ratio:* about 3 bytes of reads to 1 of writes.
- *The eager 4-worker cell* (7.55 B) sits below the lazy line at x = 16 (8.07 B).

**Bandwidth, with contention alone deciding throughput:** R(W) = R(7) · (W/7) · c(7)/c(W).

| workers, unpinned | output, contention only | DRAM B a byte | DRAM GB/s | share of the 33.1 GB/s ceiling | measured wall against 7 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 7 | 2.27 GB/s | 11.02 | 25.0 | 76% (measured 75%) | 1 |
| 8 | 2.29 | 12.34 | 28.2 | 85% (measured 83%) | 1.025 (the model says 0.993) |
| 9 | 2.27 | 13.83 | 31.3 | 95% | not run on this decoder |
| 10 | 2.22 | 15.50 | 34.5 | **104%** | 1/0.873 = 1.146 (the model says 1.022) |

**What the excess means (Conj).** The cycle model misses where DRAM utilisation is high:
- *at 85%:* +3%;
- *at 10 workers unpinned:* +12%. The measured throughput there (1.98 GB/s) implies about
  30.7 GB/s, **93% of the ceiling**.

A slowdown that grows steeply with utilisation is the signature of queueing at the memory
controller.
- *Why the cycle model doesn't carry it:* its b-term was fitted at 75–85% utilisation.
- *The reading:* the unpinned 10 → 7 gain (0.873) is about one fifth contention and four fifths
  DRAM queueing.
- *Against record 51:* this sharpens it. L3-miss latency there rose 8% from 75% to 85%, but nothing
  above 85% was measured.

## 3. What the IMC data settled, and what remains

**Settled (C):** at 7 workers the decoder uses **75%** of the measured ceiling. So DRAM does not bind
at 7, and the knee there is contention. Traffic per byte grows with W·W_run, so every worker past
7 spends bandwidth on misses, not output.

**Not settled:** whether DRAM queueing is what makes 8–10 workers slower than the cycle model says
(§2). One measurement would settle it.

## 4. Frozen now: the one measurement that would settle it

**Refuted (queueing) if:** DRAM sits under 80% of the ceiling, or L3-miss latency rises under 10%.
- *Then:* the extra 10% of the 10 → 7 gain has no named cause, and the contention fit is only a
  fit there.

## 5. The levers at 7 workers, ranked (M; kernel count needed for each)

1. **Fewer output-side bytes: the fused finish.** The measured point is 1.5–3%.
   - *By §2:* it also removes writes of about 1 B a byte, beside the L3 traffic of record 51.
   - *Where it helps more:* wherever the plan runs past 7, with DRAM near its ceiling, the
     saving counts twice.
   - **First.**
2. **A smaller hot set per worker: 0–1%.** The block-size result (k ≈ 2, record 50) does not
   transfer to layout cuts.
   - *Why not:* a smaller block changes the letters, the streams and the buffers together. W12's
     cut of the store moved misses 7% and the wall not at all (record 51).
   - *The cut that would count:* one that brings a structure into the 256 KiB L2, cutting
     L3-miss reads. By record 51 those also inflate every L3 hit. Nothing on the table does that
     yet.
   - **Second.**
3. **Fewer, larger workers (teams): negative, about −5%.** The measured crossovers:
   - three teams of three lost to lazy ten at 10 threads (0.931–0.949);
   - lazy seven beats lazy ten by 0.873–0.885.

   So lazy seven beats three teams of three by about 5–7%. Teams halve the tables per core but keep
   every thread's traffic, which by §2 is what grows. **Last.**

**SMT latency hiding** stays open. At 75% of the ceiling there is headroom, but the earlier sibling
measurements (28–49% slower) cut against it.
