# 2026-11-16 · MONODROMY 29 · Louis's question on the two Ghrist texts: nothing in them changes what the decoder computes. Discrete Morse theory taken literally reduces to an order of contractions, and its natural objective, points removed per word byte, cuts the word store by 28% with random access to original letters, but 3.5% at the stream fronts the kernel reads

Question (the Coordinator, relaying Louis, after he supplied *Elementary Applied Topology*, EAT,
and the *Applied Dynamical Systems* draft, ADS): is there anything in these texts that gives an
exact connection to our problem, beyond framing, leading to a decoder insight that could not be
seen without it, as Rauzy–Veech induction did?

**The texts.** They are cited by section and page in the edition Louis supplied; nothing from them
is in the repository. EAT's numbering differs from the relays': discrete Morse theory is §8.8,
p. 205, and "Sheaves, redux" §11.9, p. 286 (LOG 6924126823).

**Labels:** **P** proved · **C** counted (exact for its input) · **M** modelled.

## 0. Answer

**No step in these texts changes what the decoder computes.** Each candidate either maps exactly
onto an object we already have, with nothing new to compute, or imposes no constraint here. The
reasons, item by item:

| source | exact correspondence | what it computes for us |
|---|---|---|
| EAT §8.8 (Forman; Thm 8.24, p. 206), discrete Morse | P: the induction is a sequence of contractions of the cycle complex, flowlines labelled by words in the free monoid | **Nothing new.** On a complex with one cycle, any matching that leaves one critical vertex and one critical edge is acyclic, so the theory constrains no choice. The homology it preserves, over a field, forgets every label. What remains is a choice of order (§1). |
| EAT §6.5 (trees), merge trees and persistence | P: the r − 1 equal steps form the Cartesian tree of the stretch minima in cycle order (Theorem 3 of the Rauzy–Veech entry), which is the H₀ merge tree of that sequence | **Nothing new.** The sequence is in cycle order, which needs the walk. Theorem 3 already says this. |
| EAT §7.13 (p. 185), circular coordinates; Ex. 7.37, ranking | P: a byte place is the integral of the 1-cocycle −\|w\| around the stopped cycle (record 28) | **No shortcut.** On a cycle the integral is the prefix sum. The book's harmonic step smooths coordinates; it does not make them cheaper. |
| EAT §11.9 (p. 286), sheaves as functors, monoid data | the "ordered composition" of the relays: words as free-monoid sections | Framing only. |
| ADS ch. 5, pp. 27 and 29: periodic orbits as fixed points of f^(P); topological conjugacy | the itinerary certificate and F^k (records 26 and 28) | These follow from translations on intervals. Record 28 shows the only cheap conjugacy is the induction itself. |

**Where the Morse framing still pays: it names the objective.** A contraction removes λ points
for the price of one word. "Contract the most valuable pairs first" is a scheduling rule needing
no theory, and it is measurable (§1).
- *With random access* to original letters it cuts the word store by 28%, and by 55% with a stop
  at m ≤ n/32.
- *At the stream fronts,* which is all the lazy kernel reads, the cut is 3.5%. There the best
  order is free-first, already known as the stream-front order (record 17): −15%.

## 1. The order of contractions (C)

**What was run.** Every 24th Wikidata block (10 blocks), one-cell Rauzy steps at any coincidence
(`geo.rs.txt`, from `schedules_geo`), and its stream-constrained twin (`streamr.rs.txt`, from
`streamw`). Every final word is checked to be a rotation of the block.
- *The words:* a step's word costs 16 B if longer than 16, else its length.
- *The points left:* m = n − Σ cell widths.
- *The new order, ratio:* takes the coincidence with the most points removed per word byte,
  ⌊4096 · cell width / cost(|w_A| + |w_B|)⌋, highest first.

**Word bytes made, as a share of the right end's final** (14.75 B a letter on these blocks), and
the steps:

| order | access to original letters | m ≤ n/8 | m ≤ n/32 | final | steps |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| right end (the kernel) | fronts | 0.529 | 0.743 | 1 | 1.79 r |
| largest cell first | random | 0.203 | 0.543 | 0.807 | 1.45 r |
| free first, then largest cell | random | 0.494 | 0.659 | 0.790 | 1.28 r |
| **ratio** | random | **0.171** | **0.410** | **0.679** | 1.43 r |
| ratio (streams' run) | random | 0.172 | 0.451 | 0.721 | 1.47 r |
| ratio | fronts only (k = 0) | 0.491 | 0.725 | 0.965 | 1.66 r |
| free first, rightmost | fronts only (k = 0) | 0.548 | 0.688 | 0.848 | 1.54 r |

The two ratio rows with random access differ only in how ties and re-keying are handled in the two
programs.

**What it says.**
- *The order changes the word store a lot,* not only the step count: −32% in total for the ratio
  order against the right end, and −59% with a stop at n/32.
- *The gain needs random access.* At the stream fronts the ratio order keeps 3.5%, because the
  valuable cells lie far from the fronts. That matches record 17: the low-width orders take 98% of
  their untouched letters more than 512 places from either front.
- *The best order the streams allow is free-first:* −15% in total, and 0.688 against 0.743 at
  n/32. That is the parked stream-front order (REVIEW 6905606710 priced it at 8 to 12% of bytes
  touched, for a second induction).

## 2. What would test the random-access order (M)

**The price.** The ratio order needs a priority on coincidences and at most one random read of the
original-letter array a letter (record 17, §"What it means for the kernel"). Against that, it
removes about 4.1 B a letter of words in total, and more with a stop.

**Why it is not a HANDOFF now.**
- *The ring precedent:* the ring lost at about +69 instructions a letter for 4.5 B (lazy-ring
  entry), and a heap in the induction loop is in that range or above.
- *The cheaper levers first:* the compact tail (record 27; 4.9 B, no walk) and the run walk
  (record 26) are both cheaper and are with the Pathfinder.

The ratio order is recorded here as a measured lever, to price only if those fall short.

**Fields.**
- `curve_{p}` and `v{v}_k{k}_curve`: rows [k, steps, word bytes] when m first falls to
  n · 2^(−k/2).
- `wfinal_{p}` and `v{v}_k{k}_w`: final word bytes.
