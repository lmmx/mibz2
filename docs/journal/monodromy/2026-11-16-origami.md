## 1. The surface (P)

**Squares and gluings.** One unit square per row of the block, n of them:
- the right side of square x is glued to the left side of r(x) = x + 1 (mod n);
- the top of x is glued to the bottom of u(x) = LF(x).

This is the integer interval exchange's suspension, with the base cut into unit squares.

**Its two cylinder directions.**
- *Vertical:* the flow up the squares visits x, LF(x), LF²(x), …, the block's one cycle. It is one
  cylinder of n squares, and reading its labels upward spells the text backward. That is the
  inverse BWT.
- *Horizontal:* one cylinder too (r is an n-cycle).

So this is a one-cylinder origami in both directions.

**The cone points (P).**
- *Which squares the commutator moves:* c = u⁻¹ r⁻¹ u r fixes every square whose right neighbour
  is in the same run, because there LF(x + 1) = LF(x) + 1.
- *What it does to the rest:* on the last square of run α, c gives the last square of
  σ(α) = (the bottom-order predecessor of the run after α). That is the previous run with that
  run's byte, or the last run of the next smaller byte.
- *So:* the cone points are the cycles of σ of length at least 2. A cycle of length k is a
  cone angle of 2πk.
- *The genus:* by Gauss–Bonnet, 2g − 2 = Σ(k − 1). With d = r intervals, d = 2g + s − 1,
  where s is the number of cone points.
- *σ depends only on the runs' byte sequence,* and Rauzy–Veech induction keeps the stratum. So the
  whole induction runs in one stratum, H(k₁ − 1, …, k_s − 1), fixed by the run bytes.

## 3. What it says

**The surface looks generic.** All the curvature sits in a handful of zeros of very high order, and
there are few cylinders in small rational directions. For a random permutation on r letters the
mean number of cycles is about ln r ≈ 11; σ has fewer, median 5.

**The cost is not in the surface's coarse shape (Conj).** None of its invariants predicts S. The
step count is a property of the induction's path, the Rauzy–Veech path in this stratum: how the
lengths fall, not which stratum or which cylinders.

**The one exact link to the decoder,** already used (records 26 and 28):
- *Runs are rectangles:* a run of the stopped walk is a flat rectangle of the surface, between two
  horizontal paths at vertical offset δ, avoiding the cone points.
- *Zippered rectangles:* the induction's stages are the surface's zippered-rectangle
  decompositions, whose rectangles are the letters' columns.
