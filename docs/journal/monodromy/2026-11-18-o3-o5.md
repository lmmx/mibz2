**Why (P, M).** **V₀(k + 1) = E₀(k)**, exactly, at every depth tabled: refining by one more step makes
each depth-k arc a site. So E₀(k + 1) = E₀(k) + excess(k + 1). The first level grows by the excess
at every depth, while the later levels shrink by less (Σ arcs − E₀: 28,807 → 22,869 → 18,245 → …).
- *Refinement trades excess for sites at worse than par.* It lowers the excess (10,954 → 3,668) and
  the retained mass (110,273 → 7,667), not the work.
- *Coarsening fails the other way (P).* Sites larger than runs, F-buckets say, give V ≈ σ and
  E ≈ r + σ, so the excess is about r.
- *A floor (P):* any presentation with single-byte arc labels has E₀ ≥ r, since an arc carries one
  byte.
- **Within the presentations tried, runs are optimal:** Σ arcs ≥ E₀ ≥ r, plus about 0.78 r of
  later levels.
- **Record 61's P1 stands as the open question:** whether r + Ω(E − V) bounds every translation-
  interval induction. O3 gives no counterexample.

**Total critical path: 87 steps.** Division compresses no repeated passes on this block: the counts of
steps and atom passages are equal.
- *Work at row level:* Σ rows over rounds = 1,337,702 row-steps, **about 1.49 n.**
- *At atom level:* 102,234 visits, but the depth of a round's interval routing (shared incoming lists
  give the atom DAG longer paths than any one excursion) **is not measured.**
- *The 1,175× ratio of atom work to row depth* mixes the two levels. It is not an available
  speed-up.

**What this is (I).** Selecting strict local minima picks an independent set (no two adjacent on a
cycle), and contracting it at least halves the rows. That is **classical parallel list contraction**,
the deterministic independent-set route to list ranking, applied to LF's cycle. Logarithmic rounds
with short per-round paths are its known behaviour. The block shows it concretely: 12 rounds, at most
11 steps each.

**Cells (P).** Right-end Rauzy–Veech is a strictly **sequential chain of 92,570 steps** (1.81 r): each
step reads the top and bottom state the previous one left. Its stop-and-walk tail is a chain over the
stopped points, which record 23 splits into at most two. Cells has no parallelism inside a block
beyond that.

## 3. What it implies for the i9 with SMT (M, Conj)

1. **Across blocks, parallelism is already the plan's.** 431 blocks against 20 hardware threads, and
   the machine is contention- and power-bound at 7–10 busy cores (records 52, 53 and 58). The
   inverse's parallelism inside a block doesn't relieve either.
2. **Inside a block, the available parallelism is real but expensive:**
   - *at row level:* about 1.49 n work at depth 87, against Cells' r-scale work (about 1.8 r
     operations, about 0.1 n);
   - *at atom level:* r-scale, but at investigation 1's 10× cost per operation (record 61), with its
     parallel depth unmeasured.

   **Neither beats sending a sibling thread to another block.**
3. **The one SMT-relevant use (Conj):** a sibling pair sharing *one* block, which halves the resident
   blocks per core for the same running threads.
   - *Record 52's term,* contention ∝ W · W_run (blocks resident × threads running), would fall.
     That needs a cheap way to split one block's work in two.
   - *For Cells:* only the walk's two chains (record 23), worth about 10% of a thread.
   - *For the contraction:* its per-operation cost first.
   - **No experiment is recommended until O1's per-operation cost is known.** The Coordinator has
     put it after O3, and O3 has now failed.
4. **Small streams and latency (I):** a decode of one or two blocks is the only case where
   parallelism inside a block would show in wall time. Not the production workload.
