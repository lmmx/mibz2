# 2026-11-16 · MONODROMY 17 · Orders under the streaming constraint: reading original letters only at the streams' fronts keeps 0.195 r live (−38% on the right end); the 0.06–0.08 r orders need the letters at random

Question: QUEUED 6904298784. Lazy letters reads the original letters as two streams: the top
order, and the bottom order (stably sorted by byte), each from its right end. How much of the
width gain (`2026-11-15-least-width.md`) survives if an order may use an untouched original
letter only within a window of k positions of a stream's front?

**Labels:** **P** proved · **C** counted (exact for its input) · **Conj** conjecture.

## The model

- **A stream's front** is its rightmost original letter that is still untouched and alive.
  Untouched letters never move, so the front is well defined, and it only moves left.
- **The rule.** A step may use an untouched original letter only if, in one of the two streams,
  it lies at most k positions to the left of that stream's front. Touched letters are in the
  pool and always usable. A step that breaks the rule waits until a front comes within k.
- **The right end obeys it at k = 0 (P).** Its two letters are the last letter on top and the
  last on the bottom. If either is untouched, it is that stream's front. So every constrained
  order makes progress.
- **The orders** are those of the least-width entry, keyed by the change in W:
  - free-first, then the rightmost (variant 6);
  - (free, change in W) on six LIFO buckets (variant 4);
  - (change in W, free, the largest cell) (variant 3).
- **The check.** Every final word is checked to be a rotation of the block.
- **The program:** `streamw.rs.txt`, here.

**What survives.**
- **At the fronts (k = 0):** every order holds about 0.195 to 0.203 r. That is 38% below the
  right end's 0.317 r, and needs no random access and no buffer.
- **A window helps little** until it is a large share of the stream: 4,096 positions gives
  0.190 r.
- **The 0.057 to 0.077 r orders need unbounded reach.** Their gain is not a local refinement
  of the streams.

**A side finding: the order of free steps matters for width.** Free-first that takes its free
steps rightmost first holds 0.223 r unbounded, more than its own k = 0 run. The free-first of
the earlier entries took them from a stack (`schedules_geo`'s ready list, last in first out)
and held 0.107 r. The free closure is unique (#438); the path through it, and so the width,
is not.

## 2. More cursors do not help (C, every 16th block, 15 blocks)

`streamp.rs.txt`, here, splits each stream into P segments. Each segment is read right to left
by its own cursor, and a letter within k of any segment's front may be used.

**W medians ÷ r, at k = 0** (k = 64 is alike; `cursors_wikidata_every16.jsonl`):

| P cursors a stream | 1 | 4 | 16 | 64 | 256 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| free-first | 0.203 | 0.202 | 0.201 | 0.194 | 0.173 |
| six buckets | 0.195 | 0.192 | 0.187 | 0.197 | 0.179 |
| change-first | 0.195 | 0.190 | 0.190 | 0.219 | 0.233 |

Even 256 sequential cursors a stream leave 0.17 to 0.23 r.

**Change-first gets worse with partial reach.** A greedy on the change in width, with some far
letters in reach but not others, takes steps that strand letters it cannot close.

## 3. Which steps need the far letters (C)

| | at a front | 1 to 8 | 9 to 64 | 65 to 512 | beyond 512 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| free-first | 0.075 | 0.001 | 0.001 | 0.008 | **0.915** |

| change-first | 0.001 | 0.001 | 0.002 | 0.013 | **0.984** |

The low-width orders take nearly all their untouched letters far from both fronts. Their equal
and free steps fall on coincidences spread over the whole row range: tunnels throughout the
block, not near its ends.

## What it means for the kernel (Conj)

**Two regimes.**
- **Streams only.** About 0.195 r live, against today's 0.318 r: a 38% cut, with no change to
  how originals are read. The cheapest order here is free-first (fewest steps at k = 0: 1.55 r).
- **Random access to originals.** 0.058 to 0.077 r live. An untouched original is read once, at
  its first touch, so this is at most one random read of the original-letter array a letter.
  - *The array:* lazy letters' stream records, about 5 bytes a letter. Whether one scattered
    read a letter costs less than the 0.12 to 0.14 r of live pool it saves is the Pathfinder's
    clock question.
  - *What the census can give:* the read count is r (C, by construction).
- **Nothing in between pays.** Windows up to 4,096 and up to 256 cursors stay near the streams'
  figure.
