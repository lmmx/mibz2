## 1. The route class

**𝓡, rigid jump-walk routes.** An exact decoder is in 𝓡 if:
1. **It emits in orbit order by jumps.** Each serial step moves from a row to a later row of the marked orbit and emits the stored word in between. By record 92's Lemma 2, such a decoder is the first-return (trace) walk on the set S of rows it visits.
2. **It builds its jump table and words by rigid operations.** Each operation carries one interval of rows, lying in a single translation piece, through that piece. Rauzy–Veech steps, Open's endpoint induction, band tracers and composing pieces are all rigid operations.
3. **Its shortcut predicate is any function of the run records and of up to k symbols of left context** (the row's next k outputs), for any k. The predicate only chooses S. Item 2 governs what a shortcut may do.

**Membership:**
- *Open:* S is the thin runs, chosen by a context-free record-local predicate; its construction is endpoint induction.
- *`Segmented`:* S is every row, with no predicate.
- *Cells:* S is {p}, with Rauzy–Veech over everything.
- *The copy-sector routes of cycle 46* (A-Tube, U_iso, LPTQ) are in 𝓡 where they skip a row by an adjacent row's itinerary, which is a rigid bundle. Cycle 47's ribbon copies from completed output, so it is in 𝓡 only if it obtains its destination rows rigidly. I have not read its code, so its membership is left open.

## 2. The theorem

**Theorem G1, the wall [proved].**
- *The statement:* for every column and every route in 𝓡, the decoder spends **at least one dependent operation on each run of width 1, and every such operation covers exactly one row.** So:
  - **D_width-1 ≥ h₁**, and
  - D ≥ Σ_{R ⊆ S} w_R + #{R ⊄ S} ≥ r over all runs.
- *The proof:*
  - A row in a width-1 run that is in S is one walk step.
  - One that is not in S lies on the orbit between visits to S. Its successor must be supplied by some rigid operation on an interval containing it.
  - By record 92's Lemma 3, every rigid piece lies inside one run at every step. An operation that touches a width-1 run therefore touches exactly its one row. ∎
- *Independent of k:* the predicate's locality is irrelevant to G1. Whatever S is chosen, a width-1 run cannot be part of a wider unit at the step where its own row is carried.

**The observable and its estimator.**
- *Exact and free:* h₁ is read from the run records in one pass, O(r). Open's first pass already visits every maximal run (`Pass::take`, `self.f.runs += 1`), so h₁ costs one compare a run.
- *A GUID proxy, also from records alone:* width-1 runs whose byte is a hex digit, h₁^hex.
  - *Its recall:* it captures **0.923–0.929** of GUID rows in width-1 runs on all six blocks.
  - *Its precision* for GUID content is 0.31–0.73, because sha1 references, numbers and Q-id digits are hex too. Those runs are just as width-1, and G1 charges them all the same.
- *What is not observable before decoding:* the GUID share itself needs the text, so it explains h₁ rather than being the parameter.

**The price form [modelled].** T_width-1 ≥ h₁ × min(c_step, c_op).
- *On the i9 at 1 worker:* c_step is the emit's work floor, 4.79–4.92 ns a row (record 95), and c_op a Rauzy–Veech step, 22.2 ns. That gives **h₁ × 4.8 ns**.
- *Assumed:* no rigid operation is cheaper than the emit's per-row work.
- *At 10 workers* the floor is higher (6.2 ns base emit).

## 3. GUIDs instantiated (model I: the emitted byte of each GUID random nibble is independent and uniform over 16 digits, independent of the sort order)

**Definitions.** GUID rows are rows whose emitted byte (L[x] = T[SA[x] − 1]) lies inside a match of `[0-9a-fA-F]{8}-…-4…-[89abAB]…-…{12}`. *Random-nibble rows* are those at its 30 fully random nibble positions; the hyphens, version and variant nibbles are excluded.

**Proposition G2 [proved under model I].**
- *The statement:* a random-nibble row whose two neighbours in the column also emit independent uniform digits is a width-1 run with probability exactly (15/16)² = 0.879. Its run's width is geometric, with P(w) = (1/16)^{w−1}(15/16), so it is thin (≤ 16) with probability about 1 − 16^{−16}.
- *The proof:* a row's emitted byte is the text byte before its rotation. The sort order depends only on bytes from the rotation onward, so under model I the three emitted bytes are independent uniform digits. The row is a width-1 run iff its byte differs from both neighbours'. ∎

**Proposition G3, keys shatter [proved under model I].**
- *The statement:* take a key made of a row's next k emitted symbols, with those k symbols lying among random nibbles. Among N random-nibble rows, the expected number of other rows sharing a given row's key is (N − 1) / 16^k. Overlapping windows of an i.i.d. sequence also agree with probability 16^{−k}: each agreement fixes one fresh digit.

| k | expected partners a row, at the six blocks' N = 18,810–57,090 |
|--:|---|
| 4 | 0.29–0.87 |
| 5 | 0.018–0.054 |
| 6 | 0.0011–0.0034 |

- **What follows:** a route that shares work between rows by a k-local key groups at most 5.4% of random-nibble rows at k = 5, and 0.34% at k = 6. At k ≤ 4 it can group them, but the shared span is then at most 4 symbols. Record 92 adds that a shared word does not share a destination. This is §5.4's "contexts shatter after 4–5 hex digits," now derived.

## 4. Status and tightness

**Status:**
- G1 is **proved,** for 𝓡 and every column.
- G2 and G3 are **proved under model I.** The model is checked: equal-neighbour rate 0.056–0.064 against 0.0625, and width-1 rate 0.876–0.893 against 0.879.
- The price form is **modelled** on i9 measurements.

**Tightness:**
- **Open meets G1 exactly.** It spends one walk step on each width-1 retained run, the minimum the theorem allows. No route in 𝓡 can do fewer operations on those rows. **So within 𝓡 the only lever left is the price per row,** which record 95 found is the emit's work (instructions a row), not latency.
- **The §6.4 analogue, a family where the bound is the whole cost:** text i.i.d. uniform over σ symbols.
  - Then E[h₁] = (1 − 1/σ)² n + O(1), and every route in 𝓡 needs at least r ≈ (1 − 1/σ) n operations.
  - The plain walk's n meets that within a factor σ / (σ − 1). For hex, σ = 16, that is 1.07, and 1.14 against the width-1 bound alone.
  - A GUID-only block is this family with σ = 16.

## 5. What the bound does not cover (the escapes)

1. **The price per operation.** G1 counts operations, not nanoseconds. Fewer instructions per retained row is inside 𝓡 and unbounded by it. That is the lever record 95 left open.
2. **Leaving orbit order:** list ranking or independent gathers, emitting at computed offsets. That spends more total work (Laws 3–4, record 95's footprint).
3. **Non-rigid operations:** grouping non-adjacent rows by a key and computing destinations by rank within the group. G3 bounds the grouping, and the ranks need global counts.
4. **Changing the representation,** §5.3's response 3: storing GUIDs other than as BWT rows. That is a format change, outside a decoder of standard bzip2 streams.
