## 1. The census (C; all 233 Wikidata blocks, 4 prose)

**The program.** `zcensus.rs.txt`, here, computes per block:
- *r:* the runs of L;
- *z:* the number of LZ77 phrases on the block's text. It is the greedy longest previous factor,
  self-reference allowed, with a literal only where a byte is new.
- *How:* suffix array by prefix doubling, LCP by Kasai, the longest-previous-factor array from the
  nearest smaller suffix-array neighbours.
- *Checked:* against a naive parse on prefixes of 300, 2,000 and 5,000 bytes of every 48th block.

D, the distinct composite words the right-end induction makes (by a 61-bit content hash), is from
`stopwalk.rs.txt`, field `distinct`, joined block for block.

|---|---|---|

| D / r (distinct words) | 0.680 / 0.865 / 0.954 (corpus 0.8523) | 0.536 / 0.562 / 0.581 |

## 2. What it bounds (P with the C)

**The bracket.** For a straight-line program of g rules, z ≤ g (Rytter). That holds for LZ77
without self-reference, and the self-referential z counted here is at most that. The induction's
distinct words form an SLP of D rules (§5.6's corollary: equal words have equal Lyndon trees). So,
per block,

  **z ≤ g* ≤ D ≤ 2.43 z** on Wikidata, and ≤ 1.76 z on prose.

**The log factor does not appear here.** The worst-case gap between z and g* is a log factor, but on
these blocks the induction's own grammar, deduplicated, comes within 2.43 of the lower bound.

**What the decoder stores against that bracket.**
- *Cells makes S ≈ 1.84 r words* with duplicates, about 2.15 D: the duplicates are record 14's.
- *Its rules* are the 0.57 r of those longer than 16 bytes.
- *The distance to the floor:* from S to the lower bound z there is a factor of about 4.8 in word
  count. Record 14 measured the part of that from duplicates (D against S); the rest (D against z)
  is a factor of 2.2.
- *That 2.2 is the right end's.* Other orders make fewer words (records 29 and 33: free-first then
  the largest cell makes about 1.28 r), and their distinct counts are not counted here.

## 3. The circular attractor (P, framing)

**The fact.** bzip2's BWT is of the block's rotations, a circular string. For the BWT of a circular
string, the text positions of the r run boundaries form a circular string attractor: every
circular substring has an occurrence crossing one of them (Mantaci, Restivo, Rosone, Sciortino and
Versari, the circular-attractor results for the BWT).

**What follows:** γ_circ ≤ r. With γ ≤ z, the attractor is at most 0.39 r here.

**Why it is only framing.** It explains why r and z are within a constant on these blocks: both
bound the same attractor. It does not change what the decoder computes. The run boundaries are
already the induction's letters.

**No lever is claimed.** This record describes and brackets; it proposes nothing to build.
