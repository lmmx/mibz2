## 1. Substitution powers: absent (C)

**What a power is.** A segment of the path that repeats exactly with period p: the same step type
and the same two letters, step i against step i + p, for at least one full period. That is a
closed loop in the Rauzy diagram run more than once, σ^k for one substitution σ. An accelerated
step would take σ^k in one go.

- *Where powers matter:* periodic blocks, such as `(ab)^k a`, which the step budget refuses.
- *On the corpora:* nothing.

## 3. What a streak is (P)

**The setting.** A is the top order's last letter and B₁, …, B_k the bottom order's last k
letters, last first. A top-wins streak over them happens exactly when λ_{B₁} + … + λ_{B_j} <
λ_A for every j ≤ k.

**Its effect, as one substitution σ_{A;B₁…B_k}:**
- each B_i's word becomes w_A · w_{B_i}, and A's word is unchanged;
- λ_A falls by Σ λ_{B_i};
- each B_i moves, as it loses, to just after A in the bottom order. So the block ends up after A
  reversed: A, B_k, …, B₁.

**So a streak is the bottom order's tail block lying inside A's top interval.** Its k new words
share one left factor, w_A. That is why §5.6's step-before duplicates are 0.175 S of top-wins
steps: consecutive bottom letters carrying one word give equal new words.

**One accelerated step does the same work as the k steps.** It makes k words and moves k letters,
the moves as one block splice. What it saves is the k − 1 re-reads of the winner and the k − 1
step-type decisions, and those are already predictable within a streak.

## 4. What it leaves (Conj)

**Acceleration in the ergodic sense gives the kernel little.** The powers are absent. The
streaks are all one-turn, so the accelerated path makes as many words as the plain one. It only
renames 35% of the steps as parts of longer steps.

**What a streak offers is sharing:** k words with one left factor.
- *The structure:* the "words as lazy tags" design in §6.4 of the Rauzy–Veech entry uses
  exactly this: 1.257 r nodes against 1.81 r cells.
- *Its estimate:* 72 instructions a run for the induction against 116.
- *What it waits on:* an unwritten writer.
- *Where the gain is:* §2's tail says it is concentrated in the long streaks. 45% of streak
  steps lie in streaks of more than 64, where one tag covers hundreds of words.
- *The open mathematics:* that writer. It expands a tag over a block of words that share
  w_A, in output order.
