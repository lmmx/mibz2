# The default window at 4 blocks a thread: registration

Registered before any CI timing of this branch. The decision rule may be amended only in a
commit made before the first bench comment on this PR, and each amendment says what changed
and why.

## The change

The parallel decoder admits a block while fewer than `slots` blocks lie between the scanner
and the reader: read, being decoded, or decoded and not yet read. Where the caller gives no
window (`BzReader::parallel`, `decode_parallel`), `slots` was 2 × threads. It is now
`parallel::SLOTS_PER_THREAD` × threads, with `SLOTS_PER_THREAD = 4`. The occupancy
instrument's default follows the same constant.

Nothing else changes. `decode_block` is untouched, as are the scanner's and the reader's
code and every explicit window.

## Why: the workers wait between blocks

The kernels' instruction cuts have stopped moving 4-thread wall (`2026-11-05-walk-round.md`).
The occupancy instrument shows where 4-thread time goes. Its CI run 41941330659 is P87's head
ee12bb9e, master just before #171, on a Xeon 6975P-C with 4 vCPUs on 2 cores, at window 8:

- the workers decode 75.1% of their time and are idle 23.5%;
- 5.5% of their time is idle while the scanner waits for a slot, behind a block still
  decoding;
- 4.3% is idle behind a block that is ready but not yet taken by the reader;
- 13.0% is idle while the scanner reads the very block the worker needs;
- 7.9% is idle with a job queued but the worker not yet running.

The reader's next block is ready but untaken for 14.4% of the span: a ready block waits
1.98 ms on average, against a 2.19 ms decode. Seven threads share 4 vCPUs (4 workers, the
scanner, the feeder and the reader), so the reader is often not running. The instrument's
own bound on what a wider window can recover is 0.125 of 1.25 worker-seconds, 10%. After
#171, on an EPYC 9V45 (#173's bench), the workers are 62–69% busy.

The scanner is in a slot wait for 65% of the span: 49.2% behind a block still decoding, and
16.0% behind a ready block the reader hasn't taken. Its own work is about an eighth of the
workers' (0.11 s of CPU against their 0.93 s on #175's bench), so it could stay ahead of four
workers if it were allowed to.

**The hypothesis:** a window of 2 × threads stops the scanner from reading ahead. When a
worker comes free there is often no scanned block to take, and it waits while the scanner
reads one (the 13.0%). Sometimes the scanner is also waiting on a reader the scheduler
hasn't run (the 4.3% and 5.5%). With 4 × threads, the scanner keeps scanned blocks queued,
and the workers keep decoding.

## What it costs

From `just bench-ledger --threads 1,2,4,10` at both defaults (`docs/ledger.md`):

| threads | reserved at least, 2t → 4t | at most, before the segments' ratchet |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 2 | 45.4 → 56.2 MB | 70.3 → 84.7 MB |
| 4 | 68.9 → 90.5 MB | 101.0 → 129.8 MB |
| 10 | 139.5 → 193.5 MB | 193.0 → 265.0 MB |

Each extra slot reserves 2.7 MB: 1.8 MB of symbols at the format's bound and 0.9 MB of
output. A Wikidata block writes about 0.2 MB of its symbols, so RSS should rise by less than
the reservation. A block of runs only, which decodes to 46.6 MB, would raise an output
buffer's bound to 93 MB, as before, with twice as many buffers.

Weighed for a laptop of about 16 GB, 20–55 MB is small, if it buys time.

## Predictions

- **1 thread:** no change. The bench's 1-thread arm is `BzReader::new`, the sequential
  reader, so its rows are an A/A control within each run.
- **4 threads:** `wikidata` wall faster, by up to the instrument's 10% of worker time; more
  likely 2–5%, since an idle SMT sibling also speeds its pair.
- **2 threads:** little change. There, the idle time is mostly the scanner using the CPU.
- **Peak RSS** at 2 and 4 threads rises, by at most the ledger's figures.
- **The occupancy run on this PR** (the workflow runs when `examples/occupancy.rs` changes)
  shows the slot-wait share at the new default, lower than at window 8.

## The decision rule

- **Correctness (required):** CI green, Miri included; the same bytes on every corpus in
  every bench run; the trace's ledger tests pass.
- **Benefit (required):** on `wikidata` at 4 threads, the wall change excludes zero on the
  faster side in at least one run, and its point estimate is faster in the other. Only the
  4-thread wall counts here, since the 1-thread rows cannot move.
- **No harm (required):**
  - a wall row flagged slower in a run is a harm signal. One more run on the same head settles
    it: flagged again, it is a harm and the rule fails; not flagged again, it is unresolved and
    reported. At most three runs.
  - Peak RSS at 4 threads must not rise by more than the ledger's reserved increase, 21.6 MB.
- **Power:** the 4-thread wall rows' 95% half-widths run ±2–5%, so a gain under about 2%
  may not show. If no run shows a gain beyond noise, the change is reported as not shown and
  closed.
- **What a pass does not establish:** which of the idle shares the window recovered. Only the
  occupancy runs bear on that, and they are diagnostic. A pass also leaves open whether a
  byte-budgeted window, with the reader's lag bounded by bytes rather than blocks, would do
  better at lower memory.

## Result: merged as #180 (eb5363c2)

Three runs, bench 3.2.1, all on head c90b362 against 17daef2:

| run | runner | `wikidata` wall at 1 / 2 / 4 threads | CPU at 4 threads | peak RSS at 4 threads | flagged slower |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Xeon 8370C | +1.2% / **−12.4%** / **−8.1%** (−10.5, −5.5) | **+6.1%** | 66 → 74 MB | none |
| 2 | EPYC 7763 | −0.1% / **−12.2%** / **−10.3%** (−11.7, −8.9) | **+4.3%** | 69 → 77 MB | `text` 2t +3.0% (+0.7, +5.3) |
| 3 | Xeon 8370C | −0.9% / **−11.5%** / **−8.9%** (−10.5, −7.3) | **+5.7%** | 66 → 74 MB | `onebyte` 4t +2.2% (+0.3, +4.1) |

- **Benefit:** met. The 4-thread wall was faster beyond noise in all three runs.
- **The 1-thread rows** were the A/A control, through the sequential reader. They never moved
  beyond noise.
- **No harm:** none confirmed.
  - `text` at 2 threads was flagged in run 2 and not in run 3 (+1.1%), so it is unresolved.
  - `onebyte` at 4 threads was flagged in run 3 (−6.3% and −0.6% in runs 1 and 2). That was the
    three-run cap, so it is unresolved and reported here.
- **Peak RSS** at 4 threads rose by 8 MB in every run, within the 21.6 MB registered.
- **The trade-off, weighed:** 4-thread CPU rose 4.3% to 6.1%, and 4-thread amplification rose
  4.4% to 6.7%.
  - The workers are busy more of the time, so two decodes share a core's SMT siblings more
    often. Each decode then costs more CPU, and the whole finishes sooner.
  - On a machine with free cores, the wall time is the figure that counts.
  - On one where the decoder shares the cores with other work (wikidata-pq's pipeline), the
    extra CPU is a cost. It should be measured there before a release, as the bench's own
    assessment says.
- **The mechanism, diagnostic only:** this PR's occupancy run, which is unpaired, has the
  4-thread workers decoding 93.0% of their time at window 16. Before #171, at window 8, it was
  75.1% on another runner. The waits for the scanner's read, for a slot and in the handoff are
  each 0–3% of worker time.
- **The stage table** times the sequential path, which this change doesn't touch. Its walk row
  read +5.8% (+0.4, +11.6) in run 2: an A/A flag, chance by construction.
