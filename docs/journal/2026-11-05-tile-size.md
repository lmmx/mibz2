# The room walk's tile at 512 and 1024 offsets: registration

Registered before any CI timing of either arm. The decision rule may be amended only in a
commit made before the first bench comment on either PR, and each amendment says what
changed and why.

## The change

#171 stages each lane's bytes in a tile of `TILE` offsets (256: 8 KiB at 32 lanes) and copies
each lane's row to its buffer when a chunk or a segment ends. Two arms change only that
constant, each on its own PR against master:

- **512** (`perf/tile-512`): a 16 KiB tile.
- **1024** (`perf/tile-1024`): a 32 KiB tile.

The test `room_walk_staged_chunk_edges` (#174) sized its blocks for a 256-offset tile. At
1024 its blocks were too short for a pass to span enough chunks, and it failed ("the queue
running out at a later chunk's first offset" never reached). Its block size and segment
count now scale with `TILE`, so a pass spans as many chunks at any size. At 256 the test is
unchanged. Every room-walk test passes at both sizes, and clippy and fmt are clean.

## Prior evidence: local only, and weak

From the walk round on #171's head 23011895 (`2026-11-05-walk-round.md`), on the shared VM: a
Cascade Lake Xeon with a 32 KiB 8-way L1D, a 1 MiB L2, no SMT and no PMU.

- **Instructions are flat.** `decode_block` Ir is +0.05% at 512 and +0.04% at 1024 against
  256, on blocks 1, 41, 233 and 400. `spec_offset` is the same 237 instructions at every
  size, with only its store displacements changed.
  - Chunk ends fall (memcpy calls on block 1: 5,055 at 256, 3,455 at 512, 2,879 at 1024).
  - glibc's copy is cheapest per byte at exactly 256 bytes, which cancels that saving.
- **Wall time,** whole-file `wikidata-independent.bz2`, 62 paired rounds pooled over two arm
  orders, against 256:

  | arm | 1 thread | 4 threads |
  |---|---|---|
  | 512 | −1.86% (−3.12, −0.58) | −1.97% (−3.61, −0.40) |
  | 1024 | −0.58% (−1.91, +0.87) | −3.16% (−4.42, −1.95) |

- **An independent check** reproduced the arithmetic and the instruction counts, and found
  no soundness issue. It also found the evidence weaker than it looks:
  - only 1024 at 4 threads replicated cleanly in both orders;
  - per-run means of the same comparison moved by up to 2.6 points, so the setup does not
    resolve 1–2%;
  - 512 shifts every hot function by 0x20–0x30 bytes against 256, so a code-layout effect of
    1–2% can't be separated from the tile's;
  - no mechanism is identified.

## The risk this VM can't test

CI's 4-thread runs put two walks on each core's SMT siblings, which share one L1D. On the
EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) that L1D is 32 KiB:
- at 256, two tiles take 16 KiB;
- at 512, two tiles take 32 KiB, the whole L1D;
- at 1024, they take 64 KiB, twice the L1D.

On Zen 5 and the newer Xeons the L1D is 48 KiB. #171's gain was largest at 4 threads under
SMT, so this is where a larger tile can lose. The prediction, registered here: if a larger
tile harms, it harms at 4 threads on the 7763 first, and not at 1 thread.

## The decision rule

Each PR is read on its own, against master, under #171's rule:

- **Correctness (required):** CI green, Miri included; the same bytes on every corpus in
  every bench run.
- **Instructions:** not a criterion beyond the measured flatness above. The arms differ from
  master in one constant.
- **Benefit (required):** on `wikidata` at 1 or 4 threads, the CPU change or the wall change
  excludes zero on the faster side in at least one run. The same figure's point estimate
  must be faster in the other run.
- **No harm (required):** a wall row flagged slower in a run is a harm signal. One more run
  on the same head settles it: flagged again, it is a harm and the rule fails; not flagged
  again, it is reported as unresolved. At most three runs per arm.
- **Two arms:** trying two arms makes a pass by chance about twice as likely as with one.
  - A pass resting on a single run's flag is confirmed by a third run on the same head before
    merging. The figure that passed must have a faster point estimate there too.
  - If both arms pass, the one whose runs' mean `wikidata` 4-thread wall change is larger
    merges, and the other is closed. A tie, within 0.5 points, goes to 512, the smaller L1
    footprint.
- **Power:** the local gain, about 2%, is at the 4-thread rows' 95% half-width (±2–5%). If
  no run shows a gain beyond noise, the arm is reported as not shown and closed.
