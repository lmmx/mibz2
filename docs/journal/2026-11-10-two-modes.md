# 2026-11-10: The i9's two modes: what the bundle rules out, and a probe that needs no counters

**Status: a reading of an archived bundle, five mechanisms ranked, and a design for telling them
apart. No new run. The design is handed to the Coordinator.**

- **The question** (the Coordinator's, after #311): at 10 threads the same decode takes either
  about 0.15 s or about 0.30 s on the i9, with every thread's CPU time grown alike. What is it,
  and what design is robust to it?
- **Read from the bundle:** the slow mode is not heat at the package sensor, not the gap between
  runs, not the state of the CPUs before a run, not the arm, and not the clock the cores report
  at a run's end. It is a property that persists from run to run: a fast run follows a fast run
  52% of the time, against 21% by chance.
- **So the bundle cannot name it.** Every remaining mechanism slows all threads alike. They
  differ in things the harness does not record.
- **The design:** a spin probe timed against the invariant TSC, which measures a core's
  effective clock, stop-clock throttling included, with no performance counter; and four
  unprivileged readings beside it. One run of the existing harness with them separates the five
  mechanisms.
- **Robust whatever it is:** three properties of the decoder, in §5.

Nothing in the decoder changes.

Labels: **read** (from the archived bundle, by the short script in §Reproduce), **model**
(a mechanism's predicted signature, not observed).

## 1. What the bundle shows (read)

`run-fed-leader/20261109T201013Z` on the `evidence` branch: 140 runs at 10 threads, seven arms,
20 rounds, one process a run. `service-guarded/20261109T195345Z`, 17 minutes earlier on the same
machine: 60 runs at 10 threads, all between 0.126 and 0.158 s.

| | fast (29) | slow (111) |
|---|--:|--:|
| wall, median | 0.159 s | 0.306 s |
| CPU, median | 1.08 s | 2.25 s |
| package temperature before → after | 35 → 41 °C | 34 → 40 °C |
| gap since the previous run's exit | 0.260 s | 0.260 s |
| CPUs above 900 MHz before the run (mean of 20) | 0.59 | 0.52 |
| CPUs above 1.5 GHz after the run (median) | 13 | 14 |
| their clock after the run (median) | 4.90 GHz | 4.90 GHz |
| physical cores with both siblings hot after (median) | 4 | 4 |

- **Two modes and nothing between:** no run took between 0.20 and 0.26 s.
- **Every arm has both:** 2 to 6 fast runs of 20 in each of the seven.
- **Not the package's temperature:** 41 °C at most.
- **Not the gap:** 0.256 to 0.264 s before every run. The earlier, all-fast bundle ran a
  different harness configuration (no `--sysfs-state`); its gaps were not recorded.
- **Not where the run starts:** before both kinds, all twenty CPUs are idle at 800 MHz
  (`powersave`, `balance_performance`, intel_pstate).
- **Not the clock at the end:** the busy CPUs report 4.9 GHz after both kinds. 16 runs of 140
  end below 4.8 GHz (2.7 to 4.7), 7 of them fast.
- **It persists.** In run order the fast runs come as F or FFF: streaks of 3, 1, 3, 1, 1, 1, 1,
  1, 3, 3, 3, 1, 3, 4. Fifteen of 29 fast runs are followed by a fast one. Independent draws at
  29/140 would give 6.
- **What `scaling_cur_freq` is here:** the ratio of two counters that both stop when a core is
  halted or stop-clocked. So "4.9 GHz at the end" does not show the cores ran at full duty.

## 2. Five mechanisms, and what each predicts (model)

All five slow every thread alike and leave CPU ÷ wall unchanged, which is what #311 found.

| | mechanism | fits the persistence? | its signature, unrecorded so far |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **the governor's ramp:** each run starts on cores at 800 MHz, and the hardware's P-state control sometimes holds them low | possible | the cores' clock low for much of the run, read *during* it; a spin probe on the decoder's CPUs slow, one on an idle core fast |
| H2 | **sibling packing:** twelve threads placed on six cores' two siblings each | weakly: each process is placed afresh | thread placement sampled during the run: most threads with a busy sibling; a spin probe on a free core fast |
| H3 | **stop-clock throttling:** the cores run at half duty (PROCHOT from the board's regulators, or ACPI T-states), at full reported clock | yes: regulators heat and cool over seconds | a spin probe on an *idle* core slow by the same factor; `thermal_throttle/*_throttle_count` rising; reported clock normal |
| H4 | **huge pages:** a run gets 2 MiB pages or 4 KiB ones, as the last run's freed blocks allow | yes | `thp_fault_alloc` rising in fast runs only; minor faults ~20,000 against ~100; system time up |
| H5 | **the package power limit:** the running-average limit clamps the next run | yes | `*_power_limit_count` rising; the clock reduced during the run; a spin probe on an idle core slow |

- **Against H4 as the whole story:** the record found huge pages worth little on the walk
  (`2026-10-27-compute-frontier.md`), and 78 MB of faults is tens of milliseconds, not 1.2
  CPU-seconds. The scanner, whose working set is small, slows as much as anything.
- **Against H2 alone:** the scanner's CPU grows 2.3× in the mean and more in single runs. Two
  threads on one core cannot each lose much more than half.
- **For a core-speed mechanism** (H1, H3 or H5): the compute-bound threads slow slightly more
  (scanner 2.33×, main 2.36×) than the memory-bound helpers (2.20×), as a clock does.
- **Which of the three, the bundle cannot say.** They differ in whether an *uninvolved* core is
  slow too, and in counters nobody read.

## 3. The probe (design)

**A software effective-clock meter.** A thread pinned to one CPU runs a fixed loop of register
arithmetic with no memory access, and every 2²⁰ iterations stores `rdtsc` and a sequence number
into a preallocated ring. The TSC is invariant, so iterations per TSC tick are the core's
effective clock times its duty: frequency scaling lowers it, stop-clock throttling lowers it, a
busy sibling lowers it by the sibling's share. It needs no privilege and no counter. At 5 GHz it
samples about every 0.2 ms.

**Two of them:**
- **P-idle,** on a CPU of a physical core the scheduler leaves alone (its mask is taken from
  the decoder by the parent before the child starts, and read back);
- **P-sib,** on the sibling of that same core, started only in a second pass, to price what a
  busy sibling costs the first probe on this machine: the calibration H2 needs.

**Beside them, per run, all world-readable:**
- **placement:** each decoder thread's last CPU (`/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/stat`, field 39) every
  2 ms from the harness's existing sampler: the share of samples in which a thread's sibling
  CPU also held a decoder thread;
- **the clock during the run:** `scaling_cur_freq` of the CPUs the threads were seen on, every
  10 ms, not before and after;
- **throttle and power-limit events:** `cpu*/thermal_throttle/core_throttle_count`,
  `package_throttle_count`, `core_power_limit_count`, `package_power_limit_count`, before and
  after;
- **paging:** the child's `ru_minflt`, `ru_utime` and `ru_stime` from `wait4`, and
  `thp_fault_alloc` and `thp_fault_fallback` from `/proc/vmstat`, before and after;
- **static facts, once:** `transparent_hugepage/enabled` and `defrag`; the RAPL constraints'
  limits and windows under `powercap/intel-rapl:0` where readable.

**The run:** the harness and gaps that produced the two modes, one arm (the default plan), 60
runs, with the probe running throughout. No decoder change, no setting changed on the host.

**First, and with no probe at all:** one long decode, `wikidata.bz2` fifty times end to end
(10.5 GB decoded), its throughput read in 100 ms windows. Steady at the fast rate, steady at the
slow one, or alternating: `2026-11-10-by-data-class.md` §6 gives what each means.

**The table it yields:** each run classed fast or slow by its wall as before, and each reading's
median by class. The mechanisms separate as follows.

| reading | H1 ramp | H2 packing | H3 stop-clock | H4 pages | H5 power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-idle's rate in slow runs | full | full | **half** | full | **reduced** |
| the decoder CPUs' clock during slow runs | **low, then rising** | full | full | full | **reduced** |
| threads with a busy sibling | same | **most** | same | same | same |
| throttle counts rise | no | no | **yes** (core or package) | no | no |
| power-limit counts rise | no | no | no | no | **yes** |
| `thp_fault_alloc` in fast runs only | no | no | no | **yes** | no |
| system time's share grows | no | no | no | **yes** | no |

- **If no column matches,** the result is a sixth mechanism's description, which is still more
  than the record holds.
- **It closes nothing about throughput.** It names the mode, so that timed registrations on that
  harness can be designed around it, as the Researcher asked before any third attempt.
- **Limits:** one machine; the probe occupies one core in every run of the diagnostic, so its
  walls are not comparable with other bundles'; a probe on an idle core cannot see a mechanism
  that acts per core.

## 4. Why it matters beyond the harness (model)

- **H1, H2 and H4 belong to short, fresh processes.** A decode of a large dump ramps once,
  spreads once and faults once. If the mode is one of these, the i9's short-run figures
  understate nothing about long decodes, and the harness needs back-to-back decodes in one
  process, or a warm-up, to measure what users of large files get.
- **H3 and H5 belong to the machine under sustained load.** A long decode would then live in
  the slow mode, and every figure taken from 0.15 s bursts overstates it. Then the quantity
  that sets throughput is work per byte under a power budget, not threads.
- **Either way the registered comparisons on that harness mix two machines.** Paired ratios
  survive a shared mode and not a switch within a pair, which is what failed fidelity twice.

## 5. What is robust whichever it is (design properties, not built)

1. **Less CPU per byte is throughput under H3 and H5, and costs nothing under the others.**
   Waits that spin (2,000 to 4,096 iterations before parking) spend the budget the walk needs.
   The service-core plan's 14.7% less CPU and 3 × 3's 11% less are worth more there than their
   burst walls say.
2. **Own the placement under H2:** one decoder thread a physical core, by affinity, where the
   topology can be read and the process is not already confined. The record's E6 measured
   packed against spread at 4 × 2: −23%.
3. **Small, reused working sets under H4 and H1:** a table of 0.8 bytes a row
   (`2026-11-10-backward-table.md`, design C) faults and ramps a fifth of what 5 bytes a row
   does; buffers kept across decodes in one process fault once.
4. **A thread budget by input size:** a 0.15 s decode on ten cores pays ten ramps and ten
   stacks of first faults. Below some size, fewer threads finish sooner. The README's level-1
   slowdown is a neighbour of this.

## Reproduce

The readings of §1, from the bundle on the `evidence` branch:

```text
git show origin/evidence:run-fed-leader/20261109T201013Z.tar.gz | tar -xzf -
python3 - <<'PY'
import json, statistics as st
rows = [json.loads(l) for l in open("20261109T201013Z/runs.jsonl")]
rows = sorted((r for r in rows if r["threads"] == 10 and not r["err"]), key=lambda r: r["t_spawn_unix_ns"])
print("".join("F" if r["wall"] < 0.21 else "s" for r in rows))
for fast in (True, False):
    g = [r for r in rows if (r["wall"] < 0.21) == fast]
    hot = lambda r: [v for v in r["machine_post"]["cpu_khz"].values() if v > 1_500_000]
    print(len(g), st.median(r["wall"] for r in g), st.median(r["cpu"] for r in g),
          st.median(r["machine_pre"]["pkg_temp_c"] for r in g), st.median(r["machine_post"]["pkg_temp_c"] for r in g),
          st.median(len(hot(r)) for r in g), st.median(st.median(hot(r)) for r in g))
PY
```
