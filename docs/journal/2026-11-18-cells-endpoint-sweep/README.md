## Event storage and construction

Treat offset 0 in a group as row-end coordinate `64*page+1`. A positive displacement posts a bottom event. Top events include the diagonals, which are distinguished by their zero distance. Two occupancy masks permit one event on each side at the same coordinate. Each visit chooses the greatest set bit in their union. A newborn's key is strictly smaller than the present frontier, so the active group only gains events below that bit. A visit to an empty group also incurs the test. The block size of 64 comes from a single 64-bit mask. No size was fitted to traces.

* **Grouped packets.** Each packet stores the 64-bit word, unsigned 32-bit distance and 32-bit route. Entering a future group reads its head, follows every route once, fills the 128 local indices and sets the two local masks. The route stores `((next+1)<<7) | (side<<6) | offset` with zero high bits at list end. The production row bound leaves room for the index. Each popped payload is copied to its active register pair before the slot is reused. A tie offers two slots and only one child needs one. Retire the surplus index permanently. No free list, search for a slot, ordering of a chain or clearing of the local index array is required. Masks control which local indices are valid. A future head is zeroed once during setup and never reused after its group is drained. On a short marked period pending chains may be abandoned.
* **Direct slots.** Use an interleaved array of two packed 12-byte payloads per coordinate, requiring 4-byte-aligned 64-bit access on one parity. Only the two masks per group need initial clearing. A later group update incurs a mask read and write. The current masks can remain in registers. The address tracer includes the cache lines crossed by unaligned payloads. This is a priced comparison. The grouped schedule is the independently executed transducer.
* **Setup.** Coalesce adjacent capped records and isolate the primary exactly as the source does. Original letters in stable top order have both endpoint ranks computable from running byte-row totals. The ledger charges the row-histogram increment on each physical record and the per-letter stable cursor read/update. It keeps the original per-run letter census to preserve gates. Capped-record reads, their decoding/validation, primary-run discovery, literal table, output buffer and fixed canaries are common costs outside the difference. The row totals inside `Runs` are private and the production entry takes a slice of records. The model pays for them again. Ordinary bounds suffice for valid bzip2 input. Malformed input first passes through the existing validation. [Pinned run entry](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/3502dd9bbc75cee4feadb5cdebd487a5b937c822/src/cells.rs#L197-L215)
* **Footprint.** The frozen eager and lazy figures measure used geometry, ignoring constant pads, cursors and common grammar storage. Lazy touched 16,213 slot pairs and used $5r+28\cdot16{,}213$ bytes. Its production layout reserves room for $r$ pairs. Grouped storage includes 128 local 32-bit indices and two masks. Direct storage reports array capacity, with two additional live mask registers. `lines` counts actually touched lines across setup, geometry and grammar. Neither cold allocation nor OS page zeroing is priced. Cold allocation of the direct array can be particularly expensive. A linked drain, in-register masks, several active operands, and dependent loads share processor resources with word construction.

## Pricing and its limits

[traffic.py](traffic.py) replays field addresses on each full-period Wikidata tape. The eager map follows the promised slots and current bottom-to-top pointers. Lazy distinguishes raw and slotted refills against the recorded `s_fresh` bits, visits each original header once on each side, and uses paired allocation and a LIFO free list. In the top order, an original right endpoint is the boundary where control enters that original's stream entry, even when the entry is already tagged. Subsequent arrivals inside the original go through slots. The same holds on the bottom. The model retains both 16-byte reads and both 16-byte writes of a short join, and the two 8-byte stores of a long rule. Array bases are normalised to disjoint, line-aligned regions. For projected addresses the tape supplies the clock, while the grouped directory checks its own clock and returned packet IDs against it.

In `traffic.csv`, each source field read/write counts once at its actual width. `core_r/core_w` exclude `word_r/word_w` and `hint_r`. `hint_r` counts demand reads forming eager prefetch addresses. `hint_count` counts the hints. Their target lines earn neither a free hit nor a compulsory fill. `setup_bytes/core_bytes` cover just the eponymous field categories. The two cache models are separate fully associative LRU caches with 64-byte lines, write-allocate, dirty eviction and a final dirty drain. Neither models a hardware hierarchy, multiple workers, a prefetcher, TLBs, load forwarding or overlap. A recently written field can cost one resident access with no line transfer at all. The models skip allocator/reserve clearance and fixed setup outside the listed arrays. [Per-block traffic](traffic.csv)

[ledger.py](ledger.py) prices a disclosed scalar schedule as a sensitivity check. One resident unit means a field operation, integer operation, select, combined predicate/conditional transfer, or highest-set-bit operation. Ordinary indexed-address scaling and register moves are free. The sheet charges explicit index packing, mask work and prefetch instructions. Instruction selection, vector loads, branch fusion, alignment, register allocation and stalls can change this balance. Counts of scalar primitives in this schedule are assumptions about lowering. The event multiplicities driving them are exact.

Let $r$ be initial letters, $m$ physical capped records, $C$ cuts, $E,T,B$ the equal/top/bottom counts, $D$ closures and $S$ short constructions. On the priced tapes $D=1$ and the period is full. Put $G=E+D$, $H_t=G+B$, $H_b=G+T$. Both shipped kernels refill their top $H_t$ times and bottom $H_b$ times. The event model consumes $I=r+C=H_t+H_b-D$ packets. Let $M=\lceil n/64\rceil$, $J$ be remote packets including originals and $K$ be posts into the current group.

On a model that charges an additional $p$ units per 256 KiB transfer, the score is `setup_core + p*transfers256`. The break-even numbers in the journal use summed counts over each arm's admitted population. They are exchange rates between model units and line transfers. On frozen, grouped saves 89,180 transfers against eager and only 4,016 against lazy. At 32 KiB the respective savings are 130,041 and 93,926.
