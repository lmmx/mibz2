# P74 Gate 1: the result (registered verdict: mixed)

The measurement registered in `2026-10-30-p74-gate1-preregistration.md` (frozen at
0e3ca63), run on lmmx/mibz2#118 at head cfeb7aa against base ae942bd: CI run 41077291982
on an AMD EPYC 9V74 runner, 10 paired rounds × 2 passes.

**The microarchitecture, corrected:** the runner is an AMD EPYC 9V74, a Zen 4 part. The
pre-registration's "Zen 3" assumed CI's earlier EPYC 7763. Every figure below is measured
on this Zen 4 runner only, not on the i9 or any laptop CPU. Production code is unchanged.

**The records intervals are wide because of two rounds.** In rounds 2 and 6 the shipped arm

- **The other 8 rounds** (description only; the registered figure keeps every round):

  - each range is within ±0.5 points.
- So records is a consistent 10–19% regression. The outliers made P74 look better there,
  not worse.

## The rules, applied

- **Wikidata (the checkpoint kernel, the better arm: P74 free):**
  - a 33.2% gain against the 5% threshold;
  - the interval excludes zero;
  - more than twice the layout noise of 1.4%.

  **Met.**
- **Controls** (the better arm's upper bounds; rule ≤ +3%): random +9.4% plain and +9.0%
  checkpoints; records +23.0% and +23.5%. **Not met.**
- **Verdict: mixed.** The rules' next step is the one selector arm, but under the
  authorisation it waits on the maintainer's decision. It is not started.

## Correctness, disassembly, the decoder's own figures

- **Correctness:**
  - every block's output was checked against the shipped kernel's on every pass of every
    round, and no run reported an error;
  - `tests/mtf_p74.rs` passes on CI's `rust` job, as do all the other checks.
- **Disassembly:** the head stays in a ymm register on the one-shuffle and cross-lane
  paths. One defect was found and fixed before the measurement: LLVM had turned the
  checkpoint kernels' lower tail chunks into a `memmove` call. The protocol allowed that
  one fix.
- **The decoder** is unchanged, as expected: no wall, CPU or RSS change on Wikidata at 1,
  2 or 4 threads (every interval holds no change).
  - One flag, records at 2 threads, +2.6% (+0.3%, +4.9%), is on code the PR doesn't
    change. With 21 rows, about one chance flag a run is expected.

## What it means, and what it doesn't

- **On Wikidata the register-held kernel is a third faster at the stage boundary,** with
  an interval of ±0.2 points.
  - Scaled to the stage table (MTF + RLE2, 17.1% of one-core decode time, 2.20 c/B),
    33% is about 0.73 c/B, or about 5.7% of a one-core decode. That is at the top of the
    pre-registered 3–6% one-core prediction.
  - **Not measured:** the arms are timed on their own, block after block, not inside the
    decode, and no full-decoder figure is part of Gate 1.
- **The branch-free arm beats the branching one** by 11–14 points on Wikidata. That is
  consistent with the misprediction model of §3.2e(e), which predicted a 16% mispredict
  rate costing about as much as the one-shuffle path saves. It is consistent, not proof:
  no counters were read.
- **On the controls P74 is 9–19% slower.** They are 76% (random) and 90% (records)
  high-rank fallbacks, against about 1.6% on Wikidata, measured from the corpora's own
  blocks.
  - Which part of the fallback costs it is not attributed: the chunked tail shift,
    failed forwarding between close fallbacks, or the dispatch (open point 6).
  - The verdict is on this combined kernel, not on register-held MTF as such.
- **Microarchitecture:** Zen 4 (EPYC 9V74) only, not the i9 (Comet Lake) or any laptop
  CPU.

## The selector arm: the result (registered verdict: negative, so P74 closes)

The arm was frozen in the pre-registration ("The selector arm", 50ff284) and run once on
lmmx/mibz2#118 at e57400e: CI run 41081427181, 10 paired rounds × 2 passes. All other
checks passed.

**A different runner.** This run's runner was an **AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5)**, not the first
run's 9V74 (Zen 4). The shipped kernel took 0.427 ns/B there, against 0.807. So the two runs'
figures are each a within-run comparison, and neither carries over to the other.

**The figures** (registered statistic; every arm in the same binary and run):

|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Wikidata | plain | 0.418 | −8.9% (−9.5, −8.3) | −20.4% (−21.1, −19.7) | −8.4% (−9.8, −7.0) |
| Wikidata | checkpoints | 0.427 | −10.6% (−11.2, −10.1) | −19.2% (−19.6, −18.8) | **−7.8% (−8.5, −7.1)** |
| random | plain | 5.526 | +22.0% (+8.7, +35.4) | +10.0% (−2.1, +22.1) | **+7.1% (+6.2, +8.0)** |
| random | checkpoints | 5.514 | +30.0% (+26.4, +33.6) | +20.3% (+17.0, +23.6) | **+7.2% (+6.8, +7.6)** |
| records | plain | 6.429 | +25.4% (+24.8, +26.1) | +20.9% (+20.3, +21.5) | **+6.2% (+5.8, +6.5)** |

**The selector's choices:**

|---|--:|--:|

On Wikidata, the 15 blocks sent to the shipped kernel hold about 3.6 KB of L.

**The frozen rule, applied:**
- **Wikidata (the checkpoint kernel):** a 7.8% gain against the 5% threshold, an interval
  excluding zero, and more than twice the layout noise of 3.1%. **Met.**
- **Controls (upper bounds, rule ≤ +3%):** random +8.0% and +7.6%, records +6.5% and
  +6.4%. **Not met.**
- **Verdict: negative.** Under the frozen rule P74 closes, for this implementation. That
  waits on the maintainer's review: the PR isn't yet closed or commented on.

**Why the selector fails** (from the figures and the machine code):
- **The selection was right everywhere.** It sent every Wikidata byte to P74 and every
  random and records block to the shipped kernel.
- **The count costs about 0.4–0.5 ns per symbol.**
  - On random and records it reads about one symbol per output byte, adding about
    0.39 ns/B (5.92 against 5.52 on random): the +6–7%.
  - On Wikidata it reads far fewer symbols per byte, but still takes 0.34 → 0.39 ns/B
    off the free arm's gain (−19.2% → −7.8%).
- **The disassembly** (the local build of the same source): the count is inlined and
  vectorised only for baseline SSE2, with 64-bit accumulators. That is about 30
  instructions for every four symbols, roughly 2 cycles a symbol.
  - The function isn't compiled with AVX2 enabled, and the u64 sums force widening
    shuffles.
  - A count with AVX2 and 32-bit sums (or counts kept by the scanner) would likely cost
    several times less. **That is a hypothesis only:** the frozen protocol allows no
    further push, and testing it would need its own authorisation.

**The shipped kernel's bimodal timings recur.** In one round on random (plain) and one on
records (checkpoints), the shipped kernel ran about 1.8× slow. The selector arm, which runs
the shipped kernel on those corpora, was slow in the same rounds. So the anomaly belongs to
the shipped kernel. One explanation fits (not tested): its stack-resident list 4K-aliasing
the output buffer. P74's register-held fast path has no such list.

**What stands after Gate 1:**
- **The register-held kernel (free arm) is 19–33% faster** than the shipped kernel on
  Wikidata's MTF stage, on both Zen 4 and Zen 5 runners. It is correct for every alphabet
  size, and its fast path keeps the head in a register.
- **It is 9–20% slower on high-rank inputs** (random, records).
- **The registered per-block selector protects the controls' selection** but not their
  cost. Its count, as compiled, costs more than the 3% limit allows.
- **Under the frozen rules this P74 implementation closes.** This is not a verdict on
  register-held MTF in general, nor on a cheaper selector, which is untested.
