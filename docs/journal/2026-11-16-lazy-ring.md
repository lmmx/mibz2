## What it is

A cell's word is its two factors, A's word then B's. Two cells with the same two factors
make the same word. MONODROMY counted that such cells lie close together in the
induction's order (`monodromy/2026-11-15-equal-words.md`), and a ring of the last eight
words finds a third of them.

- **The key** of a word is its two factors' offsets, 32 bits each.
- **The ring** (`Ring`, 128 bytes on the stack) holds the last eight words made: each one's
  key, its key folded to 32 bits, and its offset.
- **Every cell** looks its folded key up in all eight at once (two SSE2 compares), then
  compares the key itself at the first match. A match takes the earlier word's offset;
  the bytes or the rule just written are written over by the next word.
- **A word made with the walk's row is neither looked up nor kept.** Its mark is in its
  length, not in its key, so an unmarked cell of the same two offsets is another word.
- **The oldest of the eight gives way** to each new cell.

It is the scratch of `2026-11-15-shared-ring8.md` moved to the lazy kernel, with one
change: that one used AVX2 on 64-bit keys. This one folds the key to 32 bits and checks
the key on a match, so it needs only SSE2, which every x86-64 has, and no run-time
dispatch. Elsewhere, and under Miri, the same search is a loop.

**It is a bench arm, not the default:** `bwt::RingLazyCells`, the arm
`mibz2-workers-lazy-ring`, chosen by a compile-time parameter of the induction, as the
fetch-ahead's arm is (`2026-11-16-lazy-fetch-ahead.md`; this branch is stacked on that
one). `just bench-lazy-ring` runs it against `mibz2-workers-lazy` at 4, 8, 9 and 10
threads. No cargo feature and no user-facing switch.

**What does not change:** the cells and their order; the slots, the streams and the pools;
the plan's kernel. Only which words are kept differs, so the writer has fewer to expand.

## What it saves, and what it costs

Wikidata's 196 admitted blocks (`examples/lever_counts.rs`), per letter:

| | the plan's lazy kernel | with the ring |
|---|--:|--:|
| words kept as bytes | 6.48 B | 4.25 B |
| words kept as rules | 9.15 B | 6.90 B |
| **words** | **15.62 B** | **11.15 B (−28.6%)** |
| all the kernel touches beside the output | 29.5 B | 25.0 B (−15%) |

| instructions (callgrind, this branch) | the plan's lazy kernel | with the ring |
|---|--:|--:|
| induction | 201.7 | 277.8 (+38%) |
| writer | 49.5 | 38.7 (−22%) |
| whole decoder, whole file | 5.569 G | 6.294 G (+13%) |
| memory reads and writes, induction | 81.0 | 101.8 |

- **The saving is the scratch's, to the digit:** 11.15 B a letter. The folded search finds
  what the 64-bit one did.
- **The cost is the largest of any arm so far.** Lazy letters themselves cost a quarter
  more kernel instructions for 32% fewer bytes. The ring costs 38% more induction
  instructions for 15% fewer.
- **The induction's count is not a gate** (`2026-11-16-lazy-overhead.md`): the plan's
  kernel counts 181 on master, 198 on the fetch-ahead branch and 202 here, for the same
  source. The difference between the two arms on one build is the figure to read.

**My expectation, stated before the data:** a loss or a wash at 8 to 10 threads. The
dose says there is pressure to buy down; the ring buys less of it per instruction than
lazy letters did.

## Safety (RULES B5)

The ring is safe code but for its search: two aligned SSE2 loads of the ring's own first
32 bytes (`Ring` is aligned to 64), compared and reduced to a mask. No new raw-pointer
read or write of the kernel's buffers is added. What changes for the existing unsafe code:

1. **A word's offset may be an earlier word's.** A slot or a rule then names a word made
   up to eight cells before. That word was kept: its bytes lie below `top`, or its rule
   below `rp`, and neither is ever written again, since both only grow.
2. **The bytes or rule written for a shared cell are not kept.** `top` and `rp` do not
   advance, so the next word is written over them. The writes themselves are the same
   in-bounds writes as before (16 bytes at `top`, or a rule at `rp`); the regions' ends are
   checked as before.
3. **A shared word is the same word.** Equal keys are equal factor offsets, and a word is
   a function of its two factors. The mark is the one thing a word carries that its key
   does not, and marked cells are excluded on both sides: never looked up, and kept with
   a key no word has.
4. **A folded match is not trusted.** The key itself is compared at the matched place.

## Evidence

**Tests** (24 kernel tests; 25 with `memory-lever`):
- `cells_lazy_ring_exhaustive_arbitrary_columns`: every binary column to length 10 at every
  row, and two long ones, against the plain walk; the same cells as the plan's kernel, one
  for one, and never more words.
- `miri_cells_lazy_ring_keeps_fewer_words` (Miri-listed): repeating columns at four rows
  each, buffers reused; fewer words kept; a budget of exactly the cells made accepted and
  one less refused.
- `cells_lazy_ring_folded_keys`: two different keys of one folded value are found at one
  place and are not equal; the first of several matches is taken; an empty ring matches
  nothing.
- `tests/common/cells.rs` holds the arm, and its forced route, to `Cells`' output, errors
  and routes, so the Cells fuzz does: 18,707 cases in 60 seconds, seed 42.

**Corpus:** Wikidata decodes to the same bytes from `-lazy`, `-lazy-ring` and `-lazy-hint`
at 1 and 8 threads.

**Miri:** CI's job reads this head: the ring's Miri-listed test is in its list. A local run on the 14 kernel tests was still going when this was written.

**Mutation** (`2026-11-16-lazy-ring/mutants.tsv`, script `mutate.py.txt`; debug profile):
12 mutants, 11 killed by the tests: a marked word shared or kept in the ring, a folded
match taken without its key, a key of one factor alone, a shared word's offset not taken,
its bytes still advanced, the ring keeping the wrong place or no key, the search reading
four of the eight, and the two kernels' inductions exchanged either way.
