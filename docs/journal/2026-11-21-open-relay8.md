# Eight emitters per worker, cuts on edges: the Wormhole line's Relay8, ported to 0.5.10

The Wormhole line's round-2 candidate, `DietRelay8Cells` (cycles 8–10 on
`research/carbon-wormhole`, `docs/journal/2026-11-21-wormhole-cycle{8,9,10}-*.md`), was built
and measured in a safe-Rust model of the dieted open kernel (`DietMu1Cells`), against 0.5.9.
Master is 0.5.10, which ships the diet (#692). This port puts the cycle's emit into master's
own walk (`src/open/relay.rs`, called from `emit` in `src/open.rs`). The construction, the
tables and the routing are unchanged. It adds no allocator change, no `unsafe` and no heap.

## What was ported

`emit` takes the relay when the codes are narrow (no `extra`) and the block has at least 1,024
retained rows. Every other walk, and any walk the relay hands back, is master's serial walk
unchanged.

- **Zero-bit cuts** (cycle 8, Theorem 8.1). A retained row `j` is a cut when
  `(j ^ mark) & (2^shift - 1) == 0`, with `shift = bits - 8`. No bit of `edges` is used, and the
  marked row is always a cut. There are 128 to 256 cuts. The cycle's lower bound of
  2^(k-1) + 1 holds only when the mark's low bits are 0: at m = 1,025 and low bits 7 there are
  128 (`cut_placement`).
- **The relay** (cycle 9, Theorem 9.2). Lane 0 emits from the mark while lanes 1..8 scout
  unclaimed cuts, keeping only each segment's bytes and steps. When lane 0 meets a cut:
  - a scouted cut becomes a task, and lane 0 jumps past it;
  - a cut being scouted hands its scouted part over as a task, and lane 0 resumes at the
    scout's row;
  - an unclaimed cut is emitted through.
  Then eight emitters run the tasks.
- **Post-slop restoration** (cycle 9, Theorem 9.3). Every lane copies short words as 16
  unconditional bytes. At the end, the first 16 bytes of every piece after the first are
  rewritten exactly, walking from the piece's row.

## How the port differs from the cycle's model

- **The layout is master's.** Edges are `(code << bits) | next` with the diet's `extra` for
  wide codes, as the cycle's "Diet" assumed. Wide blocks walk serially, as in the cycle.
- **No `word_len` table, no padded dictionary.** The cycle added a `u16` length per code
  (+7 KiB) and padded `inline16`, `lens` and `word_len` to a power of two. The port reads a
  scout's lengths from `lens`, and from `offsets` for a word longer than 16 (a cold branch).
  So the heap is master's exactly. The emit's state is about 10 KiB of stack: the cut states,
  the segments, the tasks and the boundaries.
- **Tasks carry their steps.** A scout knows its segment's steps as well as its bytes, so the
  emitters run in step for as many steps as the shortest task has left. They make no per-step
  end check (the cycle checks `at >= end` every step). The one task cut short by `n` is
  emitted alone afterwards. This took 3–4% off the emit.
- **Exactly the serial walk's bytes, or the serial walk.** The kernel's constructions are
  permutations of the retained rows. For any other map, the relay returns `None` wherever the
  serial walk could differ:
  - the walk passes the serial walk's `m`-step bound;
  - lane 0 meets a cut twice;
  - the scouts walk more than `m` steps in all;
  - the walk is empty.
  `emit` then walks serially. So `emit`'s bytes are the serial walk's on every input, not only
  on BWTs (`arbitrary_maps_match_serial`, `walk_into_a_cycle_without_cuts`).

## Exactness

- `cargo test --release`: every test binary passes (244 tests, 0 failed).
- **The six authenticated blocks** (`data/admitted-bwt` 41c03a5b, each checked against
  `manifest.json`'s SHA-256) and all 431 corpus blocks decode byte for byte as the plain
  inverse does, through a reused and a fresh kernel: `parity` mode, 437 blocks.
- **`examples/bzcat` on `corpora/wikidata.bz2`** (82f65ace…) at 1, 4, 8 and 10 threads gives
  `bzip2 -dc`'s SHA-256, 3f075e30…. Only 8 and 10 run `OpenCells` here; see below.
- **The moonshot correctness gate** with `Candidate = OpenCells` passes (338 cases, 0 failed).
- **New tests** (`src/open/relay/tests.rs`), each against `emit_reference`:
  - `cut_placement`: the cut rows against the predicate's set, at m from 1,024 to 900,000 and
    five marks;
  - `relay_matches_serial_on_permutations`: 1, 2, 3 and 15 cycles, m from 1,024 to 262,145,
    `n` below, at and above the cycle's bytes (the clamp, and `repeat` of a periodic walk),
    words across the 16-byte copy and the long path;
  - `boundary_restoration_on_tight_cuts`: one cycle in row order, so segments of 4 rows of
    1–3-byte words, shorter than the overrun. Also words of exactly 16 bytes, and long ones;
  - `degenerate_cuts`: every cut's segment one step (the cuts a cycle of their own); and the
    mark alone on its cycle, every other cut on a cycle it never meets;
  - `tiny_blocks`: below 1,024 rows walks serially; at 1,024 the relay; `n` of 1 to 17 bytes;
  - `arbitrary_maps_match_serial`, `walk_into_a_cycle_without_cuts`: maps, not permutations;
  - `kernel_columns_through_the_relay`: the kernel on random runs and periodic columns (a word
    repeated 2 to 7 times), against the plain inverse;
  - `miri_relay_small`, for the Miri job.
- **Hand mutations, each caught:**
  - no restoration: 12 tests fail;
  - lane 0's resume points not restored: 11 fail;
  - the handed-over part one byte short: 11 fail;
  - both step bounds removed: 2 fail (`arbitrary_maps_match_serial`,
    `walk_into_a_cycle_without_cuts`).

  Removing only the `walked == m` check is an equivalent mutant: the `walked > m` check catches
  the next step, and nothing fails.

**Which counts run Open here.** `Plan::for_threads` on this box (4 physical cores) gives plain
workers at every count (`plan_with`: fewer than `COOP_MIN_CORES` cores). Workers are lazy,

1 and 4 run Cells and never reach it. Both are oversubscribed on 4 cores.

## Reading it

The relay trades a serial chain of dependent loads for about 1.87× the loads, run eight at a
time: m + 7m/8 steps, the cycle's 15/8 to within 0.5%. Where the serial chain is long and its
loads miss, that wins. Where it is short and they hit, it loses:
- **The walk alone** improves with the table's size: 0.81–0.82× on blocks 143 and 210 (m about
  200k, edges 770–800 KiB), parity near 140–156k, and 1.10–1.13× on blocks 0 and 2 (m 90–112k).
  The serial walk costs 6.0 ns a step on block 0 and 7.8 ns on block 143. The relay costs a
  near-constant 6.5–6.8 ns per row of m on all six: it is bound by instruction throughput,
  not latency.
- **Phase split** (block 0, an instrumented build): the scouting phase runs about 11,500 rounds
  of eight lane steps at 6.5 cycles a lane step. The emitters run at 8.7 cycles a lane step,
  of which the scattered 16-byte stores of eight streams are about 40% (5.5 cycles without
  them).
- **The corpus's median full block has m = 101,795** (quartiles 95,236 and 115,544; 25 of 228
  admitted blocks have 150,000 rows or more). So most of the corpus sits in the regime where
  the relay loses on this box, and a four-worker pass is 4% slower.
- **End to end at 8 and 10 threads**, oversubscribed on 4 cores, the change is within noise.
  Peak RSS is unchanged, as expected: no heap was added.

## What transferred and what did not

- **Transferred: exactness and memory.** The cuts, relay and restoration are exact on every
  case tried, multi-cycle and periodic included, in safe Rust. The cost is 0 B of heap and
  about 10 KiB of stack, against #675's 900 KiB of staging.
- **Transferred in part: the emit's speed on large tables.** The cycle's −21 to −29% emit on
  blocks 85, 143, 210 and 207 is −7 to −19% here.
- **Did not transfer: the six-block win.** The cycle claimed 0.865–0.984× at one worker and
  0.868–0.990× at two, but against its own safe `DietMu1Cells`. That model was 1.07–1.14× slower
  than master's `OpenCells` at one worker (cycle 10 §2.2). Master's walk is safe Rust already
  (`src/open.rs` has no `unsafe`), with the diet and the 16-byte copy. Against master, the
  cycle's own Relay8 figures were 0.931–1.126× at one worker and 0.938–1.129× at two. Here they
  are 0.953–1.084× at one: the same shape, a win only on the largest blocks.
- **Untested: the claim that matters, contention on the i9.** The relay's case is a 256 KiB L2,
  where every block's edges (351–798 KiB) miss to L3 and the serial chain pays about 42 cycles
  a step. This box has a 2 MiB L2 a core, where every block's edges fit, so the serial chain
  pays L2 latency. The cycle's L2-miss chain model (critical lane 0.25–0.32×) cannot be
  checked here. Only CI's bench (which runs Open at 8 workers and more) and an i9 run settle it.
  #675's lesson, a one-thread win that reversed at 8 i9 workers, cuts both ways here. This
  candidate adds no memory, so the shared-cache argument that sank #675 does not apply. But it
  does add about 0.87m extra loads a block, hitting L3 on the i9.
