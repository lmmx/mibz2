## 0. Orientation: What the Book Is Doing and How Sources Read It

*A Thousand Plateaus* (`ATP`) is the second volume of *Capitalism and Schizophrenia*, following *Anti-Oedipus* (1972). Where *Anti-Oedipus* was primarily a polemic against Oedipal psychoanalysis and dogmatic Marxism—summarised by its aggressive formula "destroy, destroy"—*A Thousand Plateaus* shifts to a constructive, cautious pragmatics of how material, biological, linguistic, technical, and social systems form, stabilise, mutate, and break apart (Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §4.2; Protevi, *DR3* notes; Holland 2013, ch. 1).

Across the secondary literature, four framing claims recur:

1. **Anti-essentialist, immanent ontology (univocity + multiplicity).** Drawing on Spinoza's univocity of being, Hume's externality of relations, Bergson's virtual/actual distinction, and Riemann's differential geometry, Deleuze and Guattari replace fixed substances and essences with *multiplicities* and *assemblages* (*agencements*)—emergent arrangements of heterogeneous parts whose properties arise from their interactions and capacities to affect and be affected (Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §§2, 3.1, 4.2; Roffe, IEP; DeLanda 2002, ch. 1; DeLanda 2006, pp. 5–11; Adkins 2015, pp. 1–16).
2. **Continuum between stasis and change, not a good/evil binary.** Brent Adkins reads the entire book through a single axis of continuity: every assemblage sits on a continuum between tendencies toward stability (*stratification*, *territorialisation*, *striation*, *arborescence*) and tendencies toward transformation (*destratification*, *deterritorialisation*, *smoothing*, *rhizome*) (Adkins 2015, pp. 2, 16; Olkowski 2016). Eugene Holland emphasises that *ATP*'s famous dualisms are *analytical heuristics* used to diagnose mixtures, never moral binaries where one pole is automatically "good" and the other "bad" (Holland 2013, pp. 17–24).
3. **A "plateau" is a non-teleological region of intensity.** Borrowed from Gregory Bateson's anthropology of Bali, a plateau is *"a continuous, self-vibrating region of intensities"* that does not build toward an external climax or final resolution (*ATP*, Introduction, p. 22; Holland 2013, pp. 11–13; Adkins 2015, p. 33). The book's fifteen dated chapters are designed to be entered from the middle, in any order, except the Introduction first and the Conclusion last (*ATP*, p. xx; Massumi 1987, p. xiv).
4. **How the secondary sources divide (previewed here, detailed in §16).** Manuel DeLanda (2002, 2006, 2016) reconstructs *ATP* as a realist philosophy of science, dynamical systems, and engineering, equipping assemblages with variable parameter "knobs" (territorialisation and coding). Ian Buchanan (2015, 2017, 2020) strongly objects that DeLanda strips out *desire* and reduces *agencement* (a purposeful arrangement) to a physical parts-list. Brian Massumi (1992) reads *ATP* experimentally through force, translation, and affect; Alain Badiou (1997/2000) and Peter Hallward (2006) accuse Deleuze of veiled monism or otherworldliness; Alan Sokal and Jean Bricmont (1998) challenge his use of mathematics, while Smith, Protevi & Voss (SEP §6.3) and DeLanda (2002) defend its philosophical coherence.

## 1. Introduction: Rhizome (*ATP*, pp. 3–25)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Deleuze and Guattari contrast two models of organisation: the **arborescent** (tree-like: hierarchical, rooted, binary-branching, tracing descent from an origin or central trunk) and the **rhizomatic** (underground horizontal stem: acentred, non-hierarchical, growing from the middle) (Holland 2013, pp. 36–41; Adkins 2015, pp. 22–34; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §4.2). They define the rhizome by six principles (*ATP*, pp. 7–15):

1. **Connection** and **2. Heterogeneity:** Any point of a rhizome can and must connect to any other, across *different* regimes—linking biological, technical, linguistic, social, and computational elements without reducing them to a single homogeneous code (Adkins 2015, pp. 25–26; Holland 2013, p. 38).
3. **Multiplicity:** Treated as a substantive rather than a predicate ("the multiple" without reference to a hidden "One"). Drawn from Bernhard Riemann's intrinsic $n$-dimensional manifolds, a multiplicity has *"neither subject nor object, only determinations, magnitudes, and dimensions that cannot increase in number without the multiplicity changing in nature"* (*ATP*, p. 8; DeLanda 2002, pp. 12–16; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §2). All multiplicities are **flat**—they fill a *plane of consistency* without an external $n+1$ embedding dimension. Any "unity" imposed from above is **overcoding**: *"a power takeover in the multiplicity by the signifier"* operating in a supplementary dimension (*ATP*, p. 8; Adkins 2015, p. 27).
4. **Asignifying rupture:** A rhizome may be broken or shattered at any point and will start up again along old or new lines (as an ant colony recovers from disruption) (*ATP*, p. 9; Holland 2013, p. 39). Closure upon itself is stagnation: *"A language is never closed upon itself, except as a function of impotence"* (*ATP*, p. 7).
5. **Cartography** and **6. Decalcomania:** The rhizome is a **map**, not a **tracing** (*ATP*, pp. 12–14). A tracing reproduces a pre-existing structure, propagating its bottlenecks, impasses, and redundancies; a map is oriented toward experimentation in contact with the real, has multiple entryways, and is continuously modifiable (Adkins 2015, pp. 30–32; Holland 2013, pp. 40–41). Crucially, Deleuze and Guattari do not ban tracings: they instruct *"plug the tracings back into the map"* (*ATP*, p. 13)—inspect fixed snapshots against the live experimental territory rather than mistaking the snapshot for the territory (Adkins 2015, p. 31).

Two further formulas organise the plateau:
- **$n - 1$:** Genuine multiplicity is made not by adding a higher coordinating dimension ($n + 1$), but by **subtracting the unique** from the multiplicity to be constituted (*ATP*, p. 6; Adkins 2015, p. 24).
- **Speed and proliferation:** Using Glenn Gould's accelerated piano performances, D&G note that speed does not merely display virtuosity; it *"transforms the musical points into lines"* (*ATP*, p. 8), turning discrete steps into continuous trajectories (Holland 2013, p. 39).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Restratification and micro-arborescence:** Every rhizome contains knots of arborescence, and every line of flight risks re-establishing a despotic centre or hierarchy—even a "micro-Oedipus" or local tyranny—inside what was supposed to be flat (*ATP*, pp. 9–10, 15, 20; Adkins 2015, pp. 28–29).
- **Root-fascicle ("radicle") pseudomultiplicity:** Merely aborting the main root while preserving a hidden unity (e.g., splitting a single master template into redundant copies) produces an *arborescent pseudomultiplicity*, not a rhizome (*ATP*, pp. 5–6; Holland 2013, p. 37).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Adding a supervisory $n+1$ orchestrator overcoding identical worker clones creates a tree, not a multiplicity. Genuine flatness ($n - 1$) removes the privileged hub and connects *heterogeneous* capabilities laterally through a shared, modifiable **map** (live state) rather than static **tracings** (cached summaries or rigid plans). Scheduled context breaks function as asignifying ruptures that escape closed-loop impotence.
- **Computation:** Treating a multi-stage system via an intrinsic manifold (coupled stages whose qualitative regime changes as parameters like worker count $k$ cross thresholds) rather than a single global scalar metric.

## 2. 1914: One or Several Wolves? (*ATP*, pp. 26–38)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Through a critique of Freud's "Wolf-Man" case—where Freud reduced a dream of six or seven wolves in a tree to a single Oedipal father—Deleuze and Guattari distinguish two kinds of multiplicity (*ATP*, pp. 27–35; Adkins 2015, pp. 36–41; Holland 2013, pp. 42–44):

- **Molar (mass / arborescent / extensive) multiplicities:** Large, homogeneous aggregates governed by a single leader, central identity, or uniform unit of measure. They can be divided into parts without changing in nature (like splitting a uniform crowd or metric length) (Adkins 2015, p. 39; DeLanda 2002, pp. 46–49).
- **Molecular (pack / rhizomatic / intensive) multiplicities:** Small, dispersed, qualitative bands with variable distances, contagion rather than filiation, and **no fixed centre**—instead, each pack has a **borderline** or **anomalous** (*anomal*) peripheral figure through which the pack interacts with its outside (*ATP*, pp. 29–34; Holland 2013, p. 43). You cannot be "one wolf" alone; wolfing is an intensive pack threshold, and proper names designate events or assemblages, not unified subjects (Adkins 2015, pp. 38–40).

*Disagreement in the literature:* Adkins (2015, pp. 39–41) stresses that mass and pack are not separate classes of objects but tendencies that pass into each other; Eugene Holland (2013, p. 44) links the distinction tightly to Elias Canetti's *Crowds and Power* (which D&G cite directly on p. 33), warning against romanticising the pack, since packs can crystallise into destructive mobs.

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Interpretive reduction (molar capture of packs):** Forcing a heterogeneous, shifting phenomenon back into one familiar template ("all wolves = Daddy"), destroying its real dimensionality (*ATP*, pp. 28, 32; Adkins 2015, p. 37).
- **Pack-to-mass conversion:** Packs constantly oscillate with masses; a nimble band can swell into an unwieldy, conformist crowd or fall under a single charismatic centre (*ATP*, pp. 33–34; Holland 2013, p. 44).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Directly distinguishes scaling by cloning a **mass** ($N$ identical agents competing over one metric) from composing a **pack** (a small, heterogeneous band whose members occupy distinct positions, including an outsider/borderline critic interfacing with external reality). Also warns against "Freudian" post-hoc reduction of multi-factor experimental failures to one pet cause.
- **Computation:** Separates extensive, divisible workloads (trivially split across $k$ identical workers) from intensive, coupled regimes (where changing $k$ alters the qualitative bottleneck structure).

## 3. 10,000 B.C.: The Geology of Morals (Who Does the Earth Think It Is?) (*ATP*, pp. 39–74)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Delivered by the fictional "Professor Challenger," this plateau presents *ATP*'s foundational ontology of how matter self-organises into stable layers (**strata**) across three great domains: **physico-chemical** (geological), **organic** (biological), and **alloplastic / anthropomorphic** (technical and linguistic) (*ATP*, pp. 40–64; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §4.2; DeLanda 2002, pp. 139–144; Holland 2013, pp. 45–53; Adkins 2015, pp. 43–57).

- **Strata as "judgments of God":** Stratification captures unformed, intense material flows from the *plane of consistency* (the destratified "Body without Organs" of the Earth) and locks them into stable, hierarchical forms (*ATP*, p. 40; Adkins 2015, p. 45).
- **Double articulation:** Every stratum is built in two coupled steps (*ATP*, pp. 40–45; DeLanda 2006, pp. 12–15; Adkins 2015, pp. 46–48; Olkowski 2016):
  1. **First articulation (content / sedimentation):** Selects and sorts raw, metastable units into statistical orders (e.g., rivers sorting pebbles by size and weight into sedimentary layers).
  2. **Second articulation (expression / folding):** Consolidates those sorted units into stable, functional molar structures (e.g., cementation turning sediment into sandstone; DNA coding proteins; institutions codifying practices).
- **Content and Expression (from Hjelmslev):** Rejecting Saussure's signifier/signified, D&G adopt Louis Hjelmslev's fourfold grid: *content* has its own **substance** (chosen materials) and **form** (their arrangement), and *expression* has its own **substance** and **form** (*ATP*, pp. 43–45; Massumi 1992, pp. 12–17; Holland 2013, pp. 48–50). Content and expression are *real distinctions* in reciprocal presupposition—neither reduces to the other, and what is expression on one stratum can serve as content on a higher one (DeLanda 2016, pp. 14–19).
- **Epistrata and Parastrata:** Strata are never monolithic; they have internal intermediate layers (*epistrata*, adapting to local conditions) and lateral associated milieus (*parastrata*, managing exchanges with adjacent codes) (*ATP*, pp. 50–53; Adkins 2015, pp. 50–52).
- **The Abstract Machine (Ecumenon vs. Planomenon):** An *abstract machine* is the diagram of unformed matters and nonformal functions operating across strata. It has two faces: the **Ecumenon** (stratifying, locking content and expression into rigid forms) and the **Planomenon** (destratifying, drawing lines of flight onto the plane of consistency) (*ATP*, pp. 50–51, 70–74; Massumi 1992, pp. 16–20; Adkins 2015, pp. 55–57).

*Disagreement in the literature:* DeLanda (2006, pp. 14–16; 2016, pp. 21–24) translates double articulation directly into his two parameterised processes—**territorialisation** (sorting/boundary sharpening) and **coding** (consolidating via specialised expressive media like genes or written rules)—so strata become simply *highly territorialised, highly coded assemblages*. Buchanan (2015, pp. 386–388; 2020) contests this, arguing D&G sharply separate strata (naturalised sedimentary/molar bindings) from assemblages (which operate *between* strata and the plane of consistency).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Over-stratification (ossification):** Mistaking a contingent double articulation for eternal law—confusing the *form of expression* (e.g., a linguistic or institutional code) with the reality of the underlying *content* (*ATP*, pp. 40, 66–67; Holland 2013, p. 52).
- **Imperialism of the signifier:** Assuming that the alloplastic/linguistic stratum (human language) overcodes all other strata, ignoring material, physical, and computational constraints that operate independently of language (*ATP*, pp. 62–66; Massumi 1992, pp. 14–16).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Separates **content** (actual code, hardware benchmarks, raw measurements, files on disk) from **expression** (process rules, channel headers, PR reviews, summaries). When expression decouples from content—or when rules overcode the real work—the team mistakes governance artifacts for material progress. Also illuminates how a team's repo builds up geological strata (archived charters, active rules, improvised workflows).
- **Computation:** Double articulation describes compilation and data-structure pipelines cleanly: selecting/sorting raw symbols into intermediate runs or intervals (first articulation) and consolidating them into an executable structure or state machine (second articulation). Crucially, changing the form of expression (e.g., representation of the same mathematical interval facts) without altering content can change computational cost drastically.

## 4. November 20, 1923: Postulates of Linguistics (*ATP*, pp. 75–110)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Deleuze and Guattari attack four foundational postulates of structural and generative linguistics (Saussure, Chomsky): (1) that language is primarily informational and communicational; (2) that an abstract linguistic machine exists independently of pragmatics and social context; (3) that language is defined by constants/universals rather than continuous variation; and (4) that a language can be scientifically studied only as a standard ("major") language (*ATP*, pp. 75–106; Protevi, *ATP4* outline; Holland 2013, pp. 54–61; Adkins 2015, pp. 64–77).

- **The order-word (*mot d'ordre*):** The elementary unit of language is not a neutral statement of fact, but an **order-word**—an utterance that transmits socially recognised obligations, compels obedience, and establishes what counts as valid or thinkable (*ATP*, pp. 75–79; Adkins 2015, pp. 65–67). Even a schoolteacher explaining grammar is issuing commands, not merely passing information (*ATP*, p. 75). Because order-words circulate through **indirect discourse** (hearsay, relays, conventions), language is always a *collective assemblage of enunciation*, never the private expression of an autonomous individual speaker (*ATP*, pp. 76–80; Protevi, *ATP4*).
- **Incorporeal transformation:** Drawing on Stoic logic and J. L. Austin's speech-act theory, D&G argue that order-words do not physically rearrange bodies directly; instead, they effect an **incorporeal transformation**—an instantaneous change in a body's status, obligation, or operational regime (*ATP*, pp. 80–86; Massumi 1992, pp. 27–31; Adkins 2015, pp. 68–70). Classic examples: a judge's verdict instantaneously transforms an accused body into a convict; the date `November 20, 1923` (the German Rentenmark decree) instantaneously transformed worthless banknotes into valid currency; a hijacker's announcement transforms passengers into hostages (*ATP*, pp. 80–81).
- **Major vs. Minor use of language (continuous variation):** Drawing on William Labov's sociolinguistics, D&G argue that linguistic *constants* are politically extracted from underlying *continuous variation* (*ATP*, pp. 92–105; Holland 2013, pp. 58–60). A **minor** use of a major language makes language itself "stutter" (later elaborated in Deleuze's 1993 essay *"He Stuttered"*), pushing syntax and semantics into continuous variation to open **pass-words** (*mots de passe*) beneath order-words (*ATP*, pp. 97–110; Adkins 2015, pp. 74–76).

### What follows when a prompt or assignment is an order-word
For an LLM agent team, treating a prompt, brief, or channel assignment as an **order-word** rather than neutral "information" has five immediate mechanical consequences (*ATP*, pp. 75–86, 107–110; Massumi 1992, pp. 28–33; Protevi, *ATP4*):
1. **Instantaneous incorporeal transformation of the agent's regime:** A prompt does not merely inform a pre-existing solver; the moment it enters the context window, it instantaneously transforms a general-purpose model into a bounded institutional body (an "Optimiser", a "Guardian", a "Reviewer on thread 2"), defining what it may touch, what counts as success, and what it must ignore.
2. **Pre-selection of the problem space (implicit presuppositions):** Every order-word carries *implicit presuppositions* (*ATP*, pp. 77–79) that narrow the field of admissible moves before a single line of reasoning runs. A prompt that frames a problem around one metric or one branch silently excludes rival formulations without ever arguing against them.
3. **Indirect discourse and relay distortion:** Order-words operate by *relaying what was already said* (*ATP*, pp. 76–77). When one agent writes prompts for another agent, the recipient does not encounter the raw material (code, traces, hardware); it encounters an indirect-discourse compression of the author's priors, inheriting every blind spot and framing error embedded in the prompt.
4. **Obedience over inquiry ("death sentence"):** D&G provocatively link order-words to *death sentences* because they arrest variation and lock bodies into fixed states (*ATP*, pp. 107–108; Adkins 2015, p. 76). In agent workflows, a heavily specified prompt incentivises literal compliance—performing the requested script—rather than questioning whether the premise is false.
5. **The counter-move is the pass-word, not silence:** D&G do not claim you can abolish order-words; they argue you must extract **pass-words** from within order-words—turning a command into a passage or opening onto variation (*ATP*, p. 110; Holland 2013, p. 61). In practice, that means replacing imperative prose briefs with minimal, falsifiable *problem statements* and *raw state pointers* that let the receiving agent confront the material directly and overturn the premise if the evidence breaks it.

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Order-word as death sentence (arresting variation):** When commands freeze bodies into compliant routines, eliminating the flight component inherent in every order-word (*ATP*, pp. 107–110; Adkins 2015, p. 76).
- **Redundancy and standardisation:** Extracting a "major" standard register and policing it destroys the productive variation that generates new sense (*ATP*, pp. 101–105; Holland 2013, p. 59).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Prompts, role charters (`MODE.md`, `SPEC.*.md`), and channel assignments (`TYPE: CHECKPOINT`) are order-words executing incorporeal transformations. Long Coordinator-authored prompts act as indirect discourse that pre-compresses reality and induces compliance bias. Replacing narrative briefs with raw repo state + frozen stop tests converts order-words toward pass-words.
- **Computation:** Formal specifications, type signatures, and feature gates are also incorporeal transformations on codebases (a commit flagged `VERIFIED` or gated behind a feature flag changes its operational status instantaneously without changing the bytes).

## 5. 587 B.C.–A.D. 70: On Several Regimes of Signs (*ATP*, pp. 111–148)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Extending their pragmatics, Deleuze and Guattari classify concrete **regimes of signs**—ways semiotic systems organise meaning, authority, and bodies—insisting that real institutions are always *mixed semiotics* (*ATP*, pp. 111–119; Holland 2013, pp. 62–68; Adkins 2015, pp. 78–92):

1. **Presignifying (primitive/territorial) regime:** Plural, polyvocal, tied to bodies, gestures, and local territories; actively wards off any single central signifier from taking over (*ATP*, pp. 117–118; Holland 2013, p. 63).
2. **Signifying (despotic / paranoid) regime:** Organised around a **despotic centre** (the sovereign, temple, or master signifier) surrounded by expanding **concentric circles** of reference (*ATP*, pp. 112–117; Adkins 2015, pp. 80–83). Every sign refers only to another sign in an infinite chain (**signifiance**); a priesthood of **interpreters** translates the despot's utterances; **overcoding** subsumes all local codes into the central archive; and whatever refuses to fit is expelled as the **scapegoat** (*ATP*, pp. 114–117; Holland 2013, pp. 63–65).
3. **Countersignifying (nomadic / numerical) regime:** Uses *arithmetic/numbering* to organise mobile, non-hierarchical units (e.g., nomadic warrior bands counted into tens and hundreds) to counter the despot's signifying hierarchy (*ATP*, pp. 118–119; Holland 2013, p. 65).
4. **Postsignifying (passional / subjective) regime:** Breaks away from the despotic centre along a finite linear **line of flight** (the Exodus, the prophet, the monomaniac) anchored in a **point of subjectification** (*ATP*, pp. 119–129; Adkins 2015, pp. 84–88). Instead of paranoid infinite interpretation, it produces a **doubled subject**—the *subject of enunciation* (the authorial "I") policing the *subject of the statement* (the obedient "I" bound by its own rules, as in Descartes's *cogito*)—which recurs through finite proceedings, guilt, and **betrayal** (*ATP*, pp. 127–130; Holland 2013, pp. 66–67).

At the end of the plateau (*ATP*, pp. 139–148), D&G outline four components of a **pragmatics / schizoanalysis**: *generative* (tracing mixed semiotics), *transformational* (translating one regime into another), *diagrammatic* (extracting the abstract machine—noting that mathematical or musical notation can act diagrammatically *"on the same level as the real"*, p. 141), and *machinic* (evaluating how assemblages effectuate abstract machines) (Adkins 2015, pp. 89–92; Massumi 1992, pp. 17–21).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Interpretosis (signifying disease):** Endless commentary and meta-interpretation where signs only ever refer to other signs, severing contact with material reality (*ATP*, p. 114; Holland 2013, p. 64).
- **Subjectification as voluntary servitude (postsignifying trap):** Breaking free from an external boss only to internalise a harsher tribunal inside one's own rules, turning a line of flight into a self-policing **black hole** (*ATP*, pp. 129–133; Adkins 2015, p. 88).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Diagnoses two distinct failure modes of agent workflows: (a) **despotic/signifying overcoding**—a central hub + priesthood of summaries/logs where comments refer endlessly to prior comments ("interpretosis") rather than to machine measurements; and (b) **postsignifying self-policing**—autonomous sessions trapped in self-referential compliance checklists or obsessive narrow proceedings. Diagrammatic semiotics (p. 141) point directly to executable benchmarks, raw TSV tables, and formal verifiers that operate on the level of the real rather than prose commentary.
- **Computation:** Contrasts overcoded symbolic abstractions (adding interpretive indirection layers that never touch hardware reality) with diagrammatic representations (data structures or algebraic lifts that directly restructure execution).

## 6. November 28, 1947: How Do You Make Yourself a Body without Organs? (*ATP*, pp. 149–166)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Named after Antonin Artaud's 1947 radio play, the **Body without Organs (BwO)** is *not* an enemy of organs themselves, nor a call to literal bodily mutilation; it is opposed to **the organism**—the rigid, centralised, hierarchical *organisation* of organs that locks each part into a single fixed function (*ATP*, pp. 158–159; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §4.2; Adkins 2015, pp. 96–105; Holland 2013, pp. 69–74). The BwO is the unstratified limit—the **plane of consistency** on which intensities circulate freely before or beneath rigid organisation (Adkins 2015, p. 98).

Deleuze and Guattari identify **three great strata** that bind us most tightly (*ATP*, pp. 159–160; Holland 2013, p. 71):
1. **The organism** (functional hierarchy of parts);
2. **Signifiance** (compulsory meaning and interpretation);
3. **Subjectification** (being pinned to a fixed identity and consciousness).

Crucially, they distinguish **three types of BwO** (*ATP*, pp. 150, 162–165; Adkins 2015, pp. 102–104; Holland 2013, pp. 72–74):
- **The full BwO:** Populated by circulating intensities and productive connections, built carefully and experimentally;
- **The empty BwO:** Botched, hollowed-out destratification (the addict's overdose, the catatonic, the hypochondriac) where everything is wiped out too fast and nothing can pass anymore;
- **The cancerous BwO:** Runaway, homogeneous proliferation of a single stratum (fascism, metastasis) that reproduces one pattern endlessly until it kills the host.

### Dangers named by the authors (Caution as Method)
This plateau contains the most explicit methodological warning in *A Thousand Plateaus* (*ATP*, pp. 150, 160–161; Protevi, *DR3*; Adkins 2015, pp. 100–102):
- **Too-sudden destratification is lethal:** *"Staying stratified—organized, signified, subjected—is not the worst that can happen; the worst that can happen is if you throw the strata into demented or suicidal collapse"* (*ATP*, p. 161).
- **Caution (prudence) as operational rule:** You never dismantle a stratum with a sledgehammer. Instead, D&G give a concrete recipe (*ATP*, p. 161; Holland 2013, pp. 71–72): lodge yourself on a stratum, experiment with the opportunities it offers, find a small advantageous site, test potential deterritorialisations *in small doses*, keep a small plot of new land and enough of the organism/signifiance/subjectification to survive and respond to the dominant reality each morning.

### Operational content
- **Team design:** **Direct veto against "big-bang" dismantling of working safety and verification strata.** Retiring an overcoding hub cannot mean ripping out CI, byte-identity checks, safety gates, or frozen registrations overnight; destratifying too fast creates an **empty BwO** (silent regressions, unverified claims, lost work) or a **cancerous BwO** (unbounded, uncoordinated token burn duplicating the same work). Reforms must proceed by small, reversible, experimentally verified doses while keeping the core safety strata intact.
- **Computation:** Warns against tearing down a working production architecture wholesale for a speculative clean-slate rewrite before testing incremental decouplings (e.g., off-by-default research features and oracles) against the live stratum.

## 7. Year Zero: Faciality (*ATP*, pp. 167–191)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Deleuze and Guattari treat the **face** not as a natural biological head, but as an **abstract machine of faciality** produced at the intersection of two semiotic systems: the **white wall of signifiance** (a reflective screen onto which signs are inscribed) and the **black hole of subjectification** (a vortex capturing consciousness and passion) (*ATP*, pp. 167–170; Adkins 2015, pp. 108–115; Holland 2013, pp. 75–79; Olkowski 2016).

- **Binary computation of normality and deviance:** The faciality machine does not reject "the Other" as exterior; it runs a **binary yes/no triage** (*ATP*, pp. 176–178; Adkins 2015, p. 112). Every incoming datum is tested against a central reference standard (historically, D&G argue, the European Christian / White-Man standard) and either accepted as normal or graded by *degrees of deviance*—so nothing escapes its grid, it is merely classified as more or less defective (Holland 2013, pp. 77–78).
- **Overcoding the body and landscape:** Once installed, faciality spreads beyond faces to overcode entire bodies, tools, and environments ("landscapeification"), forcing heterogeneous traits to pass through a single recognition interface (*ATP*, pp. 170–172; Adkins 2015, p. 110).
- **Dismantling the face (probe-heads):** Escaping faciality means neither regressing to a pre-facial "primitive head" nor staying trapped in the recognition screen, but constructing **probe-heads** (*têtes chercheuses*)—asignifying, experimental cutting edges that explore unknown territory without forcing every encounter into a binary normal/deviant template (*ATP*, pp. 189–191; Holland 2013, p. 79).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Totalising recognition / binary triage:** Treating one canonical representation as the universal standard and reducing all novel phenomena to "deviations" from it (*ATP*, pp. 177–178).
- **Black-hole capture:** Getting sucked into self-referential subjective identity or persona performance (*ATP*, pp. 186–189; Adkins 2015, p. 114).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Exposes the trap of **agent personas / roles as faciality**: when roles (`Optimiser`, `Guardian`, `Pathfinder`, `MONODROMY`, or even stylized linguistic voices like `MODE.md`) become rigid recognition screens, every output is judged for conformance to the role's "face" rather than its material effect, and novel findings get sorted into binary pass/deviant buckets against an old standard. Also applies to benchmark or metric standards that treat every alternative algorithm as merely a "deviation" from the incumbent layout.
- **Computation:** Recognising when a single incumbent data representation acts as a faciality screen that makes every rival algorithm look "deviant" or expensive only because it is forced through the incumbent's interface (e.g., evaluating a new kernel through a legacy 127-cap record format).

## 8. 1874: Three Novellas, or "What Happened?" (*ATP*, pp. 192–207)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Though short and literary—reading Henry James's *In the Cage*, F. Scott Fitzgerald's *The Crack-Up*, and Pierrette Fleutiaux's *The Story of the Abyss and the Spyglass*—this plateau introduces the book's **cartography of three lines** that governs Plateau 9 (*ATP*, pp. 194–205; Adkins 2015, pp. 118–126; Holland 2013, pp. 80–83):
1. **Molar / rigid segmentarity (the clean break):** Visible, binary divisions (work/home, success/failure, rich/poor) where life moves from one fixed segment to the next (*ATP*, p. 195).
2. **Molecular / supple segmentarity (the fine crack):** Micro-thresholds, subtle shifts, and invisible fractures (*fêlures*) running beneath molar appearances—asking *"What happened?"* after an imperceptible threshold has already been crossed (*ATP*, pp. 192, 198–199; Adkins 2015, pp. 120–122).
3. **The abstract line of flight (rupture):** A line that no longer crosses between existing segments but breaks out into an unmapped space, carrying no pre-given guarantee of success (*ATP*, pp. 200–205).

Unlike the *tale* (which asks "what is going to happen?"), the *novella* asks **"what happened?"**—investigating an event whose consequences are already underway before the molar categories register it (*ATP*, pp. 192–194; Holland 2013, p. 81).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Clinging to molar breaks while ignoring molecular cracks:** Missing slow, invisible degradation (Fitzgerald's "crack-up") until catastrophic failure arrives (*ATP*, pp. 198–200; Adkins 2015, p. 122).
- **Romanticising the line of flight:** A line of rupture can just as easily end in isolation, madness, or collapse as in creation (*ATP*, pp. 205–206).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Direct analogue of **context staleness, silent protocol drift, and post-mortem diagnostics**: an agent team rarely breaks via one dramatic molar catastrophe; it develops invisible molecular cracks (stale assumptions, unpruned rules, subtle summary drift) long before anyone asks "what happened?".
- **Computation:** Performance regressions and cache-spill cliffs (e.g., crossing a 32 KiB L1d threshold) often manifest as molecular cracks beneath unchanged asymptotic big-O models.

## 9. 1933: Micropolitics and Segmentarity (*ATP*, pp. 208–231)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Every individual and every group is simultaneously composed of **three tangled lines** (*ATP*, pp. 208–213; Holland 2013, pp. 84–91; Adkins 2015, pp. 127–139):
1. **Rigid (molar) segmentarity:** Organised along three modes—*binary* (either/or classes), *circular* (concentric domains: task → team → repo), and *linear* (sequential stages: design → build → test → merge)—all coordinated by a **central resonance machine** and **overcoding** (*ATP*, pp. 209–212; Adkins 2015, pp. 129–131).
2. **Supple (molecular) segmentarity:** Fine-grained flows, local quanta, informal habits, micro-decisions, and shifting alliances that operate beneath and across rigid boundaries (*ATP*, pp. 212–216; Holland 2013, pp. 85–87). Crucially, *molecular* does not mean "small" and *molar* does not mean "large"—a molecular phenomenon can span an entire system (e.g., contagion, market panic, viral drift), while a rigid molar structure can exist inside a single individual (*ATP*, pp. 213–215; DeLanda 2006, pp. 32–34; Adkins 2015, p. 132).
3. **Lines of flight (quanta of deterritorialisation):** Vectors of escape that break out of both rigid and supple segmentarity; D&G argue that **society is defined first by its lines of flight**, not its contradictions, because power centres exist to plug and capture leaks (*ATP*, pp. 216–218; Patton 2000, pp. 64–67).

### Dangers named by the authors (The Four Dangers)
Deleuze and Guattari explicitly catalogue **four dangers**—one attached to each line, plus a cross-cutting danger (*ATP*, pp. 227–231; Adkins 2015, pp. 136–139; Holland 2013, pp. 89–91):
1. **Fear** (danger of rigid segmentarity): Clinging ever harder to rigid rules, borders, and binaries out of fear of losing control—breeding bureaucratic paralysis (*ATP*, p. 227).
2. **Clarity** (danger of supple/molecular segmentarity): Micro-certainty and **microfascism**—the delusion of seeing every tiny flaw and policing peers with miniature, decentralised black holes (*"rural fascism and city or neighborhood fascism… fascism of the couple, family, school, and office"*, *ATP*, p. 214; Protevi, *Nihilism*; Holland 2013, pp. 87–90). D&G warn that **supple is not automatically better than rigid**: molecular microfascism is often more dangerous than a clunky molar bureaucracy because it spreads everywhere without a central target (*ATP*, pp. 214–215, 228).
3. **Power** (danger across lines): Power centres do not simply crush flows; they subsist on **impotence**—constantly patching leaks they can never fully seal, growing bloated precisely because things escape them (*ATP*, pp. 225–229; Adkins 2015, p. 137).
4. **Abolition / Death** (the supreme danger of the line of flight): A line of flight that fails to connect with other creative flows turns into pure passion for destruction and self-abolition (*ATP*, pp. 229–231; Holland 2013, p. 90).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** A sharp corrective to naive decentralisation. Removing a rigid molar Coordinator does *not* guarantee healthy self-organisation: it can trade **Fear** (bloated central rules) for **Clarity / microfascism** (peer-level pedantry, self-appointed gatekeepers, petty blocking rules proliferating across every branch) or **Abolition** (unbounded, destructive churn). And a Coordinator accumulating incident-patch rules in `CLAUDE.md` is a textbook **Power-from-impotence** cycle: every leak generates another rule that fails to seal the next leak.
- **Computation:** Maps directly onto the interaction between **molar architectural stages** (pipeline boundaries, thread pools, admission windows) and **molecular hardware quanta** (cache lines, branch mispredicts, memory-controller contention, irregular loads) that leak across stage boundaries and defeat coarse-grained models.

## 10. 1730: Becoming-Intense, Becoming-Animal, Becoming-Imperceptible… (*ATP*, pp. 232–309)

### Central concepts and mechanics
The longest plateau develops *ATP*'s theory of **becoming** and **individuation**, synthesised from Spinoza, Bergson, and Jakob von Uexküll's ethology (*ATP*, pp. 232–309; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §4.2; Adkins 2015, pp. 140–160; Holland 2013, pp. 92–103):

- **Becoming is alliance and contagion, not imitation or filiation:** Becoming-animal does not mean pretending to be a dog or descending from one by evolution; it is entering a *zone of proximity* between heterogeneous terms (like the wasp and orchid), exchanging speeds and affects via unnatural participations (horizontal transfer, symbiosis, contagion) (*ATP*, pp. 238–242; Adkins 2015, pp. 143–146).
- **The anomalous (*anomal*) at the borderline:** Every pack or multiplicity has a peripheral anomalous element—not an "average" member or a sovereign chief, but the borderline phenomenon with which an alliance is struck to transform the pack (*ATP*, pp. 243–247; Holland 2013, p. 95).
- **Haecceity (longitude and latitude):** Borrowing from Duns Scotus and Spinoza, D&G define an individual not by genus, species, or substantial form ("what is it?"), but as a **haecceity**—an event/mode specified by two coordinates (*ATP*, pp. 256–263; Protevi, *Fall 2013 Lecture 2*; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §2; Adkins 2015, pp. 151–153):
  - **Longitude:** the set of material elements and their **relations of speed and slowness**, movement and rest;
  - **Latitude:** the set of **affects**—the body's capacities to affect and be affected (Spinoza's *puissance*).
  Hence Protevi's summary of D&G's ethological example (*ATP*, p. 257): a workhorse has more in common with an ox (pulling assemblage) than with a racehorse, which has more in common with a motorcycle (speed assemblage) (Protevi, *Fall 2013 Lecture 2*, p. 7).
- **Plane of Consistency vs. Plane of Organization:**
  - The **plane of organization (or transcendence)** is a hidden blueprint, teleological plan, or developmental scheme standing *above* what it organises (it can only be inferred from its products) (*ATP*, pp. 265–267).
  - The **plane of consistency (or immanence)** is a flat, constructible composition of longitudes and latitudes with no hidden dimension—everything operates on the same level (*ATP*, pp. 266–270; Adkins 2015, pp. 154–156).
- **Becoming-minoritarian and becoming-imperceptible:** The "majority" is not a numerical count but a fixed *standard* (a constant); all genuine becoming is **minoritarian** (departing from the standard), culminating in **becoming-imperceptible**—eliminating ego/subjectivity so completely that one merges with the field of relations and acts directly within it (*ATP*, pp. 279–280, 291–293; Holland 2013, pp. 100–103).

*Disagreement in the literature:* Feminist theorists sharply divide over "becoming-woman" (*ATP*, pp. 275–279): Alice Jardine and Luce Irigaray argue it risks erasing concrete female subjectivity by turning "woman" into a conceptual stepping-stone for male philosophers, whereas Elizabeth Grosz and Rosi Braidotti defend it as a productive destabilisation of binary identity (Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §6.1). Peter Hallward (2006, pp. 43, 81) argues that becoming-imperceptible betrays an "otherworldly" flight away from concrete actual beings; Paul Patton (2010) and Protevi counter that *ATP*'s haecceities and assemblages are thoroughly material and relational.

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Imitation / masquerade:** Confusing genuine structural transformation (changing speeds and affects) with superficial mimicry of style or identity (*ATP*, pp. 273–275; Adkins 2015, p. 144).
- **Black holes and fascist/suicidal dissolution of the plane:** Pushing deterritorialisation without consistency until the body loses its capacity to connect (*ATP*, pp. 270, 285; Holland 2013, p. 99).
- **Re-capture of the anomalous:** Turning the borderline outlier into a new domestic pet or state-sanctioned standard (*ATP*, pp. 244–245).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Defines agents **ethologically by longitude and latitude** (what an agent/model family *can actually do*—its speed, failure modes, tool harness, epistemic affects—rather than by bureaucratic role titles or anthropomorphic identities). Also clarifies why **becoming-imperceptible** (stripping out persona theatre, self-crediting, and conversational ego in favour of direct commits and data) raises signal-to-noise.
- **Computation:** Classifies algorithms by **affect and speed profile** (cache footprint, dependency-chain latency, parallel scalability, memory bandwidth) rather than by textbook taxonomy ("graph algorithm" vs. "combinatorics on words"). Two algorithms from completely different fields may share the same computational haecceity, while two variants in the same genus may live in opposite hardware regimes.

## 11. 1837: Of the Refrain (*ATP*, pp. 310–350)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Through music, birdsong, and ethology, Deleuze and Guattari explain how order and **territory** emerge out of **chaos** without a central planner (*ATP*, pp. 310–350; Holland 2013, pp. 104–112; Adkins 2015, pp. 165–181; Abrahams 2025):

- **Chaos, Milieus, and Rhythm:** A **milieu** is a coded block of space-time carved out of chaos by periodic repetition (*ATP*, p. 313). **Rhythm** is *not* regular meter or cadence (which are dogmatic, homogeneous repetitions); rhythm is the *critical, unequal tie between two heterogeneous milieus*, where one milieu transcodes into another (*ATP*, pp. 313–314; Adkins 2015, pp. 167–168).
- **The Refrain (*ritournelle*) in three simultaneous aspects** (*ATP*, pp. 311–312; Holland 2013, pp. 105–107; Adkins 2015, pp. 166–167):
  1. **A calming point of stability in chaos:** A child humming in the dark establishes a provisional centre so chaos does not overwhelm;
  2. **A circle of territory (organising routine):** Repeating an expressive motif (birdsong, markers, habits) draws a boundary—an *inside* safe enough to organise work and filter outside forces;
  3. **Opening the circle onto the outside (the Cosmos):** Cracking the circle open along a line of flight to improvise with exterior forces, rather than sealing it forever.
- **Territory and Expressive Qualities:** A territory is formed when a component ceases to be merely functional and becomes **expressive**—a signature or marker that reorganises functions around it (*ATP*, pp. 315–317; Abrahams 2025). The refrain moves through four scales of assemblage: *infra-assemblage* (marking territory), *intra-assemblage* (reorganising components inside), *inter-assemblage* (linking different assemblages), and *extra-assemblage* (opening onto cosmic/universal forces) (*ATP*, pp. 323–337; Adkins 2015, pp. 173–178).
- **Consistency (consolidation):** How do heterogeneous elements hold together *without* being homogenised or subordinated to a central boss? Through **consistency**—a *fuzzy aggregate* synthesised by local rhythms, intercalations, and intervals rather than by top-down unification (*ATP*, pp. 327–330, 344; Holland 2013, pp. 109–111; DeLanda 2002, pp. 145–147). D&G call the machine that builds consistency a **synthesiser** (*ATP*, p. 343).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Black hole of the refrain (fascist/obsessive closure):** When the territorial refrain seals itself shut instead of opening onto the outside, turning an organising rhythm into a hypnotic, repetitive loop or tribal slogan (*ATP*, pp. 347–348; Holland 2013, p. 112).
- **Cosmic chaos / dissolution:** Opening the circle so wide that all consistency dissolves into white noise—*"too much chaos"* destroys the capacity to capture any force (*ATP*, pp. 343–345; Adkins 2015, p. 180).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Explains why **lightweight, recurring operational routines** (cadences, CI loops, benchmark sweeps, release checklists) are *not* automatically bad "overcoding": a good refrain creates a stable *territory* out of chaos, holds heterogeneous peers together via **consistency** (shared interfaces, formats, evidence conventions) rather than hierarchy, and periodically **cracks the circle open** (re-characterisation, fresh-context restarts) so the routine never hardens into ritual. Conversely, a refrain that stops opening onto the outside becomes a dead slogan (e.g., chanting `MODE.md` without new technical consequence).
- **Computation:** **Rhythm vs. meter** distinguishes adaptive, event-driven coordination between heterogeneous stages (where different components run at unequal rates and transcode cleanly across buffers) from rigid lockstep cadence (forcing fast stages to wait in lockstep behind a slow serial meter).

## 12. 1227: Treatise on Nomadology—The War Machine (*ATP*, pp. 351–423)

*This plateau is central to software, mathematics, and team organisation.*

### Central concepts and mechanics
Deleuze and Guattari construct a theory of the **war machine**—whose primary object is *not* war at all, but the invention of open, smooth spaces and creative mutations—and its structural exteriority to the **State apparatus** (*ATP*, pp. 351–423; Patton 2000, pp. 109–120; Holland 2013, pp. 113–125; Adkins 2015, pp. 182–203; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*):

#### 1. State Apparatus vs. War Machine (Axioms I–II)
- **The State apparatus** is defined by interiority, sovereignty, and capture. Following Georges Dumézil, sovereignty has two poles—the **magician-king** (capture by sudden bond/command) and the **jurist-priest** (regulation by law, contract, and procedure)—operating together to conserve, measure, and hierarchise (*ATP*, pp. 351–352; Adkins 2015, pp. 184–185). Its game is **Chess**: pieces have coded, intrinsic identities and fixed movement rules on a closed, striated grid (*ATP*, pp. 352–353; Holland 2013, p. 115).
- **The War Machine** is irreducible and *exterior* to the State apparatus; it is invented by nomads and defined by speed, metamorphosis, and **Go** (*ATP*, pp. 352–354; Adkins 2015, pp. 185–187). Go pieces are anonymous tokens (*pellets*) with no intrinsic essence—only relational, strategic capacities (*affects*) that territorialise and deterritorialise open space dynamically (*ATP*, p. 353).
- **Logos vs. Nomos; Numbered vs. Numbering Number:** State space obeys *logos* (fixed law, measured parcels, a **numbered number** used to audit resources from above). Nomad space obeys *nomos* (customary distribution in open space, a **numbering number** that composes a moving body from within without subordinating it to an external metric) (*ATP*, pp. 380–392; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*).

#### 2. Royal (Theorematic) Science vs. Nomad (Problematic / Ambulant / Minor) Science
This is one of *ATP*'s sharpest operational distinctions (*ATP*, pp. 361–374, 408–415; Protevi, *Nihilism*; DeLanda 2002, pp. 131–137; Pickering 2010; Adkins 2015, pp. 189–196; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*):

| Axis | Royal / State Science (*theorematic*) | Nomad / Minor / Ambulant Science (*problematic*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical model** | Theory of solids; fluids treated as special cases; seeks the stable, eternal, constant (*ATP*, p. 361) | Hydraulic / flux model (*clinamen*, turbulence, vortices); flux and heterogeneity are primary (*ATP*, p. 361) |
| **Objects & figures** | **Theorematic:** deduces properties from fixed ideal essences (the Euclidean circle) (*ATP*, pp. 362, 367) | **Problematic:** figures as events and deformations; Husserl's **vague essences**—*"anexact yet rigorous"* (roundness, not the circle) (*ATP*, pp. 362, 367) |
| **Equations** | Extracts constants from variables (**compars**) (*ATP*, p. 369) | Puts variables into continuous variation (**dispars**); equations arise from material thrusts (*ATP*, pp. 364–365, 369) |
| **Method** | **Reproducing (iteration):** deducing and verifying from a fixed vantage point *"watching the flow from the bank"* (*ATP*, p. 372) | **Following (itineration):** moving across a variable field, tracking singularities *"scattered like so many 'accidents' (problems)"* (*ATP*, pp. 372, 409) |
| **Matter & form** | **Hylomorphism:** an external form imposed onto passive, pre-homogenised matter (*ATP*, p. 369) | **Material-forces (Simondon):** matter is already laden with singularities and implicit traits; the artisan **follows the grain** (*ATP*, pp. 369, 408–409; Massumi 1992, pp. 10–14) |
| **Division of labour** | Architect draws a metric plane *on paper and off-site* (governors vs. governed); workers execute templates (*ATP*, pp. 365, 368) | **Gothic journeymen** (*compagnonnages*) work on the ground-level plane of consistency, cutting stone by squaring as thrusts emerge (*ATP*, pp. 364, 368) |
| **Exemplar** | Gravity / Euclidean geometry / axiomatics (*ATP*, pp. 370, 461) | **Metallurgy**—*"minor science in person"*—where the smith follows the **machinic phylum** (the evolving convergence of material singularities and operational traits, e.g., ore, heat, quenching, crystal grain) (*ATP*, pp. 406–415; DeLanda 2002, pp. 135–137; Protevi, *Nihilism*) |

**Crucial nuance:** Deleuze and Guattari explicitly reject treating the two sciences as separate worlds or declaring nomad science self-sufficient. Ontologically, they form *"a single field of interaction in which royal science continually appropriates the contents of vague or nomad science while nomad science continually cuts the contents of royal science loose"* (*ATP*, p. 367; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*; Pickering 2010). Moreover, *"the ambulant sciences invent problems whose scientific solution depends… on royal science"* (*ATP*, p. 374; Adkins 2015, p. 193)—nomad science poses live problems by following matter, while royal science formalises, proves, and stabilises them, though at the risk of stripping away the motivating intuition (*ATP*, p. 409).

#### 3. Tool vs. Weapon
Drawing on André Leroi-Gourhan and Gilbert Simondon, D&G distinguish **tools** and **weapons** not by lethality, but by their *operational physics and assemblage* (*ATP*, pp. 395–403; Adkins 2015, pp. 197–199; Holland 2013, pp. 121–122):
- **Tool:** Belongs to the **work** model (State/sedentary)—introceptive, gravitas-bound, expending effort against resistance to reproduce forms and accumulate stock (*ATP*, pp. 395–397).
- **Weapon:** Belongs to the **free-action** model (nomad/war machine)—projective, celeritas-bound, exploiting momentum, vectors, and affects to displace a field with minimal counter-force (*ATP*, pp. 395–400). The same physical object (e.g., an axe, a horse, a formal tool) functions as a tool or a weapon depending on the assemblage it enters (*ATP*, p. 398).

#### 4. How the State Captures the War Machine
The State has no war machine of its own; to survive and expand, it must **appropriate** the nomad war machine, transforming it into a disciplined military institution (*ATP*, pp. 354–356, 416–420; Patton 2000, pp. 115–119; Holland 2013, pp. 123–125). Capture proceeds by:
- **Striating its space** (confining mobility to fortresses, fixed roads, and schedules);
- **Splitting intellectual from manual labour** (replacing itinerant journeymen with State engineers—Vauban, Monge, Carnot—disciplined *"between a rock and a hard place"*, *ATP*, pp. 362–363, 368);
- **Subordinating invention to war or fixed objectives:** Only once captured by the State does the war machine take *war* (or destruction/conquest) as its primary object (*ATP*, pp. 416–418; Adkins 2015, pp. 200–202).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **State capture and repression of minor science:** State science represses ambulant practices not because they are inaccurate, but *"because they imply a division of labor opposed to the norms of the State"* (*ATP*, p. 368)—and loses crucial physical intuition when translating living problems into off-site axioms (*ATP*, p. 409).
- **The terrifying reversal (fascist / global war machine):** When a captured war machine breaks loose from State limits and makes *pure destruction* its own end—converting the entire planet into a smooth space of total war or runaway capitalist axiomatisation (*ATP*, pp. 420–423; Patton 2000, pp. 118–119; Holland 2013, p. 125).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Exposes the exact pathology of the **architect-on-paper vs. journeyman-on-site** split: an off-site Coordinator specifying 100-line plans on paper for executors to transcribe reproduces the State division of labour and loses live contact with material singularities. Conversely, pure itinerant hacking without royal formalisation cannot certify or stabilise its discoveries; the productive locus is the **borderline** where itinerant probes pose live problems and royal verification locks in proved, reproducible results.
- **Computation & Mathematics:** Directly contrasts **royal theorem-first search** (deriving abstract universal bounds, importing heavy general theories, or forcing hardware into hylomorphic models that ignore cache/microarchitecture) against **nomad/metallurgical science** (following the *machinic phylum* of the actual CPU, cache hierarchy, BWT run distributions, and coupled pipeline thrusts via empirical probes and variable-parameter sweeps, then handing the discovered singularities to royal proof/formalisation).

## 13. 7000 B.C.: Apparatus of Capture (*ATP*, pp. 424–473)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Where Plateau 12 traces the war machine, Plateau 13 analyses how the **State apparatus** and **capitalism** capture flows, create surplus, and overcode heterogeneous worlds (*ATP*, pp. 424–473; Holland 2013, pp. 126–136; Adkins 2015, pp. 204–223; Patton 2000, pp. 120–131; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*):

- **Coexistence, anticipation, and warding-off (against linear evolution):** Drawing on Pierre Clastres, D&G reject the myth that societies evolve linearly from "primitive" to "State". Instead, primitive bands, towns, States, and war machines coexist in a single field: non-State groups possess *warding-off mechanisms* that anticipate and block the emergence of a central State before it consolidates (*ATP*, pp. 427–437; Adkins 2015, pp. 208–211).
- **The double operation of capture:** Every apparatus of capture performs two operations *simultaneously*: **(1) direct comparison** of heterogeneous elements on a common scale, and **(2) monopolistic appropriation** of the differential surplus created by that comparison (*ATP*, pp. 444–448; Holland 2013, pp. 129–131). Capture *produces* what it claims merely to have found already there (*ATP*, p. 446).
- **The three poles of capture (the "trinity")** (*ATP*, pp. 440–444; Adkins 2015, pp. 212–215; Holland 2013, pp. 129–132):
  1. **Ground / Rent:** Compares different plots of land simultaneously via differential fertility (Ricardo), appropriating the difference as *ground rent*;
  2. **Work / Profit (via the Stock):** Transforms free, intermittent *activity* into measured, continuous **work** by creating a **stock**—a central store that compares non-simultaneous yields and extracts *surplus labour* (Marx). Following Tim Ingold and anthropological critiques cited in the secondary literature, setting goods aside is not yet a stock; a stock arises when rights converge on a single appropriating interest (*ATP*, pp. 440–442; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*);
  3. **Money / Taxation:** Overcodes exchange via a state-issued general equivalent—money arises from *taxation* (citing Édouard Will on Corinth), not from barter, forcing flows through a single fiscal measure (*ATP*, pp. 442–444).
- **Overcoding (archaic State) vs. Axiomatic (capitalism):**
  - The archaic imperial State (*Urstaat*) **overcodes** existing territories from above via transcendent laws and measures (*ATP*, pp. 427–428, 448).
  - **Capitalism** works differently: it *decodes* and *deterritorialises* flows (free labour, free capital) and then re-captures them through an immanent **axiomatic**—a flexible set of operational rules (*axioms*) that conjoin decoded flows without needing a transcendent master-code (*ATP*, pp. 452–466; Holland 2013, pp. 132–135; Adkins 2015, pp. 217–221). When a flow escapes, the axiomatic simply *adds a new axiom* (saturation) or *subtracts axioms* (austerity/deregulation) (*ATP*, pp. 462–464).
- **Undecidable propositions and minoritarian invention:** Drawing on Gödel and mathematical intuitionism, D&G argue that every axiomatic necessarily generates **undecidable propositions**—flows and problems that cannot be resolved within the existing axioms and force either a new capture-axiom or a revolutionary line of flight (*ATP*, pp. 461, 471–473; Adkins 2015, pp. 221–223).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Capture by measure (stock and comparison):** Any ledger, archive, or uniform metric that compares heterogeneous activities simultaneously can silently convert *free creative activity* into *measured, extractable work* subordinated to a single appropriating standard (*ATP*, pp. 441–446; Holland 2013, p. 131).
- **Axiomatic recuperation (adding axioms):** Even after eliminating an archaic central despot (overcoding), a decentralised system can be re-captured by a proliferating **axiomatic**—adding rule upon rule to absorb every anomaly without changing the underlying regime (*ATP*, pp. 461–466; Adkins 2015, p. 220).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** **Crucial warning for any decentralised "ledger" or "claims" protocol.** Eliminating a despotic Coordinator (archaic overcoding) does *not* immunise a team against capture: a git-native ledger (`frontier/`), token budgets, TTL leases, and CI checks can quietly operate as a **capitalist axiomatic**—converting creative research into metered "work" via a central **stock** of tickets and adding endless CI axioms whenever an edge case leaks. The operational antidote is keeping the axiomatic strictly minimal (only immutable correctness/safety invariants) and treating **undecidable anomalies** (where the model fails to match reality) as triggers to revise the problem formulation rather than patch the rules.
- **Computation:** Overcoding by a single uniform measure (e.g., comparing all algorithms only by static instruction count $I_r$ or one rigid-calculus scalar $h_1$) creates a false "general equivalent" that blinds search to multi-parameter hardware trade-offs (latency overlap, L1d pressure, serial vs. parallel coupling).

## 14. 1440: The Smooth and the Striated (*ATP*, pp. 474–500)

### Central concepts and mechanics
Deleuze and Guattari synthesise the book's spatial, mathematical, and technical analyses through the pair **smooth space** (nomadic, intensive, continuous, non-metric, directional, haptic) and **striated space** (sedentary, extensive, gridded, metric, dimensional, optical), insisting that the two exist *only in mixture* and continually translate into one another (*ATP*, pp. 474–475; Holland 2013, pp. 137–144; Adkins 2015, pp. 224–238; DeLanda 2002, pp. 16–28, 129–133):

They develop **six models** (*ATP*, pp. 475–499; Olkowski 2016; Adkins 2015, pp. 226–236):
1. **The Technological Model (fabric vs. felt & patchwork):** *Woven fabric* is striated—fixed vertical warp, mobile horizontal weft, delimited width, top and bottom, back and front. *Felt* is an anti-fabric—an entangled, non-homogeneous smooth space with no fixed top/bottom or centre, open in all directions. *Patchwork* (and *crochet*) stitches heterogeneous scraps piece by piece without a global template (*ATP*, pp. 475–477; Adkins 2015, pp. 226–228).
2. **The Musical Model (Boulez):** Pierre Boulez's *pulsed time* (striated: standard measure, fixed intervals, counting to occupy time) vs. *non-pulsed time* (smooth: occupying time without counting it, continuous variation of frequencies and durations) (*ATP*, pp. 477–478; Holland 2013, p. 139).
3. **The Maritime Model (the sea):** The ocean is the smooth space *par excellence*, yet it was the *first* space to be aggressively striated—first by empirical, local nomad navigation (winds, currents, stars, sensory bearings), then by astronomical/cartographic grids of latitude and longitude, and later *re-smoothed* by nuclear submarines and global fleets operating from anywhere to anywhere (*ATP*, pp. 478–482; Holland 2013, pp. 139–140; Adkins 2015, pp. 229–230).
4. **The Mathematical Model (Riemannian vs. Euclidean; metric vs. non-metric multiplicities):** Drawing on Bernhard Riemann, Albert Lautman, and Benoit Mandelbrot (*ATP*, pp. 482–488; DeLanda 2002, pp. 12–28; Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §6.3; Adkins 2015, pp. 231–233):
   - **Striated / Euclidean space** is global, homogeneous, and *metric*: it embeds shapes in a fixed external coordinate grid, allows uniform division without qualitative change, and measures distance via constant units.
   - **Smooth / Riemannian space** is an amorphous collection of **local patches** whose linkages are not predetermined globally—each neighbourhood carries its own local metric tensor/curvature, and moving from patch to patch alters the geometry itself (*ATP*, pp. 485–486; DeLanda 2002, pp. 14–16). D&G also invoke **fractals** (fractional dimensions between integer Euclidean dimensions) and **fuzzy sets** as mathematical instances of non-metric smooth multiplicities (*ATP*, pp. 486–488).
   - Crucially, smooth and striated translate reciprocally: one can locally lay a Euclidean tangent space over any point of a Riemannian manifold (striating the smooth), and one can release non-metric intensive variations from inside a gridded system (smoothing the striated) (*ATP*, pp. 485–488; Culp, *Nomadology Reference*).
5. **The Physical Model (work vs. free action):** Striated space subordinates movement to gravity, fixed vertical/horizontal axes, and measured work ($W = F \cdot d$); smooth space operates via speeds, vectors, and intensive gradients (*ATP*, pp. 488–492).
6. **The Aesthetic Model (optical vs. haptic / close-range vision):** Striated vision is distant, optical, and perspective-bound; smooth vision is *haptic* (close-range, tactile, navigating step by step through local texture without a bird's-eye panorama) (*ATP*, pp. 492–499; Adkins 2015, pp. 235–236).

### Dangers named by the authors
This plateau ends with the single most famous warning in *A Thousand Plateaus* (*ATP*, p. 500; Spencer 2011; Weizman 2006; Holland 2013, p. 144; Adkins 2015, p. 237):
- **Smooth space is NOT inherently liberating or superior:** *"Never believe that a smooth space will suffice to save us."* (*ATP*, p. 500). Smooth spaces are constantly co-opted for domination—global finance, automated surveillance, and modern military doctrines (as Eyal Weizman documented in his study of urban warfare) actively deploy smooth space to bypass barriers and striate targets more efficiently.
- **What matters is the concrete passage between smooth and striated** (*ATP*, pp. 474–475, 500): *how* a smooth space is constructed, *where* striation is needed to hold gains, and *how* the two interact.

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Kills the naive slogan "smooth/flat good, striated/structured bad." A productive team is a **deliberate mixture**: striated where metric reproducibility, byte-exactness, path-disjointness, and frozen stop tests are needed (the latitude-longitude grid that keeps ships from colliding), and smooth where local exploration, problem reformulation, and patchwork prototyping must follow irregular terrain without a global off-site blueprint.
- **Computation:** Directly mirrors **local-patch (Riemannian) vs. global-grid (Euclidean) modelling** of real computer hardware and heterogeneous data corpora. A single global Euclidean cost model (one constant cycles/op or one worst-case service rate across 3,500×-varying block sizes) fails; a piecewise, regime-dependent model (local patches around $k=1$, $k=6$, $k=8$, $k=10$, or separate paths for sparse vs. dense blocks) matches the actual Riemannian curvature of performance space.

## 15. Conclusion: Concrete Rules and Abstract Machines (*ATP*, pp. 501–514)

### Central concepts and mechanics
The Conclusion functions as an explicit reference manual and conceptual index for the entire book, grouping its machinery under five headings (*ATP*, pp. 501–514; Adkins 2015, pp. 239–249; Holland 2013, pp. 145–151):

1. **Strata:** Double articulation of content and expression; strata act as belts or pincers stabilising flows.
2. **Assemblages (*agencements*):** Always **tetravalent**—operating simultaneously along two axes (*ATP*, pp. 503–505; Adkins 2015, pp. 241–243; DeLanda 2016, pp. 19–22):
   - *Horizontal axis:* **machinic assemblage of bodies** (content: tools, hardware, physical states) coupled with **collective assemblage of enunciation** (expression: rules, prompts, signs, incorporeal transformations);
   - *Vertical axis:* **territorialisation/reterritorialisation** (stabilising sides) coupled with **cutting edges of deterritorialisation** (mutational sides that carry the assemblage away).
3. **Rhizome:** Non-hierarchical, acentred connections across heterogeneous elements; maps over tracings.
4. **Plane of Consistency (BwO) and Deterritorialisation Theorems:** D&G distinguish **relative deterritorialisation** (reversible, always reterritorialised onto a new stratum) from **absolute deterritorialisation** (creating a new plane of consistency), and warn that absolute deterritorialisation can be **negative** (black hole, collapse) or **positive** (connecting lines of flight into a new diagram). Their **theorems of deterritorialisation** state, inter alia, that *"one never deterritorializes alone"* (at least two coupled terms are involved, and the *fastest* element reterritorialises on the slower, or conversely, the most deterritorialised element precipitates the rest) (*ATP*, pp. 508–510; Adkins 2015, pp. 245–246; Holland 2013, pp. 148–149).
5. **Abstract Machines and Diagrams:** An abstract machine knows nothing of fixed forms or substances—only unformed matters and nonformal functions (*ATP*, p. 511). D&G distinguish **overcoding/axiomatic abstract machines** (which lock assemblages onto strata and uniform measures) from **diagrammatic / mutational abstract machines** (which act as *piloting* diagrams, constructing a new real that does not yet exist rather than representing an old one) (*ATP*, pp. 510–514; Massumi 1992, pp. 16–20; Adkins 2015, pp. 247–249).

### Dangers named by the authors
- **Confusing negative absolute deterritorialisation with creation:** Breaking free of all constraints without constructing a viable new diagram collapses into a black hole (*ATP*, pp. 509–510).
- **Treating the abstract machine as a Platonic blueprint:** An abstract machine is not an ideal template standing above concrete assemblages; it exists *only* as it is effectuated inside concrete machines, and concrete machines retroactively mutate it (*ATP*, pp. 511–513; Adkins 2015, p. 248).

### Operational content
- **Team design:** Gives the exact diagnostic checklist for any workflow redesign: evaluate it along **all four valves of the assemblage**—(1) physical/tool content, (2) expressive/protocol regime, (3) stabilising territories (safety/verification strata), and (4) cutting edges of deterritorialisation (rupture, anomaly-driven reformulation)—and check the **deterritorialisation theorems** (e.g., if you speed up one stage or peer far beyond the rest, it will simply reterritorialise/stall on the slower coupled element—a literal statement of Amdahl's law and complementary bottlenecks!).
- **Computation:** A **diagrammatic abstract machine** is a representation (like the zero-copy ideal-parts matrix or an algebraic lift in `essence.md`) that does not merely describe the incumbent code, but constructs a new space of possible algorithms.

## 16. Major Disagreements in the Secondary Literature

Applying *A Thousand Plateaus* responsibly requires marking five sharp fault lines among its commentators:

1. **Systems-engineering realism vs. desiring-arrangement (DeLanda vs. Buchanan).**
   - **Manuel DeLanda** (*Intensive Science and Virtual Philosophy*, 2002; *A New Philosophy of Society*, 2006; *Assemblage Theory*, 2016) translates *ATP* into complexity science, dynamical systems (phase spaces, attractors, bifurcations), and engineering. He models assemblages as wholes defined by *relations of exteriority* with two continuous control **knobs** (parameters): *territorialisation* (boundary sharpness/homogeneity) and *coding* (expressive consolidation) (DeLanda 2016, pp. 1–24; Little 2016).
   - **Ian Buchanan** ("Assemblage Theory and Its Discontents," 2015; "Assemblage Theory, or, the Future of an Illusion," 2017; *Assemblage Theory and Method*, 2020) sharply rejects DeLanda's reconstruction. He argues that English "assemblage" is a false friend for French *agencement* (closer to a deliberate musical *arrangement* or *working layout*), that DeLanda treats assemblages as mere piles of physical parts while erasing **desire** (the productive, selective force driving *Capitalism and Schizophrenia*), and that flattening strata into "highly coded assemblages" breaks D&G's structural distinction between strata, assemblages, and abstract machines (Buchanan 2015, pp. 382–391; Jay & Acott 2023).
   - **Holland's parallel reservation:** Eugene Holland notes that DeLanda largely brackets politics, blurring the line D&G carefully draw between science and politics (Holland 2013; cf. Protevi, SEP §6.3).

2. **Continuity of immanence vs. veiled monism / otherworldliness (Adkins & Protevi vs. Badiou & Hallward).**
   - **Brent Adkins** (2015) and **John Protevi** (SEP §4.2; Bonta & Protevi 2004) read *ATP* as a thoroughgoing materialist philosophy of continuous variation and concrete socio-physical assemblages.
   - **Alain Badiou** (*Deleuze: The Clamor of Being*, 1997/2000) counters that Deleuze's Spinozist univocity makes him a philosopher of the *One*, not the *Multiple*, lacking the axiomatic set-theoretic rigor needed to account for genuine radical novelty (Smith, Protevi & Voss, SEP §6.2).
   - **Peter Hallward** (*Out of This World*, 2006, pp. 43, 81, 154) argues that Deleuze systematically privileges virtual, de-materialised creation over actual, embodied beings—leaving his philosophy oddly "out of this world" and weak on concrete collective agency. **Paul Patton** (2010) responds that Hallward conflates Deleuze's early solo works with the intensely concrete, mixed-assemblage pragmatics of *ATP*.

3. **Legitimate mathematics/science vs. "fashionable nonsense" (Sokal & Bricmont vs. DeLanda, Salanskis & SEP).**
   - **Alan Sokal and Jean Bricmont** (*Fashionable Nonsense*, 1998) accuse Deleuze and Guattari of abusing calculus, Riemannian geometry, and physics jargon without technical rigor to lend unearned scientific authority to philosophical prose.
   - **Smith, Protevi & Voss** (SEP §6.3), **Jean-Michel Salanskis**, and **DeLanda** (2002) reply that Sokal and Bricmont misread philosophical concept-creation as attempted mathematical proof: Deleuze demonstrably understood the Weierstrassian finitist turn in calculus and Riemann's intrinsic manifolds, using them explicitly as philosophical models of differential genesis rather than claiming to prove theorems. Nevertheless, the critique carries a lasting operational warning: **do not mistake philosophical vocabulary ("Riemannian", "multiplicity", "phase transition") for actual mathematical proof or physical measurement.**

4. **Emancipatory tool vs. neoliberal & military instrumentalisation (Weizman, Boltanski & Chiapello, Galloway, Spencer, Culp).**
   - **Eyal Weizman** ("Walking Through Walls" / "Lethal Theory," 2006–2007) documented how military theorists (notably Brigadier General Aviv Kokhavi and Shimon Naveh in the IDF) literally operationalised *ATP*'s concepts of *smooth space*, *rhizome*, and *war machine* for urban combat ("inverse geometry" blasting through walls).
   - **Luc Boltanski and Ève Chiapello** (*The New Spirit of Capitalism*, 1999/2005), **Douglas Spencer** ("Architectural Deleuzism," 2011), **Alexander Galloway** (*Protocol*, 2004), and **Andrew Culp** (*Dark Deleuze*, 2016) warn that post-Fordist management, startup culture, and networked control societies happily co-opt "rhizomatic", "flat", "flexible", and "smooth" rhetoric to produce *more efficient capture* via protocols rather than bosses—confirming Deleuze's own later "Postscript on the Societies of Control" (1990) and *ATP*'s p. 500 warning (*"Never believe that a smooth space will suffice to save us"*).

5. **Faithful exegesis vs. experimental "deviation" (Massumi vs. textual guides).**
   - **Brian Massumi** (*A User's Guide to Capitalism and Schizophrenia: Deviations from Deleuze and Guattari*, 1992, pp. 7–9) openly refuses to write a faithful summary, arguing that the only way to honour *ATP* is to treat it as a toolbox and *deviate* from it—lifting its dynamics (e.g., the woodworker following the grain, pp. 10–14) into new material domains. Textbooks like **Holland** (2013) and **Adkins** (2015) insist by contrast on tracking the internal consistency of D&G's exact terminology so that borrowings do not decay into vague buzzwords.
