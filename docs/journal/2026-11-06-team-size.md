# Team size against live tables: what holds the decoder at 8 threads

Registered before any timed run, on 2026-11-06.

## The question

The i9-10900K run of the release-scaling recipe (`bench/release-scaling/2026-11-06-intel-r-core-tm-i9-10900k-cpu-3-70ghz/`)
shows 0.2.4 and 0.2.1 both level at about 1.27–1.31 GB/s from 8 threads up.

The reason is visible in each run's threads:
- At 8, 10, 12, 16 and 20 threads asked for, the decoder runs the same 10 threads:
  - 4 team leaders, at 0.13–0.15 s of CPU each;
  - 4 helpers, at about 0.08 s each;
  - the scanner, at 0.05 s;
  - main, at 0.02 s.
- The policy (`plan_with` in `src/parallel.rs`) runs teams of 2, as many as keep the live
  T-vectors near 0.7 of the L3. On this machine that is 4 teams.

So the throughput past 8 threads was never measured: the decoder declined the extra threads.

The cap rests on the i9 measurements of 28–29 October:
- 5 teams of 2 at 10 threads were 9.7% slower than 4 teams of 2;
- the L3 hit share of the walk's loads fell from 0.96 to 0.37 between 2 and 10 one-block workers.

Those runs left one direction open: **more threads on the same 4 tables.** The cooperative
decoder supports teams of 3 and 4 (`Cooperative<3>`, `Cooperative<4>`). On 29 October, 3 teams
of 3 at 10 threads gave 1,234 MB/s, against 1,223 MB/s for 4 teams of 2 at 8 threads, with one
table fewer. 4 teams of 3 or 4 were never run.

A thread count changes two things at once:
- the tables live at once (`teams`): the cache's load;
- the threads on each table (`k`): the work each table gets.

This run varies them separately.

## The run

`just bench-team-size` on the i9, at this branch's commit. Each cell is one `ab` arm at one
thread count:
- `mibz2-teamK` at T threads runs T // K teams of K, with the default's window;
- `mibz2`, the shipped default, runs 4 × 2 on this machine from 8 threads up.

**The window is matched** (amended after the Researcher's review, inbox #162 comment
6682038133). `BzReader::parallel` keeps `SLOTS_PER_THREAD` (4) blocks in flight a thread,
scaled to 4 a team when it plans teams: 16 for the reference. The older `mibz2-coopK` arms
keep 2 a team, 8 for every 4-team plan. Compared against the reference, they would have
changed the window as well as the plan, and the window alone was worth 8–10% at 4 threads
(#180). The new `mibz2-teamK` arms keep `SLOTS_PER_THREAD` a team, so each differs from
`mibz2` in its plan alone, and the control `mibz2-team2@8` differs in its entry point alone
(`BzReader::parallel_planned` against `BzReader::parallel`). The `coopK` arms are unchanged.

A K also changes more than the thread count: a team divides each block into 512 × K
segments, so per-segment costs and the walk's handoffs scale with K.

| threads | `mibz2` | `mibz2-team2` | `mibz2-team3` | `mibz2-team4` |
|--:|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 4 × 2 (**reference**) | 4 × 2 (control) | 2 × 3 | 2 × 4 |
| 10 | 4 × 2 | 5 × 2 | 3 × 3 | 2 × 4 |
| 12 | 4 × 2 | 6 × 2 | **4 × 3** | 3 × 4 |
| 16 | 4 × 2 | 8 × 2 | 5 × 3 | **4 × 4** |

The run's settings:
- **Rounds:** 20, the arms' order rotated and reversed round by round.
- **Thread counts:** each round runs every arm at each of 8, 10, 12 and 16 threads.
- **Placement:** unpinned, as users run the decoder.
- **Input:** the bench's Wikidata sample, 11.85 MB of bz2 decoding to 209.7 MB.
- **Recorded per run:** the output hash, wall time, CPU time, peak RSS, and each thread's CPU.

**The plan is read, not assumed.** The report counts each run's team leaders (`mibz2-N`) and
helpers (`mibz2-coop-*`) from the thread names, and lists any cell whose runs disagree.

**Counters are a separate pass.** After all the timed rounds, `perf stat` wraps `ab child` (one
decode) on six cells: the reference, 4 × 3, 4 × 4, 5 × 2, 6 × 2 and 3 × 3. Each reading is 5
decodes, and each cell gets 3 readings, the cells rotated. The counters are:
- cycles and instructions;
- the L3 hits and misses of retired loads;
- the cycles stalled with an L3 miss outstanding.

These are Skylake-family events, three general counters, so nothing should be multiplexed;
each reading's counting fractions are checked, not assumed. No timing is taken from this pass.

**A counter reading counts only if** each of its 5 decodes reported, without error, the timed
runs' hash, on the plan the timed runs showed (read from each decode's own JSON: `ab child`
can report a failed decode and still exit 0), and perf counted every event the whole time.
Otherwise the reading is kept in the JSON, marked invalid, and not shown as data.

**What the counters are not:** retired-load L3 hit share is not the T-vectors' residency,
and not the total traffic. The counters cover their own pass's decodes, not the timed runs.

**The occupancy pass** (a diagnostic, with no rule). After the counters, the occupancy
instrument (`examples/occupancy.rs`, built with `--features trace`) traces 4 × 2 at 8
threads, 4 × 3 at 12 and 4 × 4 at 16, at the default's window, 6 rounds each, as two arms
paired by round:
- **`spread`:** one CPU a core first, from the machine's own sibling map;
- **`packed`:** each core's siblings first.

The whole process is pinned, so the placement is known rather than left to the scheduler.
From the trace:
- **each block's decode** (`DecodeStart` to `DecodeEnd`) **and its shared inversion**
  (`InvertStart` to `InvertEnd`), whose difference is the team leader's work outside the
  shared phase;
- **the workers' waits for a job,** the scanner starving them;
- **the scanner's waits for a slot,** a full window;
- **the reader's waits for the next block in order.**

The instrument reports each traced mode's cost against untraced runs, and a trace whose mode
perturbs the run is not read. A cost interval containing no change means no effect was
detected, not that the cost is negligible; its width is reported with the trace.

Its limits (the Researcher's review, inbox #162 comment 6682204169):
- **Placement:** `taskset` fixes the whole process's allowed CPUs, not which thread runs
  where. On the i9, spread and packed offer 8 and 4 physical cores at 8 threads, 10 and 6 at
  12, and 10 and 8 at 16. That is a topology intervention, not a measurement of a helper's
  cost on a sibling.
- **The inversion span** (`InvertStart` to `InvertEnd`) wraps the whole inversion call,
  including the leader's own segment assembly. Decode minus inversion is the time outside
  that call, not all the leader's serial work. If the inversion dominates, assembly and setup
  are split from the cooperative work before helpers are blamed.
- **Job waits** include the receivers' lock, the handoff and backpressure, not only the
  scanner starving the workers. The instrument's own breakdown of them is used, and none of
  its queues measures the uncore's request occupancy.
- **Helpers:** a team's helpers (`mibz2-coop-*`) record no marks, so a helper's own waits,
  spins and completion skew are not traced. That tracing follows if the results make it
  decision-relevant.

Its readings are hypotheses for the next experiment.

**SMT.** The i9 has 10 physical cores:
- At 12 threads, at least 2 cores run two of the decoder's threads; at 16, at least 6.
- The 10-thread cells (5 × 2, 3 × 3, 2 × 4) can each have a core per thread. Unpinned, the
  scheduler may still place two of them on one core, so they are not a no-SMT control and give
  no quantitative bound on what sibling sharing costs. The occupancy pass's pinned arms are
  the placement comparison.

## The rule

Amended before any run after the Guardian's review (D026, inbox #161 comment 6681996710).
`bench/team_size.py report` checks every predicate below and prints each one; the table is
not the verdict.

**Primary comparisons:** 4 × 3 (`mibz2-team3@12`) and 4 × 4 (`mibz2-team4@16`), each against
4 × 2 (`mibz2@8`). The figure is the reference's wall over the cell's, paired by round: the
geometric mean of the 20 ratios and its two-sided 95% t-interval.

**A primary cell passes a run** where all of these hold:
- **Bytes:** every run of every cell in the run gives the same output hash and output length,
  and no run fails.
- **The grid:** every one of the 16 cells (4 arms × 8, 10, 12 and 16 threads) has rounds 0–19
  exactly once, with no failed run, and no cell outside the grid appears.
- **Complete:** the reference and the cell each have rounds 0–19 exactly once, and all 20
  pairs exist. A duplicate round, a failed run or a missing round fails the predicate; none
  is overwritten or dropped.
- **Plan:** every reference run shows 4 leaders and 4 helpers. Every run of the cell shows
  exactly its plan's leaders and helpers (4 and 8 for 4 × 3, 4 and 12 for 4 × 4). The counts
  are compared exactly, never rounded.
- **Mean:** at least 1.03. This is a requirement on the point estimate, not a claim that the
  true benefit is at least 3%.
- **Lower bound:** above 1.00.
- **The control:** the run is not flagged (below).

**Error rate:** only the beneficial tail of each two-sided 95% interval (2.5%) can produce a
pass, so the nominal chance that either primary passes when neither helps is at most 5%. That
holds under the paired-log model; repeating a run does not remove a systematic bias.

**Qualification needs confirmation:** a passing cell qualifies only if **the same cell**
passes the same predicates, within the adoption limits, in **one** second run of this recipe,
predeclared here: 20 rounds, the same grid, the same build. There is no switching to the
other primary, no pooling of the two runs, and no further run if the confirmation fails.

**The control and invalidation:** `mibz2-team2@8` runs the reference's plan and window through
another entry point (`BzReader::parallel_planned` against `BzReader::parallel`).
- **Flagged:** where its 95% interval against the reference lies wholly outside ±2% (lower
  bound above 1.02, or upper bound below 0.98), or the control has a duplicate, failed or
  missing round, fewer than 20 pairs, or a run not showing 4 × 2.
- **A flagged run is kept and reported,** with its figures, and is not read for qualification.
- **One replacement run is allowed in the whole experiment,** for the initial run or the
  confirmation, not one for each. A second flagged run, at either stage, stops the experiment,
  reported as inconclusive.
- **Every attempt is kept:** the recipe writes each to its own timestamped directory and
  never overwrites one.
- The two entry points can differ systematically. A flag invalidates the run; it does not
  show that the machine was noisy.

**Adoption limits,** for a cell that qualifies, each a ratio of medians over a run's 20
rounds, holding in both the initial run and the confirmation:
- the cell's median peak RSS over the reference's: at most 1.15;
- the cell's median CPU-seconds over the reference's: at most 1.5.

## The prediction

- **4 × 3 at 12 threads:** 1.00–1.12 against the reference. This is a guess from the 29 October
  3 × 3 cell. It is discounted for the two shared cores, and for the helpers' smaller share of
  each block (0.08 s against the leader's 0.14 s).
- **4 × 4 at 16 threads:** 0.95–1.08. Six cores' siblings shared.
- **5 × 2 and 6 × 2:** slower than the reference, as on 29 October.
- **The L3 hit share** at 4 tables changes little with k. It falls with the table count.

## What each outcome would mean

The pass or fail is the rule's. The readings after a fail are **hypotheses** for the next
experiment. Thread CPU and process-level counters cannot by themselves show a sibling's
cost, a serial leader or helpers' cache pressure.

- **4 × 3 or 4 × 4 qualifies:**
  - more threads on the same tables is a route past the cap;
  - next: a policy PR choosing k from the cores per table, measured again.
- **Both fail, and the 10-thread cells keep pace with the reference while the 12- and
  16-thread cells fall back:** consistent with sibling sharing as the cost. The test: the
  same plans pinned, siblings apart against siblings together.
- **Both fail, helper CPU rises with k, and leader CPU stays at about 0.14 s:** consistent
  with the leader's per-block work outside the shared inversion as the limit. The test: time
  each team's stages per block.
- **Both fail, and the L3 hit share or the stall share worsens with k at 4 tables:**
  consistent with the helpers adding cache pressure. The test: memory-controller traffic per
  output byte, or a smaller per-thread working set.
- **Both fail with little change in the counters:** consistent with a shared request or
  service capacity saturating: line-fill buffers, the queues to the uncore, or a core's
  resources shared between siblings. Pointer-walk work (AMAC) shows scaling saturating with
  little change in off-chip access counts; that is a counterexample to reading the counters
  alone, not a calibration for the i9. The test: placement, spread against packed, at fixed
  plans, and the queue occupancy per request.
