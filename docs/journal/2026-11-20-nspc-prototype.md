# NSPC prototype: non-splitting affine contraction as a walk kernel (negative)

**Question.** Is NSPC, a walk over capped run records each contracted by up to K affine LF
steps while its rows don't split, worth further work as an inverse-BWT kernel for the admitted
Wikidata blocks? The Python account (scratchpad `nspc_account.py`) predicted dependent walk
steps plus scans at 0.25-0.34x the K = 1 capped walk's, at a construction cost of 0.4-0.9
independent line touches per dependent step saved.

**Verdict: NOT WORTH IT** under the rule fixed before the data. Every configuration is above
1.0x Open on all six blocks; the best is 1.82-2.38x.

## The rule (fixed before the data)

- PROMISING: some single (M, K, tau) is byte-exact on all six blocks, and its total time is at
  most 0.95x Open's on at least 4 of 6 blocks, including at least one of 143, 207 and 210,
  with no block worse than 1.10x Open.
- NOT WORTH IT: every configuration is at least 1.0x Open on 4 or more blocks.
- Otherwise INCONCLUSIVE.

## Method

- `examples/nspc.rs` (feature `moonshot`, research only). The only `src/` change makes
  `Runs::of_column` public under `moonshot`, so the harness builds the 127-capped records from
  an exact column. The default build is unchanged.
- The six admitted columns (block 0, 2, 85, 143, 207, 210; r/n 0.057-0.143).
- **Arms.** Every arm's output is checked byte for byte against a dense-LF walk:
  - `dense`, the reference;
  - `plain` and `segmented`, the crate's column kernels;
  - `lazy` (`LazyCells`) and `open` (`OpenCells`, production's), from the 127-capped records;
  - NSPC at M in {32, 64, 128}, K in {1, 4, 8} and tau in {1, 2, 4}, from the same records;
  - the 8-byte record at M = 64, K = 4 (a `u32` word, and a `u32` meta field: target 18 bits,
    len-1 6, off 6, stride-1 2; `R_64 < 2^18` asserted).
- **NSPC's timing.** Construction covers the coalescing to maximal runs, the capped records,
  the codomain merge, the contraction and the packing. The walk is timed separately. Output is
  written backward with unconditional 8-byte (or 4-byte) stores.
- **Timing.** 41 rounds plus 2 warm-up rounds, one thread, `taskset -c 3`, on the shared
  4-core cloud VM (Xeon @ 2.1 GHz, 2 MB L2 a core). The arms' order rotates each round, and each
  arm keeps its buffers between rounds.
- **Statistics.** The medians, and each round's ratio to Open in the same round (median, and
  p10-p90 spread).
- **Counters.** `perf` and the PMU aren't available on the VM. The cachegrind counts are
  differenced between 5 and 1 rounds.
- **Not timed: Scalar-cap.** It is Astra E's fused front/Cells-lineage worker,
  `research/astra-e/front-fusion/cap-batch/worker/` at d0a42980, generated by a chain of
  `prepare.py` scripts. It isn't an L-to-text kernel this harness can call. For context only,
  the i9 measured it at 0.86-0.88x LazyCells' whole-decode wall at W = 7-10. Open is 0.83-1.04x
  LazyCells here.

## Why it loses (negative findings)

1. **The walk alone costs about as much as Open's whole kernel.** The best walk is 0.96-1.25x
   Open's total, and the 8-byte M64 K4 walk is 1.05-1.45x. Construction then adds another
   0.94-1.21x Open. The contraction works as the Python account said: the steps fall to
   180-275 k for 0.9 M rows. But each step is followed by a dependent scan through
   the target's capped records: 310-690 k scans at M128 and 235-430 k at M64. Those scans are
   dependent loads too, so the chain is steps + scans, about 0.55-1.07 a byte. Open emits more bytes a
   dependent load than that.
2. **Construction is not cheap.** Its O(r + R_M) passes are the codomain counting sort, the
   two-pointer merge and the contraction. Each contraction step is a random load of
   init_b/init_off and of the max-run arrays. Together they cost about Open's entire time.
   tau = 4 cuts construction a lot (fewer short records contracted) and loses little stride.
   tau = 1 with K = 8 is the worst NSPC configuration with contraction, up to 4.5x Open.
3. **The 8-byte record helps the walk by 10-17%,** a smaller table in L1/L2. Its total is still
   1.8-2.4x Open's.
4. **The instructions per byte are 2.3-2.5x Open's.** At these r/n the cost is per-step work
   and branchy scans, not cache misses. Cachegrind's LL (the host's 260 MB L3) has no misses
   for any arm, and the 2 MB L2 holds every table.

## Uncertainty and limits

- **The machine.** The VM is shared and has no PMU. On the i9 (opend: 9-21% slower here, 6-8%
  faster there) the ranking of close arms can flip. But a 1.8-2.4x gap on every block is far
  outside any host effect seen so far.
- **One thread only.** NSPC with several couriers was not built. Split walks would cut the
  walk's latency, but construction alone is already at about Open's total, so a parallel walk
  can't bring the total under 0.95x Open.
- **Not production-tuned.** Prefetch, SIMD construction and a fused build are not done. A
  different scan structure (a successor table instead of the forward scan) would remove the
  scans at the cost of more construction. None of these is likely to close a 2x gap.
