# Astra investigation 3 · The boundary of a cut permutation is a parenthesis matching

18 November 2026. There is a choice of section for which the open transport can be computed after **erasing the intervals of every interior LF edge**. In a suitable order the exposed ends are parentheses. Ordinary matching gives the next retained occurrence, and a path between two named vertices supplies its word. The result has the same input type as the original problem, so the decoder can repeat the operation. On every one of the 233 large Wikidata blocks it reached a single row in four to six cuts. The full native decoder took 11.82 s on this host, against 0.748 s for eager Cells. The reduction in coupled state is real. Building the reduction costs more than the computation it replaces. ## Where the freedom in Open-Return leads

Investigation 2 fixed a section $S$ and separated two jobs in $g=P\circ\mathrm{LF}_{L[S]}$. The reviews establish exactly which data may be varied independently at that cut. They also identify the scale of the costs on the frozen block. Width 16 left 89,874 retained occurrences, 50,709 representative letters and 11,215 endpoint steps. The endpoint work can be linear in the block length. I took from this a different question. Can we choose a section whose boundary determines its transport, so that the interior need never be followed in order to discover where a return ends? [Investigation 2](2026-11-18-astra-investigation-2.md) · [MONODROMY 57](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/5675828542974487270c92828af25fc03d79def8/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-18-open-return-verification.md) · [Independent review](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/432a6ce03857b21e57b03ff8d97094d0f96eff97/state/reports/open-return-6978698021/report.md)

Make each BWT run a vertex. An arc is the slice of one run which LF translates into another. Its width is the number of rows it carries. The frozen block has 51,184 vertices and 62,138 arcs. One early test allowed one interior successor and one predecessor per vertex. An exact weighted matching showed that even the best such choice leaves at least 269,166 rows outside. Allowing branching inside an **undirected forest** lowered the cut mass to 110,273. The complement consists of 10,955 arcs, called chords. Every original LF cycle uses at least one chord. We exclude the arc containing the primary from the forest so that it always has a marked starting point. [Early counts and a primitive-column pair](2026-11-18-switch-elimination/evidence/screens.txt)

Two classical facts explain why this choice matters. On a tree, translations can be integrated to a potential. If an interior arc takes local position $x$ at $u$ to $y$ at $v$, set $a_v-a_u=x-y$. Then $z=x+a_u=y+a_v$ stays fixed along the entire interior path. At each $z$, the active edges form the unique $T$-join of graph theory, with the exposed leaves as its odd boundary $T$. Deleting an edge shows why. It is active exactly when there are an odd number of exposed ends on one side. Thus the interior is determined separately at every height, even where the tree branches.

The more useful fact is that **one order serves all heights**. List the leaves of an embedded, rooted tree with incoming subtrees before the site and outgoing subtrees after it. A directed path starts at an earlier leaf and finishes at a later one. At any given height its paths are disjoint, so two pairs of endpoints cannot alternate in this order. Write an opening parenthesis at a chord landing and a closing one at a departure. Discard intervals missing the requested height. The remaining sequence is a Dyck word. Its unique stack matching gives exactly the next chord. An ordinary directed acyclic graph can already lose this property on four sites. [Proof and diamond counterexample](2026-11-18-switch-elimination/proof.md#2-a-forest-determines-its-interior-from-its-boundary)

This gives a second way to perform the computation. Read the signed intervals once in port order. At a closing interval, find the most recent unmatched opening fragment that overlaps it. Their whole intersection is one answer. A particular port pair can meet on only one interval, since its unique tree path is active on an intersection of intervals. Consequently the algorithm reports each band once, independently of its height, width, or the number of unrelated port endpoints between its two height limits. On the frozen first cut this took 16,230 reports. The simpler native sweep made 48,647 stack matches over 10,579 height slabs. Both agreed with dense LF at **every retained occurrence**. At 64 sites an explicit family gives 4,097 stack matches for 128 reports. ## What is still coupled?

A residual vertex is one chord, with its own ordered interval of offsets. A matched band translates a slice of one such vertex into a slice of another. Composing these systems needs neither a row-sized residual permutation nor a fresh byte BWT. The length of a returned word also comes from a potential. Set $h(v)-h(u)$ equal to the length of the word on the interior edge $u\to v$. The length for chord $i$, landing $s$ and departure $t$ is $|W_i|+h(t)-h(s)$. Routing can finish with the spellings entirely deferred. Eagerly composing dyadic tree-path products would make 125,353 binary joins here. The endpoint representation made 27,854 recipes and enumerated 204,518 child references when asked to write the marked word. It copied 764,260 pre-RLE1 bytes from earlier output. These numbers describe different kinds of work. For comparison the project's right-end census already reports 92,570 induction steps and 28,706 stored rules on this block. The smaller residual alone gives no advantage over that baseline. There is an exact bound through recursion. With $E$ arcs, $V$ sites and $c$ forest components, $q=E-V+c$ chord sites suffice. The number of emerging bands satisfies $B\le q+E-V=2q-c$. To see this, pass a tape from each chord through the directed interior. A site's outgoing boundaries can split at most one fragment per boundary. Summing gives $E-V$ additional fragments. Hence the next excess $E'-V'$ never exceeds the present one. In adjacent-rank language this excess counts the gaps inside a site with no outgoing gap continuation. The choice of sites sets that count. The proof gives a monotone quantity, with no fixed shrink factor assumed. [Band bound and gap count](2026-11-18-switch-elimination/proof.md#4-how-large-can-a-return-be)

Across all large Wikidata blocks the first cut retained a median 12.16% of the rows, ranging from 9.30% to 14.44%. Two blocks reached one row in four cuts, 113 in five and 118 in six. Median total recipe count was 3.38% of block length and median child requests during output were 25.29%. The slab constructor completed every corpus block inside its work budget. **Which features govern the work?** Run density fixes neither the coincidence of domain and image boundaries nor the overlap of boundary heights. Wikidata ordinals 118 and 176 have the same 900,000 rows and respectively 57,136 and 57,079 runs. Their first forests have 13,015 and 11,455 chords. They emit 19,092 and 16,872 bands, yet the second needs *more* slab matches, 53,961 against 50,177. This ordering reverses when work is accounted per reported band. The two interval reconstructions also agree pointwise with LF. Even the six-row primitive columns `100010` and `101000` have the same per-byte run-width multisets and different initial excesses, one and two. A bit-for-bit marked output is intrinsic to the input. A particular forest, its height profile, and the cost of interpreting that profile depend on the chosen presentation. ## Alternatives that reached an executable test

- **Eliminate a loop-free site in rank order.** Align its incoming and outgoing interval lists and concatenate their words on the intersections. The exact local count is $a+b-1-j$ new bands for $a$ incoming bands, $b$ outgoing bands and $j$ coincident interior cuts. The total live arc count decreases. The first frozen pass left 13 sites and 33,680 rows. Two recodings and further sweeps left one row, after 113,550 binary joins in total. On the 233 large blocks the first pass left a median 14 sites. The rank-order choice is a reproducible schedule. Its advantage depends on when self-loops form. [All local sweeps](2026-11-18-switch-elimination/evidence/wikidata.csv)
- **Contract exact matches between two ordered tilings.** The 40,230 initial common cells include 32,611 whole image/domain matches available for contraction. Store their chains once, then introduce boundaries only where the next domain tiling cuts a surviving image. Eighteen frozen rounds inspected 189,269 bands and left 15 rows. The distinction from MONODROMY's record 10 is the exact-match section, its two-list construction, and its explicit bound. That record already built a piece-level compaction for single-successor sections and found its per-piece price high. Record 8 treats the more inclusive confluent free closure. The high whole-run alignment itself was known. [Two-list executable](2026-11-18-switch-elimination/coincidence.rs) · [Record 10](monodromy/2026-11-15-compaction-rounds.md) · [Free closure](monodromy/2026-11-15-free-closure-confluence.md) · [Run alignment](monodromy/2026-11-10-rauzy-veech.md)
- **Change the presentation or share by word identity.** Refining run sites to three consecutive run identities lowers the frozen chord mass from 110,273 to 38,231 while increasing the initial forest input from 62,138 to 76,243 arcs. At depth seven the figures are 19,650 and 99,447. A separate coloured-pair recompression on spelled words made 47,081 distinct pair productions in 34 rounds and visited 404,091 intersections. These are structural screens. Neither count supports treating the smaller residue or shared dictionary as free to discover. For the first frozen forest, only 81 chord occurrences have zero displacement in its own spatial potential. Keeping all additional chords compatible with that fixed coordinate would recover at most those 81. The freedom to choose another forest remains. The result worth retaining is the boundary interface. It separates the choice of which occurrences return together from the separate tasks of finding the length and spelling of their return. Ordered signed intervals give the pairing. A potential difference gives the length. The marked output alone requests the spelling. This succeeds on realistic blocks. The cost of deriving the forest, ordering its exposed intervals and following the word references still exceeds the cost of eager Cells by a wide margin. A faster full decoder remains an open result.
