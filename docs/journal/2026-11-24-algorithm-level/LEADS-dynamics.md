## Lead 1: renormalisation, for contraction and for "a recurrence among the induction's own steps"

**First-return maps are already ours.** Inducing on a section Y and recording returns is Rauzy–Veech's step, and landmark induction's (record 114; journal §38).

**Feigenbaum's renormalisation goes one step further.** The renormalisation operator 𝓡 squares a map, restricts it to a subinterval and rescales: 𝓡f = α⁻¹ ∘ f ∘ f ∘ α. It finds that the result is *again a map of the same kind*. The period-doubling cascade is the orbit of 𝓡, with a fixed point and universal constants.

**The question for us.** Is there an operator on decoding problems, (column, LF), that does three things?
- It induces or squares.
- It *rescales back into a problem of the same kind*, with a smaller or simpler description: an affine relabelling of rows, plus a relabelling of the alphabet.
- It has a fixed point, or an eventually periodic orbit, on structured data.

If so, the recursion's steps are generated by a rule rather than computed one by one. That is the Pell-rule direction made precise.

**What to check:**
- What "rescale" can mean for a permutation on rows.
- Whether templated JSON gives an approximately self-similar induced system. The scaffold repeats; the fields are the "noise".
- Whether renormalisation can skip work on the novel fields, or only on the scaffold. Rauzy paths of self-similar systems are eventually periodic, which is standard (record 113, sense D). On independent random digits, sense D was argued to give nothing. Say what renormalisation changes about that, if anything.

## Lead 2: dual-number evaluation, for enrichment

**What Fork does.** Fork's VM evaluates each expression over a richer number system, (a, a′) with (a, a′)(b, b′) = (ab, a′b + ab′). The derivative then comes out with the value, at a small constant cost per operation.

**The question for us.** Is there a "number system" the decode steps could run over (rows, ranks, run records, or the MTF and Huffman state), so that a useful rider comes out with the ordinary result at constant extra cost per step? Candidates to examine:
- a pair (row, something) whose second component composes under LF the way a′ composes under multiplication;
- a semiring in which composing steps also accumulates information a later stage needs.

**The test (ESSENCE).**
- The rider must be cheaply acquired.
- It must not restate the inversion. The text position p does not count; nor does a fingerprint that decodes nothing (N-18).
- It must make a later stage cheaper or unnecessary.
