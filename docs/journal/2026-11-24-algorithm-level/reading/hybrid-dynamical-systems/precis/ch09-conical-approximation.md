# Chapter 9: Conical approximation and asymptotic stability (pp. 185–197)

This chapter generalises "linearisation" to hybrid systems. Near a point $\xi$, the flow and jump sets are replaced by their tangent cones and the flow and jump maps by a constant or linear approximation. The main theorem says that if the origin is pre-asymptotically stable for this conical approximation, then $\xi$ is locally pre-asymptotically stable for the original system. The tool behind it is *homogeneity* with respect to dilations: in a homogeneous system, local behaviour determines global behaviour, the degree of homogeneity decides whether convergence takes finite, exponential or unbounded time, and pAS survives perturbations that scale with the state. The robustness theory of Chapter 7 (Theorem 7.12, Lemma 7.20) carries the main proof.

## 9.1 Homogeneous hybrid systems (p. 185)

- **Dilation (9.1) (p. 185).** A family of diagonal linear maps $M_\lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^{r_1},\dots,\lambda^{r_n})$, $\lambda>0$, with positive weights $r_i$. The *standard* dilation has all $r_i=1$, i.e. $M_\lambda x=\lambda x$.
- **Definition 9.1 (homogeneous hybrid system, p. 185).** $\mathcal{H}$ is homogeneous of degree $d\in\mathbb{R}$ with respect to $M_\lambda$ if, for all $\lambda>0$, $C$ and $D$ are invariant under $M_\lambda$, $F(M_\lambda x)=\lambda^d M_\lambda F(x)$ on $C$, and $G(M_\lambda x)=M_\lambda G(x)$ on $D$. Note the asymmetry: the jump map always has "degree zero" (it commutes with the dilation), while the flow map may carry a factor $\lambda^d$. Examples: conical $C$, $D$ with linear $F$ and $G$ have degree $0$ (standard dilation); conical sets, linear $G$ and *constant* $F$ have degree $-1$.
- **Example 9.2 (bouncing ball, p. 186).** The bouncing ball is homogeneous of degree $-1$ for the non-standard dilation $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^2,\lambda)$: height scales as the square of velocity, as dimensional analysis suggests.
- **Time-scaled system (9.2) (p. 187).** $\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,d}$ multiplies the flow map by $\lambda^{-d}$ and leaves jumps unchanged, so it flows faster or slower.
- **Lemma 9.3 (dilation of solutions, p. 187).** $\psi=M_\lambda\phi$ is a hybrid arc on the same domain. If $\mathcal{H}$ is homogeneous of degree $d$, then $\phi$ solves $\mathcal{H}$ if and only if $\psi$ solves $\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,d}$. Equivalently, scaling the state by $M_\lambda$ amounts to rescaling ordinary time by $\lambda^{-d}$, with the jump count unchanged.
- **Homogeneous quasinorm (p. 187).** A proper indicator $\omega$ of $0$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\omega(M_\lambda x)=\lambda\omega(x)$. Any norm works for the standard dilation; in general $\big(\sum_i|x_i|^{2/r_i}\big)^{1/2}$ works. $\mathbb{B}_\omega$ is its unit sublevel set.
- **Proposition 9.4 (from local information to global pAS, p. 188).** If $\mathcal{H}$ is homogeneous of degree $d$, and there are $R>r>0$, $m>0$ and a homogeneous quasinorm $\omega$ such that every solution starting on the sphere $\omega=r$ either (i) has a domain of length at most $m$ and stays within $\omega\le R$, or (ii) reaches $\omega\le r/2$ by some hybrid time $(T,J)$ with $T+J\le m$ while staying within $\omega\le R$ up to then, then $0$ is (globally) pAS. The proof rescales any solution starting in a dyadic shell $2^{i-1}r\le\omega\le2^ir$ onto the sphere $\omega=r$ using Lemma 9.3. The hybrid-time bound $m$ rescales by a factor depending on $\lambda^{d}$, so the halving-in-bounded-time property propagates to every scale.
- **Convergence time and degree (pp. 188–189).** For ODEs, negative degree gives finite-time convergence (e.g. $\dot z=-z^{1/3}$), degree zero gives exponential convergence, and positive degree gives convergence slower than exponential that never reaches the origin (e.g. $\dot z=-z^3$). For a nominally well-posed homogeneous hybrid system with pAS origin, let $T(\phi)$ be the supremum of ordinary times at which $\phi$ is away from the origin. Then:
  - if $d<0$, $T(\phi)$ can be made arbitrarily small by starting close enough to the origin, and every solution from the basin (other than the origin) has $T(\phi)<\infty$. With further conditions on the jumps this forces Zeno behaviour, which is how one shows the bouncing ball's solutions are Zeno;
  - if $d>0$ and every solution from a nonzero point flows for some positive time before reaching $0$, then $T(\phi)=\infty$ for all of them.
  The authors state these as consequences of Lemma 9.3 and Proposition 9.4-style arguments, without proof.

## 9.2 Homogeneity and perturbations (p. 189)

The aim is a homogeneous version of Theorem 7.21: in a homogeneous system, pAS should be robust to perturbations whose size scales with the state, so that they need not vanish relative to the state near the origin.

- **Definition 9.5 (homogeneous perturbation of size $\eta$, p. 189).** Like the $\rho$-perturbation of Definition 6.27, but the inflation ball around $x$ is $\eta M_{\omega(x)}\mathbb{B}_\omega$, a dilated quasinorm ball whose size scales with $\omega(x)$. The extra flow velocity is scaled further by $\omega(x)^d$. The jump map perturbs the input this way and adds a ball of the same scaled size to the output, scaled at $x$ rather than at the image point.
- **Proposition 9.6 (homogeneity is preserved, p. 190).** If $\mathcal{H}$ is homogeneous of degree $d$ with respect to $M_\lambda$, so is each $\mathcal{H}^h_\eta$. It rests on $M_{\omega(M_\lambda x)}=M_\lambda M_{\omega(x)}$.
- **Example 9.7 (unbounded perturbations, p. 190).** For a degree $-2$ system under the dilation $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda^3)$, the factor $\omega^d(x)M_{\omega(x)}$ has an entry $\omega(x)^{-1}$, which is unbounded near the origin. A homogeneous perturbation of a system with negative degree can therefore make the flow map fail to be locally bounded near $0$.
- **Remark (p. 191).** Perturbing jump outputs with the dilation evaluated at the *output* point, following Definition 6.27 literally, also gives a homogeneous map, but it would break Lemma 9.10(c), so it is not used.
- **Theorem 9.8 (homogeneous perturbations and pAS, p. 191).** If $\mathcal{H}$ is well-posed, homogeneous of degree $d$, and $0$ is pAS, then for some $\eta>0$ the origin is pAS for $\mathcal{H}^h_\eta$. Proof: Theorem 7.12 gives a $\mathcal{KL}$ bound and Lemma 7.20 gives semiglobal practical robustness with a constant perturbation radius. On the annulus $1/2\le\omega\le M$ a small enough $\eta$ makes the homogeneous perturbation smaller than that radius. Solutions starting on $\omega\in[1/2,1]$ therefore either halve their quasinorm within hybrid time $m$ while staying bounded, or terminate within that time, and Proposition 9.4 globalises this using the homogeneity of the perturbed system.

## 9.3 Conical approximation and stability (p. 192)

The classical fact being generalised: if $\xi$ is AS for the linearisation $\dot z=f'(\xi)(z-\xi)$, it is AS for $\dot z=f(z)$. In a hybrid system a point can be pAS even where $f(\xi)\neq0$, because the flow can push into the jump set. A point in the jump set, on the other hand, must be fixed by $g$.

- **Definition 9.9 (conical approximation, p. 192).** For $\mathcal{H}=(C,f,D,g)$ with single-valued maps and a point $\xi$, assume: if $\xi\in C$, either $f(\xi)\neq0$ with $f$ continuous at $\xi$, or $f$ is $C^1$ near $\xi$; if $\xi\in D$, $g(\xi)=\xi$ and $g$ is $C^1$ near $\xi$. Then $\mathrm{CA}(\mathcal{H},\xi)$ has flow set $T_C(\xi)$ and jump set $T_D(\xi)$ (tangent cones, empty when $\xi$ is not in the set); flow map $f_{loc}\equiv f(\xi)$ if $f(\xi)\neq0$, otherwise $f_{loc}(x)=f'(\xi)x$; jump map $g_{loc}(x)=g'(\xi)x$. Coordinates are centred at $\xi$.
- **Lemma 9.10 (properties, p. 193).** (a) $\mathrm{CA}(\mathcal{H},\xi)$ satisfies the hybrid basic conditions (tangent cones are closed) and so is well-posed (Theorem 6.30). (b) It is homogeneous for the standard dilation, of degree $-1$ if $f(\xi)\neq0$ and degree $0$ otherwise. (c) For every $\eta>0$ there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $\xi$ in which the original data, shifted by $\xi$, is contained in the size-$\eta$ homogeneous perturbation of the approximation's data. That is, $f(x)\in(f_{loc})^h_\eta(x-\xi)$, $C\cap U\subset\xi+(C_{loc})^h_\eta$, and likewise for $g$ and $D$. The set inclusion follows because the inflated cone contains $T_C(\xi)\setminus\{0\}$ in its interior, together with the definition of the tangent cone; the map inclusions follow from continuity or differentiability at $\xi$.
- **Theorem 9.11 (conical approximation and pAS, p. 194).** If $0$ is (globally) pAS for $\mathrm{CA}(\mathcal{H},\xi)$, then $\xi$ is locally pAS for $\mathcal{H}$. Proof chain: Lemma 9.10(a),(b), then Proposition 9.4 (local and global pAS coincide for the homogeneous approximation), then Theorem 9.8 (some homogeneous perturbation keeps pAS), then Lemma 9.10(c) (near $\xi$, solutions of $\mathcal{H}$ are solutions of that perturbation).
- **Example 9.12 (p. 194).** The system of Example 7.2 at $\xi=0$: since $f(0)=(1,1)\neq0$, the approximation has constant flow $(1,1)$ on the quadrant $\{x_1\le0,\ x_2\ge0\}$ and jumps $x\mapsto(-x_2/2,0)$ from the half-axis $\{x_1=0,\ x_2\ge0\}$ (Figure 9.1). Global pAS of the approximation is easy to see directly, or with $V(x)=2x_2-3x_1$ in Theorem 3.18. Theorem 9.11 then gives local pAS of the original far more quickly than Example 7.2's direct analysis.
- **Example 9.13 (p. 194).** Flow $(1,1)$ on the thin region $\{x_2\ge0,\ 0\le x_1\le x_2^2\}$, jumps $x\mapsto(0,x_2/2)$ from the parabola. Both tangent cones collapse to the half-line $\{x_1=0,\ x_2\ge0\}$, on which the constant flow points outward, so the approximation has only discrete solutions, $\phi(0,j)=\phi(0,0)/2^j$. It is globally pAS, so the truly hybrid original is locally pAS; the basin within $C\cup D$ is $\{x_2<x_1+1/4\}$.
- **Example 9.14 (H pAS, approximation not attractive, p. 195).** The same sets with rotational flow $(x_2,-x_1)$ and jump $(0,x_2)$. The original is pAS, but the approximation's discrete solutions are constant, so it is stable and not pre-attractive. The theorem gives only a sufficient condition, exactly as linearisation does for ODEs.
- **Example 9.15 (H pAS, approximation unstable, p. 196).** Flow $\dot x=x$ constrained to the parabola $x_2=x_1^2$, no jumps. The only solution that flows stays at the origin, so $0$ is pAS. The tangent cone is the $x_1$-axis, on which solutions $e^t\phi(0,0)$ diverge. The bouncing ball, with $f_{loc}=(0,-\gamma)$ and $C_{loc}=C$ if the discontinuity at $0$ is ignored, is another case: the approximation flows forever from every point, including the origin.
- **Beyond standard dilations (p. 196).** Approximations with set-valued maps or non-standard dilations are possible. **Example 9.16 (p. 196)** shows only the ODE case: $f=f_1+f_2$ with $f_1$ homogeneous of degree $2$ and $f_2$ of degree $5$ for $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda^4)$, so AS of $\dot z=f_1(z)$ (the lowest-degree part) implies AS of $\dot z=f(z)$.

## 9.4 Notes (p. 196)

Linearisation goes back to Lyapunov; homogeneous approximation to Malkin, Massera and Hahn, and with non-standard dilations to Hermes and Rosier; control designs using homogeneity to Kawski, Grüne and Tuna. Homogeneous hybrid systems are from Tuna–Teel. Tangent cones are standard in viability theory (Aubin), conical approximations of inclusions appear in Frankowska and Smirnov, and Ancona uses non-standard homogeneous approximations of sets. Related linearisation results exist for switching systems (Liberzon) and impulsive systems (Lakshmikantham et al.). An earlier approximation result for hybrid automata is due to Lygeros et al., and approximation-based Zeno detection to Ames et al. Theorem 9.11 is from Goebel–Teel, with a generalised-homogeneity version in a later Goebel–Teel paper.

## Key ideas to carry away

- A homogeneous hybrid system looks the same at every scale up to a change in the speed of flow: scaling the state by $M_\lambda$ is equivalent to multiplying the flow speed by $\lambda^{-d}$, with jumps unchanged (Lemma 9.3).
- For homogeneous systems, a "halve the size in bounded hybrid time" check on one sphere gives global pAS (Proposition 9.4).
- The degree governs convergence time: negative degree gives finite time and possibly Zeno behaviour, zero degree exponential convergence, positive degree convergence that never completes in finite time if any flow occurs (pp. 188–189).
- Homogeneous systems tolerate perturbations proportional to their own scale (Theorem 9.8). This is the hybrid version of robustness to higher-order terms.
- Linearisation generalises with tangent cones of the sets and a constant (if $f(\xi)\neq0$) or linear (if $f(\xi)=0$) flow approximation. pAS of the cone system gives local pAS of the original (Theorem 9.11), often much more simply (Examples 9.12, 9.13).
- The test is only sufficient: the approximation can be non-attractive or even unstable when the original is pAS (Examples 9.14, 9.15).
