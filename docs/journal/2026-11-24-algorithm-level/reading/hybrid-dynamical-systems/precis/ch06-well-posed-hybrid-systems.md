# Chapter 6: Well-posed hybrid systems and their properties (pp. 117–137)

The chapter replaces the classical idea of well-posedness (existence, uniqueness, continuous dependence) with one suited to hybrid systems: graphical limits of solutions should again be solutions, or else blow up in finite hybrid time. *Nominal* well-posedness asks this of sequences of unperturbed solutions; *well-posedness* asks it also of solutions to systems perturbed by an inflation that shrinks to zero. The central result is that three mild conditions on the data, the hybrid basic conditions (closed $C$ and $D$; osc, locally bounded, convex-valued $F$; osc, locally bounded $G$), guarantee well-posedness. The chapter then derives the structural consequences used throughout the stability and invariance theory of Chapters 7 and 8: local boundedness of solution sets, upper semicontinuous dependence on initial conditions and on perturbations, compact reachable sets, invariance of $\omega$-limit sets, robust non-Zenoness, and the identity between solutions, Hermes solutions and Krasovskii solutions for well-posed systems.

Notation used below: $\mathcal S_{\mathcal H}(S)$ is the set of maximal solutions from $S$; $|x|_{\mathcal A}$ is the distance to $\mathcal A$; $\mathrm{length}(E)=\sup_tE+\sup_jE$.

## 6.1 Nominally well-posed hybrid systems (p. 117)

- **Theorem 6.1** (p. 117). A restatement of Theorem 5.7: any sequence of hybrid arcs whose initial points lie in a fixed compact set has a graphically convergent subsequence, whose limit (a set-valued map on $\mathbb R^2$) has $(0,0)$ in its domain. The limit need not be a solution.
- **Definition 6.2** (p. 117–118). $\mathcal H$ is *nominally well-posed* if, for every graphically convergent sequence of solutions with initial points converging to some $\xi$: (a) when the sequence is locally eventually bounded, the lengths of the domains converge (possibly to $\infty$) and the graphical limit is a solution from $\xi$ whose length is the limit of the lengths; (b) when it is not, there is a finite $m>0$ at which the arcs reach arbitrarily large values ($|\varphi_i(t_i,j_i)|\to\infty$ along suitable points), and the graphical limit truncated to $t+j<m$ is a maximal solution of length $m$ that is unbounded as it approaches the end of its domain. In short: limits are solutions, and unboundedness in the sequence shows up as finite-time blow-up of the limit.
- **Example 6.3** (p. 118–119). For $\dot z=z^2$: initial values $z_i(0)\downarrow 0$ give solutions each blowing up at $1/z_i(0)$, yet the sequence is locally eventually bounded and converges (graphically and uniformly on compacts) to $z\equiv0$, with domain lengths tending to $\infty$; this is case (a). Initial values $1+a_i$, $a_i\to0$, give a sequence that is not locally eventually bounded; it converges graphically to the solution from $1$, which blows up at $t=1$, matching case (b).
- Failure examples (p. 119). With $F\equiv0$, $D=\emptyset$ and $C$ not closed, constant complete solutions from points of $C$ converging to a point of $\overline C\setminus C$ converge uniformly to a constant arc that is not a solution, violating (a).
- **Example 6.4** (p. 119–120). In $\mathbb R^3$, the flow set requires $x_1\ge1$, $x_2\in(0,1/x_1]$, $x_3\in\{-1,1\}$; flow makes $x_1$ blow up like $(1-t)^{-1}$ while $x_3 = 1$: the flow map is $\dot x_1 = x_3 x_1^2$, other coordinates fixed (corrected 25 November: the earlier $\dot x_1 = x_1^2$ could not decay after the jump; $x_3 x_1^2$ is verified against both stated solution pieces, $(1-t)^{-1}$ with $x_3 = 1$ and $(2\varepsilon - 1 + t)^{-1}$ with $x_3 = -1$); the jump set is the curve $x_2=1/x_1$ with $x_3=1$, and jumps flip $x_3$ to $-1$. From $(1,\varepsilon,1)$ the solution flows until $x_1=1/\varepsilon$, jumps, and then $x_1$ decays. As $\varepsilon\to0$ the graphical limit has a domain with a gap at $t=1$ (not a hybrid time domain), and the sequence is not locally eventually bounded. Case (b) would require the limit truncated before $t=1$ to be a solution, but it has $x_2=0$, outside $C$. So $\mathcal H$ is not nominally well-posed; replacing $C$ by its closure would make it so. Figure 6.1 (p. 121) sketches convergence to an ordinary solution, of Zeno solutions to an eventually discrete solution, and of a non-locally-bounded sequence to an unbounded solution.

## 6.2 Basic assumptions on the data (p. 120)

- **Assumption 6.5** (p. 120). *Hybrid basic conditions*: (A1) $C$ and $D$ closed in $\mathbb R^n$; (A2) $F$ osc and locally bounded relative to $C$, $C\subset\mathrm{dom}\,F$, and $F(x)$ convex for every $x\in C$; (A3) $G$ osc and locally bounded relative to $D$, and $D\subset\mathrm{dom}\,G$. Continuous ODEs and difference equations, constrained to closed sets or not, satisfy them.
- **Example 6.6** (p. 121). If $F$ and $G$ are locally bounded relative to $C$ and $D$, the Krasovskii regularisation $\widehat{\mathcal H}$ of Definition 4.13 satisfies Assumption 6.5: the sets are closed by construction and the maps are osc and locally bounded by Lemma 5.16.
- **Example 6.7** (p. 121–122). A hybrid automaton (Section 1.4) yields a system satisfying Assumption 6.5 if every mode's domain and every guard set are closed, every mode's flow map is continuous on its domain, and every reset map is continuous on its guard; this follows because finite unions of closed sets are closed and continuous maps have closed graphs (Lemma 5.10).
- **Theorem 6.8** (p. 122). Assumption 6.5 implies nominal well-posedness. (Stated without proof as a special case of Theorem 6.30.) The regularised systems of Examples 4.14 and 4.15 and the zero-crossing model of Example 4.18 are therefore nominally well-posed; e.g. bouncing-ball solutions from initial heights decreasing to $0$ converge graphically to the discrete solution sitting at the origin (Figure 6.2, p. 123).
- **Lemma 6.9** (p. 122–123). Partial necessity: a nominally well-posed system has $D$ closed and $G$ satisfying (A3). The argument applies Definition 6.2 to two-point solutions that jump once from $x_i\in D$ to $y_i\in G(x_i)$: unbounded $y_i$ would force a length-2 solution to blow up, which is impossible, and convergent pairs force the limit pair to be a jump.
- Non-necessity for $C$ and $F$ (p. 123). Two examples are nominally well-posed while violating (A1) or (A2): a flow on all of $\mathbb R$ with velocity $1$ for $x<0$ and $2$ for $x\ge0$ (not osc); and flow at unit speed on $C=(-\infty,0)$ with $D=[0,\infty)$ (not closed). In both, solutions coincide with Krasovskii solutions.

### 6.2.1 Definition and existence of solutions, revisited (p. 124)

- With $C$ closed, the solution conditions simplify to (S1\*) $\varphi(t,j)\in C$ and $\dot\varphi\in F(\varphi)$ for almost all $t$ in each flow interval (equation (6.1)), and (S2\*) at each jump, $\varphi(t,j)\in D$ and $\varphi(t,j+1)\in G(\varphi(t,j))$ (equation (6.2)), since membership on the interior, everywhere, and almost everywhere coincide by continuity.
- **Proposition 6.10** (p. 124). Under Assumption 6.5, for $\xi\in C\cup D$, a nontrivial solution exists from $\xi$ if $\xi\in D$ or if (VC) holds: $F(x)\cap T_C(x)\neq\emptyset$ for all $x\in C$ near $\xi$. If (VC) holds at every point of $C\setminus D$, nontrivial solutions exist from every point of $C\cup D$, and every maximal solution is exactly one of: (a) complete; (b) bounded domain ending with a nondegenerate flow interval on which it blows up in finite time; (c) ending at a point outside $C\cup D$. Case (c) is excluded when $G(D)\subset C\cup D$.
- **Example 6.11** (p. 125). For the regularised bouncing ball, (VC) is checked: inside $\{x_1>0\}$ the tangent cone is the whole plane; on $\{x_1=0\}$ outside $\widehat D$ one has $x_2>0$ and the velocity points into the half-plane. (VC) fails on $\widehat C\cap\widehat D$, but those points are in $\widehat D$. Since $\widehat G(\widehat D)\subset\widehat C\cup\widehat D$, case (c) is excluded; boundedness of solutions (argued separately) excludes (b), so all maximal solutions are complete.

## 6.3 Consequences of nominal well-posedness (p. 125)

### 6.3.1 Structure of sets of solutions (p. 125)

- **Definition 6.12** (p. 125). $\mathcal H$ is *pre-forward complete* from $S$ if every maximal solution from $S$ is bounded or complete (no finite-time blow-up).
- **Proposition 6.13** (p. 125–126). If $\mathcal H$ is nominally well-posed and pre-forward complete from compact $K$, then for every $m>0$ there are $\delta>0$ and a compact $K'$ containing $\varphi(t,j)$ for all solutions from $K+\delta\mathbb B$ and all $t+j<m$. In particular, solutions from $K$ are locally uniformly bounded in hybrid time. (Proof by contradiction via Definition 6.2(b).)
- **Proposition 6.14** (p. 126). Under the same hypotheses, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and $\tau\ge0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that every solution from $K+\delta\mathbb B$ is $(\tau,\varepsilon)$-close to some solution from $K$. This is outer (upper) semicontinuous dependence on initial conditions, uniform over the solution set; it does not say every solution from $K$ is approximated (Figure 6.3).

### 6.3.2 Reachable sets (p. 127)

- **Definition 6.15** (p. 127). $\mathcal R_{\le\tau}(S)$ is the set of values $\varphi(t,j)$ of solutions from $S$ with $t+j\le\tau$; $\mathcal R_{<\tau}$, $\mathcal R_{\ge\tau}$ are analogous; $\mathcal R(S)$ is the union over all $\tau$. Useful for robust stability arguments (Chapter 7) and for verification.
- **Lemma 6.16** (p. 127). For nominally well-posed $\mathcal H$: (a) for compact $K\subset\mathrm{int}\,K'$ there is $\tau\in(0,1)$ with $\mathcal R_{\le\tau}(K)\subset K'$ (solutions cannot leave a neighbourhood instantly; otherwise the graphical limit would be multivalued at $(0,0)$); (b) if $\mathcal H$ is pre-forward complete from compact $K$, every $\mathcal R_{\le\tau}(K)$ is compact.

### 6.3.3 ω-limit sets (p. 127)

- **Definition 6.17** (p. 127). $\Omega(\varphi)$: all limits of $\varphi(t_i,j_i)$ along points of the domain with $t_i+j_i\to\infty$.
- **Example 6.18** (p. 128). An arc that flows then decays exponentially has $\Omega=\{0\}$; an arc with bounded domain has $\Omega=\emptyset$; a saw-tooth $t-j$ on $[j,j+1]$ has $\Omega=[0,1]$.
- Properties (p. 128): $\Omega(\varphi)$ is closed; nonempty for complete $\varphi$ unless $|\varphi|\to\infty$; nonempty and compact for precompact (complete and bounded) $\varphi$. Equation (6.3): $\Omega(\varphi)$ is the set limit of the tails $\{\varphi(t,j):t+j\ge\tau_i\}$ for any $\tau_i\to\infty$.
- **Definition 6.19** (p. 128). A set $S$ is *weakly forward invariant* if from each of its points some complete maximal solution stays in $S$; *weakly backward invariant* if each of its points, for every horizon $\tau$, is reached at hybrid time at least $\tau$ by some solution that started in $S$ and stayed in $S$ until then; *weakly invariant* if both. Equivalently (p. 129), each point is reached arbitrarily late by a complete solution lying entirely in $S$.
- **Example 6.20** (p. 129). On the unit circle, flow on the upper half (rotation) and jump on the lower half (a quarter-turn map $g(x)=(x_2,-x_1)$). The whole circle is weakly (indeed strongly) invariant; the upper half plus the image $g(D)$ is weakly forward but not weakly backward invariant (points in the open third quadrant cannot be reached while staying in it). **Erratum (p. 129, line 7 of the example):** for the third set, $S_1$, the authors withdraw the printed claim that it is weakly backward invariant and weakly invariant: it is weakly (indeed strongly) *forward* invariant, and not weakly backward invariant, since points in the interior of the third and fourth quadrants cannot be reached for arbitrarily large $\tau$.
- **Proposition 6.21** (p. 129–130). If $\mathcal H$ is nominally well-posed and $\varphi$ is a precompact solution, then $\Omega(\varphi)$ is weakly invariant, with both directions witnessed by complete solutions, and $|\varphi(t,j)|_{\Omega(\varphi)}\to0$. (Proof: shift the solution in hybrid time, extract a graphically convergent subsequence, use nominal well-posedness and (6.3).) This is the basis of the invariance principles of Chapter 8.
- **Corollary 6.22** (p. 130). For precompact $\varphi$: (a) if it has infinitely many jumps and the flow intervals shrink to zero length, the weak invariance can be witnessed by discrete solutions; (b) if all but finitely many flow intervals have length at least some $\tau>0$, then at each $\xi\in\Omega(\varphi)$ either forward invariance is witnessed by a solution flowing from $\xi$ or backward invariance by one flowing into $\xi$.
- **Definition 6.23** (p. 130). $\Omega(S)$: all limits of $\varphi_i(t_i,j_i)$ over solutions from $S$ with $t_i+j_i\to\infty$. Equation (6.4): $\Omega(S)=\lim_i\mathcal R_{\ge\tau_i}(S)$ for any $\tau_i\to\infty$; hence it is closed.
- **Definition 6.24** (p. 131). A compact $\mathcal A$ is *uniformly pre-attractive* from $S$ if all maximal solutions from $S$ are bounded and, for each $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\tau$ beyond which every such solution is within $\varepsilon$ of $\mathcal A$.
- **Definition 6.25** (p. 131). $S$ is *strongly forward pre-invariant* if every maximal solution from $S$ stays in $S$ (no completeness required).
- **Proposition 6.26** (p. 131). For nominally well-posed $\mathcal H$ and any $S$: $\Omega(S)$ is closed; it is compact if some tail reachable set $\mathcal R_{\ge\tau_0}(S)$ is bounded; if moreover it is nonempty, it is weakly backward invariant and uniformly pre-attractive from $S$; if also $\Omega(S)\subset S$, it is strongly forward pre-invariant. (The last part concatenates long backward-reaching solutions with a hypothetical escaping one to contradict the definition of $\Omega(S)$.)

## 6.4 Well-posed hybrid systems (p. 132)

- **Definition 6.27** (p. 132). For $\rho:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$, the *$\rho$-perturbation* $\mathcal H_\rho$ (6.5) has: $C_\rho$ the points whose $\rho(x)$-ball meets $C$; $F_\rho(x)=\overline{\mathrm{con}}\,F((x+\rho(x)\mathbb B)\cap C)+\rho(x)\mathbb B$; $D_\rho$ analogous to $C_\rho$; $G_\rho(x)$ the values of $G$ over $(x+\rho(x)\mathbb B)\cap D$, each inflated by a ball of radius $\rho$ evaluated at that value. This is an "outer" perturbation: sets and maps are inflated, covering state perturbations and more. For any positive $\rho$, Krasovskii solutions of $\mathcal H$ are solutions of $\mathcal H_\rho$, since the regularised data are contained in the perturbed data.
- **Proposition 6.28** (p. 132). If $\mathcal H$ satisfies Assumption 6.5 and $\rho$ is continuous, $\mathcal H_\rho$ satisfies Assumption 6.5 (via Lemma 5.17: $F_\rho$ is $M_2$ plus a continuous ball, $G_\rho$ is $M_3$ built on $M_1$).
- **Definition 6.29** (p. 133). $\mathcal H$ is *well-posed* if for every continuous $\rho\ge0$, every decreasing $\delta_i\in(0,1)$ with $\delta_i\to0$, and every graphically convergent sequence of solutions of $\mathcal H_{\delta_i\rho}$ with initial points converging to $\xi$: (a) under local eventual boundedness, the lengths converge or diverge to $\infty$ and the graphical limit is a solution of $\mathcal H$ (the unperturbed system) from $\xi$ with the limiting length; (b) otherwise there is a smallest jump index $j^*$ and a time $t^*>0$ at which the arcs blow up along times $t_i\to t^*$, and the graphical limit truncated to $t+j<t^*+j^*$ is a maximal solution of $\mathcal H$ that is unbounded as $t\to t^*$ in slice $j^*$.
- Well-posed implies nominally well-posed, not conversely (p. 133): the sign-type ODE with velocity $-1$ for $x<0$ and $+1$ for $x\ge0$ is nominally well-posed, but the zero function, not a solution, is a limit of solutions under vanishing perturbations (as in Example 4.1); Theorem 6.31 gives the general reason.
- **Theorem 6.30** (p. 133–134). Assumption 6.5 implies well-posedness. Proof outline: domains converge to a hybrid time domain with the right length (Examples 5.19, 5.3); on each flow slice Theorem 5.29 makes the limit a solution of the constrained inclusion, and $C_{\delta_i\rho}\to C$ puts it in $C$; at jumps, matching pre- and post-jump points converge and $D_{\delta_i\rho}\to D$, $G_{\delta_i\rho}\to G$ graphically give (S2\*); in the unbounded case, the first slice where boundedness fails is entered from a convergent pre-jump point through the compact-valued $G_\rho$, and Theorem 5.29(b) yields the blow-up time.

## 6.5 Consequences of well-posedness (p. 134)

- **Theorem 6.31** (p. 134–135). If $\mathcal H$ is well-posed, its solutions are exactly its Hermes solutions; if furthermore $F$ and $G$ are locally bounded, they are also exactly its Krasovskii solutions. (A Hermes approximating sequence, being uniformly bounded, solves $\mathcal H_{\delta_i\rho}$ for a suitable $\delta_i\to0$ and positive $\rho$, so well-posedness makes its limit a solution; Corollary 4.23 handles Krasovskii.) This justifies concentrating on systems satisfying the basic conditions: a well-posed system already coincides with its regularisation.
- **Corollary 6.32** (p. 135). With $F,G$ locally bounded, Hermes solutions are Krasovskii solutions: the regularisation is well-posed (Example 6.6, Theorem 6.30), contains the data of $\mathcal H$, so a Hermes solution of $\mathcal H$ is a Hermes solution and hence a solution of $\widehat{\mathcal H}$. This completes Theorem 4.17.
- **Proposition 6.33** (p. 135). Perturbed version of Proposition 6.13: if $\mathcal H$ is well-posed and pre-forward complete from compact $K$, $\rho$ continuous and $\delta_i\to0$, then for each $m$ there are $\delta$, $i_0$ and compact $K'$ bounding, for $t+j<m$, all solutions of $\mathcal H_{\delta_i\rho}$ ($i>i_0$) from $K+\delta\mathbb B$.
- **Proposition 6.34** (p. 135). Perturbed version of Proposition 6.14: with $\mathcal H$ well-posed and pre-forward complete from compact $K$ (and $\rho$ as before), for each $\varepsilon,\tau$ there is $\delta$ such that every solution of $\mathcal H_{\delta\rho}$ from $K+\delta\mathbb B$ is $(\tau,\varepsilon)$-close to a solution of $\mathcal H$ from $K$: dependence on both initial conditions and perturbations is upper semicontinuous.
- **Proposition 6.35** (p. 136). Robust uniform non-Zenoness (the errata retitle it "non-Zenoness and non-continuousness"). Let $\mathcal H$ be well-posed, $\rho$ continuous, and $K$ compact and strongly forward pre-invariant for $\mathcal H_\rho$. (a) If no complete discrete solution starts in $K$, then for some $\delta>0$ all solutions of $\mathcal H_{\delta\rho}$ from $K$ are uniformly non-Zeno: there are $T,J$ such that any stretch of ordinary time of length at most $T$ contains at most $J$ jumps. (b) Symmetrically, if no complete purely continuous solution starts in $K$, there are $T,J$ such that any stretch with at most $J$ jumps lasts at most $T$ ordinary time. (Proof of (a): otherwise, tails of solutions with ever more jumps in ever shorter time converge, by well-posedness, to a complete discrete solution from $K$.) For a nominally well-posed system the analogue holds with $\rho\equiv0$.
- Caveat (p. 136). Without further hypotheses (such as the pre-asymptotic stability used in Proposition 7.13), the conclusion cannot be extended to a neighbourhood of $K$: with $C=[0,1]$, $\dot x=x$, $D=\{1\}$, $g(1)=1$, the set $\{0\}$ is strongly forward pre-invariant with only a constant flowing solution, yet every neighbourhood contains initial points whose solutions flow to $1$ and then jump for ever.

## 6.6 Notes (p. 137)

The regularity conditions have antecedents in continuous- and discrete-time work (Filippov, Aubin–Cellina, Aubin–Frankowska, Kellett–Teel); their role for hybrid systems is from Goebel–Teel and Sanfelice et al. Weak forward invariance matches viability (Aubin et al.) and, under uniqueness, invariance as used by Lygeros et al.; weak backward invariance resembles "negative semi-invariance" (Melnik–Valero). Proposition 6.21 extends classical $\omega$-limit results of Birkhoff, LaSalle and Filippov. $\omega$-limit sets of sets for hybrid systems are from Cai et al., following Hale. The outer perturbation $\mathcal H_\rho$ is from Goebel–Teel.

## Key ideas to carry away

- Well-posedness for hybrid systems is about limits of solution sequences, not uniqueness: graphical limits must be solutions, and unboundedness must appear as finite-time blow-up of the limit.
- The hybrid basic conditions (closed sets; osc, locally bounded maps; convex flow values) are easy to check and guarantee well-posedness even under vanishing outer perturbations of the data (Theorems 6.8, 6.30).
- A well-posed system has the *same solution set* as its Hermes solutions and, with $F$ and $G$ locally bounded, as its Krasovskii regularisation: nothing new appears under vanishing perturbations (Theorem 6.31). The *data* need not be equal. This is the gateway to robustness results. (Wording corrected 25 November after ChatGPT's review.)
- Consequences: locally bounded solution sets, upper semicontinuous dependence on initial conditions and perturbations in the $(\tau,\varepsilon)$ sense, compact reachable sets, all under pre-forward completeness (Propositions 6.13, 6.14, 6.33, 6.34; Lemma 6.16).
- $\omega$-limit sets of precompact solutions are weakly invariant and attract the solution; $\omega$-limit sets of sets are weakly backward invariant and uniformly pre-attractive (Propositions 6.21, 6.26). These underlie the hybrid invariance principles.
- Absence of complete Zeno (or complete continuous) solutions in a compact invariant set implies a uniform bound on jumps per unit time (or time per batch of jumps) that survives small perturbations (Proposition 6.35).
- Necessity is partial: $D$ closed and $G$ osc and locally bounded are necessary for nominal well-posedness, but conditions on $C$ and $F$ are not (Lemma 6.9 and p. 123).
