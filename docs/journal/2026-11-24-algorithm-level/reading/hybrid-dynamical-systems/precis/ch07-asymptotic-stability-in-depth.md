# Chapter 7: Asymptotic stability, an in-depth treatment (pp. 139–168)

Chapter 3 gave sufficient conditions for *global* uniform pre-asymptotic stability without any regularity assumptions on the hybrid system. This chapter instead studies *local* pre-asymptotic stability of a *compact* set, and asks what follows from it once the system is regular in the senses of Chapter 6. The answer comes in two tiers: for nominally well-posed systems (Definition 6.2), local pre-asymptotic stability is automatically uniform and is captured by a class-$\mathcal{KL}$ bound on the whole (open) basin of pre-attraction; for well-posed systems (Definition 6.29), it is in addition robust to small state-dependent perturbations, and this robustness is equivalent to the existence of a smooth Lyapunov function. The chapter is the book's converse-Lyapunov and robustness core: it justifies searching for Lyapunov functions, it supplies the robustness results that Chapter 9 uses to prove its approximation theorem, and it delivers several "free" corollaries (reduction principle, slowly varying parameters, slow switching).

Notation used throughout: $\mathcal{H}=(C,F,D,G)$; $\mathcal{S}_\mathcal{H}(K)$ is the set of maximal solutions from $K$; $|x|_\mathcal{A}$ is distance to $\mathcal{A}$; $\mathcal{H}_\rho$ is the $\rho$-perturbation of Definition 6.27 (inflate $C$, $D$ by $\rho(x)\mathbb{B}$, take the closed convex hull of $F$ over the inflated neighbourhood plus a $\rho$-ball, and perturb $G$'s inputs and outputs similarly); $\widehat{\mathcal{H}}$ is the Krasovskii regularisation (Definition 4.13); $\mathcal{R}(K)$ is the reachable set (Definition 6.15). "Pre-" means that only complete solutions are required to converge; maximal solutions may stop, by leaving $C\cup D$ or blowing up, without violating the property.

## Opening material (pp. 139–140)

**Definition 7.1 (LpAS, p. 139).** For a compact $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^n$: *stable* if for every $\varepsilon>0$ some $\delta>0$ makes every solution starting within $\delta$ of $\mathcal{A}$ stay within $\varepsilon$ of it on its whole domain; *locally pre-attractive* if for some $\mu>0$ every solution starting within $\mu$ is bounded and, if complete, has $|\phi(t,j)|_\mathcal{A}\to 0$ as $t+j\to\infty$; *locally pre-asymptotically stable* (LpAS) if both.

**Example 7.2 (p. 139).** A planar system with constant flow $f=(1,1)$ on $C=\{x_2\ge 0,\ x_1\le x_2^2\}$ and jumps on the parabola $D=\{x_2\ge 0,\ x_1=x_2^2\}$ with $g(x)=(-x_2/2,\ x_2^2)$. Solutions near the origin alternate flow and jumps and converge; those far away flow without ever meeting $D$ and diverge; one solution from $(-1/4,0)$ grazes $D$ and has two maximal continuations. A periodic orbit through $(-1/8,1/16)$ separates the two regimes. A rough estimate gives stability (solutions starting within $\delta<3/(16\sqrt2)$ stay within $3\delta$) and local attractivity, so the origin is LpAS but not globally so. The authors flag that Example 9.12 will reach the same conclusion more quickly.

## 7.1 Pre-asymptotic stability for nominally well-posed systems (p. 141)

The section shows that for nominally well-posed systems local pAS has an open basin and is uniform on it, and gives general sources of pAS compact sets.

- **Definition 7.3 (basin of pre-attraction, p. 141).** $\mathcal{B}^p_\mathcal{A}$ is the set of initial points from which every solution is bounded and, if complete, converges to $\mathcal{A}$. Points outside $C\cup D$ belong vacuously (there are no solutions from them). For Example 7.2 the basin within $C\cup D$ is the region below the line $x_2 = x_1 + 3/16$.
- **Proposition 7.4 (basin is open, p. 141).** If $\mathcal{H}$ is nominally well-posed and compact $\mathcal{A}$ is LpAS, then $\mathcal{B}^p_\mathcal{A}$ is open and contains $\mathcal{A}$. The argument is by contradiction: a sequence of "bad" initial points converging to a basin point yields, via graphical convergence (Theorem 6.1) and local eventual boundedness (Proposition 6.13), a complete limiting solution that stays a fixed distance from $\mathcal{A}$.
- **Proposition 7.5 (stability from invariance plus uniform convergence, p. 142).** For nominally well-posed $\mathcal{H}$, a compact set that is strongly forward pre-invariant and uniformly pre-attractive from some neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}+\mu\mathbb{B}$ is LpAS. Only stability needs proof, and it follows by the same graphical-limit contradiction.
- **Example 7.6 (finite-time observer, p. 142).** For an observable linear plant $\dot\xi = F\xi + v$ with bounded $\xi$ and $v$, two Luenberger-type observers with gains $L_1,L_2$ run in parallel and are reset every $\delta$ seconds by a linear combination $G_1\hat\xi_1+G_2\hat\xi_2$, chosen (via invertibility of $I-e^{F_2\delta}e^{-F_1\delta}$) so that after one reset and one further interval of length $\delta$ the combination equals the true state exactly. The set where both estimates equal $\xi$ (times the timer interval) is forward invariant and reached by time $2\delta$ or after two jumps, so Proposition 7.5 gives global pAS. This is an example of *exact finite-time* convergence inside the pAS framework.
- **Corollary 7.7 (pAS $\Omega$-limit sets, p. 144).** For nominally well-posed $\mathcal{H}$ and compact $K$ with bounded reachable set, if the $\omega$-limit set $\Omega(K)$ (Definition 6.23) is nonempty and lies in $\operatorname{int} K$, then $\Omega(K)$ is LpAS with basin containing $K$ (via Proposition 6.26 and Proposition 7.5). Useful when the attractor exists but is hard to describe.
- **Lemma 7.8 (compact subsets of the basin, p. 144).** For nominally well-posed $\mathcal{H}$ and LpAS compact $\mathcal{A}$, every compact $K\subset\mathcal{B}^p_\mathcal{A}$ satisfies (a) $\mathcal{A}$ is uniformly pre-attractive from $K$; (b) $\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{R}(K)$ is a compact subset of the basin.
- **Definition 7.9 (proper indicator, p. 145).** On an open $U$, a continuous $\omega:U\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ that tends to infinity both as $|x|\to\infty$ and as $x$ approaches $\partial U$; a proper indicator *of* $\mathcal{A}$ additionally vanishes exactly on $\mathcal{A}$. Examples: $|x|_\mathcal{A}$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$; $|x|_\mathcal{A}/|x|_{\mathbb{R}^n\setminus U}$ on a general $U$. The motivation is that plain distance to $\mathcal{A}$ cannot express uniformity over a basin that is not all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, since sublevel sets of distance within the basin need not be closed.
- **Definition 7.10 ($\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$, p. 145).** For every proper indicator $\omega$ of $\mathcal{A}$ on $U$ there is $\beta\in\mathcal{KL}$ with $\omega(\phi(t,j))\le\beta(\omega(\phi(0,0)),t+j)$ for all solutions from $U$ (bound (7.1)).
- **Lemma 7.11 (p. 145).** Stability, uniform pre-attractivity from compact subsets of $U$, and boundedness of $\mathcal{R}(K)$ relative to $U$ for each compact $K\subset U$ together imply $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$. (The $\beta$ is built as a supremum over solutions; no regularity of $\mathcal{H}$ is needed for this step.)
- **Theorem 7.12 (from local pAS to uniform pAS, p. 146).** For nominally well-posed $\mathcal{H}$, LpAS of compact $\mathcal{A}$ implies the basin is open and $\mathcal{A}$ is $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on the whole basin. Since a $\mathcal{KL}$ bound trivially implies LpAS, this is an equivalence for such systems. It is the local analogue of Theorem 3.40.
- **Proposition 7.13 (uniform non-continuity and non-discreteness, p. 146).** Under the same hypotheses and for compact $K$ in the basin: (a) if no complete purely discrete (instantaneous Zeno) solution starts in $\mathcal{A}$, then solutions from $K$ are *uniformly non-discrete*: there are $T,J$ such that any stretch of ordinary time of length at most $T$ contains at most $J$ jumps; (b) if no complete purely continuous solution starts in $\mathcal{A}$, solutions from $K$ are *uniformly non-continuous*: at most $J$ jumps' worth of index takes at most $T$ ordinary time. It complements Proposition 6.35.
- **Lemma 7.14 (from pre-AS to AS, p. 147).** Augment $\mathcal{H}$ by making $D^*=\mathbb{R}^n$ and $G^*(x)=G(x)\cup\mathcal{A}$ on $D$, $G^*(x)=\mathcal{A}$ elsewhere: any solution may jump straight into $\mathcal{A}$. Then (a) the hybrid basic conditions (Assumption 6.5) are preserved; (b) $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$ transfers to $\mathcal{H}^*$. Since $\mathcal{H}^*$ has solutions from everywhere (Proposition 2.10's existence conditions), "pre-" disappears. This trick is used in the converse-Lyapunov proof.

## 7.2 Robustness concepts (p. 148)

The results here require neither nominal well-posedness nor well-posedness.

- **Definition 7.15 (robust pAS, p. 148).** LpAS of $\mathcal{A}$ is *robust on* an open $U$ with $\mathcal{A}\subset U\subset\mathcal{B}^p_\mathcal{A}$ if some continuous $\rho\ge0$, positive on $U\setminus\mathcal{A}$, keeps $\mathcal{A}$ LpAS for $\mathcal{H}_\rho$ with $U$ still inside the perturbed basin. The perturbation must vanish on $\mathcal{A}$ but may be large elsewhere.
- **Example 7.16 (robust, but not globally, p. 148).** On $\mathbb{R}$: flow $\dot x=-x$ on $(-\infty,1]$, jump to $1$ from $(1,\infty)$. The origin is globally AS but the property is robust only on $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1\}$: any perturbation positive at $1$ lets $1$ jump to itself forever. With $\rho(x)=\min\{|x|,|x-1|\}/2$ robustness holds on that smaller set. The point is that a closed jump set touching the flow set's boundary creates a fragile point.
- **Lemma 7.17 (robustness and Krasovskii solutions, p. 148).** (**Erratum:** the hypothesis should read $\mathcal A \subset U$, not $\mathcal A \subset$ the basin.) If LpAS is robust on $U$, then $\mathcal{A}$ is LpAS for the Krasovskii regularisation $\widehat{\mathcal{H}}$ with $U$ in its basin. Krasovskii solutions are $\rho$-perturbed solutions while away from $\mathcal{A}$, and cannot flow or jump out of $\mathcal{A}$ once there. Since $\widehat{\mathcal{H}}$ is well-posed, Section 7.1's uniformity results then apply.
- **Definition 7.18 (p. 149).** (a) *Robustly $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$*: some continuous $\rho$, positive on $U\setminus\mathcal{A}$, keeps $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$ for $\mathcal{H}_\rho$. (b) *Semiglobally practically robustly $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$*: for any proper indicator $\omega$, any such $\rho$, any $\beta$ satisfying (7.1), any compact $K\subset U$ and any $\varepsilon>0$, some $\delta\in(0,1)$ makes all solutions of $\mathcal{H}_{\delta\rho}$ from $K$ obey the nominal bound up to an additive $\varepsilon$.
- **Lemma 7.19 (semiglobal practical robustness implies robustness, p. 150).** (b) implies (a), for any hybrid system. The proof stitches together the $\delta_n$ from a doubly infinite sequence of indicator levels $r_n$ into a single continuous perturbation radius and then invokes Lemma 7.11.

## 7.3 Well-posed systems (p. 151)

Here well-posedness (property (a) of Definition 6.29: graphical limits of solutions under vanishing perturbations are solutions of the nominal system) is what makes robustness automatic.

- **Lemma 7.20 (p. 151).** For well-posed $\mathcal{H}$, $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$ implies semiglobal practical robust $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$. The proof first shows the $\varepsilon/2$-practical bound over a finite horizon $t+j\le2\tau$ by a graphical-limit contradiction, then iterates it in time.
- **Theorem 7.21 (robustness of pAS, p. 152).** For well-posed $\mathcal{H}$, LpAS of compact $\mathcal{A}$ implies $\mathcal{A}$ is robustly $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on its whole basin. (Chain: Theorem 7.12, Lemma 7.20, Lemma 7.19.)
- **Lemma 7.22 (changing the basin, p. 152).** Given an open $U$, add $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus U$ to the jump set and let points there also jump to themselves. This preserves Assumption 6.5 and, if $U$ is strongly forward pre-invariant and inside the basin, makes $U$ *exactly* the new basin (points outside $U$ now have a complete solution that never converges).
- **Corollary 7.23 (from robust to $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS, p. 153).** If LpAS is robust on an open, strongly forward pre-invariant $U\subset\mathcal{B}^p_\mathcal{A}$ containing $\mathcal{A}$, then $\mathcal{A}$ is robustly $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$. With $U$ the whole basin: robustness of pAS on the basin is equivalent to robust $\mathcal{KL}$ stability.

## 7.4 Robustness corollaries (p. 153)

- **Corollary 7.24 (reduction principle, p. 153).** For well-posed $\mathcal{H}$: if compact $\mathcal{A}_1$ is LpAS with basin $\mathcal{B}_1$, and compact $\mathcal{A}_2\subset\mathcal{A}_1$ is globally pAS for the restricted system $(C\cap\mathcal{A}_1,F,D\cap\mathcal{A}_1,G)$, then $\mathcal{A}_2$ is LpAS for $\mathcal{H}$ with the same basin $\mathcal{B}_1$. Robustness (Lemma 7.20) is what lets solutions that are merely near $\mathcal{A}_1$ be treated as perturbed solutions of the restricted system. This is a cascade argument: stabilise to a manifold, then analyse dynamics on it.
- **Example 7.25 (p. 154).** A planar system with a continuous coupling term $\sigma$, constrained to $|x_1|\le k$. $V=\tfrac12x_2^2$ shows $[-k,k]\times\{0\}$ is globally pAS; on that set $\tfrac12x_1^2$ shows the origin is globally pAS for the restricted system; Corollary 7.24 combines them. Removing the constraint leaves local pAS, and global pAS if $\sigma$ is bounded (all solutions are then bounded and so captured by a large enough $k$).
- **Example 7.26 (linear feedback through a finite-time observer, p. 154).** Feed back $u=K\hat\xi_1$ from the observer of Example 7.6, with a saturation level $\mu$ so the input stays in a ball. On a bounded state set, the observer-error set is globally pAS; restricted to it, the closed loop reduces to $\dot\xi=(F+JK)\xi$, which is Hurwitz; Corollary 7.24 gives global pAS of the origin-times-timer set, hence local AS once the bounds are removed, and global AS by homogeneity (deferred to Chapter 9).
- **Corollary 7.27 (mildly changing parameters, p. 155).** If a well-posed system with frozen parameter $p$ ($\dot p=0$, $p^+=p$) has compact $\mathcal{A}$ globally pAS, then letting $p$ drift with $\dot p\in\rho\mathbb{B}$ and $p^+\in p+\rho\mathbb{B}$ gives semiglobal practical pAS in $\rho$ (i.e. for any compact set of initial conditions and any tolerance, small enough drift rate suffices).
- **Construction (7.3)–(7.7) and Corollary 7.28 (slow average dwell-time switching, pp. 155–156).** A finite family of well-posed systems $\mathcal{H}_q$ sharing a globally pAS compact $\mathcal{A}$ is switched under an average dwell-time constraint modelled by a timer $\tau\in[0,N]$ that grows at rate at most $\rho$ and is decremented by one at each switch (switches allowed only when $\tau\ge1$), with a reset map $R_q$ applied at switches. If each $R_q$ is outer semicontinuous, locally bounded, nonempty-valued and maps $\mathcal{A}$ into $\mathcal{A}$, then $\mathcal{A}\times Q\times[0,N]$ is semiglobally practically pAS as $\rho\to0$. The nominal case $\rho=0$ is pAS because at most $\lfloor N\rfloor$ switches can happen; robustness does the rest. The authors note more robustness corollaries are in Chapter 9.

## 7.5 Smooth Lyapunov functions (p. 156)

The section states that smooth Lyapunov functions exist exactly when pAS is uniform and robust, and so always exist for compact pAS sets of well-posed systems. Reasons offered: they justify searching for Lyapunov certificates, summarise robustness without reference to solutions, and are the classical tool for constructive designs such as backstepping.

- **Definition 7.29 (Lyapunov function on $U$, p. 157).** For open $U$ with $G(U)\subset U$ and compact $\mathcal{A}\subset U$: $V:U\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$, $C^1$ at points of $C\cap U$, sandwiched between two $\mathcal{K}_\infty$ functions of a proper indicator $\omega$ on $(C\cup D\cup G(D))\cap U$ (7.8), decreasing at exponential rate one along flows, $\langle\nabla V(x),f\rangle\le -V(x)$ (7.9), and contracting by the factor $1/e$ over jumps, $V(g)\le V(x)/e$ (7.10). *Smooth* means $C^1$ on all of $U$. Note that the decay is normalised to be in *hybrid time* $t+j$.
- **Theorem 7.30 (smooth Lyapunov functions imply robustness, p. 157).** If $F$ is locally bounded and a smooth Lyapunov function exists for $\mathcal{A}$ on $U$, then $\mathcal{A}$ is robustly $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS on $U$. Idea: continuity of $\nabla V$ and $V$ means the decrease conditions still hold, with rate $1/3$ instead of $1$, for velocities and jump images taken at nearby points, hence for the Krasovskii regularisation, whose well-posedness then gives robustness via Lemmas 7.19–7.20.

### 7.5.1 Existence (p. 158)

- **Theorem 7.31 (robustness implies smooth Lyapunov functions, p. 158).** If $F$ is locally bounded, compact $\mathcal{A}$ is LpAS, the basin is open, and the LpAS is robust on the basin, then there is a smooth Lyapunov function for $\mathcal{A}$ on the basin. This is described as the chapter's main contribution; the proof is in Section 7.6. Outline: build an upper semicontinuous preliminary function as a worst case over Krasovskii solutions of a weighted indicator, then smooth it, using robustness to smooth away from $\mathcal{A}$.
- **Corollary 7.32 (p. 159).** For well-posed $\mathcal{H}$, every LpAS compact set has a smooth Lyapunov function on its basin (Theorem 7.21 plus Theorem 7.31). Together with Theorem 7.30, this makes "LpAS", "robust $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS" and "smooth Lyapunov function exists" equivalent for well-posed systems.
- **Corollary 7.33 (p. 159).** If LpAS is robust on an open, strongly forward pre-invariant $U$ within the basin containing $\mathcal{A}$, a smooth Lyapunov function exists on $U$ (Corollary 7.23 plus Theorem 7.31). (The printed title repeats that of Corollary 7.23.)

### 7.5.2 Special cases (p. 159)

- **Example 7.34 (hybrid automata, p. 159).** For a hybrid automaton meeting the regularity conditions of Example 6.7 (closed domains and guards, continuous flows and resets), pAS of $\{0\}$ in the continuous state (uniformly over modes) translates into: each mode's basin $B_q$ is open; each mode has a $C^1$ function $V_q$ with mode-wise sandwich bounds and exponential decrease along that mode's flow; and every edge $(q,q')$ satisfies $V_{q'}(\text{reset}(\xi))\le V_q(\xi)/e$ on the guard. That is, a family of mode-dependent Lyapunov functions that strictly decreases across transitions. The same reasoning gives Lyapunov functions for switching systems under dwell-time, average dwell-time or persistent dwell-time constraints, but these depend on the timer variables as well as on $(z,q)$, unlike the usual sufficient conditions (Example 3.22), which use only $(z,q)$.
- **Example 7.35 (perfectly incomplete systems, p. 161).** A constrained differential inclusion on compact $C$, satisfying Assumption 6.5, with *no* complete solution in $C$. Any compact set disjoint from $C$ is then trivially LpAS, so Corollary 7.32 yields a $C^1$ function $W$, positive on $C$, with $\langle\nabla W(z),f\rangle\le-1$ on $C$. Consequently $W(z(0))$ bounds how long any solution can remain in $C$: a smooth, state-dependent upper bound on the time to leave. The authors note the closed-but-bounded-solutions variant and an analogous smooth bound on the number of jumps of a discrete system $z^+\in G(z)$, $z\in D$, without complete solutions.

## 7.6 Proof of robustness implies smooth Lyapunov functions (p. 161)

Devoted entirely to proving Theorem 7.31.

- **Lemma 7.36 (perturbations of perturbations, p. 162).** For any continuous $\rho\ge0$ there is a smaller continuous $\rho'$ with the same zero set such that (a) the $\rho'$-perturbation of the $\rho'$-perturbation is contained in the $\rho$-perturbation (data-wise inclusion of $C$, $D$, $F$, $G$); (b) several one-step "slack" inclusions hold, such as $x+\rho'(x)\mathbb{B}\subset C_\rho$ for $x\in C_{\rho'}$ and $F_{\rho'}(x)+\rho'(x)\mathbb{B}\subset F_\rho(y)$ for $y$ within $\rho'(x)$ of $x$.
- **Lemma 7.37 (perturbations of perturbed solutions, p. 163).** With $F$ locally bounded, there is $\rho'\le\rho$, with the same positivity set $S$ and smooth on $S$, such that shifting any $\mathcal{H}_{\rho'}$ solution lying in $S$ pointwise by $\rho'(\phi(s,i))\xi$, for any fixed unit-ball vector $\xi$, gives a solution of $\mathcal{H}_\rho$ on the same domain. The construction uses a locally finite cover and partition of unity with a gradient bound on $\rho'$ relative to the size of $F_{\rho_0}$.
- **Preliminary Lyapunov function (p. 164).** Using Lemma 7.17 and Theorem 7.21, $\mathcal{A}$ is robustly $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS for $\widehat{\mathcal{H}}$ on the basin; Lemma 3.41 converts the $\mathcal{KL}$ bound into an exponential-in-hybrid-time bound $a_1(\omega(\phi(t,j)))\le a_2(\omega(\phi(0,0)))e^{-2(t+j)}$ (7.13). Define $V_0(\xi)$ as the supremum over Krasovskii solutions from $\xi$ of $a_1(\omega(\phi(t,j)))e^{t+j}$ (or $a_1(\omega(\xi))$ off $\widehat C\cup\widehat D$). It satisfies sandwich bounds (7.14), decays as $e^{-(t+j)}$ along solutions (7.15) by concatenation, and is upper semicontinuous because the supremum can be restricted to a bounded time window and nominal well-posedness turns suprema into maxima.
- **Initial smoothing (p. 165).** Convolve $V_0$ with a mollifier over a state-dependent radius $\rho'(\xi)$, giving $V_1$ (7.17). Sandwich bounds persist (7.18). **Lemma 7.38 (differentiation under the integral sign, p. 165)**, a standard result cited from Lang, gives $C^1$ away from $\mathcal{A}$. Lemma 7.37 lets each shifted point of the mollifier ride a perturbed solution, so $V_1$ inherits the decay (7.19), hence the flow inequality (7.20) and jump inequality (7.21) off $\mathcal{A}$.
- **Final smoothing (p. 166).** Compose with $S(r)=\int_0^r s$, for a $\mathcal{K}_\infty$ $s$ chosen small near zero, so that $V_2=S\circ V_1$ vanishes quadratically at $\mathcal{A}$ with gradient going to zero there; hence $V_2$ is $C^1$ on the whole basin. The properties $S(r)\le r\,s(r)$ and $S(r/e)\le S(r)/e$ (7.23) keep (7.9) and (7.10).

## 7.7 Notes (p. 167)

Historical sources: uniformity for ODEs (Massera); uniformity and robustness for differential inclusions (Clarke–Ledyaev–Stern; Teel–Praly) and difference inclusions (Kellett–Teel); converse Lyapunov theorems from Lyapunov, Massera, Kurzweil, Wilson, Lin–Sontag–Wang; switched systems (Dayawansa–Martin; Mancilla-Aguilar–García); the link between robustness and smoothness traced to Kurzweil; hybrid converse theorems without smoothness by Ye–Michel–Hou. Section 7.1 follows Goebel–Teel; Example 7.6 is from Raff–Allgöwer; the main robustness and converse results are from Cai–Teel–Goebel. Further robustness consequences not covered here: temporal regularisation, simulation approximation, singular perturbation and averaging.

## Key ideas to carry away

- For compact sets, regularity upgrades stability for free: nominal well-posedness turns LpAS into uniform ($\mathcal{KL}$) pAS on an *open* basin (Theorem 7.12); well-posedness further makes it robust to small state-dependent perturbations (Theorem 7.21).
- Uniformity over a non-global basin is measured by a *proper indicator* that blows up at the basin's edge, not by plain distance to the attractor (Definitions 7.9–7.10).
- Robustness, robust $\mathcal{KL}$ pAS, and the existence of a smooth Lyapunov function (with exponential decay in hybrid time $t+j$) are equivalent for well-posed systems (Theorems 7.30, 7.31, Corollary 7.32).
- Robustness has practical corollaries: the reduction principle for cascades (Corollary 7.24), tolerance of slowly drifting parameters (Corollary 7.27) and of slow switching among stable modes (Corollary 7.28).
- Pre-asymptotic stability includes exact finite-time convergence (Example 7.6) and even systems that always terminate; for the latter a smooth function bounding remaining time, or remaining jumps, exists (Example 7.35).
- Absence of Zeno or of pure-flow behaviour on the attractor propagates to uniform bounds on jumps per unit time, or time per jump, over compact parts of the basin (Proposition 7.13).
- Robustness can fail at isolated "fragile" points where jumps can map a point to itself (Example 7.16), which is why robustness is stated relative to an open set $U$.
