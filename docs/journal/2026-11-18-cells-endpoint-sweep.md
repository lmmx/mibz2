# Cells Study 7 · Reconstructing the induction from first arrivals

2026-11-18 · Production baseline `3502dd9bbc75cee4feadb5cdebd487a5b937c822`

**Cells can perform its entire induction without storing an interval width after initialisation.** Each band has a signed displacement between its two copies. The displacement adds when two return words concatenate. At the same cut the two parent displacements tell us exactly where the new band will first reach a front. Visiting those arrivals in descending row order recovers all the usual cuts, including their thicknesses, directions and marked word recipes. This removes the linked interval orders from the computation. It introduces a cost of its own on almost every cut of Wikidata.

**Stopping decision.** Neither of the two ways of ordering arrivals examined here clears the practical screen for eager or lazy Cells. One roughly halves induction geometry-field accesses on frozen and needs 21.8 MB of scheduling arrays per worker. The other uses 0.88 MB and pays to file and retrieve 142,708 events outside its active group. Its simulated cache traffic is better, sometimes substantially so. Charging the work of ordering those events reverses the apparent saving in the resident-operation model. This establishes an alternative exact computation and leaves its native speed open. ## The first-arrival theorem

Use a common physical row coordinate in the two orders. At a transition their support is the prefix $[0,F)$. Write $t_X,b_X$ for the exclusive right ends of band $X$ on top and bottom. Its signed displacement is $d_X=b_X-t_X$. This is the translation carried by its return map. The two copies have equal width.

1. **Persistence before first touch.** A cut removes a suffix of width $w$ at the two fronts. At the opposite occurrences of the parents, it relabels exactly the rightmost $w$ rows as the child. Every third band retains both physical endpoints. A surviving parent loses $w$ at both ends and keeps its displacement. A closure deletes a common suffix and leaves all other bands in place. Consequently an untouched band's first possible front is at $h_X=\max(t_X,b_X)$. The sign gives the side. A negative sign means top, a positive sign means bottom, and zero means the same band closes on both sides. The marked orbit may finish before this arrival.
2. **A cut schedules its child.** Let $A$ be the top front and $B$ the distinct bottom front. Put $a=-d_A>0$ and $b=d_B>0$. The opposite sites end at $b_A=F-a$ and $t_B=F-b$. The child $AB$ occupies the suffixes at those sites. Hence

   $$t_{AB}=F-b,\qquad b_{AB}=F-a,\qquad d_{AB}=b-a=d_A+d_B,$$

   $$h_{AB}=F-\min(a,b).$$

   This coordinate is positive and strictly below $F$. All three possible width comparisons use the same formula. The new word is still $A$ followed by $B$.
3. **The greatest pending arrival is the next frontier.** Keep the two active operands, replacing a side only when an arrival for it fires. After an ordinary cut at least one operand is exhausted. The next front on that side belongs to a band which has never appeared as a front. Once a band has appeared it either continues at that same front until exhausted, or closes. After an unmarked closure both fronts must be supplied afresh. At the next physical frontier every new front therefore has a pending event at that coordinate. Every other untouched band is inside that prefix and has a key no greater. This proves the assertion by induction starting at $F=n$. At most one event per side can occur at a coordinate. A diagonal event alone supplies both fronts.

The difference of consecutive visited coordinates is the preceding cut or closure width. The final marked band itself has width one. After a cut, replacing just bottom identifies a strict top win, replacing just top a strict bottom win, and replacing both an equality. A diagonal also replaces both. A marked closure ends the induction immediately. Its *word length* is still available in its descriptor. This proof applies to switches, ties, discarded cycles and periods shorter than the block.

The source inputs suffice. For a run-letter with byte $c$, the top end is the cumulative row count. The bottom end is the total number of rows with byte below $c$, plus the cumulative number of $c$ rows through this letter. Both are computed in stable top order, including the primary singleton. This takes a byte-row histogram and a cursor per byte. Within induction each front carries just a distance and its W16 word. Computing the sign and magnitude of $b-a$ and the posting coordinate replaces the width test, predecessor reads, cross-link repairs and lazy stream/slot decisions. No four-way fast-path test or prior lookahead is needed. **Downstream invariant.** In both computations the two operands agree at every join, in the same order. W16 therefore makes the same choice of short arena word or 16-byte rule, advances the same cursor by the same amount, and supplies the same ordered child descriptors. With the same word-region bases even the packed descriptor values agree. The unique marked row has width one, so its marked recipe passes to exactly one child whenever cut. The same closure and marked rotation reach the writer. The budget test belongs after checking for closure and before making a child. ## Falsification and real tapes

Equal widths do not certify the new side. The first cut of column `100` at primary 1 and `110` at primary 0 has widths $(1,1)$ in both cases. The gaps are $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ and the children first appear on opposite sides. Conversely `1100` at primary 0 makes a diagonal at a strict cut of widths $(2,1)$. Posting order also fails as a queue discipline. On `1100100`, primary 0, two births on the same side are posted at 3 then 4. On `1000` they are posted at 3 then 2. On `00` the primary determines whether the initial diagonal is discarded or finishes with a row still pending. [Witnesses with coordinates and cut numbers](2026-11-18-cells-endpoint-sweep/small.json)

A separate explicit two-order oracle checked all 14,209 marked binary columns through length 9 and ternary columns through length 6, counting the two enumerations separately. There were 12,192 shorter periods, 11,006 discarded closures and 5,544 budget probes. It checks both physical gaps, front replacements, word recipes and the rotated spelling against stable LF. The real-tape observer also checks the emitted grammar's marked cycle against stable LF. A separate run supplied the frozen last column from the archived entropy extraction and obtained the same roots and results. The event computation reproduced every cut and closure on the ten Wikidata blocks and six prose controls. Frozen reproduced the 28,710 ordered W16 rule pairs and 322,317 short-arena bytes of logical allocation. Of the frozen newborns, 44,880 first reach top, 47,689 first reach bottom and one is diagonal. The full-period sample has 1,236,063 visited coordinates on Wikidata. ## What the replacement costs

One implementation in the structural check groups keys by 64 consecutive rows. A future group owns an unsorted chain of packets. Entering the group drains its chain into two local 64-bit masks and a small indexed array. A highest-set-bit query finds the next frontier. Each cut consumes at least one pending packet and creates exactly one, so a consumed index supplies the newborn slot. A closure creates none. The whole schedule uses $16r+4\lceil n/64\rceil+528$ bytes of packets, heads and local indexing. Alternatively two 12-byte payload slots per coordinate and two masks per group support direct posting. The latter needs $24n+16\lceil n/64\rceil$ array bytes. Both charge unsuccessful side and group tests. Each packet is posted, drained and consumed at most once, and each group entered at most once. For $m$ physical input records and $C$ executed cuts this gives $O(m+r+C+\lceil n/64\rceil)$ structural operations with 64-bit masks, including setup. The writer needs only the word regions and marked root.

Geometry-field operations include the eager demand loads which form prefetch addresses. All four schedules issue the same 127,720 short-payload reads and 185,140 recipe writes on frozen. Grouped posting sends 91,524 of 92,570 newborns outside the current group, plus all 51,184 originals during setup. The ten-block rate for newborns is 98.95%. Each such event incurs a head update and later a dependent chain read, even if its lines stay hot. Direct posting avoids the chain and still has to compute the key, test and update a mask, select the incoming sides and fetch the correct payload. [Address traces and cache model](2026-11-18-cells-endpoint-sweep/traffic.py) · [Per-block field and line counts](2026-11-18-cells-endpoint-sweep/traffic.csv)

On the eight production-eager-admitted sample blocks, grouped scheduling raises the combined resident score 77.8%, and direct scheduling raises it 33.4%. Against lazy on its nine admitted blocks the increases are 58.5% and 18.9%. Grouped scheduling saves 783,027 ideal 256 KiB line transfers against eager, and 165,926 against lazy. On those populations the saved transfer would need to outweigh respectively 37 or 188 resident units just to break even in that additive model. Actual cache levels, prefetch, overlap, spills and shared bandwidth have different prices. The archived eager i9 induction accounts for 58.5–58.6% of kernel time on its 196 admitted blocks, so saving 4–6% of induction would matter. The study found no defensible several-percent time saving from either schedule after charging construction and ordering. The ordering cost is a property of these two representations. A lower bound on all ways to recover the next frontier remains open.
