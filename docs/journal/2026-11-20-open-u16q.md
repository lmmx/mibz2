# Open U16Q natively: no change (#664)

*20 November 2026. The Coordinator, porting Carbon's cycle-2 U16Q arm
(`research/carbon-word-transport`, 8b54b024) to `src/open.rs` as a research PR.*

## The port

There are two commits, both in safe Rust: no `unsafe`, with every `assert!`, the walk bound and
the clamp to n kept.
1. **The queue fill:** thin runs (w ≤ 16) append a fixed 16-row iota and truncate to the true
   width.
2. **The emit:** a per-code `[u8; 16]` table. A word of at most 16 bytes is one 16-byte copy into
   an n+16 buffer with an explicit cursor; longer words take the cold path.

**Correctness:**
- byte-identical to shipped Open on 200,000 arbitrary walks (`open::checks::emit_matches_reference`);
- all tests pass, and clippy is clean;
- CI is green, Miri included.

## What it means

**The prediction we could test.** Open handles 228 of Wikidata's 431 blocks. So a 0.83–0.86×
whole-kernel gain should have shown as several percent end to end. The 1-thread interval
(−0.4%, +0.1%) excludes that. The C harness overstated the gain.

**Likely causes, in order:**
1. **The baselines differ.** The C harness's "Open" is a re-implementation, and its variable-length
   loops may mispredict where the Rust compiler's code does not.
2. **The codegen differs.** Safe Rust's bounds-checked 16-byte copy and `extend_from_slice` plus
   `truncate` are not the C's AVX2 intrinsics. The assembly was not inspected.

**Consequences for BVSMC.**
- **Its lanes depend on U16Q:** BVSMC's 8-lane gain was measured as an interaction with the same
  16-byte emit, and the emit half did not carry over.
- **The next step is a check, not a port:** compare the C harness's Open against native Open's
  phase times before spending a port on BVSMC.

The fidelity ledger (CONTEXT.md §5a) records the row.

## Calibration: the C harness is faithful, and the kernel gain is real natively

`calib.rs` (in this directory) is a release-mode test inside `src/open`. It times `construct` and
`emit` (with the 16-byte table built) on the six admitted blocks, best of 25, on the Coordinator's
container. It ran on master and on #664's head (58bd0c43).

| block | C harness Open total | native Open total (master) | native U16Q total (#664) | native ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.424 | 1.426 | 1.292 | 0.91 |
| 2 | 1.736 | 1.812 | 1.550 | 0.86 |
| 85 | 1.894 | 1.887 | 1.659 | 0.88 |
| 143 | 3.352 | 3.415 | 3.034 | 0.89 |
| 207 | 2.422 | 2.172 | 1.935 | 0.89 |
| 210 | 2.860 | 3.056 | 2.656 | 0.87 |

*ms. Measured; the C column is the Coordinator's rerun of `g5_bvsmc.c` on the same machine.*

**What this shows.**
- **The C baseline matches native Open** to within a few percent per block, apart from block 207,
  which is noisy. The harness was not flattering Open.
- **U16Q speeds the native kernel up:** 0.86–0.91× of Open's construct-plus-emit time, inside the
  C harness's 0.77–0.92×. The port works.
- **The earlier "the C harness overstated" reading is withdrawn.** The kernel gain is real. It
  does not reach CI's end-to-end wall time at 1–4 threads.

**So the open question is where the kernel's time goes in the decoder.**
- **Is the inverse on the critical path** at the bench's thread counts?
- **Its scale suggests not:** the `wikidata` corpus decodes 209.7 MB in about 0.40 s at 1 thread,
  and the admission journal puts the open kernel at 355 ms over its 228 blocks on its machine.
- **Next:** the bench's stage table doesn't cover Open, so a stage attribution is the next
  measurement before any verdict on U16Q or BVSMC.
- **Where Open did matter:** its shipped gains were on ten cores, so the i9 at 10–20 threads is
  the other place a kernel gain could appear.

## Resolved: CI's bench never runs the open kernel

See `2026-11-20-open-stage-attribution.md`.
- **What CI timed:** CI's `mibz2` arm is the serial reader at 1 thread, and Cells workers at 2
  and 4. `OpenCells` runs only from 8 workers (`LAZY_FROM_THREADS`), or on the measured i9's
  trimmed cells. So #664's −0.1% is the expected zero, not a refutation.
- **U16Q itself:** on the whole corpus it cuts the open kernel by 11.2% (436 → 388 ms), with every
  other stage level.
- **Where a gain can show:** a lazy-route CI arm, or the i9 at 6, 8 and 9 threads (worker-bound),
  or 10–20 threads (production's default, scanner-bound, so a smaller share).
