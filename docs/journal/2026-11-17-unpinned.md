**Why it is open** (LOG 6946756938, item 4; REVIEW 6947387421):
- *Every measurement behind the trims was pinned.* workers-at-cores, workers-confirm, lazy-occupancy, workers-sweep-low and eager-trim all ran their 4- to 10-thread arms under `ab run --spread`. That pins a run of T ≤ 10 threads to CPUs 0..T−1: one CPU on each of T cores, never two SMT siblings, with a held spinner on a free core below 10 threads.
- *A decoder in real use is usually unpinned.* On all 20 CPUs the scheduler may place two workers on one core's siblings. The scanner and the reader also get different scheduling opportunities there. The trimmed plan's advantage (fewer workers, less L3 and memory-bandwidth contention) may be larger, smaller or gone.
- *Production disagrees with the measurements.* `trims_to_seven` (#524) reads the topology through `Cpus_allowed_list`, so at 10 threads it trims to lazy 7/28 only unpinned (20 CPUs allowed). That was never measured. It declines pinned (10 CPUs allowed), where 7/28 was measured. The low-thread cells (8, 6 and now 4 threads) would split the same way, and their implementation (`perf/low-thread-trims`, #537) is held at the predicate until this is measured.
- *Identity is not performance* (REVIEW 6947387421): recognising the i9 from all its present and online CPUs fixes which machine it is, not that a trim measured on one mask carries to another.

## The run (`just bench-unpinned`)

The run follows #521's and #533's, count by count, with one difference: **every decoder process runs unpinned.**
- **One build** with every arm in it, by rustc 1.99.1 (`RUSTUP_TOOLCHAIN=1.99.1`, checked). `rustc -vV`, `cargo -vV` and the binary's SHA-256 are recorded.
- **One `ab run` per count,** with that count's arms alone: 10 threads, then 8, then 6, then 4. All four append to one file. Within a count the arms alternate round by round.
- **The corpora:** 4 copies of the Wikidata sample (`wikidata-x4`, the gain family), and text (`ab gen` at scale 16) as a loss and memory guard.
- **8 paired rounds,** as #521 and #533.
- **Unpinned, with `ab run --unpinned` (new).** Every run, at every count, is spawned with its affinity set to every online CPU (`/sys/devices/system/cpu/online`, 0–19 on the i9). The affinity is set between the fork and the exec, and read back there: the spawn fails unless the child's affinity is exactly that list.
  - So the decoder starts on all 20 CPUs whatever the shell's affinity, and never on a subset a cpuset quietly narrowed it to.
  - The record's `cpus` is that list (`"0,1,...,19"`), not `all`, which says only that a run inherited the harness's affinity.
  - Without the option, `ab run` is as before (tested), so no other recipe changes.
- **No spinner.** An unpinned decode may run on any CPU, so no core is free to hold one on:
  - A spinner on any CPU would share a core with the decode, which the checker already rejects (`overlap`). The scheduler could also put a worker on its CPU, taking a CPU from the decode, and by a different amount for 10 workers than for 7.
  - The spinner's purpose is to stop an idle machine starting a decode in a slow mode (cold-start/20261110T102700Z). That matters least here: these runs keep 3 to 10 workers busy plus the scanner and reader, and the release-scaling runs' unpinned counts (12 to 20 threads) never held one.
  - So this run measures the **combined unpinned, no-spinner use case**. This is a registered difference from the pinned runs at 4, 6 and 8 threads, which held a spinner, and it is part of the treatment "as in real use", not separated from it.
  - Any idle-state effect that remains is **not** assumed common to the pair: worker count can interact with turbo, idle-state recovery and scheduling. Both arms of a cell meet the same conditions, and the arms' alternating order reduces drift, but neither proves the effects cancel.

  `ab run` refuses `--unpinned` with `--hold-cpu`, `--spread` or `--pin`. The settle gate (`--max-busy 1.5`) and `others_busy` are recorded as now. The sampler finds no CPU outside the run's, so it stays among them, as in every unpinned run past the cores.
- **The masks:** the bundle keeps each record's `cpus`, plus `ab env`, `lscpu -e`, `Cpus_allowed_list` and `/sys/devices/system/cpu/online`.

## The rule (`bench/two_arm.py`, #521's and #533's `--judge-at --sparse`, with a reference by count)

- **A cell's step:** for each candidate at its count, the rounds' paired wall ratios, the candidate over that count's reference: the geometric mean and its 95% interval (Student's t on the logs). It is FASTER if the interval lies below 1, SLOWER if above, and INCONCLUSIVE otherwise.
- **Gain:** Holm's one-sided tests over **the five judged Wikidata cells together**, family α = 0.05.
- **Each count is read on its own.** For each candidate there:
  - *the SLOWER screen:* neither of its two cells (Wikidata and text) is SLOWER;
  - *the memory gate:* in both corpora, the median over the rounds of each run's peak RSS, the candidate over the reference, is at most 1.10.
- **A verdict for each candidate:**
  - ADOPTS: a gain, no SLOWER cell, and the memory gate passed;
  - HELD ON MEMORY: a gain and no SLOWER cell, but the memory gate failed (the Coordinator's to weigh under PERFORMANCE-POLICY rule 4);
  - A FINDING, NOT ADOPTED: a gain, but a cell SLOWER;
  - NOT ADOPTED: otherwise (no gain established, with or without a SLOWER cell).
- **A choice for each count:** of the candidates that ADOPT there, the lowest geometric mean of its Wikidata step (only 6 threads has two). If none adopts, the reference stays.
- **VOID** (exit 1, no verdict), on #521's conditions:
  - *the records:* one missing, duplicated or outside the sparse roster (144 at R = 8: 9 arm-count pairs × 2 corpora × 8 rounds), a run that did not succeed, a non-finite figure, or a corpus whose outputs differ;
  - *the machine:* not 10 cores and 20 CPUs with each core's CPUs listed, or under a quota;
  - **the unpinning (new, `--unpinned`):** at any count, a record whose `cpus` is not a list of all 20 CPUs, each once (`all` included); a spinner recorded; or the checker told to allow one (`--hold-max-threads` not 0);
  - **the references (new, `--ref-at ARM=COUNTS`, in place of `--ref`):** a judged count with no reference or two, a count that is not judged, a reference that is also a candidate, or `--ref-at` without `--judge-at --sparse`;
  - *the judging arguments*, the pair's included.
- **Tests:**
  - **`bench/test_two_arm.py`** (CI) has 116 checks: #533's 99 unchanged, and 17 for this run. They cover:
    - a verdict at each of the four counts against its own reference, with the choice at 6 and the pair printed;
    - a loss at one count and the memory gate at another;
    - VOID for a run pinned one CPU a core, a run recorded as `all`, a CPU missing, a spinner recorded, a spinner allowed, and the unpinned records read as pinned ones;
    - every malformed `--ref-at`.
  - **`ab`'s tests** (`cargo test --example ab`, CI) cover the option:
    - it is off by default, today's flags parse as before and today's CPUs are unchanged;
    - `--unpinned` names every CPU at every count, 1 thread included;
    - it is refused with `--spread`, `--pin` or `--hold-cpu`;
    - **the affinity a child actually starts with** equals every online CPU and the record's list. The child is spawned from a thread pinned to one CPU and reads its own `/proc/self/status`.

## How the outcomes map to decisions

Each count is read on its own, after the verdict. The question at each is the same: **does the trim hold unpinned?** The answer covers one mode only, **unpinned with the full mask** (every online CPU allowed), as this run measures it.

**The modes are kept apart.**
- *The unpinned mode:* the full mask, all 20 CPUs, as here.
- *The pinned mode:* the exactly-T-core class, one CPU on each of exactly T distinct cores, T CPUs in all, as `--spread` measured. It is not "at least T cores": with eight cores allowed at T = 4, say, the front end can be freed, and that was never measured.
- *Both:* a successful cell here qualifies the trim **in the unpinned mode only**, alongside the pinned mode already measured. It qualifies **no other mask on the i9**: not some cores, not two SMT siblings to a core, nothing between the two measured classes. Trimming in any other mask is an extrapolation, needing its own forward C6 review.
- *Recognising the i9* from all its present and online CPUs fixes which machine it is. Whether to trim then depends on the mode, read from the affinity, so the plan holds **a cell per mode**.

**The outcomes, at each count:**
- **(A) The trim qualifies unpinned:** the candidate chosen at that count ADOPTS. The plan may trim in the unpinned mode at that count, as well as in the pinned mode where it was measured.
  - *At 10 threads* this supports 0.5.3's unpinned trim, and `trims_to_seven` may also fire in the exactly-10-core pinned mode, where 7/28 was measured.
  - *At 8, 6 and 4* it gives #537 its unpinned cells.
  - Each change still goes through its own PR, CI and review.
- **(B) The trim does not qualify unpinned:** any valid result that is not ADOPTS for every candidate at that count. That is:
  - INCONCLUSIVE;
  - nominally FASTER without a Holm gain;
  - a gain with a cell SLOWER, on Wikidata or on text (A FINDING);
  - SLOWER without a gain;
  - HELD ON MEMORY.

  The plan then does not trim in the unpinned mode at that count. The pinned mode keeps whatever its own measurements qualified.
  - **At 10 threads,** 0.5.3 trims to 7 workers only unpinned, so a (B) there means **0.5.3's unpinned trim is withdrawn in the next release, and the 0.5.3 release notes' 10-thread claim is corrected.**
- **No repeat.** A valid result is final for this registration: no repeat, no extension and no second run to pass. Only a **VOID for an objective cause** (a missing or malformed record, the wrong machine profile, a failed run, the unpinning or the spinner checks) is re-run, once the cause is fixed, with the rule unchanged. A new question would need its own registration, reviewed before its data.
- **At 6 threads,** the two modes may rank the arms differently.
  - *An unpinned winner* applies to unpinned use only, and **does not replace the pinned choice** (eager 5/20, REVIEW 6947387421).
  - *If eager and lazy 5/20 both ADOPT,* the choice rule picks the unpinned cell's kernel.
  - *If only lazy 5/20 ADOPTS,* the unpinned cell is lazy 5/20 and the pinned cell stays eager 5/20.

Mixed outcomes are expected to be possible (a trim may qualify at 10 and not at 4, say), and each count follows its own row.

**The false-positive cost, stated:** Holm over five cells holds the family's error at 0.05 across the four counts.

**The power** decides between (A) and (B), and anything short of ADOPTS reads as (B), so it is stated now, from the Researcher's calculation (REVIEW 6948628221, with its report). That calculation used the paired-log spread recomputed from the verified eager-trim bundle (the 8-thread cell from its published interval), assuming it carries over unpinned.
- *Holm's strictest threshold:* one-sided 0.01.
- *At that threshold, the conditional power for a true 2% gain:*

  | cell | power |
  |---|--:|
  | lazy 7/28 at 10 | about 100% |
  | lazy 6/24 at 8 | 99% |
  | eager 5/20 at 6 | 89% |
  | eager 3/12 at 4 | 68% |
  | lazy 5/20 at 6 | 51% |
- *80% power* needs a gain of about **2.27%** for eager 3/12 at 4 and **2.71%** for lazy 5/20 at 6.
- *Later Holm thresholds* can raise these. They are planning sensitivities, not joint probabilities of ADOPTS.
- *Unpinned runs* may also be noisier than pinned ones, since the scheduler's placement varies from run to run.

The run keeps **8 rounds, with no adaptive extension**, and accepts unresolved outcomes. A true gain that is smaller, or noisier, than these figures can come out INCONCLUSIVE, and is then (B) for that count: "not shown unpinned", not "shown absent".

## Context: the knee diagnostic (appended before any data; the predictions above are not rewritten)

The knee diagnostic (LOG 6948385717, knee/run-20261117T123939Z) ran unpinned on Wikidata ×4. It changes expectations, not this rule.
- **Pinned runs leave workers idle; unpinned runs don't.**
  - *Pinned* to N CPUs, the scanner and main share the workers' cores, and the workers idle 12–16% of the time, almost all of it in the front end.
  - *Unpinned,* they compute 93–95% of the time at every count.
  - *At 4 threads,* unpinned was 0.405 s against 0.535 s pinned: 0.757, about 24% less wall time.
- **Miss traffic per output byte rises with the workers:** 6.9 B at 4, 11.0 at 7, 12.7 at 8, 14.7 at 10 pinned.

**Why a faster unpinned reference doesn't predict a smaller trim gain** (REVIEW 6948628221). The candidate speeds up unpinned too. With R a cell's ratio (candidate over reference), C the candidate's wall and B the reference's:

R_unpinned / R_pinned = (C_unpinned / C_pinned) / (B_unpinned / B_pinned)

So the unpinned ratio moves from the pinned one only as far as the two arms gain differently from the full mask.

Two cautions:
- *Placement:* full affinity permits off-worker-core scheduling, but does not prove it happened.
- *Busy time:* it includes memory stalls.

The Holm family, the loss screen and the RSS limit stand as registered.
