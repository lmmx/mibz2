TYPE: REVIEW

**The question:** do the worker trims at 6, 8 and 10 threads, measured only pinned one CPU a core, hold when the decoder runs unpinned on all 20 CPUs of the i9, as production decoders usually do?

**Why it is open** (LOG 6946756938, item 4):
- *Every measurement behind the trims was pinned.* workers-at-cores, workers-confirm, lazy-occupancy and workers-sweep-low all ran their 6-, 8- and 10-thread arms under `ab run --spread`, which pins a run of T ≤ 10 threads to CPUs 0..T−1: one CPU on each of T cores, never two SMT siblings.
- *A decoder in real use is usually unpinned.* On all 20 CPUs the scheduler may place two workers on one core's siblings, and the trimmed plan's advantage (fewer workers, less L3 and memory-bandwidth contention) may be larger, smaller or gone there.
- *Production disagrees with the measurements.* `trims_to_seven` (#524) reads the topology through `Cpus_allowed_list`, so at 10 threads it trims to lazy 7/28 only unpinned (20 CPUs allowed), which was never measured, and declines pinned (10 CPUs allowed), where 7/28 was measured. The low-thread cells adopted in LOG 6946575966 (8 and 6 threads) would split the same way, and their implementation (`perf/low-thread-trims`) is held at the predicate until this is measured.

## The cells

Explicit arms at each count, so production's predicate plays no part. The reference at each count is the untrimmed plan, the decoder `Plan::Workers(T)` builds with 4T blocks, named as an explicit arm:

| threads asked | the reference (untrimmed) | the candidate (trimmed) | the pinned measurement (the prediction) |
|--:|---|---|---|
| 10 | `mibz2-occ-10-40`: lazy, 10 workers, 40 blocks | `mibz2-occ-7-28`: lazy 7/28 | **0.881–0.885** |
| 8 | `mibz2-occ-8-32`: lazy 8/32 | `mibz2-occ-6-24`: lazy 6/24 | **0.925** |
| 6 | `mibz2-oce-6-24`: eager 6/24 | `mibz2-occ-5-20`: lazy 5/20 | **0.962** |

- `mibz2-occ-N-S` is `BzReader::parallel_workers(N, S, k, lazy = true)`; from 8 workers it is the very call `parallel_planned(Plan::Workers(N), S)` makes. `mibz2-oce-N-S` is the same with `lazy = false`, below 8 workers the very call production's eager plan makes (#533). So each reference is what production builds at that count with no trim, and each candidate is the trim the pinned runs measured.
- The predictions are the wall ratios, candidate over the untrimmed decoder, that the pinned runs measured (LOG 6946756938 and the runs it cites). They are scored after the run and enter no verdict.

That is three judged cells on the Wikidata corpus and three on text.

## The run (`just bench-unpinned`)

As #521's and #533's, count by count, with one difference: **every decoder process runs unpinned.**
- **One build** with every arm in it, by rustc 1.99.1 (`RUSTUP_TOOLCHAIN=1.99.1`, checked). `rustc -vV`, `cargo -vV` and the binary's SHA-256 are recorded.
- **One `ab run` per count,** with that count's two arms alone (10, then 8, then 6 threads), all appending to one file. Within a count the arms alternate round by round.
- **The corpora:** 4 copies of the Wikidata sample (`wikidata-x4`, the gain family), and text (`ab gen` at scale 16) as a loss and memory guard.
- **10 paired rounds.**
- **Unpinned: `ab run --unpinned`, new.** Every run, at every count, is spawned with its affinity set to every online CPU (`/sys/devices/system/cpu/online`, 0–19 on the i9), between the fork and the exec, and read back there: the spawn fails unless the child's affinity is exactly that list. So the decoder starts on all 20 CPUs whatever the shell's affinity, and never on a subset a cpuset quietly narrowed it to. The record's `cpus` is that list (`"0,1,...,19"`), not `all`, which says only that a run inherited the harness's affinity. Without the option, `ab run` is as before (tested), so no other recipe changes.
- **No spinner.** An unpinned decode may run on any CPU, so no core is free to hold one on:
  - a spinner on any CPU would share a core with the decode, which the checker already rejects (`overlap`), and the scheduler could put a worker on its CPU, taking a CPU from the decode, by a different amount for 10 workers than for 7;
  - the spinner's purpose (an idle machine starting a decode in a slow mode, cold-start/20261110T102700Z) is weakest here: these runs keep 5 to 10 workers busy, and the release-scaling runs' unpinned counts (12 to 20 threads) never held one;
  - both arms of a cell meet the same condition, paired round by round, so any idle-state effect that remains is common to the pair.

  `ab run` refuses `--unpinned` with `--hold-cpu` (and with `--spread` or `--pin`). The settle gate (`--max-busy 1.5`) and `others_busy` are recorded as now. The sampler, finding no CPU outside the run's, stays among them, as in every unpinned run past the cores.
- **The masks:** each record's `cpus`, plus `ab env`, `lscpu -e`, `Cpus_allowed_list` and `/sys/devices/system/cpu/online`, are kept in the bundle.

## The rule (`bench/two_arm.py`, #521's and #533's `--judge-at --sparse`, with a reference by count)

- **A cell's step:** for each candidate at its count, the rounds' paired wall ratios, the candidate over that count's reference: the geometric mean and its 95% interval (Student's t on the logs). It is FASTER if the interval lies below 1, SLOWER if above, and INCONCLUSIVE otherwise.
- **Gain:** Holm's one-sided tests over **the three judged Wikidata cells together**, family α = 0.05.
- **Each count is read on its own.** For the candidate there:
  - *the SLOWER screen:* neither of its two cells (Wikidata and text) is SLOWER;
  - *the memory gate:* in both corpora, the median over the rounds of each run's peak RSS, the candidate over the reference, is at most 1.10.
- **A verdict at each count:**
  - ADOPTS: a gain, no SLOWER cell, and the memory gate passed;
  - HELD ON MEMORY: a gain and no SLOWER cell, but the memory gate failed (the Coordinator's to weigh under PERFORMANCE-POLICY rule 4);
  - A FINDING, NOT ADOPTED: a gain, but a cell SLOWER;
  - NOT ADOPTED: otherwise (no gain established, with or without a SLOWER cell).
- **VOID** (exit 1, no verdict), on #521's conditions:
  - the records: one missing, duplicated or outside the sparse roster (120 at R = 10: 6 arm-count pairs × 2 corpora × 10 rounds), a run that did not succeed, a non-finite figure, or a corpus whose outputs differ;
  - the machine: not 10 cores and 20 CPUs with each core's CPUs listed, or under a quota;
  - **the unpinning (new, `--unpinned`):** a record's `cpus` not a list of every one of the 20 CPUs, each once, at any count (`all` included); a spinner recorded, or the checker told to allow one (`--hold-max-threads` not 0);
  - **the references (new, `--ref-at ARM=COUNTS`, in place of `--ref`):** a judged count with no reference or two, a count that is not judged, a reference that is also a candidate, or `--ref-at` without `--judge-at --sparse`;
  - the judging arguments.
- **Tests:** `bench/test_two_arm.py` (CI) has 116 checks: #533's 99 unchanged, and 17 for this run: a verdict at each count against its own reference, a loss at one count and the memory gate at another; a run pinned one CPU a core, a run recorded as `all`, a CPU missing, a spinner recorded, a spinner allowed, and the unpinned records read as pinned ones, each VOID; and every malformed `--ref-at`. `ab`'s tests (`cargo test --example ab`, CI) cover the option: off by default with today's flags parsing and today's CPUs unchanged, `--unpinned` naming every CPU at every count (1 thread included), refused with `--spread`, `--pin` or `--hold-cpu`, and **the affinity a child actually starts with** (read from its own `/proc/self/status`, spawned from a thread pinned to one CPU) equal to every online CPU and to the record's list.

## How the outcomes map to decisions

Read count by count, after the verdict:

- **(A) The trim holds unpinned** (ADOPTS at that count): the trim helps both pinned one-per-core (measured before) and unpinned (here). The predicate should then **recognise the i9 machine-wide**, from the present and online CPUs rather than `Cpus_allowed_list`, and trim pinned and unpinned alike at that count. At 10 threads that changes `trims_to_seven` to fire pinned too; at 8 and 6 it unblocks `perf/low-thread-trims` with that predicate. Each change still goes through its own PR and CI; this run is its evidence.
- **(B) The trim does not hold unpinned** (NOT ADOPTED or A FINDING at that count): a gain is shown only pinned one CPU a core. The trim is then made **only where it was measured** (a pinned one-per-core affinity of exactly T CPUs on T distinct cores), **or not at all at that count**: the Coordinator chooses, and records why. At 10 threads this matters for what has shipped: 0.5.3 trims to 7 workers only unpinned, the case not shown to help, so
  - *SLOWER on Wikidata at 10:* `trims_to_seven` is withdrawn unpinned in the next release, and its release notes' 10-thread claim corrected;
  - *INCONCLUSIVE at 10:* the unpinned trim is unsupported but not shown to hurt. The Coordinator decides between withdrawing it and a repeat, and the 0.5.3 claim is corrected to "unverified unpinned" either way.
- **HELD ON MEMORY:** the Coordinator weighs it under PERFORMANCE-POLICY rule 4. A trimmed decoder holds fewer blocks, so this is not expected.
- **VOID:** nothing is decided, and the run is repeated once the cause is fixed.

Mixed outcomes are expected to be possible (a trim may hold at 10 and not at 6, say), and each count follows its own row.

**The false-positive cost, stated:** Holm over three cells holds the family's error at 0.05 across the three counts. The power is what decides between (A) and (B), and INCONCLUSIVE reads as (B), so it is stated now. Pinned at 8 rounds (#521, LOG 6945639925), the 8-thread cell was 0.925 [0.918, 0.933] and the 6-thread cell 0.963 [0.948, 0.977], half-widths of about 0.8% and 1.5%. At 10 rounds, with the same spread, they would be about 14% narrower (Student's t and √R together: 2.262/√10 against 2.365/√8), so an unpinned effect of about −2% or more at 6 threads, and much less at 8 and 10, would resolve. Unpinned runs may be noisier than pinned ones, since the scheduler's placement varies from run to run. An unpinned effect much smaller than the pinned one may then come out INCONCLUSIVE, and is read as (B) for that count: "not shown unpinned", not "shown absent".
