## 3. Why the Transfer Ratio Splits This Way (Microarchitectural Diagnosis)

1. **Why Station `S` transfers ~`1.0×` (`0.99–1.13×`):** Serial Huffman decoding (`A2` + `A3`) and window maintenance (`in_maint`) are branch-, shift-, and sequential-`memcpy`-bound with a tiny `24 KiB` L1d-resident LUT. Neither Haswell-EP nor Comet Lake hits deep L3/DRAM pointer-chase stalls in `S`, so relative instruction and byte-copy reductions translate ~1:1 between VM and physical i9.
2. **Why `W` compute/ALU levers transfer at `0.20–0.47×` (median `0.45×`):** On the 5.3/4.9 GHz physical i9-10900K, `OpenCells` worker wall is dominated by L3/DRAM pointer-chasing latency inside `A7` emission (random walks through `edges`), whereas on a 2.3 GHz Haswell-EP VM with 45 MiB L3, compute stages (`CRC`, `runs::front`, `A6b`, `A6c`) represent a much larger share of `W` — and Haswell's unaligned `base` JCC-erratum penalty inflates VM `OpenCells` `base` denominators further (`+13.7..+15.6%`). Removing compute instructions without reducing pointer-chase latency therefore shrinks i9 `W` by only `~0.2–0.5×` of the VM percentage.
3. **Why `W` MLP levers transfer at `1.24–1.97×` (`> 1×`):** Four-lane forward emission (`#1217`, `#1312`) directly attacks the pointer-chasing miss-latency bottleneck that dominates Comet Lake (`4×` concurrent loads inside a 224-entry ROB). That exact same miss-latency dominance that compresses compute levers (`0.45×`) **amplifies** MLP levers (`1.24–1.50×` vs `base` VM, `1.73–1.97×` vs `JCC-32B` VM).
4. **Why `JCC-32B` on the VM is the right control despite failing on i9:** Comet Lake-S stepping 5 has the hardware JCC-erratum fix (`#1119`), so `JCC-32B` must never ship in release flags, yet on the Haswell-EP VM it removes artificial branch-alignment swings (`#1328` `base` `−6.23%` vs `JCC` `−2.37%`; `default-a5a6a` `base` `−10.01%` vs `JCC` `−5.68%`; `M5` `mibz2 1W` `base` `+2.56%` vs `JCC` `−0.66%`). Price local VM probes off their **`JCC-32B`** column first, then multiply by the mechanism prior.
