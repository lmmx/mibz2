mibz2 beside other bzip2 decoders on master's latest recorded commit, decoded in the same rounds on one runner (methodology). A ratio above 1× is the alternative taking longer (or more memory) than mibz2: there, mibz2 is faster. These are decoder-level results; end-to-end pipeline results are in wikidata-pq's tracker.

decoder what runs threads
pbz2 Soof Golan's Rust decoder: DecoderReader, or ParallelDecoder::with_threads block-parallel
lbzip2 the C program: lbzip2 -dc -n T, read through a pipe block-parallel
indexed_bzip2 C++, from Python: IndexedBzip2File(path, parallelization=T) (bench/ib.py; the interpreter's start left out) block-parallel
fbz Answer.AI's Rust decoder: fbz::Reader, threads: T block-parallel
lbzip2-rs the lbzip2 crate (pure Rust): StreamingBz2Read, on the CPUs it may run on (it takes no thread count) block-parallel
libbzip2 the reference C library, 1.0.8: the bzip2 crate's bundled source, MultiBzDecoder (as bzip2 -dc and Python's bz2 decode) single-threaded
libbz2-rs libbz2-rs-sys, the pure-Rust port of libbzip2, through the bzip2 crate 0.6 single-threaded
bz2ngrs Softleif's safe-Rust port of bz2-ng, at a pinned commit: DecompressReader single-threaded (one thread a stream)
bz2ng bz2-ng's C, at a pinned commit: its bzip2 -dc, read through a pipe (the reference bz2ngrs ports) single-threaded

Single-threaded decoders run at 1 thread only. libbzip2, libbz2-rs, bz2ngrs and bz2ng have no thread count, so they never decode on 4 threads. At 4 threads, their figures are their 1-thread runs against mibz2's at 4 threads: in the chart, hollow points with dashed whiskers, under "single-threaded (1 thread)"; in the tables, marked ¹.

mibz2's inverse BWT against bz2ngrs's and libsais's. Below the decoders' tables, the kernels run on the same Wikidata blocks, with the build, the walk and the output's assembly timed apart where a kernel allows it. libsais 2.12.1 (Ilya Grebnov) is the fastest inverse BWT measured here. It is a library of BWT algorithms, not a bzip2 decoder, so it appears in that table only, never in the chart. Its build and walk happen inside one call, so it shows a total. bz2ngrs and mibz2 store the same successor link in each 32-bit entry, but a different byte beside it: bz2ngrs the row's first-column byte, mibz2 its last-column byte (lbzip2's packing). The table says which phase a whole-decoder difference comes from (the study).

Not included: bzip2 -dc and Python's bz2 module, which are libbzip2 itself (its arm stands for them); and bz2k, mibz2's own prototype, which it replaced.

Each round decodes every corpus once with each decoder at each thread count (ab run), their order rotated round to round. Against mibz2 is the decoder's wall (or CPU, or peak RSS) over mibz2's: the geometric mean of the rounds' paired ratios and its 95% interval; above 1, mibz2 is faster (or leaner). 1-thread runs are pinned to one CPU.

Block-parallel: pbz2, lbzip2, indexed_bzip2, fbz, lbzip2-rs. Single-threaded (¹): libbzip2, libbz2-rs, bz2ngrs, bz2ng, run at 1 thread only; at 4 threads their figures are their 1-thread runs, against mibz2's at 4 threads.

Resources: CPU per byte and scaling, the wikidata corpus¶

CPU per byte is the process's CPU (every thread, user + sys) over the output. Amplification is that at N threads over the same build's at 1, paired by round: 1× is no extra CPU for the same bytes, and above it the work costs more as threads are added (stalls, sharing, synchronisation; CPU counts a thread stalled on memory as running). Efficiency is the speedup over N. Run-queue is the threads' mean count runnable but not running.

Best: mibz2 at 4 threads (509 MB/s); lbzip2 at 4 threads (185 MB/s); pbz2 at 4 threads (290 MB/s).

Conditions: 4 CPUs on 2 physical cores (0,1 | 2,3); no CPU quota; the run-queue counter checked and counting; 2-thread runs on CPUs 0,2 (one CPU a core); 4-thread runs share cores' SMT siblings.

How to read it, and what it cannot show - **The four comparisons the assessment reads,** declared in bench/report.py (`assessment`): best against best (wall), CPU per byte at the most threads, amplification at each thread count, and peak RSS at the most threads. Each moves only where its 95% interval excludes no change. With no real change, about one run in five flags one by chance: a flag that matters reproduces on a re-run. - **A trade-off is not a failure.** Faster for more CPU or memory, or the reverse, is for the reviewer to weigh against the deployment; the report names it, and nothing here fails CI but the outputs and the accounting. - **What it can't show:** which shared resource a rise in amplification comes from (cache, memory bandwidth, SMT, the scanner's handoffs): no hardware counters are read. CI's runners have 2 physical cores, so it cannot show a knee past 2 (the workstation's is at 4-5 workers, #39). The cores' L3 is shared with other tenants and its size unknown. - **Run-queue wait** (threads' mean count runnable, not running; schedstat): mibz2 1t 0.00, mibz2 2t 0.63, mibz2 4t 0.94, lbzip2 1t 0.09, lbzip2 2t 0.33, lbzip2 4t 0.52, pbz2 1t 0.00, pbz2 2t 0.29, pbz2 4t 0.40. A pinned 2-thread run has four threads (two workers, the scanner, the reader) on two CPUs, so some wait is its shape, not interference. - **The per-thread split's unattributed CPU** (the process's total that no sampled thread holds, sampled every 10 ms): mibz2 2t 6%, mibz2 4t 6%, lbzip2 2t 4%, lbzip2 4t 5%, pbz2 2t 3%, pbz2 4t 3%.

Modelled, not measured: a whole dump¶

The decode of wikidata-20261019-all.json.bz2 (103 GB of bz2; 1.83 TB of JSON at the sample's 17.69×), scaled from the Wikidata blocks' runs above, round by round (mean ± its 95% interval, the sample ratio's own uncertainty added in):

mibz2
1 thread 1h58m ± 14m23s
4 threads 59m57s ± 8m26s
4 threads, the sample's overlap gap as a fixed cost 42m12s ± 5m55s
the scanner alone (its serial part) 16m35s ± 32.2s
the workers alone, over 4 42m12s ± 5m55s

At 4 threads the workers bound it. Per byte: the scanner 9.6 ns a byte of bz2, the workers 5.53 ns a byte of output at 4 threads (CPU, summed over them), 3.35 ns at 1 thread (wall, less the scanner's part); the scanner would bind from about 10 worker threads (mibz2).

What the model assumes - **Linear in bytes:** the scanner's time in the dump's bz2 bytes, the workers' in its output bytes. - **The sample stands for the dump:** 12 windows of 1 MiB at (2i + 1)/24 of the dump, 431 of its own blocks; their mix of entities and their ratio, 17.69× (the windows' 95% interval ±14.0%), stand for the whole dump's. Another sample of the same dump (wikidata-pq's bench/full, 24 windows of 25 MB) measured 18.68×; wikidata-pq's CI slice, lines recompressed, is 13.69×. - **The wall at N threads** is the larger of the scanner's time and the workers' time over N (the scanner is one thread; the workers run blocks in parallel), times the sample's own wall over that bound: its overlap and fill taken to grow with the work, which errs long. Read as a fixed cost instead, the same gap gives the third row. - **The machine is this runner,** reading the dump from page cache: no download, no disk, no other work. The output is hashed by the reading thread and discarded. - The uncertainty is the rounds' spread, with the sample ratio's added in quadrature to the part that scales with the output; not the runner's: another CPU model gives other figures.
