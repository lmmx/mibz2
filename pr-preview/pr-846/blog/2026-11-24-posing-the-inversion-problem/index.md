# Posing the inversion problem

The search for a new inversion algorithm became an eight-step programme, from a cost referee and measured ceilings to the model that will say where new mathematics fits.

24 November 2026 · 14 min read · [Research synthesis](<#8-where-things-stand>)

The work began as a search for a new mathematical basis for undoing bzip2's block sort, and it has become a programme in eight steps. The first two are done: a cost referee says what would count as beating the floor ([What else moves together](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/pr-preview/pr-846/blog/2026-11-24-what-else-moves-together/index.md>)), and oracle measurements say where whole-decoder time can be won at all, up to 26% at ten workers, hidden until now behind the serial scanner ([Masked bottlenecks in a two-stage decoder](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/pr-preview/pr-846/blog/2026-11-24-the-scanner-hides-the-emitter/index.md>)). The middle steps treat the decoder as what it is, a closed queueing network, and apply the methods of classical performance analysis to it: operational laws as completeness checks, designed experiments across every worker count, and a model fitted on some counts and judged on the rest. That model's purpose is the seventh step, to say which activities really control throughput at the operating points that matter, and so where a new mathematical device would have to act to move the whole decoder. The mathematics itself, the eighth step, already has a map of the routes ruled out and a conservation law that bounds one class of them. Every figure here comes from [the formulation report](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reports/2026-11-24-from-search-to-formulated-problem.md>) and the records it cites.

## 1\. The programme

The chain follows [Raj Jain's course](<https://www.cs.wustl.edu/~jain/iucee/index.html>) on computer systems performance analysis, built on his *The Art of Computer Systems Performance Analysis* (1991): metrics and workloads, then measurement, experimental design, queueing models and simulation ([the programme](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/programme.md>)).

| Step | What it is | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The task and the referee: what counts as success, in which currency | done |
| 2 | The toy problem: a fixed machine, workload and referee, with payoff ceilings by oracle | done; three ceilings unmeasured |
| 3 | The system's structure: activities, buffers, shared resources and the feedback loop | designed |
| 4 | Measurement across 1 to 20 workers | the scanner profile has run; results pending |
| 5 | Experimental design: a baseline at every count, then interventions at chosen counts | being built; the second stage awaits a design review |
| 6 | The model: operational laws, a simulation fitted and held out, a roofline per activity | after step 5's data |
| 7 | Where new mathematics fits: the activities with real control, and what a device must change there | after step 6 |
| 8 | The mathematics, aimed at step 7's targets | continuing |

## 2\. Steps 1 and 2: the referee and the toy problem

The problem statement agreed by three independent answers asks for one uniform program, exact on every column, that builds its structures from the column within fixed budgets and pays at most half the old floor on every block. The currency is real machine cost: unpredictable memory reads (Q), the length of the chain of reads that wait on one another (D), passes over the data (P, at most two) and working memory (S), with the cost of building every structure included ([journal §33](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). How candidates are found, by changing what moves together and by making existing steps yield more, is the subject of the earlier post; the cost rules referee candidates only once they exist.

Two statements made in earlier summaries, including ours, had to be withdrawn ([journal §42](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)):

- **G1 is not a lower bound on machine cost.** It bounds charges in a calculus of rigid interval moves; carrying it into machine cost needs a bridge theorem that holds for most of the old family but not all of it.
- **The 16% figure was never a ceiling.** It described one intervention, supplying the emitter's codes, at one thread. The four-arm test has since shown that the same intervention is worth nothing measurable at ten workers until the scanner is removed, and then a further 8%.

The referee's rulings close the remaining loopholes. A breakthrough means fewer than one unpredictable read per [width-1 row](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/pr-preview/pr-846/blog/2026-11-24-halving-the-single-row-steps/index.md>), with the target at half of one, within two passes; a large cut in the chain of dependent reads is a real but lesser find. The number of live cursors is limited to about one per letter of the alphabet, so a route that needed some two thousand concurrent cursors to reach its figure was ruled inadmissible ([journal §49](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

The toy problem fixes the rest: an i9-10900K with AVX2, ten cores and twenty threads; the six admitted blocks, with synthetic hex beside them; and that referee. Its payoff ceilings come from the four-arm oracle ([journal §44](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)): emission is worth 15 to 17% at one to six workers, and at ten workers the serial scanner is worth up to 19% on its own and 26% together with emission. The decoder's work is divided into six targets, each tied to a stage: emission of the inversion (T1), its construction (T2), move-to-front and run-length decoding (T3), the Huffman scan (T4), riders across stages (T5) and the final run-length expansion and checksum (T6). Only T1, T4 and T5 have ceilings so far ([the targets](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/math-targets/targets.tsv>)).

## 3\. Step 3: the decoder as a closed network

The scanner and its workers are only a coarse picture. The measurement plan, merged from three independent answers to [a brief](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/decomposition.md>), divides the decoder into thirteen activities, from the input feeder through the scanner's table and Huffman decoding to the workers' move-to-front and run formation (one fused activity), the three phases of Open's construction, emission, the fallback route and the final checksum, and adds a serial tail in which a consumer takes finished blocks in order and writes them out ([the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §2; [journal §51](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

A fixed population of blocks, circulating

Schematic of the measurement plan's structure (§2 of the plan). The admission window holds a fixed number of blocks, so the decoder is a closed network, the case that classical mean-value analysis solves.

The feedback loop in the figure is what makes the network closed. Admission is limited by the blocks issued but not yet taken in order, and by a 12 MiB threshold on finished output, so a block that is queued, running, or finished out of order all hold a slot; one slow early block can stall the scanner through the consumer, and a waiting scanner does not by itself show that the workers are too few ([the outside answer](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/external/chatgpt-decomposition.md>)). Two further corrections from that answer shape every measurement. Requested threads are not actual workers, since a request for ten or more runs seven. And the summation theorem of [metabolic control analysis](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_control_analysis>) is no test of completeness: for an activity whose rate is the minimum of two others, the control coefficients sum to two under slowdowns and to zero under speedups.

Completeness comes instead from the operational laws of queueing theory, which hold on any measured trace without assumptions about distributions: Little's law, the utilisation law U = X·S, and the forced-flow law. The plan checks them explicitly for every activity and buffer ([the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §7a). Because the window holds W = 4k blocks, the closed-network results apply directly. Each activity's demand Dᵢ, its visits times its service time, gives asymptotic bounds on throughput, X ≤ min(1/D\_max, N/(D + Z)), with a knee at N\* = (D + Z)/D\_max, beyond which queueing is certain; the knee is the analytic counterpart of the worker count at which the scanner takes over. Mean-value analysis, the exact recursion for closed product-form networks, sits beside the simulation as a cheap check on it ([the methods](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/methodology/performance-courses.md>)).

## 4\. Steps 4 and 5: measurement and design

The first stage measures a baseline at every worker count from 1 to 20, in twelve randomised complete rounds, never as a rising sweep: 240 observations, each logging every block's route, stage boundaries, queue transitions and the four kinds of waiting, with separate ledgers for wall states and time on the CPU ([the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §3). Because delay depends on the variability of service and not only its mean, the plan reports the second moment of each activity's per-block service time as well. In a single-server queue with random arrivals (M/G/1), the mean wait is λE\[S²\]/(2(1 − ρ)), so in an illustrative case at 80% utilisation and a 10 ms mean service time, it is 20, 40 or 200 ms as the service time's squared coefficient of variation goes from 0 to 1 to 9 ([the outside answer on the courses](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/external/chatgpt-performance-courses.md>); [journal §54](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). Long-tail blocks are the mechanism behind the admission stalls.

The second stage perturbs the activities one at a time at six worker counts, 1, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 20, slowing each by 20%, with 10% follow-ups to test linearity, using a calibrated busy loop that touches no memory ([the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §5). One-factor-at-a-time cannot see interactions, and the four-arm result was itself an interaction. So at 8 and 10 workers, where control is expected to change hands, the plan replaces it with a 2⁹⁻⁴ fractional factorial of resolution IV: 32 runs over nine activities, which estimates every main effect clear of two-factor interactions, at 768 observations against 216 for one-at-a-time ([the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §7a). Before that stage is frozen, the design goes to three independent reviewers. They are asked which competing mechanisms it must tell apart, the most informative design for about two to three hours of workstation time, how measurement on the i9 could be biased, whether every input each model needs is logged, and what to cut ([the design review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character-design-review.md>); [journal §55](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

## 5\. Step 6: the model

The primary model is a discrete-event simulation of the network above, driven by each block's measured service times in their original order, with a roofline per activity (compute, dependent reads and bytes against what the machine allows at each worker count) to reject infeasible predictions. Mean-value analysis and a fluid approximation of scanner rate against worker demand give closed-form checks. The model is fitted on nine worker counts and judged once on the other eleven, on the 10% follow-ups, on a held-out combined slowdown and on the four-arm arms, against a target fixed in advance: at most 3% median and 5% maximum error on mean runtime, with the waiting fractions within five points. A model that fits throughput but gets the queues wrong fails ([the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §7).

## 6\. Step 7: where new mathematics fits

The model's purpose is to price a mathematical idea before anyone builds it. Modification analysis, from [Mor Harchol-Balter's course](<https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/Perfclass/class.html>) on performance modelling, asks what happens to a closed system when one device's demand changes; a candidate device is then a change to one Dᵢ, and the bounds and mean-value analysis say what it would buy the whole decoder ([the methods](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/methodology/performance-courses.md>); [the plan](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/briefs/character.md>), §7b). A recitation in that course shows why this matters: raising an LRU cache's hit ratio can lower throughput, by intensifying contention on the hit path, so that measurement, simulation and a bound together trace a rise, a plateau and a decline. It is a classroom version of the masked bottleneck, in which speeding up one stage did nothing until another was cleared ([the outside answer on the courses](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/external/chatgpt-performance-courses.md>)).

Engineering leads wait for the same answer. Stream compaction, the `copy_if` that does not autovectorise because each output position depends on every earlier predicate, can be done branchlessly by compressing the selected lanes of a vector and advancing by the popcount of the mask ([the post that measured it](<https://loonatick-src.github.io/posts/vectorized-copy-if-analysis/>)). The workstation has AVX2 but not AVX-512, so the decoder would use a shuffle table indexed by an 8-bit mask, or BMI2's `pext`, in the workers' filter steps: run-record extraction, the thin and thick partition of Open's construction, and the appends of emission. It lowers the unit price of those steps without changing what the algorithm does, and it is held until the decomposition shows that one of them controls throughput ([the lead](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/leads/vectorised-compaction.md>)).

## 7\. Step 8: the mathematics

Every route so far wins one cost by paying another

Schematic, with figures from the reviews ([the six senses](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-critique.md>), [the fourth cycle](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-cycle4-critique.md>)) and journal §§39 and 41. Routes that need few unpredictable reads take far more than two passes; routes that shorten the chain of waiting reads leave the reads where they were; routes that share reads need repeated text, which novel random IDs lack. With every charge counted, no route reaches the middle.

Twelve candidate senses of moving together were built, measured on the six real blocks and reviewed, and none brought the reads below one per width-1 row once every charge was counted. The best composed routes cost 1.3 to 2.8 reads per width-1 row all in, against 4.3 to 6.9 for [Open](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/pr-preview/pr-846/blog/2026-11-19-open-returns/index.md>)'s own walk measured the same way ([the fourth cycle's review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-cycle4-critique.md>)). Pointer doubling and independent-set contraction reported zero unpredictable reads, but at 117 to about 600 passes, and building their inputs costs reads after all; both are known list-ranking algorithms for external memory ([the review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/neon-movement-critique.md>)). Landmark induction, recounted, brought the chain of waiting reads to 0.07–0.22% of the block, a record that has been reproduced, but left the reads at about one per row ([journal §41](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). Four further objects, boundary chords, radix-fibre cones, quotient slabs and unitigs, were ruled out, two of them repeating an investigation from four days earlier ([the review](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/carbon-cycle1-critique.md>)), and lazily contracting the thin runs was closed by a rule registered in advance, because removing interior rows fragmented the order of reads 4 to 9 times over ([journal §49](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

Of the sweep that keeps its form and loses its compactness

One result explains why so many routes fail in the same way. [Record 119](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/8cf099eb24d9943417cfccdf3763b54fbcd9c26b/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-closure-problem.md>) shows that a sweep over the block is itself an induction, and that composing steps preserves the *form* of the access schedule but not its *compactness* on high-entropy rows. On the first block, the shipped decoder's next-row addresses fall into 2,148 orderly segments, 0.061 per width-1 row; one round of composition shatters them into 44,814–52,319, some 21 to 24 times as many ([journal §42](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)). The law accounts for that figure, and settles the closure problem negatively within its class: no cleverer sweep of this kind can compose without fragmenting. Smaller results stand beside it ([the review of records 118–125](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level/reviews/monodromy-118-125.md>)):

- a lower bound on failing steps for every interleaving of monotone cursors ([record 118](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/ad169f87d657edca999d503b3637da1b6ca0a597/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-interleaved-orders.md>));
- the block length, the run count, the number of width-1 rows and the run-width histogram are free by-products of the Huffman scan, and parsing blocks in parallel is exact, as [lbzip2](<https://github.com/kjn/lbzip2>) already does ([record 122](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/c348f8f5135959cf6d3dbf318001797673ec7201/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-scanner-corrected.md>));
- an exact composable summary of each chunk for move-to-front, run-length decoding and the run records ([record 121](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a3facefcb7d927e8284381a299c6a80f0b58409f/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-scanner-summary.md>));
- building the structures by interval-exchange acquisition costs 0.10–0.34 reads per width-1 row, which puts construction back at its true scale ([journal §49](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/a4bef380a2585687d773064617d5713f8cdc66b5/docs/journal/2026-11-24-algorithm-level.md>)).

Five threads remain open. Acquisition is the binding cost on real blocks, and a search-free strip contraction projects about 0.6 reads per width-1 row but is unmeasured ([record 120](<https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/5d0795c81f073c44cf1b2f9d62f0ac190ca07dd3/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-24-thin-strip-acquisition.md>)). A representation outside record 119's class could compose without fragmenting, which by the law no cleverer sweep can. The relation that pairs the same offset in repeated JSON records is open again, since the obstruction claimed against it proved invariance only. Riders across stages are not excluded by the theorems against them, which rule out final values before the block ends but not deferred ones. And at the scanner there is engineering to do: a parallel scan gated by worker count, which the four-arm test prices at up to 19%.

## 8\. Where things stand

The first two steps are done, and the structure is designed. The profile of the scanner across all twenty worker counts, recording memory, scheduling and waiting for each decode, has run on the workstation, and its results have not yet been read. The first stage of the characterisation, the baseline at every count, is being built. The second stage waits on the design review, whose three answers will be merged into the plan before it is frozen. Nothing in the model, and so nothing in the ranking of where mathematics fits, has been measured yet; the mathematics continues meanwhile against the targets as they stand.

The ceilings came from oracles run before any candidate was built, and the routes' figures from reviews made independently of the work they judged; most of the headlines shrank under that review. What remains is a referee for any candidate, a price for each stage, a record of the routes already closed, and a plan to find out which stage the next idea must change.
