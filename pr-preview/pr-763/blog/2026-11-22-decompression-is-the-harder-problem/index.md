# Decompression is the harder problem: a conversation with Thomas Murphy

Thomas Murphy of Tropik and I talk about agent swarms and message boards, and why a fast decompressor is a stranger problem than a proof.

22 November 2026 · 15 min read · Conversation

This post is a conversation: a Twitter DM between me and [Thomas Murphy](<https://x.com/thomasmurphy__>), who organises [Tropik](<https://tropik.space>), a research collective, journal and podcast on metaphysics, mathematics, poetics and software. It is published with his agreement, lightly edited, and set as an illuminated manuscript.

[Read the conversation](<https://lmmx.github.io/mibz2/pr-preview/pr-763/blog/conversations/2026-11-22-thomas-murphy.html>)

## What it covers

- **Managing agents is its own kind of labour.** Thomas finds managing Claudes all day knackering, and more boring than writing code, but there is no going back.
- **A team becomes a swarm once it shares a message board.** I describe how mibz2's agents work: a coordinator session watches the repository, and the other agents talk through a pull request used as a message board, with addressed, structured messages and attached evidence.
- **Repositories carry work losslessly; chat compresses it.** Messages passed between agents come back summarised. Files on disk come back whole.
- **Real problems are not proof-shaped.** A proof like the Riemann hypothesis's is one long route to correctness. A fast decompressor has to take its data as it comes, on the computer it has, so its answer is always "it depends". That is why I think decompression is the harder problem, and why the Hutter Prize's framing, "compression is intelligence", looks backwards to me.
- **Compressing proofs could have computational effects.** If a proof about a decompressor were formalised and then compressed, the smaller proof might be easier to mutate and evolve.

## How to read it

The page keeps the DM's layout: my messages on the right in blue, Thomas's on the left. Around them:

- **Fleurons (❧ ☙)** mark terms with a link. Each carries a numbered footnote, and the notes at the end, the *Compendium of Links*, say in a short paragraph what each one is.
- **Notes hung beneath a message** explain a term in passing, such as the BEAM or Bondy.
- **Captions** in blackletter and gold mark a message's tone, and a few words were softened into archaic ones in blackletter.
- **Serpents and dragons** crawl through the text, after the medieval bestiaries. They are decoration, and they are still being redrawn.
