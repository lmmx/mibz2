# Sliding literal-history representation of MTF

Take a list of N distinct ordinal identities, each associated with its output byte. Record its last W literal selections in a circular array, newest at age zero. An age is active exactly when the identity at that age was selected and has no newer occurrence. The active identities occupy the first d ranks of the current MTF list, in increasing order of age, where d is the population count of the W-bit mask. The remaining identities retain their previous relative order. Initially the mask is zero and the tail consists of the header’s ordinal order. This statement holds for every prefix of every sequence, including repeated selections whose displayed byte values coincide.

**Selection.** For a rank r below d, select the r-th one in the age mask. Its physical slot is `(head + W − 1 − age) mod W`. Clear its bit, removing its former rank before reinsertion. For r at least d, remove ordinal r−d from the tail. The invariant gives the same selected identity as ordinary MTF in each case. A mask has at most W active bits. With W=64, every rank from 64 through 255 selects the tail even when its low six bits point to an active age. Read a high rank through the tail branch before attempting a word shift on the rank.

**Age and emission.** After selection, inspect age W−1. If its bit is still set, this identity had no later occurrence and ceased to belong to the window. Among the surviving identities it was less recent than every active one and more recent than every previously inactive one. Prepend it to the tail. If the bit was clear, the overwritten slot is stale, unwritten or was itself just selected. Shift the mask one bit toward older ages, truncate to W bits, set bit zero, write the selected identity in the overwritten slot and advance the head. These operations make it the first rank and leave every other identity in the same relative order. This proves the inductive claim. A RUNA/RUNB digit changes no MTF rank. Its weight and error checks remain the original front's responsibility. EOB makes no selection. A new block starts from the new header and an empty history.

**Right-aligned tail.** Store the inactive suffix at absolute indices `T[d..N]`. A miss at overall rank r reads `T[r]`. Copy `T[d..r]` to `T[d+1..r+1]` with overlap-safe movement, then increment d. A hit leaves the tail untouched. On expiration decrement the tentative d and write the expired ordinal into `T[d]`. A selected oldest identity cannot expire a second time because its bit was cleared before the expiration test. The resulting d equals the population count of the new mask. Every copied ordinal at the r−d destination positions differs from the previous identity there. Those writes are necessary for this physical suffix layout if it must be immediately materialized. The argument does not bound other tail representations or the number of SIMD instructions that can execute the copy. A miss with r=d shifts zero ordinals, and a hit needs no tail copy. At full W=64 occupancy the largest possible shift is 191 bytes. At block start a rank-255 miss shifts 255 bytes. These costs exclude rank selection, ring access, bit maintenance, expiration, output stores and fallbacks.

**Circular-tail comparison.** A separate reference tail stores its first inactive ordinal at a wrapped physical head. It removes the selected relative offset j by closing the shorter side of the gap, shifting at most `min(j, tail_len−1−j)` ordinals, then prepends any expired identity. The executable compares this representation, the right-aligned tail and a flat original MTF list at every transition. Holding identities separately allows two distinct ordinals assigned the same byte to occupy two different live ages. The explicit three-ordinal example maps the first two ordinal identities to byte 17. Two rank-one selections leave two active ages. Deduplicating by output byte would count only one.

## Exact falsifier

For each N=1 through 4, W=1 through 6, and history length h=0 through W, enumerate all N^h most-recent identity words and all permutations of the identities absent from that word. This includes every legal abstract state reachable from any prefix; for an incomplete history the test also admits additional possible initial orders. Test every history head, every circular-tail head, and every possible next rank. Assert equality of the selected identity, route, entire resulting list, active mask and both tail layouts. A separate 256-ordinal test covers the oldest bit of a full 64-entry window, high-rank misses, repeated ordinals, resets, synthetic rank clamping and duplicate displayed byte values. All 888,478 small transitions and 32,608 full-width rank trials passed in both layouts. These are structural results. [Executable](reference.rs) · [Raw results](checked.txt)
