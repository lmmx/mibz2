# First-arrival history for asynchronous first returns

Let Ψ be any finite permutation with labels on outgoing edges. Cutting every Ψ cycle at its visits to a section U produces one directed arc per row of U. A cycle disjoint from U contributes no arc. An interior rank occurs in exactly one arc and at exactly one interior offset. Each section rank has two root incidences, the source of one arc and the destination of another. These incidences can belong to the same arc.

On an unfinished arc of length L, write l and r for the distance already traversed from the source and destination. Before advancing a head, l+r<L. All visited offsets lie in the two end segments [0,l] and [L−r,L]. Advancing the left head one edge to l+1 either reaches a fresh interior, or l+1=L−r and it meets the current right head. It cannot first meet a previously departed right head: that would require crossing the current right head earlier. It cannot revisit an interior previously reached from the left: interior offsets on this arc never repeat. The right-head proof is symmetric. A movement to another arc's visited interior is impossible by uniqueness of the interior rank.

Thus at a positive interior arrival, the first-arrival descriptor still names exactly the opposite head, cohort and root owner at that rank. Tag the collision rank permanently and immediately subtract its width from that cohort’s mass. When the cohort is later scheduled, skip tagged ranks in its retained original packet. A positive never encounters a third valid head: after meeting, that arc is final. No packet deletion, predecessor query on the opposite live set, or eager split is required. The archive does need a search over immutable descriptors at a positive. Section intervals require no history search. The opposite root cohort owns that endpoint until its arc's arrival. If that cohort had already traversed an edge on the arc, the heads must have met at an interior offset instead.

Root sides need independent tombstones. In the genuine two-byte cyclic BWT L=10, Ψ interchanges 0 and 1. Choose U={0,1}. Finishing the forward step 0→1 retires the **backward** root at 1. The forward root at 1 still owns the distinct arc 1→0. A shared bit erases that second incidence prematurely. Length-one self-loops, adjacent section ranks, multiple components, cycles with a single section rank and disjoint marked cycles are covered by the same incidence convention.

If N ranks lie on cycles meeting U and m=|U|, the archive visits exactly N−m distinct interior ranks. A completed length-L arc contributes L−1 first interior arrivals independently of the relative head speeds, and exactly one positive meeting, at an interior or at the opposite root. The two page-clipped descriptor bands for different owners cannot share a rank. Consequently at most N−m descriptors are required and each bucket of P rows contains at most P descriptors. A query needs at most P linked descriptor comparisons for a returned fragment. The number of first arrivals is fixed; the number of affine descriptors and the cost of querying them depend on the order of movement and input.

Replacing live trees preserves largest-mass scheduling. Cohort masses are decremented at the meeting, children are born in byte order, and the same depth/side/birth tie-break and stale-key repair operate on the same masses. Packet fragmentation and local row-processing order do not affect child masses or the parent spelling. Each emitted source band has the same destination, left/right prefix depths and joining byte as the original tree; adjacent bands may be split by a history page. The native checks compare these seam values on every source rank.
