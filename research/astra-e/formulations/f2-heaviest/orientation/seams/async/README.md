# Asynchronous return-word discovery

A common clock requires all live strands to move in the same direction on each tick. A weaker invariant suffices. On every positive-length arc between successive visits of a section, the two endpoints delimit a contiguous unconsumed substring. Choose any live cohort with a common current forward prefix or backward suffix. Advance it by one symbol. Retire each endpoint that meets the other side at its new original rank, and split the surviving cohort by its next byte. This removes exactly one byte for each participating occurrence. The section's cycles have finite total mass; arbitrary choices of nonempty cohorts terminate and recover the same first-return permutation, with potentially different seams. When initialized at coincident section ranks, the two frontiers stand for different ends of adjacent returns. Compare endpoints only after a positive move. Ordinary marked rewind, clipped expansion or a disjoint-cycle fallback remains necessary.

**Optimum using known spelling contexts.** An optimum interior-seam cut from the parent programme can be realized by exhausting its selected contexts in ancestry order on each side. A still-live word has an unpaid byte, hence a paid prefix or suffix covering it. A currently open ancestor of that context remains to be expanded. Conversely every completed two-front run produces a seam per word and pays its distinct activated contexts. Thus the min-cut is an attainable optimum for the spelling-state model when the optimal context set is supplied. It omits the cost of learning that set and detecting endpoint meetings. [Exact cut oracle](../)

**A bound that uses only current occurrence counts.** Advance a cohort of maximum live occurrence mass M at every step. Give each of the M newly consumed bytes price 1/M so the prices sum to the number of expansions. Fix an optimal seam assignment. It assigns every occurrence of every byte to one of its paid oriented contexts v. While k bytes assigned to v are still unconsumed, all their occurrences are under one live ancestor of v on that side. Every such occurrence still has to reach v to consume that byte from this direction. A single trie node expands once over all its live words, so those occurrences cannot have been split between different open ancestors of v. The largest available mass is therefore at least k. If a step consumes z of these bytes, its total price for them is at most z/k ≤ H(k)−H(k−z), with H the harmonic sum. Telescoping shows the total cost is at most the sum of H(qᵥ) over optimal contexts, where qᵥ denotes the number of byte occurrences assigned to v. In particular the factor is at most H(|S|) since a given context represents at most one such byte per section occurrence. This bound is on state expansions. Dynamic updates of the two rank frontiers have an independent price.

**An endpoint-pair oracle gives a second bound.** Select a word occurrence that remains open and obtain the two live cohort IDs enclosing that same occurrence. Expand both cohorts if still live. Any seam assignment, including the global optimum, must pay for at least one of these two particular contexts: if its seam is after the currently exposed forward prefix, it includes the forward context; otherwise it includes the current backward context. No later witness uses a previously expanded or retired node. These disjoint witness pairs lower-bound the optimum and at most two states are charged per pair. This gives a factor-two constructor in the paired-endpoint-oracle model. In an original LF index, identifying the opposite endpoint before the two fronts meet can itself require reconstruction.

**A realizable counterexample to a factor-two claim for maximum mass.** For each edge (a,b) in a bipartite graph, make a word delimiter·code(a)·xᴸ·code(b), using two fixed-length binary codes and two other distinct bytes as delimiter and x. Concatenate words on a cycle with positive multiplicities. At each of the L interior positions, the active prefix and suffix contexts of a feasible seam assignment give a vertex cover of the graph: the forward context identifies a and the backward context identifies b. The L layers have distinct contexts. Hence every assignment costs at least L times its minimum vertex cover. A minimum cover can pay all interior contexts for its vertices and only encoding overhead outside the interior, independent of L for the fixed graph. The exact code checks a layered graph with twelve left and 25 right vertices. For L=256 its 127,680-byte cyclic source costs 3,112 with optimal seams and 6,451 under maximum mass, a factor of 2.073. This distinguishes the endpoint-oracle guarantee from the deployable priority rule. [Graphs and exact cut results](graphs.py) · [All nine witnesses](graphs.txt)

## Coarse priorities and batched same-direction work

An occurrence count only falls within an open cohort. Store its ID in a FIFO bucket indexed by the highest set bit of its count. A nonempty-bucket mask selects the largest available class; on extraction demote a stale count or discard an empty cohort. Separate queues for each direction permit up to G consecutive activations on one side while both queues have work in the leading class. Choose the other side when only it has work in the leading class. Every activation then has more than half of the actual maximum live mass. The charging proof above gives a factor at most 2H(|S|) in the ideal state model. The number of possible mass levels is at most 20 for a bzip2 block of at most 900,000 rows. A live cohort can move downward at most that many times. Rank indexing, cohort data and output assembly remain separate costs.

At G=64, the ten tapes paid 541,125 quote states and 608,046 deeper-section states. Exact-priority counts were 538,868 and 604,132. The FIFO policies needed 8,459 and 9,164 side changes, compared with 534,276 and 578,974 for one-at-a-time side alternation in the same logarithmic buckets. The 64-batch policies used 2,629 and 3,017 demotions across ten inputs. All schedules repriced the resulting seams exactly. This makes batched rank intersection an implementable follow-up without a per-state general heap. [All 60 schedules and costs](buckets.csv) · [Bucket implementation](buckets.py)
