# Freeze the schedule for a shared two-ended discovery

Fit a forward/backward schedule only on ordinal 0 of the ten systematic tapes, for each of two sections separately (quote and its single most frequent return). The other nine tapes have previously appeared in this investigation. Their dictionaries and lengths are excluded from fitting this schedule. Freeze a deterministic backward tie break in the shortest path, then append alternating forward and backward steps starting with forward beyond the fitted maximum length.

For a return of length L, the forward half spells its first t(L) bytes, where t(L) counts forward ticks in the first L steps of the common schedule. The backward side spells the other L−t(L) bytes. Price the distinct proper oriented nodes actually used by the assigned substrings. Compare to fully backward, fully forward, alternating starting with forward and the unconstrained per-word interior-seam optimum. A direction schedule is worth retaining for further structural attention if it costs at most 90% of backwards and 95% of the alternating schedule on at least eight of the nine excluded tapes in the same section. Report every input and all results regardless of this screen.

For each proper prefix p, it suffices to know the maximum length A(p) of a dictionary word having that proper prefix. The charged expansion is active exactly if t(A(p))>|p|, because t is nondecreasing. Proper suffix s is charged iff A(s)−t(A(s))>|s| since backward ticks are nondecreasing too. Grouping charges by maximum length yields an additive price at each length. Shortest-path dynamic programming with predecessor t or t−1 gives the optimal common clock on training. An actual two-front decoder must still pay rank fragmentation, matching frontiers, computing spellings and output. The measured index and replay will compare these costs separately.
