Starting from each first reduced BWT, use its ordered integer return-class alphabet directly. Try second-level k=1 and k=2 separately, recording all context counts, errors, return symbols and runs, and every source/destination identity. Continue an ordinary marked trajectory while positive event mass drops; if a marked cycle is pure, recover its word by its state cycle, and account for its size. Measure total active symbols and distinct return classes at all depths plus the amount of explicit lower-level output that expansion would write. New alphabet and per-level setup must be charged; a tiny terminal record can still cost more than direct sparse ordering. First prove byte recovery through two complete levels on the smallest original and all ten, compare reduced partner order independently, and keep phase offsets and multi-cycle fallbacks explicit.
