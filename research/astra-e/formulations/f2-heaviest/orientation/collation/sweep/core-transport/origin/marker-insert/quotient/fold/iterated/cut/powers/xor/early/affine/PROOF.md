# Selective intersections and ordered complement gaps

Use Astra 3's LF orientation. Every old labelled band e maps one contiguous source interval to one contiguous image interval by translation. Both orders are known. Contract a middle row z precisely if the incoming band a has color zero, the outgoing band b has color one, and z is not the protected mark. This is exactly Astra 3's simultaneous disjoint oriented-pair contraction. A joined arc has source in a, image in b and forward spelling W_b W_a. The proposal changes its construction.

**Hit bound.** Scan only the zero-color bands in image order and the one-color bands in source order. The lists have R entries in total, exactly one per old band. Both consist of disjoint increasing intervals. Compare the two current ends and advance every list with the smaller end, emitting a hit only on positive overlap. At most R−1 comparisons occur when both lists are nonempty, since every step consumes a list entry and reaching an empty list leaves at least one unconsumed entry or consumes two at the final tie. Removing the marked point can split at most one hit into two. Write J for the resulting number of positive hit intervals. Each hit has an old zero owner a, an old one owner b, width, rule ID and common middle interval. For each old owner e, all its hits occupy one consecutive slice of this global list. Its geometric interval is contiguous and joins are globally ordered by their disjoint middle intervals.

**Gap enumeration.** For a zero owner use its old image interval; for a one owner use its old source interval. Subtract that owner's ordered hit slice. Each positive complement gap yields a surviving old single arc with the owner's word and is translated back to the other end by the same old band. The protected row is an ordinary gap. Let G be the sum of these positive gap counts. J+G distinct raw packets suffice to carry the whole contracted map, with G≤R+2J since each hit is incident to two owners. Every S-to-S incidence is represented exactly once. Zero-old source rows survive and give single or joined arcs according to whether their image lies in a gap or a hit. A one-old source row survives exactly on its gap. Dually, all one-old image rows survive, represented by gaps or joined arcs; zero-old image rows survive on their gaps. Thus the omitted set from both original orders is precisely the deleted middles.

**No sorting.** Enumerate owners in old source order. For each, emit the positive gaps in coordinate order and insert the intervening hit packets only for zero owners. This orders all surviving source rows, since a zero owner's middle coordinates differ from its source by a constant. Enumerate owners again in old image order, inserting the intervening hit packets only for one owners. This orders all surviving image rows. Each gap ID occurs once in both lists and each hit ID is shared by its two owners. Width prefix sums on the two lists give both compact endpoints. The new mark is its old rank minus total hit width below it.

Coalesce consecutive source packets of equal word with consecutive compact images. Mark the representative ID of each raw packet; walking the earlier image order and suppressing consecutive equal representatives then recovers image order for the next generation. No general sort, old noncrossing overlay intersections, or stable source-alphabet publication is needed. Cost is O(R+J+G) records, plus word-pair interning. The two complete output tilings still require writes; fewer visited contacts alone does not establish a faster decoder. For the initial byte column the image order is obtained by stable grouping of the maximal source runs into 256 byte buckets. The historical oracle calls four comparison sorts per round. Generic counting/radix sorting can also remove their logarithmic factor. The selective incident lists additionally remove contacts at unrelated old band boundaries.
