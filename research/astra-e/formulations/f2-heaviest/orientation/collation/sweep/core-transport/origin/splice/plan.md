# Native first-object validation and pricing

Check every H source's compressed successor and exact word against an independently walked original physical section, including the original marked byte stream and offset. Check the unchanged J and the spliced H through the same existing native second-stage ABI, including its own direct fallback count. Exhaust small ordinal columns with distinct and shared byte ranges, arbitrary reversed numeric source IDs, all deletion subsets, all marks and every in-word skip. Add the independent bounds verifier and sharp witnesses.

For the paid stage take the ten frozen frequent h8 and ten quote h12 cases. In 24 rotated rounds compare the original J consumer, a same-code twin, sparse splice plus H consumption, and an equal-splice control that consumes original J. Every arm occupies every position six times per case. Linux thread CPU covers splice construction, compound copying, the unchanged consumer and output generation, and releasing the splice object. Each returned output is checked outside timing. The original dictionary's once-grown capacity is retained across laps, and its cold capacity and pointer behavior are reported separately. Full J construction, finding the holes, data fixture parsing, dense independent validation, the full decoder and workstation wall time lie outside the measurement. The result tests an already-constructed J object.
