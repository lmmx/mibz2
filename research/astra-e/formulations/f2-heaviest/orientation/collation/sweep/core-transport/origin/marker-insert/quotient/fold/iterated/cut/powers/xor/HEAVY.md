# A matching certificate using the same histogram

Each nonzero XOR difference t pairs source i with at most the one type i XOR t, in either direction. All the mass D[t] lies on a matching of type pairs. Let t* attain max D[t] over nonzero bins. A uniformly random character conditioned on v·t*=1 makes every t* edge cross. Every other nonzero t is linearly independent of t* over GF(2), so its parity remains unbiased under that condition. The signed coefficient therefore has conditional mean e−D[t*]. Balance gives an attainable directional ingress at least (m−e+D[t*])/4. The exact Walsh-arm optimum always meets this purely histogram-based lower bound. It specializes the forced matching construction to a matching whose mass is available from the histogram for free.

There is a linear-time deterministic witness. Apply an invertible linear bit transformation P mapping t* to unit bit 0. Choose its highest set bit p, XOR the other set bits of t* into every input with bit p set, then swap bit p and bit 0. Remap histogram bins by P, fixing the first bit of the mask to 1. The two resolved bins contribute e−D[t*]. Resolve all higher bits by the previous conditional-expectation rule, each reducing the signed coefficient or leaving it constant. Transform the resulting character back onto the original type IDs via its action on the basis vectors. Overall work remains O(Q) and this construction can be compared with the original linear greedy witness by taking the larger of their exact ingresses.

The dedicated enumerator checks the attained bonus on every small balanced histogram also used for the earlier quarter certificate and compares the forced witness to its unrestricted Walsh optimum.
