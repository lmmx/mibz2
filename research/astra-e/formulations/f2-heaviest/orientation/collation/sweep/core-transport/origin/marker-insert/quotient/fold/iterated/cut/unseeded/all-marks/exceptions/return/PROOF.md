# First return of the physical LF map on exceptional rows

Let the stable LF permutation act from each physical row to its preceding row. On one maximal original L run J=[a,b), LF(x)=y+(x−a), where y is the cumulative byte base plus the number of earlier occurrences of that run's byte. Let E contain the physical modal exceptions. Put U=E∪{p}, where p is the original primary, retaining the bit p∈E. Let M be the number of maximal physical intervals of U, R the number of L runs and e=|U|. All rows of U are distinguished individually even when the representation combines them into one interval. The primary needs no cut when it is inside a larger U interval.

## One static overlay

The images LF(J) form a partition. Traverse them by (byte, original run order), which gives physical image-rank order without comparison sorting. Split each image at the strictly internal borders of U. Pull those split locations back to the source J by its translation. On each resulting source atom, the destination interval either lies wholly outside U and has a constant physical translation, or lies wholly inside one U component and has a constant translation to the dense U rank. The initial identity of every source row in U is its dense U rank. A border aligned to an image endpoint does not generate a further interior split. There are at most R+2M atoms, fewer when some U borders are external or coincide with image endpoints. Two monotone overlay passes, a stable counting order on the byte alphabet, and a prefix of per-run atom counts produce source-ordered atoms in O(R+M+256) time and space. The implementation additionally builds a 256-row directory in O(A+n/256) time and O(n/256) words for A atoms.

Checking U only at source-rank borders would be wrong. On L=(b,b,a,a), LF is (2,3,0,1). Take U={0,1,3}. The source packet [0,2) lies entirely in one L run and U component, but its two destinations are respectively outside and inside U. The rank-three destination border must be pulled back to source rank one. Equivalently one can split the translated packet after each LF step.

## Packet correctness and the lifetime split bound

Launch each maximal U interval. A packet describes consecutive anchor ranks [s,s+w), consecutive current physical rows [x,x+w) and a common elapsed distance t. Its invariant is that the row with offset i is LF^t of anchor s+i and no positive earlier iterate through t has hit U. At launch t=0. Restricting to one overlay atom preserves both order and width. An outside destination advances to another valid packet in one step. An inside destination ends all its component first returns with distance t+1 and translated U-rank destinations. The first return exists because LF is a finite permutation, and every marked anchor belongs to U.

The row-by-row paths from U up to, and excluding, their next positive hit partition precisely the LF cycles that intersect U. Every physical source row in those cycles belongs to exactly one visit of one anchor. Consequently the sum of the processed packet widths V is their total cardinality, at most n. A fixed source-atom border can split a packet only when the rows immediately to its left and right are both present at the same visit before translation. After that split, both rows already belong to those unique anchor visits. The border cannot split any other packet at any later depth. Thus the total number S of splits is at most A−1. There are exactly M+S terminal leaves before optional merging, and at most e. There are at most V actual packet translations, independent of the split bound. With L=b followed by n−1 copies of a, LF sends rank 0 to n−1 and each other rank i to i−1. With U={0}, R=2 and M=1 for every n, but a singleton packet needs n translations. A bound on the number of final intervals alone supplies no sublinear bound on construction work.

The explicit work stack defers the upper source fragment whenever a packet splits. All output for the lower fragment precedes it, including descendants at a later LF depth. Processing U components in source order gives terminal descriptors in dense anchor order without sorting. Adjacent affine translations with the same distance may merge, including across physical holes if their dense U destinations agree. The marked clock follows the resulting induced permutation from the dense rank of p, advancing time by the positive stored first-return distance. It emits the mark at time zero precisely when p∈E and every other visited U row. Stopping on the first return to p gives its original LF cycle length, without assuming every physical cycle is marked. Storing one destination/distance pair per anchor costs 8e bytes in this implementation and allows direct marked lookup. Searching the smaller interval table instead costs a predecessor query per marked anchor.

## Marked horizons and scope

For a finite forward horizon N ending at Ψ^N(p), the backward starting time on the original marked cycle of length ℓ is (−N mod ℓ). Therefore an exception at marked backward phase j appears at each time t for which t+(−N mod ℓ)≡j mod ℓ, restricted to 0≤t<N. An alternative is to reverse one recovered full lap and repeat its forward tape to length N. The finite checks covered all binary physical columns and masks through length six, each primary, both map representations and horizons zero through 2n+2. They include all-default marked cycles, true and artificial primary events and foreign cycles. The 30 real masks all had ℓ=n. [Program](induce.cpp) · [Finite receipt](exhaustive.txt)

The constructor consumes only physical L and U intervals. The independent real-data gate built a dense LF after construction, checked the exact destination and distance for *every* U row, and compared all marked event times and bytes to the pre-existing oracle. Neither that dense verification nor the historical all-position hierarchy supplies a constructor query. Acquiring physical context classes, modal defaults, exception payloads, restart states and preparing/using a spelling table are separate costs. The first-return map directly recovers the physical exception identity as well as its time; the native output retains its ordinal for a subsequent payload lookup.
