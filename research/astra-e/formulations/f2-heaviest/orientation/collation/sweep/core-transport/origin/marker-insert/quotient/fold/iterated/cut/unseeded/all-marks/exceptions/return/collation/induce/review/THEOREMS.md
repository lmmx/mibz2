# Geometry, spelling, and lower bounds for inducing a physical section

Let T be stable LF on n physical ranks. Each of the R maximal runs of the original last column is a translation interval labeled with its predecessor byte. Fix a nonempty retained set V containing the primary, and write m=|V|. A conjugation P sends V to the first m ranks. Send both ends of each edge through P. The resulting discrete interval exchange has the same itineraries, with the mark at P(primary).

## Initial refinement and the final boundary

Suppose P lists contiguous chunks by translations of their interiors. Let t count their internal boundaries in the physical axis. A common reflection inside **all** chunks works as well. Cut each original run at all source boundaries and wherever its image meets a target boundary. Each axis can add at most t cuts, since the run domains partition the source and their disjoint images partition the target. The number A of nonempty initial atoms is at most min(n,R+2t). An atom has one source interval, one equal-size target interval, one byte and slope +1. With a common reflection, the two sign changes cancel. Reversing the rows of individual chunks instead can create a slope −1 atom when an edge runs between opposite signs. Such a strip needs explicit orientation or further splitting into singletons.

Retaining V in increasing order fixes its ranks. One may permute the complement chunks arbitrarily while preserving their interiors. Reflecting both retained and complementary rows reverses the numbering on V. Reversing the reduced physical rank numbering afterwards restores its original order. All three operations preserve the physical first return when their coordinate maps and original mark are retained.

With the membership boundaries present, both initial orders have a boundary at m. Each ordinary suffix induction leaves every prior interior partition boundary below the new end present on its own axis. A shorter loser moved on a list occupies a piece just split from the winner's other copy. These two pieces together occupy exactly the earlier interval. Equal and coincident removals delete the physical tail. Thus even one initial boundary at m suffices to prevent a proper final clip shorter than both tail widths. Strict steps keep the atom count, and full equal or coincident steps each remove one atom. Consequently the final atom count B satisfies B≤A and S≥A−B, for S tail steps. If optional coalescing erases both m boundaries, a clipped distinct step can add one final atom. The same lower bound S≥A−B still holds. A one-atom identity exchange on two rows with V={0} requires a clipped coincident step after that coalescing.

## Marked output and rank order of the weighted alphabet

Backward first return from x visits some y in V with spelling W consisting of the predecessor bytes along that positive path. In forward direction y goes to x with spelling reverse(W). Assign one ordinal per final atom in **LF-target** interval order and put that ordinal on its LF-source interval in the reduced last column. Stability then maps each interval onto its intended LF destination in order. This construction produces an ordinary weighted BWT and permits distinct ordinals to store the same physical word. If LF-source order is used for numbering, even L=(1,0) with both rows retained turns its transposition into the identity. The forward terminal may iterate the reduced Ψ and truncate its concatenation after n bytes. Every orbit it follows then starts at the original primary, including when its period does not divide n. Computing the first full return lap costs no traversal of closed complement-only components at output time.

Acquisition still has to handle every input rank's declared cap/domain checks through the calling decoder. This component only describes the marked inverse.

## Complement order cannot change the final atom-itinerary partition

Assume the same initial **physical** atoms are kept, each tagged by a unique identity, with the V boundary explicit on both axes. At each retained rank record the list of original atom IDs used by its complete positive T return. In increasing retained order, the final induced bands are exactly the maximal contiguous intervals with one such list. This identifies an invariant of all reorderings of the same complement chunks. It does not equate different atom IDs that happen to carry the same byte.

Every final exchange atom follows a single original atom itinerary, by concatenation at a step. Conversely, take adjacent retained ranks with the same itinerary. Their starting pair is inside one original source atom. Following one step gives adjacent rows inside the corresponding target copy. Every intermediate pair in the complement belongs to both one incoming target interval and one outgoing source interval. These pairs stay together during induction. At an ordinary step the boundary of the discarded suffix belongs to the current source or target partition, so it cannot split any such intermediate pair. Pairs outside the suffix keep the matching part of their path. Pairs entering the suffix take the same extra step and become one adjacent pair in the new target copy. The start and return pairs are in V and both survive. This inductively prevents a source band boundary between the two starting ranks. With a common reflection, the same argument applies to the reflected list and its boundary sequence is reversed.

The argument allows the two paths to lie on distinct cycles, or to visit the same original atom many times. The initial membership cuts ensure that all intermediate positions with a given itinerary agree on retention. Altering the initial cuts or merging different atom labels before induction can alter the final partition. The verified small model computes the intrinsic itinerary independently for every tested neighboring pair. [Finite tests](cylinders.json)

## An order-sensitive tail-step lower bound

Consider any nontrivial original conjugated LF edge i→j whose endpoints both belong to the complement. For a distinct-tip step the discarded suffix W lies within the last source atom and the last target atom, with different identities. The image of the first source atom is disjoint from that target atom. Therefore the current permutation has no edge with both endpoints in W. A coincident-tip suffix is fixed pointwise. Before either i or j is removed, their original one-step edge is still a current one-step edge. They must be removed in different steps.

The steps partition the complement into contiguous discarded suffix intervals. For every such edge, an inter-step cut must land at an integer position in [min(i,j),max(i,j)−1]. Sort these constraint intervals by their right endpoint and pick that endpoint each time the previous pick lies before the next interval. Picking the earliest right endpoint preserves a possible hit in every overlapping interval. The picked constraints are pairwise disjoint. Hence this greedy construction gives both a minimum hitting set and a maximum disjoint set. With nonempty complement its size H gives S≥H+1. In particular every linked, adjacent pair forces its own individual cut. This condition uses only original direct edges. New first-return edges created later can force many more steps.

With explicit T and Ψ arrays one can scan both incident edges in increasing order of their larger endpoint and compute H in O(n) time and O(1) extra space. It also admits an atom-only calculation. Remove self translations. Clip each other atom x↦x+d to source ranks whose two endpoints are in the complement. Its constraints have all consecutive starts u,…,v and the common length |d|. Sort the A or fewer families by u, and precompute suffix minima of u+|d|−1. After a chosen cut c, let l=c+1. The earliest still-uncovered right endpoint is the smaller of (i) l−1 plus the shortest length of a family with u≤l≤v and (ii) the suffix minimum for families with u>l. A heap of lengths discards expired families lazily; each family is inserted and removed at most once. Total work is O(A log A+H), with O(A) memory. This calculation screens one proposed ordering after its conjugated affine atoms are available.

## Long returns and the cost of moving a poor example

For L=(1,0,…,0), T is subtraction of one modulo n and V={0}. In the stable layout every adjacent pair within its n−1 complementary ranks is linked. S=n−1, although the original last column has two runs and only one return band is published. More generally, if the complement is reordered as k internally monotone contiguous physical chunks and remains an ordinary exchange, at least n−1−k complementary adjacencies are linked. Hence S≥n−k. This rules out curing that example by reordering a bounded number of intact physical intervals. The two-run example belongs to the same family as the earlier linear packet-tracing obstruction. The bound here applies to endpoint steps and survives the stated class of conjugations. [Earlier packet construction](../../../PROOF.md)

A different obstruction comes from spelling. Starting from byte literals, after g binary joins the longest represented word can have length at most 2^g, so a length-D single return requires at least ceiling(log₂ D) joins. Some primitive binary length-n words need Ω(n/log n) straight-line joins. With g joins there are at most (g+2)^(2g+1) choices of rules and output root over two known input literals. There are exponentially many primitive binary words of length n, even after identifying rotations. Their cyclic BWT, appropriate primary and singleton section must spell each whole primitive lap. This lower bound concerns explicit binary straight-line spellings. A representation allowed to reuse an externally supplied word or add other instructions requires its own accounting.

An expensive conjugation illustrates the distinction. For n=2^k order the two-run example by the bit-reversed orbital time of each row, and keep V={0}. The reordered map adds one in bit-reversed order. Its entire lower half translates to the upper half, while the upper half transitions according to the (k−1)-bit map into the lower half. The exceptional literal at the marked rank splits the first lower atom. If all original coordinate seams are retained, the model starts with n singleton arcs at k≥2 and takes n−1 steps. Merging adjacent intervals with identical input byte and translation gives k+2 initial atoms for k≥2. For k≥3, removing the upper half takes k+1 steps: remove the k−1 smaller source atoms at its right, then the large remaining atom in two pieces because the first lower row had its own byte. The lower half now has exactly the smaller bit-reversed problem with two possible equal-length pair spellings. The recurrence S(k)=S(k−1)+k+1 with S(2)=3 gives (k²+3k−4)/2. At n=512 this was 52 steps instead of 511. Generic orbital-time acquisition and initial seam refinement can already cost Θ(n). The example separates an intrinsic obstruction under limited seams from a consequence of one expensive coordinate layout. [Exact cases](long-laps.csv)

## Full resource account and historical overlap

Given a sorted inventory of the V/complement chunks and true maximal runs, stable LF images can be bucketed by source byte, with a cursor for each occupied byte. A joint source and image sweep reports A pieces in O(R+t+A+σ) operations for byte alphabet size σ≤256. Output top and bottom orders can be grouped by destination chunk and the physical order internal to that chunk; arbitrary chunk permutation adds O(t) list assembly once its order is given. Ascending width order can use full counting buckets in O(t+w_max) work and memory, or sort the distinct widths and their bucket lists. If V is provided as unordered ranks, sorting, or a bitmap and a scan, is a further cost. Deriving the shifted k8 events from caps is an additional context problem.

A doubly linked width exchange consumes O(A+S) time for its ordinary steps, excluding input acquisition and any published spelling expansion. Working storage for the link/width object is O(A). Each distinct-tip step can append one chronological binary rule, so an implementation retaining all rules uses O(A+S) cells. The final B bands and lengths alone do not account for those rules or their actual materialization. Given all m reduced ranks, direct successor publication adds O(m+B) work and O(m) cells. Materializing one spelling per final band by recursively expanding its live straight-line grammar charges the sum of their expanded lengths, including repeated visits, or needs an explicitly verified sharing scheme. Those lengths summed *without* band widths are at most the sum weighted by their positive widths, which equals the number of physical rows on section-touching cycles, at most n. All n requested marked bytes still have to reach the next stage. Compression of the initial alphabet does not make band order, lengths, mark recovery or copying free.

Open-Return at 2b53453e already built a first return on a retained union, bounded the number of bundles and moved open entrances and exits to a prefix. Its virtual terminal completion avoided the preserved narrow-run wiring and used a different initial exchange. The newer construction can directly refine the full LF on an arbitrary physical section. Grammar bands and sparse sections have earlier precedents.
