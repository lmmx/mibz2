# Recover the exact explicit-graph greedy cut from ordered destination buckets

For any fixed visit order, the earlier greedy assigns the current class j to the side opposite the greater weight of its edges with previously assigned classes, including both directions. Access the old incoming-column fragments of source bucket I_j. An incoming label k of width w exposes E_kj mass directly. Maintain one signed pending weight per class, with positive values for edges to an already assigned outside class and negative values for edges to an already assigned inside class.

Before visiting j, pending[j] is the signed sum of edges **j→k** for earlier k. In a first scan over I_j, add w with the decided sign of each earlier incoming k, accounting for **k→j**. These are disjoint directional contributions. Ignore self and not-yet-assigned k. Choose j's color from their sum, using the same tie rule. In a second scan over I_j, add w times j's sign to pending[k] for each not-yet-assigned incoming k. These are exactly the k→j edges needed later at k. Induction over the visit order proves identical colors and entering mass to the explicit weighted undirected graph. The singleton anchor visits first with forced outside color; any physical position of its bucket works. A single final scan may compute the exact directed ingress and check the guard.

Two scans per bucket therefore take O(H+K) time, with O(K) additional words for the colors, offsets and pending totals. No adjacency lists, type pair hashing or parity character restriction is needed. The same proof applies to other prespecified orderings.
