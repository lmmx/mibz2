# A graph-free quarter certificate from XOR differences

Old source types have dense ordinal IDs 0,…,K−1. Put Q equal to the least power of two at least K, and form D[t]=Σ E_ij over old transitions with i XOR j=t. The original source-bucket/incoming-run overlap supplies one histogram addition per fragment. Q<2K for every K≥1 except equality of Q=K=1 still gives the same strict inequality. No per-pair table is needed for this choice. D[0] is exactly the old self mass e. The protected singleton has ID a.

For any mask v in [0,Q), color k inside if parity(v AND (k XOR a))=1. The anchor is outside. An edge i→j crosses if parity(v AND (i XOR j))=1. Its signed spectral sum C(v)=Σ_t D[t](−1)^parity(v AND t) thus gives crossing mass (m−C(v))/2. Balanced in/out masses for the permutation imply the directed ingress q=(m−C(v))/4. This identity counts each directed edge once, including self-edges.

Choose the mask bits from low to high. At bit b, only the bin indices 2^b,…,2^(b+1)−1 become completely determined. Let S be their signed sum under the already chosen lower bits. Choose bit b=1 if S>0 and b=0 otherwise. They contribute exactly −|S|. The never-changing zero bin contributes e, so the final C(v) ≤ e and q ≥ (m−e)/4. Each bin is processed once. On bounded-word ordinal IDs, parity and histogram lookup therefore give O(H+K) work and O(K) extra storage for the certificate, with H old run/bucket fragments. A portable fixed-word parity implementation needs a constant number of operations. The exact maximizer over this family can alternatively be obtained by an in-place fast Walsh–Hadamard transform of D in O(Q log Q) additions and O(Q) words. Choose the smallest minimizing mask on ties. Both methods use the actual q to skip power normalization whenever 4q≥m. If q fails, normalization provides e=0 and the quarter bound on the fallback.
