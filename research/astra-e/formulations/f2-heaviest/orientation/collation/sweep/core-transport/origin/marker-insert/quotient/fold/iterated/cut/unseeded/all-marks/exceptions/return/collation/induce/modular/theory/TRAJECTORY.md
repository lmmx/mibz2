# Bound the actual residue trajectory and isolate its costly operations

At level j let mⱼ be the retained physical mass, Bⱼ its current raw band count, and Aⱼ₊₁ the number of emitted progressions before prefix induction. B₀=R is the original L-run count. All counts here allow duplicate rule handles and adjacent semantically equal bands. No canonical word coalescence is required. Nested retained rows have original ranks x≡p mod qʲ with the original primary p. Their current ranks are (x−(p mod qʲ))/qʲ, so the next marked rank is floor(p/qʲ). Thus mⱼ≤ceil(n/qʲ), and one row remains after at most ceil(log_q n) levels.

**The cutoff is never partial.** The retained residue-group end c is a physical boundary in both reordered lists before a nonterminal round's first contraction. As long as the current right end exceeds c, the respective boundaries below it survive a full tip reduction. On a strict step the axis whose last band was shorter drops that tail; on that axis the other band becomes a shorter band followed by the moved part of exactly the removed width, preserving its old endpoint. The other axis shortens its last band. On an equal step replacement at the nontail location uses the same width. A closed self tip disappears from both tails. Consequently c persists on both axes until reached, and each final band's width is at most the remaining gap down to c. A double-partial contraction and a partial self contraction are impossible. This also applies to a one-shot stably conjugated section with its end initially on either axis; the equal/self counting identity below uses the modular case with both boundaries present.

Let Eⱼ and Dⱼ count equal and closed self-tip steps in this round. Each removes one band; a strict step preserves the count. Therefore

    Aⱼ₊₁ ≤ min(mⱼ,q Bⱼ),       Bⱼ₊₁ = Aⱼ₊₁ − Eⱼ − Dⱼ ≤ min(Aⱼ₊₁,mⱼ₊₁).

In particular Bⱼ≤Rqʲ. A directly computable upper bound on the total setup pieces is Σⱼ min(ceil(n/qʲ), qR qʲ), summed over actual levels. Bound the total surviving bands by Σⱼ min(ceil(n/qʲ⁺¹), Rqʲ⁺¹). For q fixed the bounds are O(√(qnR) q/(q−1)+log_q n) and O(√(nR) q/(q−1)+log_q n) respectively. To see the constants, split the sum of min(n/qʲ, qR qʲ) at qʲ≈√(n/(qR)). Each side sums geometrically to at most its peak times q/(q−1), and rounding contributes at most one per level. The analogous crossover for the survivor expression uses original group size qʲ⁺¹. If the crossover precedes j=0, omit the empty side. All equal and self steps together satisfy the progression bound. Extra contractions above that order must be strict joins.

Across all steps the removed positive widths sum to n−1, so the raw grammar uses at most n−1 binary joins. An expanded terminal word of true marked lap length t has t−1 internal node occurrences and therefore at most t−1 distinct reachable join rules. Counts of all constructed, reachable, and repeated expanded nodes are distinct quantities.

## Quadratically long powers with two original runs

Fix q≥2. Choose g=qᵛ, t=g+1 and n=gt. The base column L₀=1^(t−q)0^q is a single cycle: LF adds q modulo t. Replace each base row by g equal consecutive rows. This is the ordinary periodic cyclic BWT of g copies of the base word. Mark lane zero or any lane of base rank zero.

In each of the first v rounds every residue group is invariant under the current permutation, since current run widths and translations are divisible by q. Its projected exchange is a scaled copy of the same two-band base exchange. Both top and bottom place every discarded group entirely to the right of the retained group. All joins made to eliminate those q−1 complete groups to zero are unreachable from the final primary. Within each closed group all integer widths share the same scale and every step consumes a full tip, so its number of operations and its join syntax are exactly those of the unscaled two-band base induced to zero. The retained exchange is an unmodified projection, and after v rounds its bands and rule handles are exactly the original unscaled base.

Write D(t,q) for the joins to eliminate that closed base to zero. In reversed two-band order the last top width is q and the last bottom width is t−q. While the second exceeds q, subtracting the smaller creates one new join and returns the same tip pair to the end. Thus D≥floor((t−q−1)/q) for t>q, while D≤t by unit mass decrease. The initial stages therefore produce exactly v(q−1)D dead rules.

The unscaled primary stage also has a linear live cost for fixed q. Write t=qm+1. In the last nonretained group r=q−1, the long original run contributes m−1 translated positions advancing by one inside that residue, while its final rank exits as a singleton. The top ends with the singleton and the bottom ends with its long translated band. Moving the singleton back after that same band leaves it at the top tip and reduces the long band by one. Repetition down to equality produces at least m−1 nonself joins. Every nonself join in a single-cycle input participates in its final marked word: each new rule labels a nonempty row set in an intermediate first-return permutation which is still one cycle through all retained rows. Each such row's accumulated word is used when the marked lap is recursively expanded. Thus the final live grammar has Θ_q(t) distinct joins. The complete raw grammar of this two-run, quadratic-size periodic input has Θ_q(t log t) joins, of which Θ_q(t log t) arise from discarded closed groups while the reachable output word has Θ_q(t) rules. This proves the sharp order of the t−1 live upper bound even when n is quadratic in t. It also exhibits Θ(n) actual strict joins on primitive inputs with R=2 by using the unscaled base alone. ## The setup bound is sharp on fixed-radix towers even with two original runs

Use the original two-run family registered in the canonical wide-strip study, now with its sampling width M=qʲ for a fixed tower radix q and an increasing level j. Let n=M²+3M+1, L=1^(n−M−1)0^(M+1) and p=0. LF adds δ=M+1 modulo n. The orbit is primitive since n=(M+1)(M+2)−1. The full positive first-return word at normalized level-j row i, corresponding to original row iM, is given below. Target indices are also normalized.

| i | Target | Backward return word |
|---|---:|---|
| 0,1,2 | i+M+1 | 1ᴹ |
| 3,…,M+1 | i−1 | 1^(M+2−i) · 0 · 1^(i−2) |
| M+2,M+3 | i−M−2 | 0 |

Before wraparound each step increases the physical rank residue by one modulo M. A wrap subtracts n≡1 (mod M). The first three inputs reach a multiple of M at step M without a wrap. The final two wrap immediately to another multiple. For each middle input i, the first position that emits zero is after M+2−i nonwrapping steps, since its source rank first reaches n−δ = M(M+2) there. The wrap occurs at a step between 2 and M. The next possible positive hit after it is step M+1. Since (M+1)δ=n−M, the final target is (i−1)M. This proves all the words including the shift in zero placement.

There are M+1 distinct actual return words. No intermediate choice of grammar syntax or artificial splitting can encode two different fixed words in a single uniform word band. Thus Bⱼ≥M+1 and also Aⱼ≥M+1 in the raw fixed-radix tower. Since n is quadratic in M, the cumulative O(√n) setup/survivor order at constant R and q is sharp. This lower bound is independent of the strict-step linear family above and concerns a required intermediate object. It does not assert a lower bound for arbitrary BWT decoders that avoid that object.
