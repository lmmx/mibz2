# Complete marked inversion through a conjugated physical section

The worker accepts the original capped last column and primary. Its three sections retain the first max(1,floor(n/22)) physical rows, all rows belonging to maximal L-runs of width at most four, or whole F buckets of the sixteen least frequent present bytes. Equal frequencies are ordered by byte value. Each selector takes the union with the original mark. A monotone span writer splits at the mark and combines adjacent equal choices, recording its ordinal among retained rows. The F selector scans sorted byte counts. The thin selector scans the coalesced runs. No selector makes an n-entry membership array.

The implementation uses the joint interval-order conjugation and labelled finite Rauzy constructor at cd35d808. A surviving record of width w maps backward source interval [s,s+w) to target interval [t,t+w) and carries a grammar rule for the traversed L bytes in successive backward LF order. Populate the compact forward table by writing (s+j,rule) at t+j. No comparison sorting is needed. The table uses two 32-bit words per selected row. Follow its forward successor from the selected primary until that physical ordinal first returns. The full forward spelling of a rule is the reverse of its stored backward word. At a binary join this emits the right child's reversed spelling followed by the left child's reversed spelling.

An explicit stack stores pending grammar handles and completion frames. After the first expansion of a join finishes, its memo records the start and positive length of its contiguous spelling in the current block's output. Subsequent uses copy from that completed earlier occurrence. Children always have smaller handles, so an active memo cannot be read recursively from its own unfinished expansion. Copy sources end at or before the current output cursor. Output storage is sized to n before emission, and memos are reset on each call. Every selected path contributes a positive return length. A full selected lap spells the true marked original lap. If it has fewer than n bytes, repeat its prefix by at most logarithmically many disjoint memcpy calls, with a final partial copy at the original n-byte horizon. This covers multiple cycles and nondividing periods using only the chosen marked component.

The complete stage includes cap coalescing and histogram, selection, conjugation, induction, all grammar allocation, writing the forward table, memo initialization, marked output materialization, lap repetition and destruction of the per-call result.
