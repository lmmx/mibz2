# Two return words can require linearly many forward packets per byte

Take a primitive binary cyclic token sequence T of length m. Replace each A token by d aᵏ and each B token by d b aᵏ, using distinct bytes d < a < b and k≥1. Each delimiter d identifies exactly one token start, so the expanded cyclic word is primitive and the section U of rotations beginning in d has exactly m elements. Its two first-return words have 2k+1 distinct proper prefixes, counting the empty root.

For every j between 1 and k, consider the rows beginning in aʲd. There is one per token, and they form a contiguous lexicographic interval. After the common prefix, the ordering is that of rotations starting at the *following* token. The order of expanded token rotations agrees with the binary token order because the first differing codewords compare at their second byte. Coloring those m rows according to the *current* token's type A or B therefore spells exactly the cyclic BWT of T.

Let R be its number of linear runs. When charging the j-th remaining a of either phrase, the forward constructor holds precisely those A-colored or B-colored rows for the corresponding spelling. An affine packet represents a contiguous set of current ranks. A missing color separates two pieces, regardless of how the constructor found the starting ranks. Counting across both phrases requires at least R contiguous pieces at this a offset. The initial mixed node can meet a byte boundary, which its stable-select translation must split. Across k offsets this gives at least kR translations to any constructor that materializes these live affine pieces separately at each byte.

In a binary de Bruijn cycle of order q, m=2ᵠ and every (q−1)-bit prefix has two occurrences. In sorted rotation order they occupy an adjacent pair. Their predecessor bits differ because 0u and 1u each occur once for every (q−1)-bit word u. Each of the m/2 disjoint pairs thus contributes a change in the cyclic BWT, giving R≥m/2+1. For q=7 and k=32 the genuine cyclic example has 65 proper prefixes. Its 114 token-BWT runs require at least 3,648 translations, and the native constructor reports 3,762. The lower bound applies specifically to explicit per-byte affine packets. Compressed or shared representations of the repeated row coloring are outside its scope.
