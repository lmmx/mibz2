# Can a later phase pay for the forward staging cost?

This paired screening comparison asks what could be gained if all remaining work after the candidate's first stage were free. The compact/preload control performs its complete byte inverse, including original preparation, first discovery and merge, second induction, marked traversal and full raw output. Its bytes are compared to Segmented after every timed run. The candidate arms stop immediately after paying first discovery, publication, Ψ construction and grouped tail staging. They receive no charge for second induction or final output. A compact first-stage-only arm reproduces the original matched endpoint.

Across 40 balanced four-arm rotations on nine systematic blocks, the width-one first stage alone took 1.1666 [1.1165,1.2188] of the compact **complete inverse**. The width-eight first stage took 1.2472 [1.2027,1.2933]. Against the compact first stage in this panel the ratios were 1.8385 and 1.9655. Later-stage savings cannot rescue this measured implementation on these inputs without reducing its first-stage cost.
