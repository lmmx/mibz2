# Updating the marked cyclic BWT after exact RLE1 edits

For a circular string with fixed row identities, let Q(P) be the permutation obtained by sorting each position x by (first byte of x, rank in P of its successor). Q iterated d times sorts d-byte prefixes with P as continuation tie-breaker. For distinct rotations a fixed point of Q is therefore the cyclic suffix order, and iteration reaches it within n applications. A transient periodic intermediate text may have equal rotations; this primitive-cycle experiment can abort under an explicit cap and retain the original finish.

If the order of every row outside M is the same in P and P′, the order of every predecessor outside pred(M) is the same in Q(P) and Q(P′). Their first bytes agree with their previous keys, and their successors preserve relative order. In particular, after moving one active row, only its text predecessor needs reconsideration in the next application of Q. Its target in Q(P) is C[c]+rank_c(L_P, rank_P(succ(x))), where L_P holds the actual preceding byte in the current list order. If this target is its present position, Q(P)=P.

Replacing one byte initially changes only its own sort key. Deleting y initially drops its row from the original order. The surviving keys retain their relative order except possibly the predecessor a of y, whose successor changes to the following row j. For inserting a fresh symbol b just before j with former predecessor a, first place the new row among the old list minus a at C_old[b]−[F(a)<b]+rank_b(L_old, rank_old(j)). This counts rows other than a by their key relative to (b,rank_old(j)); a's own successor equals j, so the strict second term excludes it. Reinsert a's old slot into the coordinate system and insert the new row at that location. Now only a can be misplaced. Updating the L label at j and the row identities at each operation gives the true dynamic predecessor map. The verifier rebuilds and independently sorts the decoded primitive circle to check every final coordinate.

On the canonical frozen block, 34 semantic count edits required 125 elementary insertions/deletions/replacements. Sequential repair moved 6,638 rows, with a longest predecessor chain of 540. Stream 134 required 23,887 moves for 94 count edits, compared with a 214-original-literal minimum for the final simultaneous transformation. Single-edit propagation is exact but several independently updated sites can reorder the same contexts repeatedly. The page-based verifier maintains dense source identities and uses simple per-page character counts to measure this work. It supplies no claim of low worker memory.
