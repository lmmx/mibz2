# Bounded reservations for several forward arcs

Given a word-labelled finite permutation with positive words, choose k distinct seeds including the mark. Each seed spells its half-open path up to the first subsequent seed. Seeded cycles partition into these paths. Their total byte length is at most n. Their successor seed pointers determine the marked order.

Reserve the existing n-byte output and give the k fragments consecutive byte extents in physical seed order. Write forward on all active arcs, pausing each at its next whole word if that word exceeds its extent. Once the lanes are finished or paused, fix the final lengths of finished arcs. Reallocate the remaining free bytes among the unfinished arcs, first giving all their already initialized prefix bytes back. If every unfinished fragment lacked room to finish at the next pass, summing its true remaining size would exceed the sum of these reservations. That contradicts the byte budget. Each pass therefore retires at least one arc, with at most k−1 redistributions. This proof applies to positive words of any size and to separate seeded cycles.

For each redistribution, use the old physical order. Shift fragments whose start decreases in ascending physical order, then fragments whose start increases in descending physical order. The target intervals are disjoint and ordered as the old intervals. A left-shift can meet only an earlier source which shifted left first. A right-shift can meet only a later source which shifted right first. Finished arcs and paused prefixes are both preserved. When all arcs close, compact the prefixes to remove holes, then permute only the marked fragments to the front by block rotations, reusing constant metadata for fixed k. Repeat that marked prefix to exactly n. A short marked cycle and unmarked arcs are charged.

Test equally spaced physical-rank seeds relative to the primary, with floor(j*m/k), for k=3 and 4; use one lane when m<k. Split initially and at redistributions by equal shares of the available free bytes. Report total dependent issue steps when every active lane gets one successor per step, resulting lane occupancy, number of extra passes, bytes relocated during redistribution, final compaction and block rotations separately. Compare against the observed two-seed geometry and the full extent n, then time the strongest credible variant against the frozen stage two-seed and single controls with both programs' actual byte counts. Treat the issue counts as structural. The open four-tape writer already used four independent lanes; this test concerns bounded placement in n bytes and the complete cost of final rotations.
