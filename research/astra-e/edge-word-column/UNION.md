# A positive physical quotient with sparse high-ID publication

Write X = S for the original retained physical ranks and K = LF(S). Stable sorting retained ranks by (original L byte,physical rank) gives exactly the physical order of K. For each old source x, let T(x) be its first positive return to S and let W(x) be the original positive byte spelling. The band-domain ordinal P(x) is the position of LF(T(x)) in K. The old two-phase lift emits the prefix W(x) without its last byte, then emits that last byte at P(x). Every zero-prefix row, exactly every old literal x, occurs at the *same original physical rank* as its P(x): W is one byte if and only if Ψ(x) belongs to S, equivalently x belongs to K. For all other old x, LF(T(x)) is the last interior vertex of its original return path. There is no additional S or K strictly between x and that predecessor. Identifying coincident vertices and sorting the physical union Y = S ∪ K therefore gives the true first-return column of the expanded section. The merged-row count is m plus the count of original nonliteral rows. Every source weight is now positive.

A K row physically emits one original F byte and moves to its Ψ successor in S. An S-only row emits W without its last byte and moves to the K ordinal P(x). Each original marked row is still present and needs zero leading-byte skip. Members of Y outside the marked component need no new global order on cycles. For identical infinite rotations, stable Ψ and the distinct original rank identity give the order. Equal new spellings have globally increasing destinations by the cylinder argument in PROOF.md, so splitting only at changes of literal byte spelling in physical Y order assigns minimal convex stable IDs. The finite independent check verified these short paths directly against Ψ across all binary L and all nonempty S through seven ranks, including 37,680 marked cases whose cycle length did not divide n. ## Publication from the original capped records and bands

1. While scanning maximal L runs, collect the retained subinterval in original rank and the corresponding LF interval. Within a given byte the latter is its cumulative occurrence count at the retained start plus the global frequency base for that byte. Merge adjacent intervals in each stream and use one two-way interval sweep to find the ordered union mask (S only, K only, or both). It is sufficient to retain its run lengths after deleting gaps.
2. In that mask order, K supplies its source byte from the per-byte frequency bins. Original physical band order supplies the prefix of W for an S-only span, including the whole part before its final byte. At an intersection the two source descriptions agree on the original one-byte weight. Coalesce equal spellings. Record the resulting IDs projected on K separately by queue byte, and on each old S-only band. The latter need not fit in one piece when K-only rows intervene.
3. New incoming at every S rank y comes from K at LF(y). Scan the original captured retained L tape in physical order, advancing the K-piece cursor for its byte. New incoming at each remaining K-only rank is the source ID of its nonliteral prefix arc. Consume those already computed span lists in the old queue order, skipping literal bands. Finally interleave the two monotone claim streams with the mask and merge equal adjacent IDs.

This recipe uses no dense successor, no expanded rank-sized mask, and no row-wise lookup to obtain the column. Its additional large objects are thin-byte runs, the union-mask tape, source IDs/widths, band order, the two projected class streams, and whatever incoming representation is requested. The code materializes the ordinary decoded successor and checking embeddings only *after* or alongside constructing the independent spans, so its allocations are unsuitable for reporting candidate peak RSS. A fused constructor could consume the low/high streams incrementally. The existing retained scan, grammar, alias matching and index construction still have to be paid. Across 233 admissions the original 26,567,581 retained rows expand to 31,462,249 positive union rows; 21,672,913 were coincident. The serial scan delivers 544,612 retained physical intervals and 503,341 merged LF intervals, yielding 496,272 ordered mask spans. Source publication consumes 1,004,440 homogeneous spans, of which 128,383 have width one, to give 297,765 distinct convex IDs. Source entropy is 7.3321 bits per union row. The K-projected side has 56,971 pieces across the byte queues; replacing each byte's piece ID by its most frequent one would mislabel 8,255,767 of its 26,567,581 claims without exceptions. Assigning the correct ID during the retained L scan makes 14,291,725 claims, only 361 more than its original 14,291,364 byte runs. The S-only side consumes 368,867 records and coalesces to 339,722 high-ID runs. Interleaving and coalescing these streams consumes 14,692,374 claims and emits 14,671,133 true ordinary incoming runs. Per-queue piece thresholds are an exact alternative to storing a high ID on every low-byte run. [All construction and order counts](union.csv)

The largest new convex class needs 21,725,702 second-level interval queries and 188,720,969 bytes to expand every terminal typed spelling once. Quote is the heaviest leading-byte bucket on all 233 here. It needs 16,129,899 queries, 19,071,349 incoming-run intersections and 71,425,077 expanded second-dictionary bytes. For comparison the old S quote-leading bucket takes 14,893,227 queries and 77,891,723 dictionary bytes. The new quotient trades 6.47 MB of expanded spelling volume for 1.24 million extra interval queries, higher initial row count, a mask merge and a new directory.
