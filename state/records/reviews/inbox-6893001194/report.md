# Price the four-worker phase-concurrency intervention before registration

Inbox [6893001194](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/160#issuecomment-6893001194), with [addendum 6893034788](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/160#issuecomment-6893034788) and Louis's [LOG 6893034258](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6893034258). Scope: Coordinator [draft LOG 6892999618](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6892999618), A baseline, B induction cap 2, C writer cap 2, D both cap 2, E two blocks in flight. **No F or Cooperative build in the screen.** Paper pricing only. The earlier residual decomposition is closed, unrun. The exploratory phase CPU shares are descriptive, not a registered causal verdict.

## The cheapest correct gate and its debit

The current `try_invert` layout in [src/cells.rs](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/master/src/cells.rs) calls `slots`, then `induce_cells`, validates the marked period, then `write_cells`; the `phase-counters` scopes directly surround the latter two. Put one **sleeping counting semaphore** with capacity 2 immediately before induction, and one before writer, with a short RAII permit scope and release immediately after the phase. A single `Mutex<count> + Condvar` per gate is sufficient; no custom unsafe or spin. Do not hold either permit while waiting for the other, while holding the Workers job-receiver/shared-state locks, or through the writer's fallback. Ensure refusal, panic and cancellation paths release permits. The acquisition must occur **outside** the measured phase scope; count wait wall and waiting task-clock separately. D uses both semaphores, sequentially. A same-binary **capacity-4 gate control** is needed to charge uncontended gate/bookkeeping overhead against shipped ungated A, or the screen must include an equivalent no-wait calibration; without it B/C versus A mix the treatment with instrumentation. This is a proposed design detail for registration, not an added intervention verdict.

A permit path costs an extra branch plus lock/atomic acquire and release per phase invocation, and a contended path costs a condition-variable park, wake and scheduler handoff. No nanosecond price is measured yet. At 196 admitted Wikidata blocks, even 100 ns per gate pair would sum to only ~20 microseconds per decode; 10 microseconds per contended handoff across 196 events would sum to ~2 ms, already ~1.2% of the 0.162 s four-worker wall. These are sensitivity examples, not forecasts. Record exact acquisitions, failed tries, wait wall, task-clock spent waiting, wakeups and permit occupancy by phase; wall and active worker CPU/byte are both judged. A sleeper's CPU falls trivially, so **worker CPU outside waits** must be summed over completed blocks and normalised by the same output bytes. Whole-decoder wall remains the production target. An active-CPU improvement with worse wall is mechanistic evidence, not a production gain.

Waiting workers have **no other block to work on**; a cap deliberately idles cores and can lengthen the critical path. In B, `slots` has already built the slot buffers before a worker waits. In C, its induction has built the grammar. The worker keeps its reusable scratch allocation and may keep hot lines, so two active phases do **not** guarantee only two resident working sets. E must take a whole-block lease *before* a worker receives/starts its job, maintaining the same scanner window and four-worker binary; otherwise it is merely a two-thread configuration with a changed pipeline. E is the occupancy-loss control, **not a numerical correction** to subtract from B/C/D: the latter still overlap other phases and can park with allocated buffers. Prior i9 measurements show two independent Workers at 0.252 s against four at 0.162 s in that build, so E can be substantially slower while lowering bytes and active CPU. Compare E's active CPU and IMC with A; report its wall cost separately.

A cheap exposure check follows from existing [phase LOG 6892999618](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6892999618): the 1-worker shares are induction 0.329, writer 0.147; applying their observed 1→4 inflations gives approximate four-worker active shares 0.321 and 0.150. Four workers then have mean induction occupancy ~1.28 and writer occupancy ~0.60. Under an **independent-phase heuristic only**, P(3 or 4 simultaneously in phase) is ~10% and ~1%, respectively. Real phases can synchronize, so this is not a measured conflict rate; it warns that a cap of 2 may barely touch writers. Pre-register a minimum demonstrated exposure (actual cap blocking/active overlap reduction) for interpreting a null. If C never blocks, its null says nothing about writer bytes. This exposure can be counted in the same proposed run, without another experiment.

## Readout and direction-specific decision

Every arm needs **both** whole-decoder wall and active per-byte worker CPU, plus wait time and IMC reads/writes on the same i9. Check the whole active-CPU sum and each phase, including “rest”; a phase that suffers may not cause the footprint. In a valid cap screen, compare B,C,D,E with A and one another, but infer only the effect of *phase concurrency*, not bytes saved by a new implementation. A large fall in active CPU/IMC with a rise in wall means co-running interference exists but idle workers cost more than it saves.

| Direction | Screen observation that makes its **next pricing** worthwhile | Observation that lowers its priority here, without eliminating the hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Writer byte-reduction | With adequate C exposure, C or D reduces IMC and whole active CPU/byte, especially the induction/rest outside the writer, and either lowers wall or incurs a wait penalty small enough that a byte-saving design could plausibly recover it. Price avoided zero-fill/copies against the actual output writes and new bookkeeping, on the shipped writer. | C blocks but active CPU/IMC stay flat and wall rises: concurrent writer residency is not a demonstrated cause. This does **not** reject a writer that removes bytes at unchanged concurrency. If C barely blocks, mark non-identifying. |
| Induction representation/residency | With adequate B exposure, B or D lowers IMC and active CPU/byte in writer/rest or the whole decoder, with a coherent direction after charging wait/overhead. Price smaller slots and write-avoiding induction against current 20 B layout, changed codegen and cache traffic. B-only active CPU gain with wall loss may still motivate a no-wait representation change. | B blocks but no active CPU/IMC benefit and wall worsens: a cap is a poor intervention on this path. It does **not** refute the already shipped 20 B slot or any independent representation change. |
| Cooperative Cells, **not an arm** | If **B, D or E** show a material fall in active worker CPU/byte when fewer blocks or inductions overlap, irrespective of their wall loss, **price Cooperative Cells properly**: useful work on the parked cores, shared-buffer/metadata traffic, serial share, balance, and whole-reader wall at four cores. E is the cleanest “fewer complete blocks” contrast; B/D test narrower activity. | If B/D/E have real exposure but show no meaningful active-CPU reduction, outcome is **NOT YET**: there is no newly identified four-core interference for a team to recover. Do not “park” Cooperative Cells or infer it from an induction-cap wall regression. |

A four-core shared-block proposal faces a quantitative hurdle independent of this screen. With the prior census s=0.742 used **optimistically as whole-decoder time share**, a two-person team has g₂=1/[s+(1−s)/2]=1.148, so two teams need at least 0.450/(2×1.148)=**0.196 s** of ideal compute against four independent Workers' **0.162 s** on the earlier i9 build. To reach 0.162 in that model requires g₂>1.389, i.e. s<0.44, before synchronization. This is not a current timing verdict: s is instruction share in admitted Cells, not measured whole-decoder cycle share, and builds differ. It is a mandatory hurdle for any four-core Cooperative steelman; lowered active CPU alone does not solve it. The team plan at ≥6 cores is a separate comparator.

## CI versus i9, and the cheaper preliminary check

A 4-vCPU CI runner can run the **same-binary functional and software screen**: byte correctness, error/fallback paths, gate exposure, wait wall, active task-clock outside waits, and whole wall, with a frozen pairing and validity rule. Those results answer whether the gate works and whether a severe wall regression is obvious *on that VM*. CI has no IMC, its virtual CPU/cache placement and co-tenancy differ from the measured i9, and software task-clock cannot identify physical traffic. **The causal ranking of the three i9 footprint hypotheses needs the i9's paired IMC ledger**; CI cannot substitute. Do not pool CI and i9 ratios. A prospective registration should name which readout decides which question, and retain all attempts.

Before building a gate, the cheapest desk check is to calculate its **possible exposure** from existing per-phase occupancy/timeline records, if entry/exit timestamps are already retained. The scope-time shares above already show why C at cap 2 risks near-zero treatment. If timestamps are absent, this is a *design warning*, not permission for a fresh unregistered capture. Include overlap/exposure in the one proposed screen or choose a stronger cap under a newly frozen rule. The existing IMC concurrency curve shows four independent sets move more DRAM than one/two; it cannot attribute the cause to induction, writer or rest. The phase active-CPU inflation is nearly proportional across all three. Neither old record identifies the winner without an intervention.

**Recommendation:** revise the registration *before data* to include same-binary gate-overhead calibration, explicit wait/exposure validity, paired wall + active CPU, and a two-stage CI/i9 interpretation. Keep A–E; F remains out. A cap result can prioritise an implementation's **pricing**, never qualify it or close a hypothesis alone. No implementation, measurement, registration, merge or authority change by the Optimiser.
