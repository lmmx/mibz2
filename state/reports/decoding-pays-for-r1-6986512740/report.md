## Factual corrections

### 1. Use separate symbols for internal gaps, historical counts and forest chords

§3 defines qₖ as a count of **internal gaps** of exact separation depth k. The banana$ example correctly gives q₀=4 and q₁=q₂=1. Call that count S(k), or reserve qₖ for it consistently. The proof is that a chain of length ℓ has one vertex at each distance 0…ℓ, so the number of vertices at exact distance k equals the number of chains surviving to at least k. On the same example D={1,3,4,5}, the internal image cuts are {1,2,4,5}, C=3, and E₀=6. The sole nonzero chain is 2→6→3 using right-row gap indices. Therefore internal q₁=E₀−r=1, whereas the forest chord count E₀−r+1 is 2.

The historical counting script also includes row zero if a preimage orbit reaches it. That is a row, with no gap before it in the linear convention. Write its historical profile as q̃ₖ=qₖ+[k=d₀]. On this corpus d₀=1 for 229 blocks and 2 for four, as record 65 checks in all 233. The statement that q₁=r−C was verified on every block conflates the chord count with both profiles. On frozen, internal q₁=10,954 and q̃₁=10,955 equals the chords. On the primitive column 100, row zero follows 0→2→1. Its historical extra count occurs at k=2. There q̃₁=q₁=1 and the chord count is 2. This independently refutes a universal row-zero correction at k=1.

Keep the cited regression on its recorded inputs. The tail from record 63 includes the row-zero contribution at k=2 in four blocks. Its printed medians and regression can be labelled as the historical convention. A claim for exactly corrected internal inputs requires a rerun. Record 65's fit script uses (q̃₁−1)/r uniformly for its excess proxy and sums the original tail. On the four d₀=2 blocks this proxy is one gap low. This affects the exact labelling of the regression inputs, and the article should avoid implying that the convention issue was removed from the fit. The direct excess (E₀−V₀)/r and the 0.393 baseline are independently reproducible to the reported precision. The public sources do not provide the per-block profile arrays.

Add two short results from record 65 alongside the working example. On a primitive block, the full positive-depth sum is n−r, so the **short tail** distinguishes blocks where a full sum would not at fixed n,r. A chain's length is the increase from starting to terminal common-prefix length, so it is independent of how large the terminal value was to start with. All 233 studied blocks are primitive. Periodic blocks may have cycles between equal rotations and the profile counts only terminating chains. For example the valid abab column bbaa has two cyclic internal gaps and no positive-depth terminating gaps.
