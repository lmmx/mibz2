# Independent baseline · What decoding pays for

In a BWT run, LF moves neighboring rows by the same shift. Take a gap between neighbors and move it along with them while they still share a run. The paths end at run boundaries. The number of paths lasting at least k steps is a property of the block, and its short tail helps explain how much counted work Cells and the forest do on the studied Wikidata blocks.

- Let D be the internal run-boundary gaps. Inside a run the gap successor is the right row's LF image, and the partial map is injective on internal gaps. An internal gap ends when it first reaches D. For a primitive block, the finite common prefix of the two cyclic rotations grows by one on every defined step, ruling out a cycle. Periodic blocks can have separate cycles of equal rotations. Count only terminating chains in their survival profile.
- Write q_k for the internal-site count at exact distance k from the terminal. Each chain of length at least k contributes one such site, so q_k is also the number of chains that survive to length k. q_k−q_(k+1) counts chains that end at length k. Exclude row zero from the gap set. Record 63 used a row-zero-inclusive calculation, and record 65 reconciles the extra point at depth one in 229 blocks and depth two in four.
- A common refinement of domain and image run tilings has E₀=2r−1−C intervals where C counts coincident interior boundaries. E₀−V₀=E₀−r is the newly distinguished internal gaps at depth one. It differs by one from the frozen forest chord count 10,955. On the frozen block E₀=62,138 and E₀−r=10,954.
- The first-generation fit leaves a 0.393 residual Cells/forest correlation after run density and first excess. Adding the published sum q₂…q₈/r changes it to −0.061 on these 233 large Wikidata blocks. Leave-one-out RMSE falls about 34% / 17%. This is an empirical relationship between counts of distinct objects. The forest schedule itself can change counts under a tie perturbation on the same input, so the profile cannot be their exact accounting identity or prove optimality. The adjacent-gap algorithm's visit count does not fit comparably well.
- Truncation matters. Sum q_k over the first K positive depths gives sum min(chain length,K). On primitive blocks the full positive-depth sum is n−r, so it cannot explain residual variation at fixed n and r. Chain length is terminal LCP minus starting LCP, not the magnitude of the terminal. The prespecified mean-irreducible-LCP predictor adds too little. A profile tail need not rank two algorithms or imply a bound.
- Distinguish avoided work from total work and wall. Cells is 92,570 cuts on frozen. Adjacent-rank contraction has a modeled row dependency span of 87 over 12 rounds with 1,337,701 executed row-steps, about 1.4864n, and 102,233 interval visits. The executed total excludes the terminal snapshot. Row span cannot be used to estimate interval speedup. Open Return matches byte output and shrinks requested heap on frozen, but its VM paired decoder ratios to eager are 1.171, 1.211 and 1.091 at calling thread, two and four pool workers. The forest loses on native wall time despite avoiding an interior walk. Neither candidate nor the fit establishes the i9 production optimum.
- The experiment should be able to show what changes the counts and what changes actual wall separately. The first inspectable follow-up is to trace original gap IDs, affected slices and event kinds through both algorithms on the tie witness before trying a big benchmark.
