# Fresh-reader review

Review scope: source-text review of `post.md` only. I did not inspect research records, code, links, other files, or live rendering. I read prose, equations, captions and SVG labels/source; I cannot judge actual visual layout, clipping, responsive behaviour, slider operation or accessibility behaviour. Reader perspective: programmer comfortable with caches and pointers, new to bzip2 internals and this project's history.

## What I understood

The useful finding is that maximizing decoder workers does not maximize throughput. On the tested i9, seven workers at a ten-thread budget used 0.885 of the baseline time and roughly a quarter less peak memory. Six were best among the counts tested at eight threads. Reducing workers sacrifices aggregate busy processor time, but reduces cycles per output byte by more: at ten threads, roughly 15% less active processing capacity is outweighed by 25% cheaper bytes. Unchanged clock/instruction count/private-cache traffic and lower L3 misses make contention in the shared memory hierarchy a plausible explanation.

Workers finish independent blocks while a scanner and reader handle ordered portions. The approximately 2.1 MB active footprint per worker exceeds private L2 and competes in shared L3. The pipeline diagram and cache explanation are enough to follow the central argument without learning the inverse transform.

Measured: paired wall-time ratios, peak memory, confirmation on held-out files, hardware-counter patterns, the clock ratio, processor use at ten threads, and the 12-byte-rule variant's miss/time changes. Modelled: the quadratic contention curve, the extrapolated solo-worker cost, fitted 0.8 cache fraction, CPU-budget extrapolation, eight-thread non-worker use, proportional scheduler slack, 44% contention attribution, all transfer predictions, and the proposed detailed L3/ring queueing mechanism. The text labels much of this well, but several sentences promote inference into established cause.

At ten threads the stated procedure compares cores=10, cache=7.9 and CPU=8.2, then floors the smallest to seven. At eight it compares 8, 7.9 and 6.6, then floors to six. Those are clear procedural answers. They are not both mathematical consequences of the preceding throughput objective: flooring an optimum is an additional policy, and the draft admits its eight-thread model predicts a tie while the observation favours six.

On another machine I would need allowed processors and physical cores, actual shared-L3 groups and capacities, a block-size/kernel-dependent footprint, and worker placement across cache groups. I would sum capped per-cluster cache estimates, compare with a process CPU budget, and validate the prediction against timing. The article correctly says that general use remains gated on transfer tests; it does not yet provide a complete reproducible way to obtain the non-worker/slack inputs on arbitrary machines.

Users get no merged change yet: PR #524 is pending. Seven workers at ten threads and beyond physical cores have passed review for the measured i9 profile. Eight threads and below await more testing. Other profiles retain today's behaviour. The intended future system decides once at startup rather than adapting during a stream.

## Held-out questions

**Does a null W12 time result identify the cause? No.** Here W12 appears to mean the 12-byte-rule variant, not twelve workers; the draft itself wisely calls it “12-byte rules.” Fewer L3 misses with no detectable wall-time improvement establishes that this particular intervention did not yield a detectable net benefit under those conditions. It does not establish that footprint/miss costs are absent, that queueing is the remaining cause, or that a different footprint intervention cannot help. The draft says “the remaining contention cycles are not paid in footprint bytes or in the number of misses” and later “which is why the 12-byte rules did nothing.” Both outrun the stated evidence. Tradeoffs, overlap, small effect size and measurement uncertainty remain compatible with the null. The explicit conjecture label helps but does not retract those earlier causal conclusions.

**Does the model mathematically select seven rather than eight? No, not from the displayed objective.** For t(W)=0.42/W+0.0068W, t(7)=0.1076 and t(8)=0.1069 in the displayed normalization, so eight is slightly better. A continuous optimum near 7.9 is not a hard feasibility ceiling. “A count is a whole number, so the model takes the whole number below” inserts a flooring policy where a discrete objective normally requires comparing neighbouring integers. The 8.2 CPU budget does not exclude eight either. The text can honestly say a conservative floor policy selects seven and the sweep measured seven best, or revise the actual objective to explain seven. It should not claim that integer rounding itself mathematically selects seven.

**Does 44% imply recoverable wall time? No.** It is a model attribution whose extrapolated intercept and exponent are uncertain, not a measured stall-time bucket. Even within the model, the counterfactual 1.8x assumes the fitted contention term can vanish without changing instructions, available parallelism, pipeline utilization or another bottleneck. The draft currently says removing it would “run about 1.8 times faster,” and then proposes ways to win back the 44%. It should explicitly present 1.8x as a conditional model counterfactual, not an observed or established recoverable improvement. The 34–60% sensitivity is helpful, but uncertainty in the attribution and feasibility of recovery are separate issues.

## Dense prose that needs a figure or smaller argument steps

The long paragraph beginning “A shrunken footprint has already shown…” carries at least six transitions: rule layout change → misses/time measurements → cycles-per-avoided-miss comparison → residual attribution → 35x more L3 accesses → proposed interventions and a pending test. This is the hardest passage. Split it at the evidence/hypothesis boundary and add a compact measured-versus-hypothesized diagram or table. Do not draw an additive “44% stall bucket” waterfall because those terms are not directly measured or established as additive.

A small evidence table could list: intervention, observed miss change, observed wall-time change, what it supports, and what it leaves open. A separate diagram could show L2 → shared L3/ring → memory, annotating measured access frequency versus hypothesized extra latency. That would clarify both the null-result limitation and the mechanism test.

The existing three-limit figure appears useful from its source labels, but currently makes the rounding problem visually authoritative (“cache binds: 7 workers”). The label should distinguish a fitted optimum near eight from a conservative seven-worker choice. A two-point calculation for seven and eight would expose the distinction more effectively than more prose.

## Overall

The empirical finding, why cheaper bytes can beat more parallel workers, and current release status are understandable. The largest remaining obstacles are scientific interpretation rather than bzip2 prerequisites: conservative flooring is presented as mathematical optimization; a null layout intervention is treated as identifying the residual mechanism; and a fitted cycle share is too readily translated into recoverable wall time. Resolve those before considering the explanation self-contained for a new reader.
