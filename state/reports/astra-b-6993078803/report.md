## Staged arrays versus credited savings

The frozen queue stages 48,853 retained-source records and 48,931 residual records. Charged once for writing and once for reading them, that is (2(48{,}853+48{,}931)=195{,}568) record operations. The field ledger recomputes both baseline and residual geometry from equality, closures and the two kinds of wins, and counts word fields separately. It then credits eight eager or six lazy setup fields per removed band while granting bottom order and usable descriptors. The frozen credits are 73,068 eager and 72,838 lazy. On the matched admission populations, the debit and credit are 1,661,348 versus 635,595 (eight eager blocks), and 2,140,008 versus 792,511 (nine lazy blocks). In the equal-unit screen the two arrays alone exceed the credit on every block, even before the hash index and queue traffic.

The units are different. If one staging record operation is priced at λ times a credited field operation, the aggregate break-even price is about 0.383 on the eager population and 0.370 on lazy, before adding omitted constructor costs. Neither one record touch nor one field operation has a measured cycle price. The comparison supports dropping this staged representation as a candidate on the evidence available. It does not prove a wall-time loss or rule out a fused consumer.

The separate trace charges two line-aligned 12-byte arrays and an open-addressed index of 16-byte slots, under cold fully associative write-allocate LRU with 64-byte lines and final dirty drain. Frozen touches 22,431 distinct lines and charges 77,138 and 73,306 transfers at 32 and 256 KiB. It does not replay a baseline end-to-end alongside that stage. It excludes the queue nodes, original input, sort/order construction, residual induction and writer. The bitmap-order alternative's 237,172 frozen field operations are a separate constructive estimate. Cache sharing, dependence and overlap at seven to ten hardware workers remain unmeasured. The lead's phrase “costs more than it saves” should be read under the stated field-count ledger.
