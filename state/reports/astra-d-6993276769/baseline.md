# Astra D · evidence baseline before reading the summary

The archived frozen model has 900,000 decoded output bytes. At the modeled 2 MiB last-level cache, stock, final-slot repair and tiled forward inversion charge 103,045, 78,685 and 48,363 64-byte memory-line transfers. Those totals come from demand-read fills, read-for-ownership fills and writebacks, including a final drain, giving 7.327644, 5.595378 and 3.439147 B per decoded byte. The frozen replay can derive them from a trace archive and verify its digests. The numbers describe a deterministic reference hierarchy, and the backing traces describe guest references and instruction counts. Neither is an i9 IMC observation.

The pool census reports peak requested allocation for one scanner, reused worker states and four output slots per worker, with sequential invocation and no process-RSS or concurrent memory measurement. Seven-worker stock/direct/tiles peaks are 217,712,280 / 213,093,176 / 101,243,602 bytes. The frozen tile layout's 57,836 affine pieces and 7,031 tile headers plus 256 character ends take 260,492 bytes of navigation arrays. Its 899,931 dependent header visits and 4,148,528 piece probes show that lower outer traffic can move work inward. In the frozen stock dependency receipt, top/bottom linked loads are 16,426 / 24,961, and marked-spine / rule-position / copy-position counts are 32 / 28,710 / 9,628. The two segmented controls have 9,216,681 and 12,335,693 tile piece probes. Those are counts with different dependency structures and no latency price attached.

Final-slot repair keeps the production inverse and changes where its output and end repair live on explicitly eligible blocks. It has much lower added modeled work than the tiled arm, and it is a candidate for a matched machine comparison. A low modeled DRAM number is insufficient to claim a gain or to choose the tiles. The conditional useful-byte bound, the correctness proofs, allocation census, trace replay, and any cited external hardware counters answer separate questions.
