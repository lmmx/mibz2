Cutting after # makes the whole indexed word one token, with token rank 0 every time. Cutting after | produces #| once, 0| exactly m−h times and 1| exactly h times. ## 2. Support, terminal cycles, and marks

The relevant prior art supplies extension graphs of factor languages, their BWT/de Bruijn interpretation, and reconstruction ambiguity from assembly spectra. The result here is a specific deterministic presentation of all periodic futures using unnamed extension tables and a separately supplied mark. [Berthé et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4260) · [Belazzougui and Cunial](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2019.10) · [Bresler, Bresler and Tse](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.0068)

For a context w that splits on the right, assume its right children and the ordered predecessor links into I(aw) are known. Since every row has a predecessor, a unique predecessor a must cover every right child. When several precede w, the extension support specifies exactly which children each a covers. In the order of those children, subdivide I(aw) or reuse it if there is one. Stable LF orders the subset preceded by a exactly as the corresponding a-prefixed rows. Thus the new child links require only the incidence bits, equality, and ordering. The FIFO schedule is sufficient: deleting the first symbol of any right-special factor leaves a right-special factor, so each actual split is discovered from its shorter suffix. Unary continuation does not change a future class. This is the decoder invariant, and deterministic event order fixes which unnamed matrix to consume next. The theorem needs the complete ordered tape; an unlabeled bag of matrices or a truncated tape is a different input.

There are D distinct infinite periodic futures. Every two distinct ones differ at a finite place. Refinement reaches their exact ordered partition, with at most D−1 splits and 2D−1 total nodes. Periodic left and right shifts on this finite set are inverse permutations. Each terminal cycle comprises the distinct rotations of one primitive necklace. The word “primitive” refers to that period, even for a source that repeats it.

For each terminal class, LF permutes its rows onto the predecessor class. Consequently width is constant **within** that necklace. If its period is d_j, its positive width m_j contributes d_j m_j rows. Conversely, choose positive integer widths with sum_j d_j m_j=n and emit the predecessor byte at each lexicographic class with that width. For a given byte a the predecessor destinations are ordered by the source futures, because adding the same a preserves lexical order. Thus the resulting stable LF preserves the within-class offset and realizes the chosen multiplicities. Distinct width vectors yield distinct L: L itself defines stable LF, which recovers those multiplicities. There are n/d equal rows for each rotation of a single primitive necklace of period d, and n/d LF cycles of length d. Counting a single distinct necklace does not imply one LF cycle when d<n. Several distinct necklaces can admit multiple width vectors, but the length can sometimes force a unique one.

**The mark carries numerical information.** SUP1 stores a zero-based rank among D distinct futures in a 32-bit field, in addition to n, the alphabet mask and bit length. Choosing an arbitrary future among D can require ceil(log2 D) bits for a fixed-width rank. On the frozen primitive input D=n, so its rank 264,045 equals the bzip2 primary. For one necklace with m=n/d copies, class widths are uniform: original primary p gives floor(p/m). The rank carries the rotation and discards which of its equal rows was marked. For multiple primitive necklaces, p cannot generally be translated without the discarded widths. The columns `aab` and `abb` share support and n. At p=1 they emit `aaa` and `bbb`, respectively. With distinct-future rank 0 they both emit `aaa` and have different columns. Identical valid inputs **including the prescribed rank** cannot give distinct marked outputs. Follow the rank's predecessor cycle, reverse the period and repeat or truncate to n. Scope the result to valid periodic LF languages and parsed tape instances.

## 3. Exact ambiguity witness

I sorted the original reference word's rotations independently by their exact 4,823-byte prefixes. These prefixes distinguish every rotation and recover the original BWT and primary. At context length 4,743 there is exactly one mixed predecessor bucket and it is `6d 6e 65` at row 138,927. Its six permutations exhaust the last columns with the counted 4,744-spectrum. Stable LF gives exactly two single circuits, the original and `65 6d 6e`. Both corresponding words are primitive and are not rotations of each other. The alternative's marked pre-RLE digest is `19c4094a807a9886a8f48ec95cefbaa9c57af41c2cddabd91399a2300b35eea4`.

The submitted `compare_spectra.rs` checks the spectra directly from the two words: joint equality IDs for power-of-two spans, then exact pairs of spans covering the full cyclic substring. HashMap checks equality of keys, so hashing cannot collapse unequal spans. I recomputed with joint integer sorting in NumPy, bypassing the BWT interval proof for this comparison.

The four changes replace two once-occurring strings with two different once-occurring strings. At K=4,745, the original has a two-row mixed bucket at 138,928 with bytes `6e 65`. Its swapped version has cycles **442,958 and 456,973** and is a separate alternative from the primitive K=4,744 witness. At K=4,745 only one of the two columns fits an ordinary primitive BWT. ## 5. What the encoder pays

`pack` walks all bytes of L into occurrence-position lists. It allocates row-indexed cells and a log-factorial table, discovers incidence via rank queries on the positions, constructs weighted margins, and computes the weighted EXT1 tape before extracting SUP1. The wrapper also needs the final weighted partition to translate the numerical primary. The implemented encoder therefore already requires linear scans and storage and performs extra weighted work even when its public output is unweighted. The low table count is a statement about which decisions need transmitted bits. At hundreds of thousands of forced splits per frozen block it is no demonstrated bound on the cost of finding those decisions, or on cache traffic. Conversely these costs are for this reference construction, and they do not prove a lower bound for a direct run-based extractor. Any production comparison must charge BWT-to-support discovery, mark translation, reconstruction, RLE1 and CRC against existing decoding of the same inputs. The earlier phrase timing applies to different algorithms.
