# Sustained-load registration review · #576

The recipe explicitly passes that value, so the Python CLI's 60-second default has no effect. The adjudicator also accepts a placement if the recorded pre-warm failed. Align the recipe and registration to 60 seconds, and validate successful reference-arm pre-warm before producing that placement's verdict. A failed or missing pre-warm fixture should leave no judged output. Retain the five cells, two families, existing clamp cutoff, loss screen, Rule 4 thresholds and fixed rounds.

## What clock regime the recipe can establish

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| 120-second probe | The recipe runs lazy 10/40 unpinned after the quiet gate and 30-second rest. It keeps starting full Wikidata ×16 decodes until 120 seconds and logs completion times. The report gives first and late clocks, power and the suffix within 2% of the final-minute mean. The probe is diagnostic. Its completion and onset never gate a comparison. |
| Pre-warm | The recipe's PREWARM default is 30, passed as an explicit --prewarm. The Python default is 60. The journal's 30 seconds and 2.5 minutes across cells also describe the shorter method. Judging filters only warm and measure rows, skipping all prewarm rows. A synthetic record with rc=1, an error and absent counters for the sole pre-warm still produced judged records. Freeze and check 60 successful seconds rather than relying on a default or a wall timer that includes failed starts. |
| Continuity | There is one quiet gate and idle baseline at each cell start. Each arm then warms on main for at least six wall seconds and measures main, text, random, main with no intervening gate or sleep. Separate process and perf launches leave small real gaps. “Back to back” describes the planned cadence. |
| Clamp screen | The recipe registers u10 and p10. Either measured main decode at cycles/ref-cycles ≥1.2 VOIDs the placement, even if the pair's mean is below the cutoff. A synthetic [0.80,1.20] pair with last warm at 1.00 confirms the equality boundary. Warm and guard clocks are recorded. The frozen cutoff deliberately excludes them. |
| Steadiness | The other screen voids if more than 10% of a cell's arm-round blocks have the mean of their two measured main clocks more than 3% from the last warm clock. A synthetic [0.96,1.04] measured pair at warm=1.00 passes both gates. Each comparison is therefore screened for the high-frequency regime and for pair-average drift, with no guarantee of a constant clock within either decode. |
| p8, p6, p4 | p8 is not registered as clamped and may be mixed. Report p6 and p4 as turbo-state observations if their measured Wikidata decodes all have clock ≥1.2. Label a mixed case from its measured clocks. The report currently prints warm and measured combined per arm, and the raw rows support the separate measured fraction. A sustained load can produce a turbo-state result at a low count. |

Record 64 predicts u10/p10 clamp around 0.93–0.97 and 8/32 ratios of 0.970 and 0.975, respectively. A placement that violates the preregistered clamp cutoff is VOID for adoption. A probe that never reaches the assumed regime is evidence about this exposure, and cannot by itself rank 7 and 8 under a clamp. The raw clock is a system-wide cycles/reference-cycles ratio for the decode window. It supports an operational state classification at this threshold, with background load and within-decode transitions unresolved. ## Statistics and power

The recipe carries the prior paired-log reader forward. It averages the two main wall times within each arm-round first, then makes one log ratio per round against that count's shipped reference. Unpinned has three Wikidata hypotheses. Pinned has ten, from 3+3+2+2 at counts 10, 8, 6 and 4. At α=0.025 each, Holm protects the corresponding families, and the union bound is 0.05 across the placements given valid marginal p-values. Shared references and dependent tests within a placement do not invalidate Holm. Serial carry-over can affect the paired test's marginal validity. The rotation assigns each arm every position twice, which helps interpret order effects.

The losses use the per-corpus 95% two-sided paired-log interval. Its lower bound above 1 blocks adoption, on either guard as on main. This is a significant-loss screen. An inconclusive guard does not establish parity. The point threshold for Rule 4 is separate from Holm's main-corpus gain test.

I solved the one-sided noncentral-t power equation at each family's strictest Holm step. Inputs are SD of a paired log wall ratio and discovery power of 80% for one alternative, with the paired-t model's independence and variance assumptions. Percentages below are wall-time reduction, using 1 − exp(−effect). The modest difference from the design table comes from using exact noncentral-t and ratio-scale conversion.

| Family entry | Rounds | Critical t | SD .005 | SD .010 | SD .020 | SD .040 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unpinned, first of 3 | 8 | 3.128 | 0.72% | 1.44% | 2.86% | 5.64% |
| Pinned 10/8, first of 10 | 8 | 4.029 | 0.90% | 1.79% | 3.55% | 6.97% |

At SD .020 the 0.970 u10 prediction has roughly 84% power at its strict step. The 0.975 p10 prediction has roughly 42%. Their 80% thresholds for SD are .0210 and .0140. At SD .040 those powers fall to roughly 25% and 7%. A true 2% gain at the six-round cells has about 60% power at SD .010, and 12% at .020. The clamped SD is unknown. These are resolution limits for a bounded test. A noisy or non-significant contrast leaves the shipped count under the frozen rule. Later Holm steps can be less stringent, and passing the memory and guard tests is separate from the power tabulated here. ## Rule 4

The reader checks the median across rounds of each corpus's block peak RSS. The driver first takes the maximum across the two main decodes in each arm-round. All corpora within 1.10 allows ADOPTS if Holm rejects and no corpus is SLOWER. A single over-limit primary corpus can trade only if it is FASTER, has a point ratio at most .95, growth no more than twice the gain, and at most 62,500 KiB absolute growth. The flags correctly let text trade as a policy primary corpus even though it serves as a speed guard, and leave random secondary. Two corpora over 1.10 always preclude trade.

For 8/32 at 0.97 and historical growth spanning 8–16%, at most 10% on every corpus can ADOPT if Holm and the guards clear. Above 10%, a 3% point gain cannot qualify. Growth of 12% or 16% needs at least 6% or 8% gain respectively, plus the confidence and absolute-RSS conditions. The expected forecast at that RSS is HELD ON MEMORY if a gain was established and no SLOWER result blocks it. With no established gain, the verdict is NOT ADOPTED. The rule makes an adoption by trade available before data, with the maximum 64 MB and single-primary restriction preserved. ## Provenance, counters and carry-over

- The previous failure passed an all-arm counter file to a pair-only reader. This recipe uses ungrouped RAPL, cycles, reference cycles and IMC events for each decode, writes associated values in that same JSON record, and reports all arms through the matching multi-arm reader. Judged inputs are split by placement. It avoids the old roster mismatch without a separate file per candidate comparison. Raw perf CSV is temporary and the durable records retain parsed sums, enabled fraction and entry counts. There is a saved preflight CSV. This confirms the wiring, while actual PMU admission still depends on the i9 preflight and live counts. A hardware failure on a judged warm/measured decode voids that placement. Power and IMC rates use the wrapper's elapsed window, and IMC net rate subtracts a per-cell idle baseline. These are package/window diagnostics rather than per-thread attribution.
- The producer records both prev_arm and prev_block. For a measured decode, prev_arm normally names the current arm because there is already a same-arm warm-up. prev_block preserves the previous arm-round block throughout the current block and is the cross-arm carry-over key. Position balance does not balance predecessor pairs. For four arms, excluding the first pre-warm edge, C→D appears four times, C→A zero times and A→C once. For three arms B→C appears three times and C→A twice. Use the registered predecessor and drift summaries to interpret that asymmetry. They do not alter the frozen test or turn the clock probe into a significance result. [Prior counter failure](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/pull/145#issuecomment-6984061702)
- The target is the i9 and byte-identical v0.5.5 source, with fixed occupancy arms. The unpinned 10 comparison directly covers its 10/40 arm and provides a shared-count inference where production uses identical plans across thread requests. It does not test different 10-worker windows at 12–20 threads. A run that shows p4/p6 still turbo does not establish a clamped optimum there.
- Ran the pinned stub-driven sustain suite (30/30) and the paired reader suite (144/144). The independent untimed audit exercises the exact cutoff, cancellation in the mean-drift screen, failed pre-warm admission, schedule counts and exact power. The actual prolonged run and its register/PMU behavior remain unmeasured.

The scoped correction is to seal the 60-second pre-warm in both the recipe and the adjudicator. The comparisons and their frozen decisions can otherwise proceed at the stated resolution.
