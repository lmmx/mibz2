## 1. Exact composition and operational limits

Use A for the old top tail and B,D for the old bottom tail and its predecessor. Let a,b,d be their widths, with b < a ≤ b+d and s=a−b. The first top win removes B and puts its replacement X after A in bottom order, with width b. A remains at the top tail with width s. Because the old predecessor D is distinct from A, D is next at the bottom tail. The second cut removes A and inserts Y immediately before X at A's old bottom site. On the top it replaces D if s=d, or comes after D with its residual width d−s otherwise. The simultaneous substitutions preserve the complete top and bottom orders and the remaining widths. Those determine both source and image boundaries and the translated row map. The fresh names X and Y remain distinct regardless of their word handle.

The products are u·v and u·w in Cells' stored orientation. The word problem is left cancellation of the same fully marked u. With untouched D, w is a canonical literal. A materialized B has length at least two. An original B agrees exactly when the byte and mark bits agree. A marked byte counterexample is L=11100 with primary 1. Treating equality of raw bytes as enough would lose the distinguished occurrence. Given descriptor equality and the normal short-product test, both X and Y can reference the same immutable advertised bytes. The physical rule and arena offsets can shift. Each unchanged logical offset and marker is recovered through its descriptor. Marked or long products and mismatches still take the normal distinct-descriptor path. For a later short join, the two fixed-size copies determine all advertised bytes even if padding read past a shared word differs.

**Recognition boundary.** The source dispatch is the existing mandatory bottom read after T. A fresh header comes from the stable stream, which top or bottom settlement would already have tagged for A or B. The strict T writes paired slots, retains A and consumes B, and does not borrow or return a slot. Therefore this header's identity, width and literal can be read before the writes. A linked or tagged source must load its payload after the scalar update. L=100, primary 0, witnesses the alias danger. Old D=A, and the next opponent is the inserted X, with a different width.

For a slot-level check, name A's top front f and opposite slot k, B's bottom front g and opposite slot t, h=old previous[k], and fresh D's allocated pair (u,j). After both cuts, X occupies t/k, Y u/g. Bottom k links to g and g links to h. TE returns f/j and retains the previous link at t. TB instead has remaining D at bottom front j, promised top slot f with previous=0, and u links to f. The TB zero is available directly, eliminating one prior initialization and one read of it. The top load stays after these assignments because the predecessor can now contain one of the newly made cells. Both 12- and 16-byte rule arms share the lazy loop. Eager Cells has pre-laid slots and different hint paths, so the untouched-stream cost argument does not transfer.

The proof preserves the distinguished marked return, including for periodic columns. Budget-limited execution needs the ordinary debit per logical cell and the one-cut refusal path when the second debit is unavailable. The Python transducer verifies completed inductions. It does not exercise native early refusal, pointer bounds, canaries, CRC mutations, register allocation or fallback behavior. ## 3. Stronger geometric corollary

Label every live top interval by its original site. Cutting at B inserts after B in the same site and replaces it if it is exhausted. A surviving top tail stays in its site, while removing it or closing it can empty only the last active site. Therefore live sites remain a prefix, ordered within the top domain. On a shared hit, B and D are distinct original unmarked literals of the same byte. Their relative bottom order is the same as the initial stable byte order, hence site(D) < site(B). Cells coalesces capped run records before splitting the primary singleton, so either a different-byte run or that singleton originally lies between these equal literals. At least one descendant of an intervening site survives while B's site still exists.

After fusion, Y at site(D) and X at site(B) are adjacent in the bottom image, with Y first. Let the positive top gap width between them be g. The difference between their top starts is s+g. The difference between their bottom starts is s. If δ maps top to bottom, δ(X)−δ(Y)=−g. Thus even a representation that allowed two disjoint domains with the same translation could not unify these translations. One shared word can remain attached to two independently wired intervals. On L=101000, p=3 the two words are 01, with domain starts 0 and 2 and image starts 2 and 3. The displacement difference is −1. This limit concerns this cut and this interval order. The separate archived-order replay found 195,859 separated shared hits across the ten Wikidata and six prose traces, with zero gluable. ## 4. Correctness evidence and scope

I independently compared pointwise translated rows and ordered product words for 10,188 small relative-order/width cases. The two-cut state matched both scalar steps in each case. The pinned slot model also replayed 14,209 small marked columns and all 16 archived records, checking virtual slot and stream activity, closures, final word and mark. The sixteen-line output reproduced SHA-256 7c4701a87139c31143fab52eb60264c6fc5e995ff4b9d7cda8aaff5022e5ad66.

My separate oracle rebuilt the last column and its primary from initial widths, then inverted its dense stable permutation. In all 16 cases it agreed byte for byte with the rotated exact archived grammar. The frozen mark offset is 289,568 and the marked pre-RLE1 SHA-256 is f87e5caa2d5f0df9d5958fe60901e2023ae04ac406603d4c1584c8bb4017fbd6. Undoing RLE1 in the six prose replays gives 4,947,388 bytes, equal to Python's bzip2 decompression of the archived prose stream. This validates the model against the baseline's independent marked output. The archived data branch publishes ten of the Wikidata traces and the prose controls. The draft reports regeneration from a full compressed Wikidata corpus. I did not independently regenerate that complete corpus run or compile a fused decoder. ## 5. Prior art and measured-time scope

[The 15 November equal-words result](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0bb1d4bd4cbd30e45502f5aebb2446ea1d7c569d/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-15-equal-words.md) gives contiguity in bottom order and counts duplicates via the just-created word and the bottom neighbor. The T→E/B shared product is in the adjacent-step bucket, and the bands' positions illustrate that image contiguity. The [one-bit certificate, record 55](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/049464756c76addb72cbfaed42d421d006126eab/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-18-rank-gap-verification.md) proves equality in a different way. Adjacent excursions can be translated one row within unchanged initial atoms at every step when the boundary bit is clear. This witness uses equality of the literal operands of the same live winner, even across nonadjacent domains with different displacements. Image adjacency here follows from the explicit substitution. The bit is neither needed for the proof nor shown clear for these hits.

The previously timed ring already shared exact producer descriptors. It incurred general lookup overhead. The new mechanism isolates sibling products for which the live bottom refill already exposes the second operand, combines their boundary update, and needs neither the general ring nor a bottom-forward neighbor search. The old equal-cell elision result also matters. Removing copies while keeping slot writes did not deliver its expected throughput gain. [The recorded one-thread Cells profile](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/0bb1d4bd4cbd30e45502f5aebb2446ea1d7c569d/docs/journal/monodromy/2026-11-15-replay-and-team.md) over 196 Wikidata blocks attributes about 50% of kernel time to induction, 21% to slots, and 29% to the writer. This is a historical kernel split, without a same-build lazy wall attribution. In a proportional scenario, removing an average whole step's time on each of 2.371% of events would save 1.19% of Cells kernel time. The study preserves the logical step and its placement. If the saved part were 10–25% of an average step, the same calculation gives 0.12–0.30% of kernel time **before** any added cost. Those fractions are sensitivity inputs. This is neither a confidence interval nor a ceiling in a memory-stall regime. Whole-decoder wall also includes other stages, admission and overlapping workers. The sign of the marginal change remains open. The useful next experiment would isolate generated code and per-phase work for the exact lazy arm before spending a full paired throughput run, with failed lookaheads and one-debit refusal kept in the comparison.
