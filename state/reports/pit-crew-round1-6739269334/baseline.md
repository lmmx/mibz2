# Independent baseline, before reading the post

A prototype moves each block's preparation and finishing out of its decoding teams and onto two shared service threads. In a registered test on one i9-10900K and one Wikidata sample, two teams of three plus those two service threads delivered 1.045× throughput (95% interval 1.017–1.073) with 12.7% less aggregate CPU time than the nine-thread pipelined default, using eight decode threads. This is evidence for that whole arrangement, not an isolated mechanism or a production/default change.

Opening takeaways: (1) what service workers do and why teams can keep working; (2) registered 4.5% speed increase and ~13% CPU saving, with one-machine/one-corpus scope and eight-versus-nine decode workers; (3) removing a team alone loses—the service bundle matters, its internal causes remain unseparated; (4) one service worker bottlenecks, three teams plus two services at budget12 is a speed/CPU trade, distinct from budget10; (5) greater preparation admission alone is inconclusive, queue labels do not identify the bottleneck; (6) opt-in prototype, scoped safety evidence closure is not production qualification; guarded integration needs its own performance evidence.
