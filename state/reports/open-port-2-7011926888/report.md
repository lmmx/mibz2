## 2. Power and dependence

I reran the pinned `plan.py` with 1,500 replicates and obtained the committed `plan.txt` byte-for-byte. Each observation is the log ratio of the two builds in the same corpus, thread count and round. The simulator subtracts a cell median, pools that corpus's residuals at the three thread counts, mirrors them, then draws rounds independently with replacement and adds a fixed log effect. It estimates a separate rejection probability for each cell under that pooled-corpus model. It calculates the probability of any failure by multiplying cell pass probabilities, which assumes independence across cells. It does not preserve within-count correlation, temporal dependence or the two-stage extension schedule. G's 0.957 and the simulated G2 0.048 are model estimates. A shared-round sensitivity resampling the within-count vector and giving the vector a shared sign gave 0.945 for G under that still stylized model. [Pinned planner](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/9e0220a29e5886aaf4fc9b6dc9628a25b14b044a/docs/journal/2026-11-19-open-port-2-registration/plan.py#L28) · [Released table](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/9e0220a29e5886aaf4fc9b6dc9628a25b14b044a/docs/journal/2026-11-19-open-port-2-registration/plan.txt)

**The power statement is narrower than the draft claims.** The 0.76–1.00 range and no crossing at +2, +3, +5 and +10 are from this model, with two percentage points of allowed shortfall and 1,500 Monte Carlo replicates. Four points do not establish an inequality at every slowdown ≥2%. Both power curves rising to one gives no ordering between them. Finite replicate error and a pilot of only 12 pairs per guard cell matter. At +1%, accept the disclosed weakness only if the prospective rule explicitly prioritizes ≥2% and its separate policy protections for smaller primary losses remain in force. The old rule's high null rejection rate explains why its +1% rejection percentage is not clean evidence of useful power. It does not show that every lost detection would have been noise.

## 3. What the test bounds

The exact signed-rank dynamic program is correct for a null under which the joint nonzero signs are independent fair coins conditional on their magnitudes. I checked small zero and tied cases by enumeration. Zeros are discarded and equal absolute differences get averaged ranks. This handles rank ties under that null. More zeros shrink effective (n). Outliers can take the largest ranks and shift the test, with bounded influence from their magnitudes.

A symmetric common location shift equates the center with the mean of log ratios when the mean exists. Under asymmetric slowdowns the signed-rank test targets a rank location, which need not track the mean log ratio. The old wall-time cap depends on `exp(mean(log(port/reference)))`. For a 60-pair cell with 57 log ratios at (-0.001) and three at (+0.5), the geometric ratio is **1.02434153** and the exact one-sided p-value is **0.999999999963**. The pinned judge returns ADOPTS on a valid synthetic bundle containing this cell. This matters even if spikes are infrequent, since they count towards the old performance metric. A failed rejection under G2 also does not establish a bound on that metric.

**Null calibration.** The harness deterministically cycles `AB, BA, BA, AB` for two arms. This balances position, but gives no independent randomization of the signs. Even marginal symmetry does not suffice. Consider a common fair sign for a whole 60-pair cell, each magnitude 0.02. Each pair is marginally symmetric, yet the scorer rejects with probability 1/2 because the all-positive vector has (p=2^{-60}). Justify independence or define an assignment/resampling unit that addresses serial dependence, order, carryover and the separate stages before asserting exact size. Bonferroni over **39 guards plus sustained** is appropriate if that is the declared timing family and each p-value is valid. It makes no assumption about dependence *between* those 40 valid tests. The memory error estimate is empirical. An independent 120,000-replicate bootstrap drawing both arms from their v0.5.6 cell had no memory false failures, which supports low risk under that empirical model and does not turn it into a finite-sample upper bound for the workstation. State the 0.05 combined figure as a planning target with assumptions. [Arm order](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/9e0220a29e5886aaf4fc9b6dc9628a25b14b044a/examples/ab.rs#L2921)

**Correction:** retain the geometric-mean estimand and calibrate a paired statistic based on mean log ratios against a declared +2% alternative. An independently randomized within-pair order with a mean-based randomization test could handle the sharp no-effect null if carryover is addressed. If assignment or inference is by blocks, size and power must use those blocks. For a claim about a weak mean-zero null, justify the calibration under the stated noise conditions. Set a quantitative power floor and demonstrate the comparison under prospectively fixed plausible models. An alternative rank estimand would be a change in the adoption target and needs an explicit policy ruling.

## 5. Workstation cost and allocation

The proposed extension has 2,592 pairs (5,184 decodes). At a two-second settle before each arm, it spends **2 h 52 m 48 s on settle alone**. About 3 h 55 m overall is plausible with the decision, sustained, context and census. A cheaper fixed allocation is worth considering. The screen over multiples of four used the draft's 0.76 minimum power and each cell's own old-G power at +2%, with a one-point cushion. It identified fewer extensions for tight cells (e.g. records 8t, 32 total versus 60) and more or unresolved rounds for others (e.g. ho-structured-l9 8t, about 88 versus 60). Keeping current counts in the seven unresolved cells, four of which exceed the feasible cushion near power one, gives 1,732 extension pairs and about **57 minutes** less settle time. That is a screening result and leaves unresolved cells, so it is not a certified schedule.
