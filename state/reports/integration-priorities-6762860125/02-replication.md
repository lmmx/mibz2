**Minimum proposed programme: 12 distinct sequential dispatches, R=240 per cell, 42 cells, nine shards; no separate A/A calibration (k_cal=0).** This is the smallest-cost grid entry meeting the stated >=.80 all-cell power target under the draft's strict neutral point-variance model (.934; ~2583 runner-minutes). R480 gives .969 for ~3546: ~963 extra runner-minutes for that scenario's 3.5 percentage points. Do not call R480 the first qualifying grid design. This is a fixed planning choice, not a theorem that twelve dispatches suffice for all runner mixtures. No automatic increase after seeing qualification data. If a more conservative predeclared variance scenario is the required planning envelope, the provided grid has not established its minimum; do not disguise that as a power guarantee.

The dispatch-level t bound is mathematically sound **conditional on independent identically distributed approximately normal dispatch contrasts**. The intersection-union rule needs no42-fold NI correction: under any global null at least one cell null is true. It cannot repair that cell's size. All required dispatches admitted, distinct run identities, no replacement; publish one verdict after all. Within-dispatch trends need not be separately vetoed if the estimand is explicitly the mean under the frozen scheduling policy. They do not become innocuous merely by averaging: persistent cross-dispatch trends, carryover or arm-position bias remain. Fresh VMs, sequential launches and fixed spacing do not prove iid or a stable fleet mix. State "scheduled-period runner-draw mean under these assumptions", not a guaranteed fleet-wide no-harm bound. Freeze a feasible spacing rule (e.g. starts at least one hour apart and never before predecessor completion) including overrun handling.

**A/A is not a required qualification gate and six R240 dispatches cannot validate small-k t coverage.** The t interval uses the qualification's own across-dispatch SD; an external tau is for planning, not inserted into that bound. A/A can find gross harness defects or estimate neutral dispersion, but cannot measure the candidate×CPU-model interaction. Even six independent null-test trials with zero failures would give a one-sided95% upper false-positive bound of39.3%; six dispatches do not even provide six independent twelve-dispatch tests. A calibration prerequisite would add cost without establishing the missing guarantee. Retain existing structural smoke requirements; don't build a new open-ended calibration campaign.

The "doubled tau gives zero power" wording is incorrect: **zero union-bound lower bound**, not zero true all-cell success probability. Likewise DerSimonian–Laird tau after subtracting iid SE is not a proved conservative upper bound; omitted positive serial dependence may inflate tau, but estimation noise, heterogeneity and negative covariance preclude a general guarantee. Keep it labelled a planning scenario.

For a bounded neutral-only sensitivity check, use the existing19 links: center per-cell distributions at log1.02 to examine boundary size, retain model strata and within-dispatch joint structure, and evaluate the frozen12-dispatch statistic across explicit model weights (including leave-one-model-out/reweighting). Compare iid normal and empirical-mixture scenarios; report binomial Monte Carlo uncertainty. These are sensitivity models, not empirical validation of candidate effects or unobserved rare hosts. No selecting the weights, critical value or k by candidate outcomes. A non-normal rare-mixture counterexample exists for any such small-k t rule; do not promise universal coverage.

Concretely, a population with95% contrasts tightly near0 and5% near.4 has mean.02>log1.02. Twelve all-low draws occur with probability .95^12=.5404 and can falsely pass the NI bound. Tiny continuous jitter avoids zero sample variance. This is a model limitation, not evidence that GitHub has that distribution. A/A of identical binaries would not detect a candidate-only interaction of this kind. More A/A does not fix the inference by itself.
