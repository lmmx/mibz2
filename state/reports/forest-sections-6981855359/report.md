The reported VM cost is much higher than eager Cells. I used a separately written dense LF and permutation-network oracle. It checks every retained coordinate and every active forest-edge parity, the stack destination, composed word and length, the preserved mark and full periodic output at successive levels. It passed 5,227 marked LF column cases and 1,000 seeded arbitrary labelled networks. It uses a different rooting order from the prototype. ## 1. Presentation closed under first return — VERIFIED

An outgoing tiling gives one image per coordinate and the incoming tiling gives one preimage. Each composed interior walk is a translation over the intersection of its shifted slice constraints. Split at its finite boundaries. On a retained chord i, offsets at its source identify the new site, including through each band that has a different subsequent chord. First return is bijective on the retained section, so the output slices tile the new site in both orders. For labels already stored in output order, traversing a then b under LF stores W_b W_a. First returns to nested sections compose by grouping consecutive returns. Weighted mass across **all** cycles is conserved when the section meets every cycle, as it does with a forest interior. The mark's new offset is its old source offset minus the marked chord's start. For the initial presentation an LF run image is a stable byte-bucket interval. Splitting it by target run boundaries gives exactly the translated intersections. [Initial and recursive graph](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/3832bb12857181adb2f46b35f5c7fa8ebaa29a77/docs/journal/2026-11-18-switch-elimination/forest.rs)

## 2. Shared coordinate — VERIFIED

Each interior edge gives the potential difference a_v−a_u=x_e−y_e. Integrate it along the unique undirected root path, using the negative increment when traversing against the stored direction. An occurrence x_e+t becomes y_e+t; adding the two site potentials gives the same z. The scalar potential is unique up to an independent constant per component. This uses absence of undirected cycles in the multigraph, including self-loops and parallel edges. A directed acyclic graph can still have incompatible potential increments around an undirected cycle. Even when its shifts are compatible, the K₂,₂ diamond can switch between two matchings at the same height with identical ports. The diamond in the proof demonstrates that second failure.

## 3. Boundary uniqueness — VERIFIED

At a fixed integer height there is at most one coordinate at each site. For an occupied site the incoming and outgoing tilings each activate one arc, counting a chord endpoint as a distinct leaf. Thus active vertices have one in and one out, with degree-one leaves at the boundary. The underlying augmented forest ensures paths rather than cycles. Removing any interior edge defines a cut crossed by that edge alone, so the parity of degrees on one side equals the parity of active leaves on that side. This yields the unique T-join, with existence supplied by the tilings and uniqueness from the empty even subgraph of a forest. If both port ends are active at the same site they are two leaves with a path through their common site and no interior edge. Counting them as one undirected site terminal would lose that return. The tree T-join property is classical. The fixed-height lift and the shared signed order are the investigation's additional steps. [Classical tree T-join statement](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/9/2/182)

## 4. One order and the mark — VERIFIED

Root each tree. A depth-first traversal which emits incoming child subtrees, landing leaves, departure leaves and then outgoing child subtrees places every directed edge forward in the token order. Each child subtree occupies one interval and its sibling order specifies a plane embedding. Attach ports as separate leaves. Traversing the thickened tree boundary reads their leaf order up to a root seam. At a given z the active paths are vertex-disjoint. Alternating leaf endpoints would force an intersection in that disk. Since each landing precedes its departure, the projected port word is balanced and its unique noncrossing matching is the LIFO stack matching. A same-site landing and departure are ordered in that direction even with zero interior length.

At the eleven-row example I checked LF [5,6,0,7,1,8,9,2,10,3,4], the chord sources (0,1), (8), (10), and the three fibre matchings. At z=−1 the pair is A⁺→B⁻, at 0 it is A⁺→A⁻, and at 1 it is B⁺→C⁻ inside C⁺→A⁻. Offsets give original rows 0→8→10→1→0, also obtained by dense LF and by the pinned executable. The Rust cut test excludes the *entire* arc interval containing the current mark before union, so even a heaviest marked arc is retained as a chord. `new_mark` uses the source-relative offset and the process repeats at each level.

The interval reporter's newest intersecting opening is top-of-stack at every point in that intersection. Restoring only remainders gives the correct frontier. One named pair has one unique tree path, hence an intersection of interval constraints and at most one connected block of heights, including single-height and empty-interior cases. The 64-site control reproduced 4,097 slab matches and 128 maximal interval reports. The stated routing bounds are after the forest, potentials and port order have been built. The timed Rust decoder uses the slab method. A boundary representation alone is enough for unlabelled routing, while the trees and labels are still needed for spelling. ## 5. Band bound and decreasing excess — VERIFIED and tight

A forest with c components has V−c kept arcs, hence q=E−V+c. Start with q landing fragments. A directed topological sweep through sites overlays incoming fragments with the outgoing tiling. Each internal boundary of that tiling can cut the interior of at most one fragment. Coincident boundaries add nothing, and stitching ordered lists adds no pieces. This gives B≤q+Σ_v(deg⁺v−1)=2q−c. The residual has exactly q sites and at most B arcs, so its excess is at most the old E−V. An interior DAG with all placements known is sufficient for this count; it is insufficient for boundary-only recovery. The forest has to span each weak component to identify c with the weak-component count. Weighted flow is conserved at every site, so each arc belongs to a directed closed walk at the site level and the forced marked cut cannot be a weak bridge. Thus on a primitive input the particular Kruskal forest has c=1. Kruskal minimises the chord mass for the given sites and protected chord, rather than optimising all presentations or wall cost.

**Tight primitive witness.** L=`1000`, p=0 gives LF [3,0,1,2]. Two run sites have three arcs. Kruskal excludes 0→3 as marked and the width-two self-arc at source rows 2 and 3; it keeps 1→0. The new chord sites are {0} and {2,3}. Their returns are 0→3, 2→0 and 3→2, in three distinct bands. So (E,V,q,c,B)=(3,2,2,1,3), B=2q−c, and excess remains 1. The witness generalises to primitive `1 0^w` at mark 0. For w≥2 the kept width-one arc always removes exactly one coordinate, the nonterminal two-site shape repeats and excess stays 1 until the final two-row cycle. This takes w cuts and rules out a constant shrink factor or logarithmic depth theorem. The model reproduced this at w=3,8,64. An alternate arbitrary-network tight family uses one site with q self-arcs of widths 1,…,1,q and reversed incoming order. Its incoming and outgoing interior boundary sets are disjoint. Overlaying them gives all 2q−1 bands for any q, although the prototype stops when no interior edge is eligible. [Band proof](https://github.com/lmmx/mibz2/blob/3832bb12857181adb2f46b35f5c7fa8ebaa29a77/docs/journal/2026-11-18-switch-elimination/proof.md#4-how-large-can-a-return-be)

## 6. Length, spelling and periodicity — VERIFIED

Set h(v)−h(u)=|W_e|. The sum of lengths along the directed path from s to t is h(t)−h(s). Add the starting chord's word length. The semantic output word concatenates earlier output-order edge **words** in reverse path order, followed by the chord word. It does not reverse the bytes of those already oriented words. Construction records endpoint and layer references without enumerating children. The writer climbs to the LCA, pushes in LF order and pops in output order. Its cache can only see a strictly earlier-layer dependency during a first expansion, so a repeated request refers to a completely written segment unless output already stopped. This accounts for word expansion and copies, apart from final inverse-list sorting and predecessor lookups when the residual is larger than one row. The claimed expansion-tree bound uses positive lengths and at least two children in every nontrivial recipe.

The marked inverse walk preserves phase and can close on a short cycle. For the valid periodic example L=`bbaa` with p=0, the marked lap spells `ab` and repeats to `abab`. For arbitrary L=`1001`, p=0 the marked cycle contains three of four rows. Its lap spells `001`, and marked extension truncates at `0010`. That is the specified arbitrary-column convention, independent of whether the column represents a valid byte stream. The word-length conservation and inverse output checks in the independent audit include arbitrary cycles and all marks. **Conclusion.** The boundary interface is proved and the observed reductions are reproducible from the recorded census. They coexist with a roughly 15× measured stream cost on the VM. The interval reporter reduces one measured structural count while its native cost remains unmeasured. The tight family sets a concrete limit on what a small excess can promise about recursive work.
