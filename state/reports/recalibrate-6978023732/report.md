# Worker-count recalibration registration review

**Verdict: TARGETED CORRECTION before timing.** Review of #562 at `58c3e68809dd8b0e16d71ef48ff1d43ee00bb819`, replying to #162 comment 6978023732. The worker-count design is suitable for §5c. The memory verdict needs to match the standing policy and the registration needs a realistic power statement for pinned 8. I reviewed the recipe and its reader before seeing any results from this registration.

## Worker ceiling and kernel comparison

The timed roster respects workers ≤ requested threads in both placements. In the unpinned 4, 6 and 8 cells the shipped decoder is already at the ceiling. In pinned 4 and 6 the +1 is the same arm as the untrimmed plan, so it enters the family once. I checked the recipe against the deterministic `with_machine` test and the worker-name check on every record. The named worker check attests the workers observed. Window and kernel equivalence come from the constructor and deterministic test. The named workers' ceiling follows from the registered roster.

The four same-count lazy arms are legitimate candidates. Each holds the worker count and window fixed against its shipped eager reference, and the newly wide kernel is precisely what changed. Each lazy arm is present in its placement's Holm family and faces the same guards. Treat a result as a local kernel choice at that trim entry. Record 56 has no forecast for these arms. A future integration PR still needs a route assertion and the ordinary correctness and CI checks. The registration supplies no evidence for changing the generic lazy threshold.

## Settle and power

A quarter-second is reasonable for a busy-CPU screen under the run budget. The exact code samples background activity for 0.25 s, checks total busy cores ≤1.5 and no other CPU above 0.5, and sleeps and resamples on failure. It measures occupancy, so it does not establish thermal or frequency settling. Rotating and reversing the order balances position even over 12 rounds for four arms. In a four-arm rotation `A B C D` the immediate within-block predecessors are cyclic neighbours even after reversal. That leaves arm-specific carryover possible and makes the short gate more relevant than an argument from position balance alone.

The nearest target-host comparison is the prior pinned 8-thread crossover. I verified the submitted bundle digest `2aec7c34bafbeb494c68e97551272a88d7ca290e82ccb54e77bd3ac723c2d346` and recovered ten paired log ratios for 8 workers over the shipped 6. Their geometric mean is 0.98262 and their SD is 0.03955. The shorter Wikidata crossover is an empirical sensitivity case, since the registered input here is Wikidata ×4. The old crossover's same-plan cell at 9 threads also read 0.975 [0.953, 0.997], a useful example of movement at the scale of the forecast.

| Pinned 8, 8/32 vs 6/24 | 0.983 | 24 | 0.012 / 0.020 / 0.030 / 0.03955 | >99% / 83% / 38% / 18% |

These are noncentral Student t calculations under independent normal paired logs with noncentrality `sqrt(n)*log(ratio)/SD`, at 0.025/12 pinned and 0.025/14 unpinned. The latter first-step cutoff is t₁₁ = −3.689. Later Holm steps can have more power. At the old pinned-8 SD, 24 rounds put an approximate 95% log-interval half-width of 0.0167 around the estimate. For the reverse direction, the SLOWER interval label is a separate nominal screen, with a different threshold from a Holm gain. Replace “must tell” with the conditional sensitivity and retain the registered fallback if unresolved. Keep the 24 rounds and 0.25 s if useful for the schedule. Before starting, record how the archived per-record `pre_busy`, `others_busy`, waits, throttle, and order or early/late contrasts will be inspected, with pre/post clock and temperature from `--sysfs-state` if the target exposes them. Use those summaries to qualify interpretation with the registered rounds and no selective removal, retesting or post hoc switch in the test. C5 admission should verify the exact i9 and pinned spinner from the bundle. The reader's topology test checks shape and quota, and the recipe derives its spinner expectation from `HOLD_CPU`. Those checks alone would accept a same-shape host or a deliberately spinner-free invocation. The registered invocation uses the pinned spinner at 4, 6 and 8.

## Replicates and family error

The recipe runs four distinct batches for the same explicit 7/28 and 8/32 decoders on the same unpinned machine. They estimate the same contrast under four requested counts with the untrimmed arm's window and run context differing. Treat them as repeated batches on one host. Shared environment, fixed batch order and shared per-cell baselines make independence unjustified for a joint forecast or as four votes for one route. Keep their separate cell decisions. The 6/24 result at 10 provides context for the explicit arm at higher requests, without a registered verdict at an unsampled count. Any follow-up must pin the supported scope and affinity. A blanket `TRIMMED_WORKERS` substitution also changes the pinned 10 route and unmeasured requests, so it needs evidence for its full predicate.

I counted 12 pinned and 14 unpinned main-corpus hypotheses, including the kernel alternatives. Holm's control needs valid marginal p-values but no independence across hypotheses. At the first true null rejected, its threshold is at most α divided by the number of true nulls. A union bound over these p-values yields family error ≤ α, and another union bound gives ≤0.05 across placements. That guarantee does not fix biased paired logs or serial dependence within a cell. The simulation's “at one count or more” is conditional on its independent-batch model. It provides neither power for each cell nor authority to select one win and apply it across four. The guard label is a loss screen, so a pass is absence of a detected loss under that screen.

## Rule 4 and the forecast

The reader uses the ratio of median per-process peak RSS values and the difference of those same medians. It screens every measured corpus, checks primary membership, requires a FASTER 95% interval and a point gain of at least 5%, and compares the growth with twice that gain and with 62,500 KiB (64 decimal MB). Wikidata additionally needs a Holm gain for the candidate to adopt, including when the trade is on text. Text's FASTER label is the registered per-corpus beyond-noise screen. The interval establishes gain relative to zero, and the 5% size floor applies to the point estimate.

**Correction before freezing.** Merged rule 4 (#558) says “no other corpus grows by more than 10%.” With the present `all(trade_ok(...))` predicate, both Wikidata and text can exceed 10% and still receive ADOPTS BY TRADE. I ran a synthetic reader case with both at 0.92 wall and +12% RSS (+12,000 KiB each), random unchanged, and a clear Holm gain. It prints ADOPTS BY TRADE. With just Wikidata over the cap it correctly trades, and with just random over it prints HELD ON MEMORY. Encode one over-cap primary at a count and HELD ON MEMORY for two, with a regression case. If simultaneous independent trades are intended, obtain a recorded clarification of rule 4 before freezing that behavior. On a valid single-corpus trade, carry the corpus, count, wall gain, RSS growth in percent and MB, and the C5 run into the integration PR.

A 1.8–2.1% gain for 12–14% more peak RSS should be expected to **hold on memory conditional on a Holm gain and clear loss screen**. A 12% or 14% increase would require at least a 6% or 7% point gain respectively, in addition to the other requirements. Without a Holm gain the verdict is NOT ADOPTED. With a detected loss it is A FINDING, NOT ADOPTED. The historic RSS numbers are a forecast, and this registration measures the actual ratios anew.
