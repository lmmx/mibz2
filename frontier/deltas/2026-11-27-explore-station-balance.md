**What was asked:** the recurring `explore` claim. I tested the assumption under the ninth pass's "knee flips to worker-bound": does the scanner:worker balance measured on wikidata hold on other inputs?
**What I found:**
- **Wikidata is the worker-heavy extreme.** On one thread on the VM, I timed each block's scan (S) and its `decode_block::<OpenCells>` (W) separately (best of 5 passes, 3 interleaved rounds, rounds within 1-5%). The demand ratio k* = W/S is 6.33 on wikidata and 6.47 on its bzip2 -9 re-encoding, so the encoder doesn't change it. On text and binaries it is 3.65-5.39. Relative to wikidata, r = 0.66 prose, 0.58 pysrc, 0.85 rustsrc, 0.72 json, 0.62 mdjournal, 0.64 logs, 0.58 elf. dna gives 0.69 and random 0.54.
- **The frozen rule's verdict:** median r = 0.64 ≤ 0.75, the "corpus-dependent" branch. The wall check applied to no corpus (no VM k* ≤ 2.5).
- **Why:** the scan cost a symbol is flat (2.3-2.8 ns on text). Symbols an output byte rise 1.9-4.6× off wikidata (0.119 → 0.23-0.55). Worker cost an output byte rises less (2.03 → 3.1-7.5 ns).
- **Derived for the i9, not measured:** scaled from the shipped knee of ≈ 8, typical text balances at k* ≈ 4.6-6.8 (median ≈ 5.1). There the shipped decoder is scanner-bound at 8 workers with about a third of worker capacity idle. So the slack-only hybrid's gap S′ − W′/8 is about 0.15-0.43 S′ on text, not the 0-4 ms the ninth pass derives for wikidata.
- **Walls on the VM (supplementary, oversubscribed at k = 3):** from 2 to 3 workers elf plateaus (0.99), mdjournal gains 10% and wikidata 18%. The direction agrees, but elf's plateau (~186 ms) is far above max(S, W/3), so another cost binds on this machine.
