**What was asked:** the recurring `explore` claim. Start cold from primary evidence beyond the surface, avoiding the earlier explore's ground (R, D, cold stores) and the Rauzy dead ends.
**What I found:**
- **The scanner's Huffman decode already does 1.8 symbols a lookup.** A standalone census of the corpus's code lengths (24,979,299 symbols, the scanner's own count) gives 0.5556 root-table lookups a symbol under the shipped 10-bit pair table. At §57's 1.41 ns a symbol that is ~13 cycles a lookup, not "one L1 load a symbol" (record 129). So the dependent load is about 7-8 of those cycles, and ~5 go to per-group work: a second lever.
- **The multi-symbol lever §75 ranked first is about half its conjecture.** 3 or 4 codes an entry at 10 bits cut lookups 12.2% or 15.2%. At constant cost a lookup that is ~10-12% of the scanner, roughly 5-7% of the 8-worker wall, not record 129's 13-17%. Wider roots (B = 12-13: −26% to −36% lookups) leave the L1d: each table's lookups spread over most of it (90% of them over a median 742 of 1,024 entries), and a block's six tables are all hot.
- **The 198 non-Open blocks are runts.** 197 decode to under 1,000 bytes (median 22) and one to 1,183. In 186 of the 197, runt plus predecessor is exactly 900,000 bytes. The dump's compressor cuts its input every 900,000 bytes, and bzip2's level-9 block holds 899,981 RLE1 bytes, so the overflow becomes a block of its own. Every block of real size is Open. The runts cost 0.55% of the scan now (1.29 µs each, VM), but any per-block cost added to the scan is paid by all 431 blocks.
- **Checked and not a problem:** the READY_BYTES floor (`threads + 2` places) never binds on this corpus. 12 MiB ready is ≥14 full blocks, ≈27 places.
